## 3. Topic slate by section

### 3.1 — 01 AI IN MARITIME (lead): recommended thesis **"From Advise to Act"**

**Recommended lead.** Maritime AI is crossing from systems that *advise* a human to systems that *act*: issuing control commands, executing workflows. Governance readiness is far behind that transition. This continues Issue 31's evidence argument logically. Issue 31 asked "who checked the claim?" Issue 32 asks "who authorised the action?"

The lead rests on three items, each with its own label:

**(a) Thetius × Marcura, *Earning Trust: AI in Maritime*** (released around 5 Oct 2026)
- Status: INSTITUTIONAL STUDY / SURVEY. [Likely]
- Reported figures:
  - 63% of respondents use AI daily.
  - 8% describe their organisation as mature and governed.
  - 15% say appropriate AI governance is already in place.
  - Nearly half report colleagues using unapproved AI tools.
  - 55% spend at least one hour a week checking or correcting AI output (the "verification tax").
  - **72% are using, piloting or planning AI agents, but only 15% say governance is ready for AI that acts rather than advises.**
- **Evidence caveat (must be printed):** the survey has **n = 60**, and 78% of respondents are middle management or above. It is a signal, not a census. Do not mix its figures with Marcura/Thetius's separate *Beyond the Hype* study (n ≈ 150).
- Primary to read: the Thetius report page.

**(b) Avikus HiNAS Control: an AI that commands, not advises**
- In the window: on **16 Sep 2026**, HD Hyundai Samho, Avikus and ClassNK signed a joint development project to assess designing HiNAS Control into a 180K m³ LNG carrier. ClassNK will review it against its *Guidelines for Automated/Autonomous Operation of Ships*. Status: ANNOUNCED (design-stage). [Likely]
- Context, outside the window: the HMM order for 40 vessels (Jan 2026). Avikus claims about 350 vessels supplied. Avikus describes the system as "Level 2" with **active vessel control**. Status: OPERATIONALLY DEPLOYED / VENDOR-REPORTED.
- Why it matters: this is the clearest case of AI moving from decision support into the control loop, under class *guidelines* rather than rules. It sits inside the MASS Code's voluntary period.

**(c) ZeroNorth Propel: agentic AI in voyage, vessel and fuel workflows**
- Launched 4 Aug 2026, which is inside Issue 31's window but was not carried there. Launch partners are Cargill, Ultrabulk and CMB.TECH. Wider availability is stated for "later in 2026". Status: ANNOUNCED / EARLY ACCESS. [Likely]
- The "hours saved" claim comes from a single aggregator source. **Do not print it** (see §6).
- Use: carry it as context for the agent trend, not as news.

**Why it reaches the engine room.** When a bridge AI commands speed or heading, the main engine's load programme, shaft-generator load and slow-down/shut-down interactions are being driven by an algorithm. Who signs off the authority boundary between the AI and the engine safety system? (See §3.6.)

**Alternative lead (B): "The engine room is now a cyber target"** (the VL Prosperity story, §3.2). It has the stronger news value. It is weaker on AI as such: no AI involvement in the incident has been reported. Recommendation: **run it as section 02, not as the lead**, so the issue keeps its AI-first focus. Then link the two stories in the Editor's Synthesis: the more systems act on the machinery, the more it matters who else can reach those systems.

**Short items for the AI section (one paragraph each):**
- **CyberLogitec × BNCT, Busan New Port (Oct 2026).** Tag-free AI collision prevention between workers and straddle carriers, with alerts at 15, 10 and 5 m. Status: OPERATIONALLY DEPLOYED / VENDOR-REPORTED. The accuracy figures (< 3 cm equipment, < 20 cm worker) are the vendor's own field validation. [Likely]
- **BIMCO AI-in-contracts survey (5 Aug 2026)**, a late carry:
  - About 20% of Documentary Committee members already use AI for contract work.
  - About 70% expect to adopt it within 3–5 years.
  - About a quarter have seen AI-drafted clauses replacing established wording.
  - BIMCO also issued a *Contract Authenticity Clause*.
  - It is out of window, so print it only with a "not carried in Issue 31" disclosure. Otherwise drop it. [Likely]
- **Onboard offline AI assistants** (ShipGod AI profiled on Splash247 around Oct 2026; also Full Fathom, SeaSense, ShipSmith): a vendor-marketing cluster. These are **not news-grade**. They are worth one Engineer's Desk line about grounding answers in vessel documentation and the citation question. **Do not name vendors as endorsed.** [Speculative as to adoption]

### 3.2 — 02 DIGITAL / AUTONOMY / CYBER

**(a) VL Prosperity: investigators say hackers reached a propulsion-connected system** (strongest news item of the window)
- Facts consistent across Bloomberg (2 Oct), gCaptain, Baird Maritime and Security Magazine. [Likely]
  - Liberian-flag VLCC, about 320,000 DWT, built 2015, IMO 9683697. Ship-data sites list HMM Ocean Service as ISM manager (verify).
  - A joint USCG–FBI team boarded on **21 Aug 2026**, after communications were lost and indications of network compromise were received.
  - A second vessel was boarded in the Gulf of Mexico on **24 Aug**.
  - Investigators concluded hackers *briefly accessed a digital system connected to propulsion*. **How long the access lasted, what it could control, how entry was gained and who was responsible are all unknown.**
  - FBI statement: no reports of operational disruption, vessel instability, danger to crew or environmental impact.
  - Bloomberg (16 Sep): US agencies are tracking cyber threats against **nearly 20 vessels** worldwide. USCG has asked for advance notice of US port calls by those ships.
- Status: UNDER INVESTIGATION. The sources are anonymous officials plus a short FBI statement.
- **Precision rules for this story:**
  - Write "accessed a system connected to propulsion", **not** "took control of the engine".
  - The claim that engine-room, fuel and lube-oil systems were "reported affected" (Red Eye Security blog) is **unverified**. Hold it back.
  - The **Vivit Africa** LNG carrier allegation is unverified. Hold it back.
  - **Do not connect this incident to HMM's Avikus order**, or to any AI system. No such link has been reported.
- **Engineer angle (high):** a 2015-built ship is outside IACS UR E26/E27 as applied to new ships contracted on or after 1 Jul 2024 (verify the applicability text). This is the legacy-fleet gap. The USCG cyber rule (33 CFR Part 101 Subpart F) is already in the Watchlist for 16 Jul 2027.

**(b) MSC 112 preview (14–18 Dec): the MASS Code Experience Building Phase framework**
- MSC 112 re-establishes the MASS Working Group to develop the EBP framework. Extension to passenger ships remains deferred. [Likely]
- **Cross-product check required (L3):** the IMO autonomous-shipping FAQ, as summarised in search, lists **"2028: development of the mandatory MASS Code"**. Issue 31 and the Engineer's Desk MEO box carry **adoption 1 Jul 2030, EIF via SOLAS 1 Jan 2032**. These may be compatible (develop in 2028, adopt in 2030), but this must be **reconciled against the IMO primary**. The 2030/2032 figures are also probably in the Question Bank. Run the cross-product check before Issue 32 repeats them.

**(c) EU AI Act and marine equipment** (Watchlist candidate; status UNVERIFIED)
- Article 108 of the AI Act amends the Marine Equipment Directive 2014/90/EU, which could bring AI in navigation equipment into the high-risk pathway (per secondary sources).
- The application dates are contested. The "Digital Omnibus" stop-the-clock proposal (19 Nov 2025) would defer Annex III high-risk obligations to as late as 2 Dec 2027, and embedded-product obligations to 2 Aug 2028. One commentary says it was adopted.
- **Requires the Official Journal text.** Do not print any date until that is read. [Speculative]

**(d) GNSS:** Continue the Issue 31 withholding policy. The new item found is IRClass Technical Circular No. 12 on GNSS spoofing and jamming (date not confirmed). Print it only after reading it.

### 3.6 — 06 ENGINEER'S DESK / MEO CLASS I (four items, one MEO box)

1. **Who can reach your propulsion control?** Arising from VL Prosperity. Map every path into the ME control system and the AMS: vendor remote-service links, satellite-connected PCs, USB ports and maintenance laptops. Test whether "segregated" means physically separated or merely on a separate VLAN. Know the local/manual fallback and when it was last drilled.
2. **When the bridge AI commands the engine.** Arising from Avikus Level-2 control. When an algorithm changes speed and heading:
   - Which ME load-up/load-down limits still bind it?
   - What happens at the barred speed range?
   - How does it interact with the shaft generator or PTO?
   - What does the engine safety system (slow-down/shut-down) override?
   Each one is a commissioning and FMEA question for the Chief Engineer.
3. **UK ETS data starts on board now.** The data for the first surrender (Apr 2028) is being produced in the engine room from 1 Jul 2026, and the same MRV reconciliation discipline as EU ETS applies.
4. **AI tools that read your manuals.** Onboard document assistants are only as good as the documents they index and the citation they show. The engineer's check is whether the tool cites the manual page or merely paraphrases it.

**MEO box (one only).** IACS UR **E26 vs E27**:
- E26 covers the ship's cyber resilience as a whole: design, commissioning and operation.
- E27 covers the cyber resilience of individual onboard computer-based systems and equipment.
- Both apply to ships contracted for construction on or after **1 Jul 2024**.
- The trap: an existing ship is **not** retroactively in scope.
- [Certain on structure; verify the exact applicability wording against the IACS primary before printing.]

### 3.8 — 08 EDITOR'S SYNTHESIS (angle)

**"Authority, not intelligence."** Issue 31 asked whether AI claims were checked. Issue 32 should ask whether AI *actions* are authorised.
- The survey says 72% of firms are moving toward agents, and 15% are governance-ready for them.
- A Level-2 system is going into an LNG carrier design under class guidelines, not rules.
- In the same weeks, investigators found that someone reached a system connected to a VLCC's propulsion.

These three facts are unrelated in cause. They point to one question: who may command the machine, and how would anyone know if someone else did?
