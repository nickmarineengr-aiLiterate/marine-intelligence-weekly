Concept Map — The Strict-Liability Convention Architecture

The pattern answer Nair rewards: channelled owner liability + short exceptions + compulsory insurance + direct action — recited once, it answers WRC, CLC and Bunkers questions alike.

Confusable Pairs — Examiner Favourites

⚠ Confusable Pair: Strict vs Absolute vs Unlimited Liability
Strict = no fault needed, but listed exceptions exist (WRC/CLC). Absolute = no exceptions at all. Unlimited = quantum question — strict liability is still generally limitable under LLMC. Three separate axes; mixing them is the classic fail.
⚠ Confusable Pair: Safety Investigation vs Liability/Blame Proceedings
Flag-State marine safety investigation (Casualty Investigation Code, SOLAS XI-1/6) exists ONLY to prevent recurrence — report to GISIS, fair treatment of seafarers. Formal investigation/court & civil/criminal processes run separately and can touch certificates and damages. Never merge them in an answer.
⚠ Confusable Pair: Articles of Agreement vs SEA
Articles = classical collective crew agreement, signed/closed before the Shipping Master under the MS Act. SEA (MLC Std A2.1) = individual agreement, reviewed before signing, copy onboard, neutral discharge record (A2.1.3). Successor relationship — same function, rebuilt individually.
⚠ Confusable Pair: P&I vs Own-Loss Covers (delay claims)
P&I pays the member's liabilities to THIRD parties. The ship's OWN delay/trading loss needs Loss of Hire cover; hull pays damage, not time. And GA never pays delay (York-Antwerp exclusion). Four routes: 1910 proportionate fault / off-hire clause / LoH policy / nothing.
⚠ Confusable Pair: MS Act 1958 vs MS Act 2025
The 1958 Act (with the famous ss. 358–360 casualty family) is repealed. The MS Act 2025 (Act 24 of 2025) governs from 15 Mar 2026; DG Shipping is now DGMA. Cite the 2025 Act at Part level with sections pending verification — quoting 1958 numbering is the stale-law trap.

Topic Clusters — Tap to Recall

Conventions & Casualty Machinery
Q1. Amendment ratification in India
Tap to reveal the 15-second answer →
Ratification is EXECUTIVE: sponsoring ministry proposes, Union Cabinet decides, MEA deposits with IMO SG. Parliament enters only for implementing law (Art. 253) — e.g. MS Act 2025 (EIF 15 Mar 2026). Thereafter most SOLAS/MARPOL amendments bind via tacit acceptance (SOLAS Art. VIII), implemented by DGMA notification.
Q2. Ship casualty — step by step
Tap to reveal the 15-second answer →
Phase 1 emergency: save life, SOPEP, VDR/evidence preserved. Phase 2 notify: DPA, flag, coastal State (MARPOL Art. 8), class, P&I. Phase 3 investigations in parallel: flag marine safety investigation (MSC.255(84)/SOLAS XI-1/6 — mandatory for very serious casualty, report to GISIS), company ISM Sec. 9, class, liability tracks separate. India: DGMA/MMD, MS Act 2025 machinery [Part-level].
Q11. Casualty definitions — MS Act
Tap to reveal the 15-second answer →
Use Code definitions: marine casualty (death, ship loss, material damage, stranding, collision, severe environmental damage); very serious casualty (2.22: total loss/loss of life/severe env damage) → mandatory investigation; marine incident (endangerment only). Indian machinery (report → preliminary inquiry → formal investigation with certificate powers) now under MS Act 2025 — Part-level, never 1958 s.358.
Liability & Insurance
Q3. Casualty delay — who compensates
Tap to reveal the 15-second answer →
Cause decides — four routes: third party at fault → detention damages in proportion (1910 Convention), capped by LLMC; owner-charterer → off-hire clause; own casualty → Loss of Hire insurance or bear it; GA NEVER pays delay (York-Antwerp exclusion). P&I = third-party liabilities only.
Q7. WRC — strict liability
Tap to reveal the 15-second answer →
Nairobi WRC 2007 (EIF 14 Apr 2015): registered owner strictly liable — no fault needed — for locating, marking, removing a hazardous wreck in the EEZ (+opt-in waters). Four exceptions: war / exceptional natural phenomenon / third-party intent / nav-aids authority negligence. ≥300 GT compulsory insurance + direct action vs insurer. Strict ≠ absolute.
Q8. Types of liability in shipping
Tap to reveal the 15-second answer →
Six categories: fault (collision 1910) · strict (CLC/Bunkers/WRC family architecture) · contractual (Hague-Visby, charterparties) · vicarious (owner for crew) · statutory (MS Act penalties) · criminal (ORB falsification, individuals included). Two overlays: LLMC limitation (broken only by intent/reckless-with-knowledge) and maritime liens/arrest.
Q12. Strict liability in marine insurance
Tap to reveal the 15-second answer →
Correct the premise: marine insurance is contract-based indemnity, uberrimae fidei (MIA 1963/1906) — not strict liability. Two honest senses: (a) proved insured peril pays despite crew negligence — only the assured's wilful misconduct defeats it (s.55 logic); (b) insurers stand behind CLC/Bunkers/WRC via blue cards, compulsory certificates and direct action.
MLC, Crew & Management
Q4. No salary/sign-off — grievance route
Tap to reveal the 15-second answer →
Ladder: (1) onboard complaint procedure Reg 5.1.5 (written, dated, SEA+wage figures, no victimisation); (2) DPA/flag; (3) onshore complaint to PSC Reg 5.2.2 — unpaid wages detainable; (4) ITF, RPSL/DGMA, Shipping Master. Closers: ≥2 months unpaid = abandonment limb → A2.5.2 security (up to 4 months wages); wages = maritime lien → arrest (Admiralty Act 2017).
Q5. Junior officer appraisal
Tap to reveal the 15-second answer →
Five steps: publish criteria at joining → dated evidence continuously → score to behavioural anchors (STCW A-III/1 as technical yardstick, bias controlled) → face-to-face interview with recorded comments → both signatures + development plan, routed per SMS. Adverse appraisal never a surprise — documented counselling first.
Q6. Evaluating a new company / MLC
Tap to reveal the 15-second answer →
Three layers with sources: agent — RPSL licence on DGMA portal, no placement fees (Reg 1.4); employer — Equasis/MOU detention record, abandonment database, flag list; paper — SEA reviewed BEFORE signing (A2.1), CBA, MLC Certificate + DMLC I/II, posted A2.5.2 & A4.2 financial-security certificates. Red flags: fees, sign-on-arrival, no CBA, wage detentions.
Q10. Articles of Agreement
Tap to reveal the 15-second answer →
Classical collective crew agreement — master (for owner) & crew, signed/closed before the Shipping Master under the MS Act: names/capacities, wages, provisions, voyage limits, crew list annexed. Modern successor: MLC SEA (A2.1) — individual, reviewed before signing, copy onboard, neutral discharge record (A2.1.3). Indian machinery continues under MS Act 2025 [Part-level].
Commercial & Certificates
Q9. Contract of Affreightment
Tap to reveal the 15-second answer →
Two senses: broad — any carriage-of-goods contract (charterparties + B/Ls); specific CoA — quantity over a period in multiple voyages, ship NOMINATED per lifting (GENCOA), each lifting on incorporated voyage terms, B/L issued per shipment (Hague-Visby). B/L dual function: charterparty governs between owner-charterer; B/L is the contract in a third holder's hands.
Q13. Certificates the CE manages / SMS revision
Tap to reveal the 15-second answer →
Families: SAFCON (machinery) · MARPOL set — IOPP+Supplement/ORB, IAPP + per-engine EIAPP/NOx Technical File, SEEMP I–III/DCS/CII, ISPP, garbage · BWM · class machinery/boiler/shaft. SMS revision: NOT from the certificate — DOC/SMC endorsements only show last verification; the ISM Sec. 11 controlled-document master list states the current revision.
