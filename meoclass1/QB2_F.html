What are the latest amendments governing carriage of IMDG cargo?
SOLAS VII|IMDG
15-Second AnswerIMDG Code Amendment 42-24 became mandatory on 1 Jan 2026, tightening rules for charcoal/carbon cargo, lithium and sodium-ion batteries, and updating the EmS Guide to Rev.3.
60-Second AnswerAs Chief Engineer I track IMDG Amendment 42-24 (mandatory since 1 Jan 2026): tighter charcoal self-heating tests, new UN entries for lithium/sodium-ion batteries and battery vehicles replacing UN 3171, and a revised EmS Guide (MSC.1/Circ.1588/Rev.3). I verify hold ventilation, DoC segregation limits and gas detection are matched to the manifest before loading.

Regulatory Context

Carriage of dangerous goods by sea is governed by SOLAS Chapter VII and detailed technically in the IMDG Code. The 2024 Edition, incorporating Amendment 42-24 (adopted under Resolution MSC.556(108)), applied voluntarily from 1 January 2025 and became mandatory from 1 January 2026, fully superseding Amendment 41-22.

Key Changes

  • Carbonaceous/charcoal cargo: tightened testing and weathering-certificate scrutiny under Special Provision 925 following container fires linked to self-heating charcoal.
  • Lithium & sodium-ion batteries: new UN entries (e.g. UN 3551/3552 sodium-ion, UN 3556-3558 battery-powered vehicles) replacing the generic UN 3171 entry, plus State-of-Charge restrictions for bulk transport.
  • EmS Guide: updated to MSC.1/Circ.1588/Rev.3 to align emergency schedules with the new entries.

Operational Segregation

  • Self-reactive/oxidising classes (4.2, 5.1) require documented thermal buffer distance from heat sources such as fuel-oil deep tanks.
  • The vessel's Document of Compliance for carriage of DG must match bay/slot/stack limits for the loaded UN classes.

Key Numbers

Amendment 42-24 mandatory from 1 Jan 2026 (voluntary from 1 Jan 2025); EmS Guide at MSC.1/Circ.1588/Rev.3.

CE Oral Tip: Nair frequently tests candidates on charcoal declarations. State clearly: an unverified non-hazardous certificate is not sufficient — the cargo must pass the UN Class 4.2 self-heating test before under-deck stowage is considered, and even then boundary heat sources must be avoided.
CE Relevance

The Chief Engineer verifies hold ventilation is spark-proof, bilge lines serving DG holds are isolated from machinery-space bilges, and gas-detection heads are calibrated to the manifest's specific hazard classes.

Trap Questions

"A shipper certifies the charcoal is non-hazardous — do you load it under deck?" — Only after independent Class 4.2 self-heating test verification, and even then stow with a thermal buffer clear of engine-room bulkheads.

Common CE Failures
  • Quoting the superseded Amendment 41-22 as current after 1 Jan 2026
  • Not knowing which UN entries replaced the generic UN 3171 vehicle entry
Numbers & Regulations to Memorise
  • 1 Jan 2026: Amendment 42-24 becomes mandatory
  • UN 3551/3552: new sodium-ion battery entries
Casualty Link

A container catches fire and the UN number shows different EmS symptoms — how do you approach and fight it?
Fire Safety|EmS
15-Second AnswerThe UN number is used to pull the correct EmS Fire Schedule; water-reactive cargo (4.3) is fought differently from self-oxidising cargo (5.1/5.2), which cannot be smothered by CO2.
60-Second AnswerI extract the EmS Fire Schedule from the UN number, then decide tactics: sustained water cooling for oxidisers that supply their own oxygen, dry-agent/controlled-burn with boundary cooling for water-reactive cargo. I run pumps in parallel, isolate reefer power, trip ventilation as directed, and deploy the lance or monitors to attack the seat of fire without direct entry.

Regulatory Context

Firefighting tactics for declared DG cargo are governed by the EmS Guide (IMDG Code Supplement), cross-referenced from the UN number on the manifest, together with SOLAS II-2/10 fire-fighting arrangements.

Reading the EmS Symptoms

  • Class 5.1/5.2 (oxidisers/peroxides): generate their own oxygen — CO2 suffocation fails; sustained water cooling is the primary tactic.
  • Class 4.3 (water-reactive): water triggers explosion/toxic gas; EmS directs dry agents or controlled burn with boundary cooling only.

CE Actions

  • Run main and emergency fire pumps in parallel for monitor pressure at height.
  • Trip hold mechanical ventilation unless EmS calls for continuous exhaust of toxic gas.
  • Isolate reefer power distribution boards in the affected bay.

Tactical Deployment

  • Boundary-cool all six sides of the affected slot.
  • Use a water-mist lance pierced through the container skin to attack seat-of-fire without opening doors.
Examiner Trap"EmS says do not use water, but adjacent containers are igniting — what do you do?" — Do not apply water to the source container; apply massive boundary cooling to surrounding boxes and deck plating instead.
Regulatory References
SOLAS II-2/10Fire-fighting arrangements
IMDG Code Supplement — EmS GuideFire (F) and Spillage (S) emergency schedules keyed to UN number
MSC.1/Circ.1458Guidelines for open-deck container fire-fighting evaluation
CE Oral Tip: Nair probes decision-making under EmS constraints. Show you prioritise hull integrity: never dump water onto a water-reactive fire, but never withhold boundary cooling from adjacent stacks.
CE Relevance

Fire-main hydraulic capacity is the limiting factor — running multiple monitors plus deck lines can drop pressure below the required minimum; coordinate with the Second Engineer on auxiliary generator loading to avoid blackout.

Trap Questions

"Will you release fixed CO2 into an unidentified hold fire immediately?" — No; a Class 5.1/5.2 self-oxidising cargo will not be extinguished by CO2 and the fixed charge would be wasted.

Common CE Failures
  • Forgetting CO2 cannot extinguish self-oxidising (5.1/5.2) cargo fires
  • Not isolating reefer power before deploying water near the bay
Numbers & Regulations to Memorise
  • EmS schedules split into Fire (F) and Spillage (S) categories
  • Minimum fire-main pressure benchmark: 0.27 MPa at deck monitors
Casualty Link

Maersk Honam (2018): catastrophic under-deck chemical-cargo fire — illustrates the danger of oxidising cargo reacting against conventional tactics.

What are the latest SOLAS Chapter II amendments and their key changes?
SOLAS II-2|PFOS|Ro-Ro
15-Second AnswerTwo distinct packages, both in force 1 January 2026. Res. MSC.532(107) prohibits PFOS fire-extinguishing media under new SOLAS II-2/10.11 and adds II-1/3-13 for lifting appliances. Res. MSC.550(108) rewrites II-2/20 for vehicle, special category, open and closed ro-ro spaces and vehicle-carrying weather decks — smoke and heat detection, video monitoring and weather-deck monitors, mostly on passenger ships — with the FSS Code support in Res. MSC.555(108).
60-Second AnswerDo not roll the 2026 tranche into one resolution — two separate instruments carry it. MSC.532(107) (adopted 8 Jun 2023) inserts II-2/10.11 prohibiting use or storage of PFOS fire-extinguishing media, and new II-1/3-13 for lifting appliances and anchor handling winches. MSC.550(108) (adopted 23 May 2024) is the fire-safety package: it replaces II-2/20 for vehicle, special category, open and closed ro-ro spaces and now weather decks intended for vehicles, requiring individually identifiable smoke and heat detection, video monitoring, weather-deck monitors and matched deluge/alarm sectioning — and it also rewrites II-2/7.5.5, so smoke detection in all control stations and cargo control rooms is MSC.550(108), not MSC.532(107). Application sits in each chapeau, not in the requirement: the video monitoring, weather-deck monitors and structural package are passenger-ship duties, a new cargo ship gets very little, and existing passenger ships with those spaces retrofit three regulations by the first survey on or after 1 Jan 2028.

Regulatory Context

SOLAS Chapter II is split into II-1 (construction — structure, stability, machinery, electrical) and II-2 (fire protection, detection and extinction). Several amendments entered into force on 1 January 2026.

Chapter II-2 — Package 1: Res. MSC.532(107) (adopted 8 Jun 2023)

  • PFOS ban: inserts Regulation II-2/10.11. The regulation text is an unqualified prohibition — "use or storage of extinguishing media containing perfluorooctane sulfonic acid (PFOS) shall be prohibited" — and media removed from the ship must go to shore reception facilities. The familiar 10 mg/kg (0.001% by weight) figure is not in SOLAS: it comes from the unified interpretation in MSC.1/Circ.1694 (4 Jul 2025), mirrored by IACS UI SC309. Reg. 10.11 applies to ships constructed on/after 1 Jan 2026; new II-2/1.2.10 brings all ships built before that date into 10.11.2 by the first survey on/after 1 Jan 2026.

Chapter II-2 — Package 2: Res. MSC.550(108) (adopted 23 May 2024), with FSS Code Res. MSC.555(108)

This — not MSC.532(107) — is the ro-ro and vehicle-space fire-safety package. The resolution is titled Amendments to chapters II-2 and V — its chapter V limb is the new container-loss reporting duty under V/31 and V/32, which is outside this question. The fire-safety limb was driven by the EMSA FIRESAFE studies and the car-deck fire record. Regulation 20 is retitled "Protection of vehicle, special category, open and closed ro-ro spaces, and weather decks intended for the carriage of vehicles", taking SOLAS fire protection onto the open weather deck for the first time.

  • Detection on PASSENGER ships (II-2/20.4.1.1–.1.4): the 20.4.1 chapeau limits these to passenger ships built on/after 1 Jan 2026. They require an individually identifiable fixed detection and alarm system giving smoke and heat detection throughout vehicle, special category and ro-ro spaces (linear heat detectors may be accepted for the heat limb); detection sections matching the deluge sections where a fixed water-based system is fitted; an interface giving logical, unambiguous presentation for quick, correct decision-making; and separate detection on the weather deck intended for vehicles, with different settings permitted for loading/discharge and sea passage to cut false alarms.
  • Detection on CARGO ships (II-2/20.4.1.5): read this one carefully, because it is where candidates over-claim. A cargo ship built on/after 1 Jan 2026 needs a fixed detection and alarm system complying with the FSS Code, capable of rapidly detecting the onset of fire, with detector type, spacing and location to the Administration's satisfaction and tested after installation under normal ventilation conditions. It does not import individual identifiability, mandated smoke and heat coverage, or deluge-section matching — those are the passenger-ship limbs. Cargo ships also pick up revised 20.3.1.5 on the siting of permanent openings in the side plating, ends or deckhead, so that a cargo-space fire does not endanger survival-craft stowage and embarkation stations or the accommodation, service spaces and control stations above. Note that 20.3.1.5, unlike 20.4.1, 20.4.4, 20.5, 20.6.1 and 20.7, carries no construction-date chapeau of its own.
  • Video monitoring (II-2/20.4.4) — entirely new, PASSENGER ships: continuous video coverage of vehicle, special category and ro-ro spaces with immediate playback, cameras mounted high enough to see over loaded vehicles. Replayable at a continuously manned control station or the safety centre for at least seven days on ro-ro passenger ships built on/after 1 Jan 2026 and 24 hours on existing ones. Each camera must be cross-referenced, at the monitor, to the fixed water-based section it covers. Continuous watching of the image by the crew is not required.
  • Weather-deck extinguishing (II-2/20.6.2) — RO-RO PASSENGER ships: 20.6.2.1 opens "In passenger ships", and the section 6.1 chapeau confines 6.2.1 and 6.2.2 to ro-ro passenger ships built on/after 1 Jan 2026: a fixed water-based monitor system covering weather decks intended for vehicles, with drainage sized to remove no less than 125% of the combined monitor and required hose-nozzle capacity. FSS Code Ch. 7 (new 2.5), which applies to the same population, sets the spec: the combined capacity of all fixed monitors at least 2.0 L/min per m² of protected area, and in no case any monitor below 1,250 L/min; the farthest point protected forward of a monitor not more than 75% of its still-air throw; and 5.0 L/min per m² nozzles for any area a monitor cannot reach.
  • Structural fire protection (II-2/20.5) — PASSENGER ships built on/after 1 Jan 2026: "A-60" boundary bulkheads and decks to special category and ro-ro spaces on ships carrying more than 36 passengers; "A-30" internal decks where the fixed water-based system cannot cover above and below simultaneously. The "A-60" standard may be reduced to "A-0" where a category (5), (9) or (10) space is on one side of the division, and the deck may be "A-0" where fuel oil tanks sit below a special category space (20.5.1.1). Then two distinct distance regimes, which candidates routinely merge:
    • Openings in ro-ro and special category spaces (20.5.2.1): not permitted on any deck directly below, or within a minimum 6.0 m measured horizontally of, survival-craft stowage, embarkation and assembly stations and accommodation, control stations and normally occupied service spaces. There is no 3.0 m relief here. The only relief is 20.5.2.3, and it is a boundary-integrity condition rather than a distance: an opening below accommodation, control stations or normally occupied service spaces is accepted where the ship's side including windows and doors is "A-60" over a rectangle 6.0 m forward and aft of the opening and at least two deck levels above — and it does not extend to survival craft or embarkation stations, for which 20.5.2.2 instead requires steel or minimum "A-0" ramps and doors.
    • Weather-deck vehicle lanes (20.5.3): more than 6.0 m to accommodation, control stations and normally occupied service spaces, and more than 12.0 m to survival craft and embarkation stations. This is the distance that 20.5.3.3 allows to be cut to 3.0 m, where boundaries within 6.0 m are "A-60", or "A-0" protected by a water-based system at 5.0 L/min per m².
    Machinery and emergency-generator air intakes must be sited to minimise contamination by a ro-ro or weather-deck fire.
  • Fire patrol and decision-making: an efficient fire patrol system in special category spaces (20.4.3.1), and, where fixed pressure water-spraying systems are fitted, photoluminescent section signage inside the spaces matching the section-valve and safety-centre numbering (20.7, passenger ships built on/after 1 Jan 2026). No water-spraying system, no signage duty.
  • Detection outside reg. 20 (II-2/7): 7.5.5 is rewritten so that for cargo ships built on/after 1 Jan 2026 all three protection methods (IC, IIC and IIIC) require smoke detection in all control stations and cargo control rooms — MSC.550(108), a point commonly and wrongly credited to MSC.532(107). 7.5.2 is also replaced, extending smoke detection on passenger ships carrying more than 36 passengers to service spaces, control stations and accommodation including corridors, stairways and escape routes, with cabin detectors also sounding locally; private bathrooms, galleys and low-fire-risk spaces such as voids and public toilets are excepted.
  • Detector disconnection during cargo work (FSS Code 9/2.5.1.4): on ro-ro passenger ships built on/after 1 Jan 2026 the smoke detector function in special category and ro-ro spaces may be disconnected during loading and unloading — the exhaust-fume false-alarm problem — but only for a predetermined period matched to the cargo operation, with automatic reset afterwards and the central unit indicating which sections are disconnected. Disconnecting the heat detection function or the manual call points is not permitted. FSS 9 also now requires a consistent alarm presentation scheme and alarm addressability with alarm history on those ships.
  • Fuel quality (II-2/4.2.1.9) — the CE's own line in this package: the same resolution adds a new sub-paragraph requiring that "oil fuel delivered to and used on board ships shall not jeopardize the safety of ships or adversely affect the performance of the machinery or be harmful to personnel." It is short, it is Chapter II-2, and it is the hook for a fuel-quality follow-up.

Who has to do what, and when — four populations, not two

This is where the question is won or lost. Reg. 20 puts its application in the chapeau of each section, not in the requirement, so quoting a requirement without its population is the classic error.

  • NEW passenger ships (constructed on/after 1 Jan 2026): 20.4.1.1–.1.4 detection, 20.4.4 video monitoring (7-day replay on ro-ro passenger ships), 20.5 structural fire protection and openings, and 20.7 decision-making signage. Ro-ro passenger ships additionally get 20.6.2.1/.2 weather-deck monitors and drainage, specified by FSS 7/2.5.
  • NEW cargo ships (constructed on/after 1 Jan 2026): far less than candidates assume — 20.4.1.5 detection and II-2/7.5.5 detection in all control stations and cargo control rooms, plus revised 20.3.1.5 on permanent openings (which states no construction-date limit of its own). A new pure car and truck carrier is a cargo ship: it gets no mandatory video monitoring, no weather-deck monitor system and no reg. 20 structural package under these amendments. Say so if you are asked.
  • EXISTING passenger ships with vehicle, special category or ro-ro spaces (built before 1 Jan 2026, including those built before 1 Jul 2012): new 20.2.1.3 pulls them into exactly three regulations — 20.4.1.6 (smoke and heat detection throughout, with heat detectors required only where a smoke detector already exists and to smoke-detector spacing and coverage), 20.4.4 (video monitoring, 24-hour replay) and 20.6.2.3 (weather-deck monitors of at least 1,250 L/min each, where there are weather decks intended for vehicles, and the Administration may permit a lower rate or accept an existing installation) — all not later than the first survey on or after 1 January 2028. Note that FSS 7/2.5 does not apply to this retrofit, so the 2.0 L/min per m² figure is not their number.
  • EXISTING cargo ships: no reg. 20 retrofit at all — they continue to meet the previously applicable requirements. The retrofit burden in this package is a passenger-ship burden.

Currency note — in force versus adopted: everything above is in force as at August 2026. There is one further Chapter II item you should name, and be clear that it is adopted but not yet in force: Res. MSC.549(108), adopted at the same session as MSC.550(108) on 23 May 2024, adds a new section 2 to SOLAS II-1/3-4 requiring emergency towing arrangements on ships other than tankers of not less than 20,000 GT constructed on or after 1 January 2028 — capable of rapid deployment without main power on the ship being towed, of adequate strength for the ship's size and expected bad-weather forces, with design, construction and prototype testing approved by the Administration to IMO guidelines. It enters into force 1 January 2028. The other 2028 items (pilot transfer arrangements under chapter V) are outside chapter II and should not be pulled into this answer.

Chapter II-1 — New Reg. II-1/3-13 (MSC.532(107))

  • What counts: the definition (new II-1/2.30) is deliberately wide and expressly includes engine-room cranes and stores cranes, along with cargo handling gear, hatch-cover and movable-bulkhead gear, hose-handling and personnel-handling cranes and tender launch and recovery. Anchor handling winches (2.31) and loose gear (2.32) are defined separately.
  • Lifting appliances installed on/after 1 Jan 2026: designed, constructed and installed to the requirements of a recognised classification society or equivalent standards acceptable to the Administration; load tested and thoroughly examined after installation and before first use and after major repair, modification or alteration; and permanently marked with documentary evidence of the SWL.
  • Anchor handling winches installed on/after 1 Jan 2026: a different test — designed, constructed, installed and tested to the satisfaction of the Administration, based on IMO Guidelines. Not the class-standard route, and no SWL marking requirement.
  • What "installed on or after 1 January 2026" actually means (new II-1/2.33): for a ship whose keel was laid on/after 1 Jan 2026, any installation date on that ship. For every other ship — including one built before 2009 — the test is the contractual delivery date of the appliance or winch to the ship, or failing that its actual delivery date. So a crane delivered in 2027 to a 2010-built ship is "installed on or after 1 January 2026" and takes the full new-equipment route. This is the definition the examiner probes.
  • Installed before 1 Jan 2026: lifting appliances must be tested, thoroughly examined and SWL-marked; anchor handling winches must be tested and thoroughly examined only — both no later than the first renewal survey on or after 1 Jan 2026.
  • Exclusions worth knowing: the regulation does not apply to lifting appliances on MODUs, to offshore construction ship gear meeting standards acceptable to the Administration, to integrated hatch-cover opening and closing machinery, or to LSA Code launching appliances. The Administration also decides how far paragraphs 2.1 and 2.4 apply below 1,000 kg SWL.
  • Regardless of installation date: all lifting appliances and anchor handling winches, and all loose gear used with them, must be operationally tested, thoroughly examined, inspected, operated and maintained to IMO Guidelines. And — except as provided in regulation I/11(c) — a malfunction shall not be assumed to make the ship unseaworthy or to justify delaying her in port, provided the master has taken the inoperative equipment into account in planning and executing a safe voyage.

Key Numbers

Both packages in force 1 Jan 2026. PFOS (MSC.532(107)): threshold 10 mg/kg (0.001% wt) — from MSC.1/Circ.1694, not from SOLAS; existing ships by first survey on/after 1 Jan 2026. Ro-ro fire safety (MSC.550(108) / FSS MSC.555(108)): video replay 7 days (new ro-ro passenger ship) / 24 h (existing); weather-deck monitors on new ro-ro passenger ships — combined output ≥ 2.0 L/min per m² and no single monitor < 1,250 L/min (FSS 7/2.5), drainage 125%; on the existing-ship retrofit the only figure is 1,250 L/min per monitor and FSS 7/2.5 does not apply. Distances: openings 6.0 m with no distance relief; weather-deck lanes >6.0 m (reducible to 3.0 m for "A-60" boundaries) and >12.0 m to survival craft. Existing passenger ships: first survey on or after 1 Jan 2028.

CE Oral Tip: Nair targets PFOS compliance evidence. If asked how you prove compliance: state that the flag/RO reviews the manufacturer's PFOS-free declaration or lab test report against the 10 mg/kg threshold, checked at the first survey on or after 1 Jan 2026, and that removed media go to appropriate shore-based reception facilities. Do not claim an official log book entry — II-2/10.11.2.1 requires the delivery, not a log entry, and MSC.1/Circ.1694 points to declarations and test reports rather than to logging. On the ro-ro limb, name the resolution before you describe the requirement — MSC.550(108) for SOLAS II-2/20 and MSC.555(108) for the FSS Code — then give the population before the requirement: new passenger ships from 1 Jan 2026, existing passenger ships with those spaces by the first survey on or after 1 Jan 2028, and very little for cargo ships either way. Two ways to lose this question: quoting MSC.532(107), and answering as though a car carrier gets the passenger-ship package.
CE Relevance

Update foam inventories, PMS tasks and SMS procedures to reflect PFOS-free certification; ensure disposal receipts from a licensed facility are retained for PSC.

On the ro-ro side a good deal of the work lands on the engine department. The detection system must be tested under normal ventilation conditions after installation and give an overall response time acceptable to the Administration — so ventilation-fan line-ups belong in the test procedure, not just the fire plan. Section numbering has to agree across three systems at once: detector zones, deluge section valves and the camera legend at the monitor; a mismatch introduced during a retrofit is a survey finding. For the weather-deck monitors, confirm the pumps can serve the monitors and the fire main simultaneously, that the segregating valve works, and that drainage is sized for 125% of combined output — free surface on a vehicle deck is a stability problem, not just a housekeeping one. For an existing passenger ship, the 1 Jan 2028 first-survey date is a planning date: detector coverage, cabling, cameras, recording capacity and monitor foundations are drydock work, not voyage work. Check the population before you budget any of it — on a cargo ship most of this package simply does not bite. The same resolution's new II-2/4.2.1.9 is the one line here that applies to every ship, and it is a fuel-quality duty.

Trap Questions

"Does the PFOS ban apply to all PFAS chemicals?" — No; the amendment specifically targets PFOS, not the wider PFAS family (though EU REACH imposes broader restrictions separately). Note too that SOLAS itself states a flat prohibition; the 10 mg/kg figure is the unified interpretation in MSC.1/Circ.1694.

"Which resolution brought in the ro-ro fire-safety requirements?" — MSC.550(108), with the FSS Code amendments in MSC.555(108). MSC.532(107) is the same entry-into-force date but a different package entirely: PFOS, lifting appliances, electronic inclinometers and the Polar Code extension. Same 1 Jan 2026, different instrument.

"So my existing ro-ro passenger ship must comply now?" — The amendments are in force now, but for a ship built before 1 Jan 2026 the duty bites at the first survey on or after 1 January 2028, and only for 20.4.1.6, 20.4.4 and 20.6.2.3. An existing cargo ship gets no II-2/20 retrofit at all.

Common CE Failures
  • Citing an incorrect or fabricated resolution number for the PFOS ban
  • Confusing the PFOS threshold with a general PFAS ban
  • Attributing the ro-ro and vehicle-space fire-safety package to MSC.532(107) because it shares the 1 Jan 2026 date — it is MSC.550(108)
  • Saying "in force 2026" and stopping, without the existing-passenger-ship first survey on or after 1 Jan 2028
  • Quoting one video-replay period — it is seven days for a new ro-ro passenger ship and 24 hours for an existing one
  • Describing the detection as "addressable"; the regulation says individually identifiable, and the smoke and heat duty is a passenger-ship limb
  • Quoting a reg. 20 requirement without its population — video monitoring, weather-deck monitors and the structural package are passenger-ship duties, and a new cargo car carrier gets none of them
  • Applying the FSS 7/2.5 figures to an existing-ship retrofit; FSS 7/2.5 is for ro-ro passenger ships built on/after 1 Jan 2026 only
  • Offering the 3.0 m reduction against the openings rule — 20.5.3.3 reduces the weather-deck vehicle-lane distance only; 20.5.2.1's 6.0 m has no distance relief
  • Saying the smoke detectors may be switched off for cargo work and stopping there — heat detection and manual call points may never be disconnected
  • Asserting an official log book entry for PFOS disposal — SOLAS requires delivery to shore reception facilities, not a log entry

A fire breaks out in an undeclared dangerous goods container — how will you fight it?
Fire Safety|DG
15-Second AnswerUndeclared DG fires must be fought defensively: assume worst-case reactivity, boundary-cool all sides, and hold CO2/water application until smoke and thermal signs rule out oxidising or water-reactive cargo.
60-Second AnswerWith no manifest UN number I treat the fire as potentially reactive, toxic or self-oxidising. I maximise fire-main pressure by running pumps in parallel, trip hold ventilation to limit oxygen, isolate local reefer power, and deploy lances/monitors from a protected distance while reading smoke and thermal-imaging signatures before committing to a water or CO2-based tactic.

Regulatory Context

With no manifest UN number, the EmS Guide cannot be consulted directly, so the fire must be treated on a worst-case defensive basis under the general fire-fighting arrangements of SOLAS II-2/10.7.

Identification & Assessment

  • Use thermal imaging to map heat spread across adjacent stacks; assess smoke colour/odour as an indicator (acrid yellow — acids/oxidisers; thick black — plastics/polymers).
  • Treat as reactive/toxic/self-oxidising until proven otherwise — no direct manual entry.

Engineering Actions

  • Run main and emergency fire pumps in parallel, targeting 0.27-0.35 MPa at open-deck monitors.
  • Seal hold ventilation flaps if under deck; hold CO2 release until an oxidising cargo is ruled out.
  • Align cargo-hold bilge 3-way valves to keep hazardous runoff out of machinery-space bilge tanks.
Examiner Trap"Do you release CO2 immediately since the cargo is unknown?" — No — if the cargo is a self-oxidising Class 5.1/5.2 substance, CO2 will not extinguish it and the fixed charge is wasted; boundary cooling and smoke assessment come first.

CE Oral Tip: Nair pressures candidates on the water-lance decision for unknown cargo. If sparks and white smoke suggest a reactive metal fire, state clearly you will NOT pierce with water — maintain boundary cooling only and let the internal reaction burn out.
CE Relevance

Manage electrical load configuration to prevent an automatic blackout if a deck reefer cable short-circuits under fire conditions, protecting propulsion and manoeuvring availability.

"Bright sparks and white smoke — will you pierce with a water lance?" — No; this pattern suggests a Class 4.3 water-reactive metal fire; piercing with water risks a hydrogen explosion. Maintain external boundary cooling only.

Explain the water mist lance and mobile water monitor: how to use them, on which fires, where the requirement is mentioned, and the functional requirements.
SOLAS II-2|FSS Code
15-Second AnswerSOLAS II-2/10.7.3 mandates lances (piercing 2 mm steel) and mobile monitors (2 units if breadth <30 m, 4 if ≥30 m, each ≥60 m³/hr) on container-deck ships built from 2016, with technical detail set in the FSS Code and MSC.1/Circ.1472.
60-Second AnswerThese are mandated under SOLAS II-2/10.7.3 for ships carrying containers on/above the weather deck (built from 1 Jan 2016). The FSS Code sets performance benchmarks: the lance pierces 2 mm steel to mist-flood a container without opening doors; monitors are carried at 2 units under 30 m breadth or 4 units at 30 m and above, each discharging at least 60 m³/hr to reach the highest tier while crew retreat to a safe distance.

Regulatory Context

Carriage is mandated by SOLAS Regulation II-2/10.7.3 for ships designed to carry containers on or above the weather deck (built on/after 1 Jan 2016); technical performance is set by the FSS Code and MSC.1/Circ.1472.

Functional Requirements

  • Water mist lance: piercing tip capable of penetrating 2 mm steel container skin; delivers fine high-velocity mist into the interior without opening doors.
  • Mobile monitors: breadth <30 m — minimum 2 units; breadth ≥30 m (typical Maersk main-line size) — minimum 4 units. Each must give continuous unattended discharge of at least 60 m³/hr reaching the highest deck tier.

Method of Use

  • Lance: mallet/slide-hammer punches the tip through the container skin, then the fire-main valve floods the interior.
  • Monitor: positioned on deck/lashing bridge with stabiliser legs, connected via dual-feed hose, aimed and locked so the crew can retreat while cooling continues.

Key Numbers

Lance pierces 2 mm steel. Monitors: 2 (breadth <30 m) / 4 (breadth ≥30 m), each ≥60 m³/hr.

What are the ventilation requirements for a totally enclosed lifeboat?
LSA Code|Lifeboats
15-Second AnswerUnder IMO Resolution MSC.535(107) — new LSA Code paras 4.6.6/4.6.7 — every totally enclosed lifeboat must provide ventilation of at least 5 m³/h per person for the permitted complement, sustained for 24 hours, operable from inside. Powered or passive; if engine-driven, sufficient fuel per 4.4.6.8 and the source can't be the radio battery. Mandatory from 1 Jan 2026, applies to lifeboats installed on/after 1 Jan 2029.
60-Second AnswerMSC.535(107), adopted at MSC 107 (June 2023), inserts new paragraphs 4.6.6 and 4.6.7 into LSA Code Chapter IV following the 2013 MOL Comfort casualty, where survivors in a totally enclosed lifeboat suffered dehydration/heat stress from inadequate air exchange. The rule requires a ventilation rate of at least 5 m³/h per person, for the number of persons the lifeboat is certified to carry, maintained for not less than 24 hours, operable from inside the lifeboat, and arranged to avoid stratification or unventilated pockets. Ventilation may be powered or passive. If powered, the source must not be the radio batteries, and if the lifeboat engine drives it, sufficient fuel must be carried per para 4.4.6.8 (the standard 24h engine-endurance provision). Consequential amendments: MSC.81(70) test procedures updated, MSC.559(108) adds the ventilation system to the annual thorough examination under MSC.402(96). Keep the dates apart: in force internationally 1 Jan 2026, but applies to lifeboats installed on or after 1 Jan 2029 — and the resolution defines "installed" itself: the ship's contract or construction date for new ships, the equipment's delivery date to the ship for all others. So it is not new-build only.

Regulatory Context

Governed by IMO Resolution MSC.535(107) (adopted 8 June 2023, MSC 107), which inserts new paragraphs 4.6.6 and 4.6.7 into LSA Code Chapter IV, Section 4.6 (Totally Enclosed Lifeboats). The amendment was driven by the MOL Comfort (2013) casualty, where crew who abandoned ship in a totally enclosed lifeboat suffered dehydration and heat stress due to inadequate ventilation while sealed inside.

Core Rules (LSA Code 4.6.6)

  • 4.6.6.1 — Ventilation rate: at least 5 m³/h per person, for the number of persons the lifeboat is permitted to accommodate, sustained for not less than 24 hours. Must be operable from inside the lifeboat and arranged so the boat ventilates without stratification or unventilated pockets.
  • 4.6.6.2 — Power source: ventilation means may be powered or passive. If powered, the source shall not be the radio batteries (para 4.4.6.11); if dependent on the lifeboat engine, sufficient fuel must be provided to comply with para 4.4.6.8 (standard engine fuel-endurance requirement, i.e. continuous running for 24h).
  • 4.6.7 — Closing arrangements: ventilation openings need means of closing, operable from inside, positioned to minimise water ingress.

Scope & Timeline

Three dates, and the examiner is testing whether you keep them apart. Adopted 8 June 2023. Enters into force internationally 1 January 2026. The provisions are applied to totally enclosed lifeboats installed on or after 1 January 2029. Entry into force is not the application date.

MSC.535(107) does not leave installed on or after 1 January 2029 to interpretation — it defines the expression in two limbs:

  • (a) New ships: for ships for which the building contract is placed on or after 1 January 2029, or in the absence of a contract which are constructed on or after that date — any installation date on the ship.
  • (b) All other ships: a contractual delivery date for the equipment or, in the absence of a contractual delivery date, the actual delivery date of the equipment to the ship, on or after 1 January 2029.

So this is not a new-build-only requirement. Limb (b) reaches existing tonnage: a lifeboat contracted for, or delivered to, an in-service ship on or after 1 January 2029 must comply. What the amendment does not do is force retrofit of lifeboats already installed. Note also that the resolution's wording is constructed, not keel laid.

Consequential Amendments

Test procedures in MSC.81(70) (Revised Recommendations on Testing of LSA) updated to include ventilation performance testing with hatches closed. MSC.559(108) amends MSC.402(96) to add ventilation system condition/operation checks to the annual thorough examination of lifeboats.

Key Numbers

Ventilation rate: 5 m³/h per person. Duration: 24 hours minimum. Resolution: MSC.535(107), adopted 8 June 2023. In force: 1 Jan 2026. Application: lifeboats installed on/after 1 Jan 2029, as defined by the resolution — new-ship contract or construction date, or for any other ship the equipment's contractual or actual delivery date.

Trap Questions

But do not answer "new-builds only": if a replacement lifeboat is contracted for, or delivered to, this ship on or after 1 Jan 2029, limb (b) of the resolution's own definition catches it and it must meet 4.6.6/4.6.7.
