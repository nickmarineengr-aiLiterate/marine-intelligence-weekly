SEQ (Safety Equipment) survey — documents required and how do you prepare for it?
Survey Simon
⏱ 15-Second Answer The Safety Equipment (SEQ) Survey verifies the readiness of a ship's Life-Saving Appliances (LSA), Fire-Fighting Appliances (FFA), and navigational gear as per SOLAS Chapter I. Preparation centers on reviewing the Record of Equipment (Form E) and ensuring all physical equipment perfectly matches the approved LSA and Fire Control plans.
🎯 60-Second Answer Governed by SOLAS Chapter I, Regulation 8, the SEQ survey requires checking the validity of statutory certificates, PMS records, and physically verifying equipment. Documents required include Form E, Fire Control Plan, LSA Plan, Muster List, and servicing certificates for liferafts, BA sets, and fixed fire-extinguishing systems. As Chief Engineer, preparation involves testing the emergency fire pump, ensuring the lifeboat engine starts promptly, verifying fixed CO2/Water Mist system readiness, testing Quick Closing Valves (QCVs), and ensuring emergency generator load tests are up to date. The goal is to prove 100% operational readiness of all safety systems.

Governing Instrument & Principle

The SEQ survey is a statutory survey mandated under SOLAS 1974, Chapter I, Regulation 8 (Surveys of life-saving appliances and other equipment of cargo ships). It ensures that all safety, firefighting, and navigational equipment complies with the International Life-Saving Appliance (LSA) Code and International Code for Fire Safety Systems (FSS Code). The scope and item-by-item conduct of the survey — annual, intermediate, and renewal — is standardised globally by the IMO Survey Guidelines under the Harmonized System of Survey and Certification (HSSC), currently Resolution A.1207(34), 2025, which MMD/DGMA checklists (Form SEQ-Intermediate) are built directly from.

Documents Required

  • Form E: Record of Equipment for Cargo Ship Safety Equipment Certificate — the primary reference document for the survey
  • Cross-Certificate Validity Check: The surveyor also confirms the validity of the SMC/DOC, ISSC, Load Line Certificate, IOPP Certificate, and Class certificates as a consistency check against the SEQ certificate itself
  • Continuous Synopsis Record (CSR): Confirmed present and up to date (SOLAS XI-1/5)
  • Approved Plans & Manuals: Fire Control Plan (permanently exhibited or duplicate in an external enclosure), Fire Safety Operational Booklet, Maintenance Plan for Fire Safety Measures, and LSA training manual/training aids in the ship's working language
  • Certificates: Servicing certificates for inflatable liferafts, hydrostatic release units (HRUs), self-contained breathing apparatus (SCBA) cylinders, emergency escape breathing devices (EEBDs), and fixed fire-extinguishing systems
  • Records: Updated Muster List, PMS maintenance records for all LSA/FFA, records of fire/abandon-ship drills, and log-book entries showing the last full crew muster, lifeboat equipment inspection, and lowering/swinging-out dates

Chief Engineer's Preparation (Engine Room Focus)

  • Fire-Fighting Appliances (FFA): Test the emergency fire pump for adequate pressure at the two highest/furthest hydrants, producing two simultaneous jets. Verify fire main integrity. Ensure fixed CO2/foam/water mist systems are armed and alarms work
  • CO2 Release Box — Dual Control Requirement: Confirm the fixed CO2 system release box has two separate, clearly identified controls — one to open the gas piping to the protected space, and a second to discharge gas from the storage containers (SOLAS II-2/10.4.1.5). Examiners frequently probe this as a trap since candidates often describe it as a single lever
  • Remote Shutdowns: Demonstrate remote closing of skylights, funnel/ventilation openings, power-operated doors, and stopping of ventilation fans, boiler forced/induced draft fans, and oil-fuel/flammable-liquid transfer pumps from the Fire Control Station
  • Life-Saving Appliances (LSA): Ensure lifeboat and rescue boat engines start within 2 minutes at ambient temperature and run ahead/astern; fuel tanks full; steering operational. Confirm launching-appliance winch brakes have been dynamically tested and on-load release gear (including free-fall release systems) serviced. Falls must have been renewed, or turned end-for-end, within the last 5 years
  • General Emergency Alarm: Test and confirm audibility at all normally occupied spaces, including the engine room and ECR (SOLAS III/20)
  • Emergency Power & Shut-offs: Run the emergency generator on load. Test all Quick Closing Valves (QCVs) for oil fuel/lube oil tanks, emergency stop buttons for pumps/fans, and fire dampers for free movement. Confirm emergency lighting is functional at muster/embarkation stations and alleyways on the emergency source of power

Sending an LSA Item Ashore — Authorised Service Provider Control

If a davit, winch, launching appliance or release gear has to leave the ship for workshop attention before the survey, the control that matters is who is permitted to touch it. Under SOLAS III/20 and the requirements adopted by Resolution MSC.402(96) — effective 1 January 2020 — any activity requiring disassembly of the equipment, or any work outside the on-board maintenance and emergency-repair instructions, is a repair, not maintenance.

  • Who may do it: repair, any overhaul and the five-yearly thorough examination must be carried out by certified personnel of either the manufacturer or a service provider authorised by the Administration. The Administration makes the list of authorised providers available — a capable local workshop is not by itself sufficient.
  • What the authorisation rests on: personnel certified for that make and type of equipment, the manufacturer's specialised tools, access to the correct parts, the manufacturer's instructions for disassembly of on-load release gear and davit winches, and a documented, certified quality system.
  • What must come back with the item: the completed report and checklists signed by the person who carried out the work and countersigned by the Company's representative or the Master, together with the provider's certification for the work performed.
  • What the SEQ surveyor expects to see: those records filed on board and retained for the service life of the equipment, traceable to the item, and consistent with the maintenance manual and the Record of Equipment.

The failure mode here is a davit returned with an invoice but no authorised-provider certification — the equipment may be perfectly sound and the survey item still cannot be credited.

Scope Beyond the Engine Room — Why It's Still Asked

The SEQ survey's full scope (Annex 5 of A.1207(34)) also covers navigational equipment operational checks (ECDIS with back-up arrangements, BNWAS, gyro/magnetic compass, radar, VDR, AIS, LRIT conformance) and pilot transfer arrangements (SOLAS V/23) — these are Deck/Master's responsibility, not the CE's. A CE candidate should acknowledge this scope exists in one sentence, then pivot immediately to the machinery-side items above — this framing itself often earns credit with Simon.

Examiner Angle (Simon)

Simon assesses whether the candidate clearly separates Deck from Engine responsibilities. He wants to see priority given to machinery under the Chief Engineer's direct jurisdiction — the emergency fire pump, lifeboat engines, and engine room fixed fire-fighting systems — rather than broadly listing deck navigation equipment.

CE Oral Tip (Simon): For Simon at Kochi MMD, mention "Form E" in your very first sentence. The most common failure point is candidates rambling about bridge navigation lights or EPIRBs instead of immediately outlining the CE's responsibility for the Emergency Fire Pump, QCVs, and Lifeboat engines.
🔧 CE Relevance

Routine execution of the Planned Maintenance System (PMS) for safety equipment is crucial. The CE must ensure all test protocols (blowing through CO2 lines, testing water mist nozzles) are logged accurately, as the surveyor cross-references PMS entries with physical equipment condition.

⚠ Trap Questions

"Does the SEQ survey cover the GMDSS and radio equipment?" — No, that falls strictly under the Cargo Ship Safety Radio Certificate (SRRC), governed by SOLAS Ch I, Reg 9.

"How many controls does the CO2 release box have, and what do they do?" — Two, not one: a separate control to open the gas piping into the protected space, and a separate control to discharge gas from the storage containers (SOLAS II-2/10.4.1.5). Candidates who describe "one lever that releases CO2" are marked down immediately.

"What's the difference between an EEBD and SCBA, and does the SEQ survey check both?" — Yes, both. EEBD is a short-duration escape-only device (typically 10–15 minutes); SCBA is for firefighting entry with a rechargeable cylinder. The survey checks both are complete, in good condition, and cylinders (including spares) are suitably charged.

✗ Common CE Failures
  • Failing to mention testing of Quick Closing Valves (QCVs) and emergency stops for engine room ventilation fans and fuel transfer pumps
  • Describing the CO2 system as having a single release control instead of the mandatory dual-control arrangement
  • Omitting the General Emergency Alarm audibility test — examiners expect this named explicitly, not folded into "testing alarms" generically
  • Not knowing the 5-year falls renewal interval when asked about lifeboat launching-appliance maintenance
🔢 Numbers & Regulations to Memorise
  • Certificate Validity: maximum 5 years
  • Annual Survey Window: within 3 months before or after each anniversary date
  • Lifeboat/Rescue Boat Engine Start: must start within 2 minutes at an ambient temperature of 15°C and run ahead/astern (LSA Code)
  • Falls Renewal: lifeboat/liferaft launching falls renewed, or turned end-for-end, within 5 years (SOLAS III/20)
  • CO2 Release Box: 2 separate controls — open gas piping / discharge from storage (SOLAS II-2/10.4.1.5)
  • Current HSSC Survey Guidelines: IMO Res. A.1207(34), 2025 — updated by the Assembly every 2 years
⚓ Casualty Link

The Norman Atlantic fire (2014) highlighted catastrophic failures in fixed firefighting systems, emergency generator operation, and lifeboat deployment, emphasizing the critical need for rigorous, uncompromised SEQ surveys.

Simon: SEQ Survey → Emergency Fire Pump parameters → fixed CO2 system operation → Quick Closing Valves working principle.

Corrosion limits — what are the acceptable corrosion/diminution limits for hull structure?
IACS / Class Simon
⏱ 15-Second Answer Hull corrosion limits are defined by IACS Unified Requirements and Class rules. Generally, a maximum diminution of 20% to 25% of the original as-built thickness is permitted for local framing and plating, while the global hull girder strength (section modulus) must not be reduced by more than 10%.
🎯 60-Second Answer Structural diminution limits dictate the maximum allowable steel wastage before renewal is mandatory. Governed by IACS UR Z7 for dry cargo ships, thickness measurements are taken via UTM. Global limits dictate that the hull girder section modulus cannot experience more than a 10% reduction. Local limits allow for up to 25% reduction in individual plates or stiffeners from their as-built thickness. When wastage exceeds 75% of the allowable margin, it is legally termed "Substantial Corrosion," which triggers mandatory annual thickness measurements for those specific areas. Any steel exceeding the absolute diminution limits must be cropped and renewed.

Governing Instrument & Principle

Corrosion limits are regulated under the International Association of Classification Societies (IACS) Unified Requirements (specifically UR Z7 for general cargo/container ships) and individual Classification Society Rules (e.g., ABS, DNV, Lloyd's Register).

Key Diminution Limits

  • Global Hull Girder Strength: total cross-sectional area and section modulus of the hull girder must not be reduced by more than 10%
  • Local Diminution (Plating & Stiffeners): localized reduction in thickness generally acceptable up to 20% to 25% of original as-built thickness, depending on location and Class society calculation
  • Substantial Corrosion: defined as a wastage in excess of 75% of allowable margins, but within acceptable limits

Practical Application & Measurement

Diminution is measured using Ultrasonic Thickness Measurement (UTM) by Class-approved service providers, typically during Special Surveys (every 5 years) or Intermediate Surveys. If a plate exceeds the 25% local limit, temporary repairs (like doubler plates) are strictly prohibited for structural hull integrity; the affected area must be permanently repaired by cropping out the wasted steel and inserting a new marine-grade plate with approved full-penetration welding.

Examiner Angle (Simon)

Simon targets the candidate's understanding of structural safety boundaries — a clear, numerical distinction between local structural degradation and global hull collapse risks. He heavily penalizes candidates who confuse the 10% and 25% thresholds.

🔧 CE Relevance

Operationally, the CE must actively monitor the condition of the Impressed Current Cathodic Protection (ICCP) system and the condition of sacrificial anodes in sea chests and ballast tanks to prevent rapid localized diminution.

⚠ Trap Questions

"We found a heavily corroded patch on the engine room tank top. Can we weld a doubler plate over it to pass the survey?" — No. Doubler plates are only acceptable as temporary, emergency repairs. Permanent repairs require cropping the wasted steel and renewing it with full-penetration insert plates.

✗ Common CE Failures
  • Confusing the 25% local limit with the 10% global limit
  • Stating that the Chief Officer is solely responsible for all hull corrosion, neglecting engine room double bottoms and sea chests
🔢 Numbers & Regulations to Memorise
  • Global limit: 10% section modulus reduction
  • Local limit: 25% plate/stiffener diminution
  • Substantial Corrosion: 75% of the allowable margin
⚓ Casualty Link

The sinking of the Erika (1999) and Prestige (2002) due to catastrophic structural failure were directly linked to undetected severe localized corrosion in ballast tanks, leading to the strict IACS Enhanced Survey Programme (ESP) and precise diminution limits.

🚢 On My Vessel (Maersk Container)

During dry docks on Maersk vessels, the engine room team closely monitors UTM readings of the main sea chest cooling water boundaries and bilge wells, as these areas are highly susceptible to accelerated galvanic corrosion and pitting.
