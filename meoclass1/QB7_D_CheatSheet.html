QTopicCore FactKey Reg
Q1CO2 Sequestration vs CCSSequestration = permanent geological storage step (aquifer/depleted field/basalt mineralisation); onboard capture = extraction step. Together = CCS lifecycle. CII is TtW only — captured CO2 doesn't currently improve CII.MEPC 80/81; IMO Revised GHG Strategy 2023
Q2Onboard Carbon Capture vs ScrubberOCC removes CO2 from exhaust and liquefies for offload; a SOx scrubber only washes out sulphur oxides — NOT the same system, does not reduce GHG.MEPC 80/81 guidance
Q3Amine SeparationFlue gas contacts amine solvent (e.g. MEA) in absorber column, CO2 chemically binds; solvent regenerated in stripper column with heat, releasing pure CO2 for compression/liquefaction. Energy penalty 10–15%.IPCC CCS Special Report
Q4FLL / WtT vs TtWWell-to-Tank = upstream fuel production footprint; Tank-to-Wake = combustion emissions. FLL = WtT+TtW (Well-to-Wake). Matters because CII is TtW-only, so FLL is needed for genuine lifecycle GHG accounting.MEPC.376(80) LCA Guidelines
Q5GFI / Net-Zero Framework2008 baseline = 93.3 gCO2eq/MJ (NOT 91.16). Two-tier target (Base/Tier 1, Direct Compliance/Tier 2). CURRENT STATUS: approved in principle MEPC 83 (Apr 2025); adoption POSTPONED Oct 2025; resumed MEPC/ES.2 vote 4 Dec 2026 (after MEPC 85, 30 Nov–3 Dec 2026); not adopted, no EIF date.MARPOL Annex VI amendments (NZF); FuelEU Maritime 2023/1805
Q6Ammonia Fuel TypesGrey = steam methane reforming (fossil, high CO2); Blue = grey + CCS; Green = electrolysis using renewable power (near-zero carbon). Key hazards: acute toxicity, corrosivity, N2O slip risk from imperfect combustion.IGF Code amendments (ammonia); IMO interim guidelines
Q7GHG & GWPMain shipping GHGs: CO2, CH4, N2O + black carbon (non-gas forcer). IMO NZF applies GWP20 (not GWP100) for methane — much higher factor (~82.5) to penalise short-lived climate pollutants.IPCC AR6; MARPOL Annex VI
Q8Methane SlipOccurs mainly in low-pressure dual-fuel (Otto-cycle) engines from unburnt scavenge-port gas; also from tank venting/boil-off. High-pressure (Diesel-cycle, e.g. ME-GI) engines have far lower slip.MEPC methane guidelines; IMO GHG Strategy 2023
Q9IGF Code Key RequirementsMandatory for low-flashpoint fuel ships under SOLAS II-1 Part G; core principle = Single Failure Principle (no single fault causes unsafe/explosive condition); double-walled piping, ESD, hazardous-area classification, STCW V/3 crew training.SOLAS II-1 Reg 56/57; MSC.391(95); STCW V/3
Q10Latest MEPC HighlightsTrack most recent MEPC session outcomes for CE-relevant operational directives — verify against IMO MEPC meeting summaries before oral (fast-changing area).Latest MEPC session summary — verify currency
Q11FLL & CE DutyCE's duty: accurate BDN/LHV data entry, correct fuel-type record keeping, avoiding fuel stability/incompatibility risk when switching between conventional and lifecycle-tracked fuels.MEPC.376(80); BDN documentation requirements
Q14LNG bunkering — procedure, safeties, dangersIGF Code 18.4. Two PICs agree the transfer procedure and the emergency actions in writing, then sign the bunker safety checklist. Since MSC.551(108) (in force 1 Jan 2026) that written agreement also covers min/max limiting transfer pressure and temperature and bunkering line PRV settings — five items, not three. Lost communications = stop. Sequence: purge/inert → cool down at the manual’s rate → ramp in stages → monitor → drain, purge, disconnect → signed BDN. Dangers: −162°C cryogenic, 600:1 expansion, 5–15% flammable, RPT on water, rollover.IGF 18.4.1 / 18.4.3–18.4.6; MSC.391(95); MSC.551(108); SOLAS II-1 Pt G
Q15LNG fuel specification when orderingThere is no ISO 8217 for LNG. Order to the LNG Bunker Delivery Note, the annex to IGF part C-1: methane number, LCV/HCV (MJ/kg), Wobbe Ws/Wi (MJ/m³), density, delivery pressure and temperature, and full composition C1–C7 plus N2 and S. The Code mandates disclosure, not limits — its only limit is H2S, hydrogen, ammonia, chlorine, fluorine and water < 5 ppm. The minimum methane number is the engine maker’s figure (Otto is knock-limited, ~70+; gas-injection tolerates far more) and goes in the contract, not in a regulation.IGF 18.4.1.2 and annex to part C-1; MSC.391(95); maker fuel spec; MARPOL Annex VI
Q12Wake equalising ductA pair of ducts fitted ahead of the propeller that condition the inflow — straightening and equalising the wake field so the propeller works in a more uniform stream. Effect: improved propulsive efficiency and reduced fuel consumption at the design condition, with less thrust variation and vibration. For the owner it is an energy-saving device retrofit whose value is a fuel and CII improvement, and it must be justified against the actual operating profile — the gain falls away outside the speed and draught range it was designed for.Energy efficiency / CII context; no standalone mandatory requirement
Q13LNG bunkering — manifold leak, and minor vs major fireBoth start the same way: hit the ESD and stop the transfer. Leak: ESD, alarm, water curtain to protect steel and disperse vapour, control ignition sources, people upwind, and never a solid water jet into the pool — water boils LNG and makes more gas. Minor fire: ESD, isolate, dry chemical powder, boundary cooling. Major fire: ESD, emergency release the coupling, boundary-cool and protect the ship, and do not extinguish a gas fire you cannot isolate — a burning leak is safer than an unignited vapour cloud.IGF Code ch. 11 and 18.4; ESD1/ESD2; SIGTTO and SGMF guidance
