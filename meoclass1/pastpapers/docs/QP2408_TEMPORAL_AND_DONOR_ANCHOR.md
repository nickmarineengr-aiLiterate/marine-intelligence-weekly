## 1. WHAT MAKES THIS PAPER'S TEMPORAL SHAPE DISTINCTIVE

Two things, and they pull in opposite directions.

**First, the donor position inverted between Batch 3's two papers.** QP2407's anchor recorded
that its single donor travelled *forward* — the exception to the 2024 rule. QP2408 is the
ordinary case restored, but with an unusual mitigation: its two strongest donors,
`QP2411-Q4` and `QP2411-Q5`, sit at **November 2024, three months after this sitting**. That
is the shortest backward reach any 2024 paper in the corpus has had. The systemic warning at
`DESKTOP_QP_ALLOCATION_2024.md` §6.3 — that every donor available to a 2024 paper is a 2025
or 2026 answer whose later currency must be stripped — still applies in form, but over three
months rather than eighteen there is very little currency to strip. That does not make the
check optional; it makes it cheap, and it is run and recorded at §5.

**Second, and less comfortably, this paper has a live "adopted but not in force" boundary
that a present-day reader will get wrong by reflex.** MEPC 81 sat five months before this
sitting and its Annex VI amendments are now long since operative. At August 2024 they were
not. The detail is at §3 and it is the single most important line in this document.

## 3. THE DECISIVE BOUNDARY — MEPC.385(81) IS ADOPTED AND NOT IN FORCE

This is the finding this anchor exists for, and it was **established at source, not assumed**.

> ### MEPC.385(81): adopted **22 March 2024**, in force **1 August 2025**
>
> The resolution's operative paragraph 3 states that the amendments *"shall enter into force
> on 1 August 2025"*, following deemed acceptance on 1 February 2025. At the **August 2024**
> sitting the amendments are therefore **adopted and not in force**, with **eleven months**
> still to run.

**Adopted ≠ in force.** This is the exact confusion the protocol names as the most common
source of a wrong answer, and here it is live and load-bearing rather than theoretical.

### 3.1 What MEPC.385(81) changes, and why it bites on Q6

Read in full this session. The amendments that matter to this paper are all in the fuel-oil
group:

| Provision | Post-amendment (from 1 Aug 2025) | **Position at August 2024** |
|---|---|---|
| **Reg 2.1.14** definition | *"Fuel oil means any fuel delivered to and intended for use on board a ship."* | the **pre-amendment** definition stands |
| **Reg 2.1.33** | new definition of **gas fuel** inserted | **does not exist** |
| **Reg 18.3 chapeau** | *"Fuel oil delivered to and used on board a ship…"* | *"Fuel oil **for combustion purposes** delivered to and used on board ships…"* |
| **Reg 18.4** | disapplies only paras 5.1, 8.1, 8.2 to low-flashpoint/gas fuel | disapplies paras **5, 6, 7.1, 7.2, 8.1, 8.2, 9.2, 9.3 and 9.4** to gas fuels |
| **Reg 18.5** | **split into 5.1 and 5.2**, creating a separate BDN regime for low-flashpoint and gas fuels | **a single paragraph 5** — one BDN regime, appendix V |
| **Reg 18.9.2** | introduces the term *"MARPOL delivered sample"* | pre-amendment wording |

**Consequence for Q6.** The question asks about marine fuel standards, the fuel oil analysis
report and the Bunker Delivery Note. An answer written from present-day knowledge will
describe the split BDN regime, the "any fuel delivered to and intended for use on board"
definition and the gas-fuel carve-outs — **all of which are wrong for August 2024**. The
answer must be written from the pre-amendment Regulation 18 and the ten-item appendix V, and
it is.

**The corpus copy is the right one, and that is not luck.** The held Annex VI states on its
own first page that it is a *"Consolidated version as of 01.05.2024"* incorporating
MEPC.328(76), MEPC.361(79) and MEPC.362(79). 1 May 2024 falls **after** MEPC 81 adopted the
amendments and **before** they entered into force, which is exactly the window this sitting
occupies. Reading it is correct; reading a later consolidation would not be.

### 3.2 The 1 July 2024 SOLAS boundary — inherited in substance, re-verified independently

QP2407's anchor §3 established that **MSC.521(106) entered into force 1 July 2024** and that
the SOLAS Consolidated Edition 2024 is therefore operative for both Batch 3 papers. That
line was **not taken on trust**: the title page (*"Incorporating all amendments in effect on
1 July 2024"*) and foreword §2.56 were re-read at source this session and both confirm it.

August 2024 sits a full month past the boundary, so the edition question is settled *a
fortiori* — no day-level assumption is made or needed. The boundary is **engaged** on Q1
(SOLAS XI-1/1 is the RO Code's hook) and **Q3** (SOLAS I/21 and XI-1/6), and in both cases
the effect is on which edition is cited, not on the substance: MSC.521(106) introduced
chapter XV and the IP Code and does not amend chapter I or chapter XI-1.

### 3.3 July → August 2024: re-run independently, and NOT nil

QP2407's anchor §3.1 recorded the July→August search as a **searched nil** and explicitly
required this paper to re-run it rather than inherit it. It was re-run, and the finding is
different — not because July was wrong, but because the question asked of August is not the
same question.

- **No instrument entered into force, was adopted, or was revoked between the July and the
  August 2024 sittings.** That part of the July finding holds and is confirmed.
- **But the "no change" conclusion must not be generalised.** QP2408 engages subjects QP2407
  did not — fuel oil quality and the BDN, market-based measures, the RO Code, casualty
  investigation, salvage and SUA. Each was checked against its own instrument, not against
  the calendar. That check is what produced §3.1: **an amendment adopted before both sittings
  and in force after both** is invisible to a boundary search between two dates, and it is the
  live risk on this paper.

That is the methodological point worth keeping: **a nil result on a date-window sweep does
not clear a paper whose risk is an instrument in the adopted-but-not-yet-in-force state.**

### 4.1 The three decisive exclusions

**Q4 — market-based measures at MEPC 81.** The stem asks what MBMs were *discussed during
MEPC 81* and what their implementation challenges are. At August 2024 the honest answer is
that the basket was **a set of competing proposals under development, not an agreed
instrument**, and that its shape was fixed by MEPC.377(80) §4.5: a technical element (a
goal-based marine fuel standard regulating the phased reduction of the marine fuel's GHG
intensity) and an economic element (a maritime GHG emissions pricing mechanism), to be
finalized and agreed by 2025. Everything the industry now associates with the answer — the
approved basket, the IMO Net-Zero Framework, the GFI mechanism, the agreed price per tonne —
belongs to **2025** and must not appear. This question has no donor, so there is no donor
text to strip; the risk is that present-day knowledge leaks in unmarked.

**Q6 — the fuel oil question.** Governed entirely by §3.1 above. Pre-amendment Regulation 18,
single paragraph 5, ten-item appendix V, "fuel oil for combustion purposes". MEPC.385(81) may
be *named as adopted and not yet in force* — that is a correct and creditable statement of
the August 2024 position — but none of its content may be presented as the requirement.

**Q9 — the ISM question.** The donor is a **June 2026** answer pulled back **twenty-two
months**, the longest reach on this paper. Its limb (b) asks about the Code's evolution in
response to *emerging* challenges, which is precisely the limb where a later donor's currency
is invisible when wrong. Everything after August 2024 is stripped: the answer runs to
MSC.353(92) and MSC.428(98) and stops.

## 5. DONOR ADJUDICATION — RECOMPUTED, NOT INHERITED

### 5.1 Why the board's figures were not used

`DESKTOP_QP_ALLOCATION_BOARD.md` carried QP2408 as the weaker of the two Batch 3 papers, and
the stored `reuse_tier` fields in the intake spec record **eight questions at tier C with the
note "no family member with a built answer"** and one at tier D. **Neither was carried
forward.** Both are stale, and demonstrably so: the intake was written on 2026-08-10, before
QP2407 was solved and before the current built set existed.

Donor readiness was recomputed from the **actual built set** — the Batch 3 baseline plus
**QP2407 read from its branch git object at `7e71066`**, giving **243 built questions across
27 solved papers**. Two independent derivations were run:

1. `recurrence_model.donor_readiness` through the same path `build_reuse_map.py` uses — the
   derivation the product itself uses.
2. A **topical sweep** over `topic_tags` and `subject_tags` for every QP2408 stem against all
   243 built stems, scored on overlap.

**The two disagree, and the disagreement is the finding.** The lexical family model returned
a donor for **Q9 only** and "set once in the sittings MIW has transcribed" for the other
eight. The topical sweep surfaced **strong candidates for Q1, Q2, Q3 and Q7 that the lexical
model missed entirely**. That is the under-reporting the Batch 3 setup pass predicted for Q3,
and it turns out not to be confined to Q3.

Every candidate above threshold was then adjudicated **by reading both printed stems**, as
`DESKTOP_QP_PRODUCTION_PLAYBOOK.md` §7 rule 2 requires. A row in a sweep is not a donor.

### 6.3 The ISO 8217 edition question — declared, not guessed

Q6's stem asks candidates to *"Name the present ISO standards for Marine Fuel"*, which is
edition-sensitive on its face, and the intake flagged it MEDIUM for that reason.

**The flag is answered as follows.** ISO 8217 is the international specification for marine
fuels and is named as such — that much is settled and is corroborated inside the MIW built
set at `QP2411-Q2` and `QP2512`. **Which numbered edition was current on the day of the
August 2024 sitting is NOT asserted**, because MIW holds no copy of the standard in any
edition and therefore cannot verify a publication date against a primary source. Under
`PASTPAPER_PRODUCTION_PROTOCOL.md` §2.1 an unverifiable particular is omitted rather than
given false precision.

This is a deliberate, declared limitation and it costs the answer nothing: the marks in limb
(a) are for the **salient features** of the specification and for the **corrective action**
against adverse analysis findings, neither of which turns on the edition year. Where the
answer needs a hard requirement it uses **MARPOL Annex VI regulation 18.3**, which *is* held
and read at source, and which carries the general fuel-quality requirements in mandatory
form.
