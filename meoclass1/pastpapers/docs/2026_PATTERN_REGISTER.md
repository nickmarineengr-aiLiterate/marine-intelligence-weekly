## 2. Question archetypes

| Archetype | QP2607 | QP2601 |
|---|---|---|
| `explain` | 2 | **6** |
| `legal` | 2 | **3** |
| `procedure` | 2 | 0 |
| `evaluate` | 2 | 0 |
| `compare` | 1 | 0 |

**Observation (1/2, and flagged as possibly an authoring artefact rather than a paper property).**
July used all five archetypes; January used two. Part of this is real — January sets no "you are the
Chief Engineer, state your actions" scenario of the QP2607 Q2 / Q4 kind, and `procedure` is the
natural shape for those. But a 6-way `explain` classification is also what happens if `explain`
becomes the default when a question does not obviously fit elsewhere.

**Action for QP2602: assign the archetype before drafting and justify anything classed `explain`.**
If February also lands 6+ `explain`, the archetype vocabulary is doing no work and should be
reconsidered. **No archetype was added or changed for QP2601** — the five remain as frozen.

**Two observations.**

- **Marine Insurance & Commercial Law is 2/2 papers, 2 questions each.** The steadiest signal in the
  set so far.
- **Indian content did not disappear in January — it changed shape.** July had one wholly Indian
  question (Q7, Merchant Shipping Act 2025). January has *no* wholly Indian question but carries
  India **as a limb** in two: Q6 ("Who can issue these certificates in India") and Q7 ("What
  mechanism is used in India"). The `Indian Maritime Legislation` count of 0 therefore
  **understates** the real Indian demand. If this repeats, the category may need to be read as
  "wholly Indian questions" rather than "questions requiring Indian law".

### Taxonomy pressure — one genuine case, handled without change

**QP2601 Q6** (WHO, disease vectors, ship health certificates) has no comfortable home in the frozen
seven-category tree. It was placed in **Statutory Framework & Class** on the reasoning that three of
its four limbs concern institutions and ship certificates — the same shape as QP2607 Q3 (IACS and the
RO Code). **No new primary category was created**, per the standing rule that categories are not to
proliferate. Two new *secondary* subject tags were added instead (`Occupational Health`, `UNCLOS`),
which is an additive change the vocabulary explicitly permits.

**Watch item:** if a health, welfare or public-health question appears in February, March, April or
June, that is 2/6 evidence and a dedicated category becomes arguable. On one instance it is not.

## 4. Two authoring drifts found in QP2601 — for Founder decision

Both are recorded here rather than silently corrected, because both are judgements about the frozen
template rather than defects.

**(a) Model answers ran long. 9/9 questions exceeded the 450–650 band; range 744–981.**
July had 2/9 over band, with a documented ceiling of 709. A trimming pass was run and removed
roughly 400 words across the paper by moving explanation into the study guide — which is where the
three-layer rule says it belongs — but the answers remain above band. Part of the cause is genuine:
**January's questions carry more printed limbs than July's** (Q3 has six, Q5 four sub-items, Q6 four
tasks, Q7 three limbs), and every limb must be visibly answered. Part is authoring drift.
**Recommendation:** decide whether the 450–650 band should be expressed per *limb-count* rather than
per question before QP2602 is built.

**(b) `understand_first` was used on 9/9 questions; the design says it is conditional.**
July used it on 6/9. The design rationale is explicit that adding it to a self-evident question is
clutter, and that clutter is what expertise reversal punishes. Nine out of nine is not "conditional".
**Recommendation:** on Founder review, mark any QP2601 `understand_first` that adds nothing, and
delete those before QP2602 sets the habit.

## 5. Verification patterns

| Pattern | Evidence | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Source copy is a third-party scan with watermark, marketing and host-added recurrence tables | **2/2** | Neutral provenance model handled it unchanged both times. |
| Recurrence tables printed on the copy support `topic_recurrence` only | **2/2** | The rule held. QP2601 made exactly one `near_recurrence` claim (Q3 ↔ QP2607 Q5), and only after reading both wordings. |
| At least one question turns on a **date** rather than a fact | **2/2** | July Q7 (was the 2025 Act in force?); January Q7 (it was not, at that sitting). |
| Licence-gated instrument blocks a claim to P1 | **2/2** | July: IMSBC. January: **STCW Code** and **ESP Code**. |
| Class A blocking flags at completion | 0 in July, **0 in January** | 2/2 |

**The strongest single verification finding of QP2601** is recorded in `verification/QP2601/Q9.md`: a
freely available IMO circular copy of STCW section A-VIII/1 turned out to be the **pre-Manila 1995
text** (70 hours per seven days, no 14-hour interval rule), while a search summary asserted the
modern 77-hour figure *citing that same document*. **Wrong edition of the right instrument** is the
most dangerous source error available, because it looks fully verified. Added as a standing
production caution.
