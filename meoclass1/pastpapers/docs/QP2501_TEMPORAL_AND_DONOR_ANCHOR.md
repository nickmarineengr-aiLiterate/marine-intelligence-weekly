## 2. THE FIVE BOUNDARIES THAT ACTUALLY DECIDE ANSWERS ON THIS PAPER

Everything in §1 is context. Five entries are load-bearing, in the sense that getting one wrong
changes what a candidate is told to write.

### 2.1 The 34th Assembly is eleven months away — the easiest boundary in Batch 2

`QP2511` and `QP2512` had to reason about a boundary falling inside or beside their own sitting
month. January 2025 does not: the 34th Assembly adopted on **3 December 2025**, eleven months
*after* this paper. The `A.12xx(34)` family is excluded without argument, and `A.1185(33)`
is the operative Port State Control edition.

**This is exactly the case the daily health check exempts.** A current-law trap rule would call
`A.1185(33)` wrong. It is wrong *today* and right *for this sitting*, and that is the whole
reason historical sittings are not swept against current law.

### 2.2 The Indian statute wave of 2025 is entirely post-sitting — all four of it

The board's standing anchor (§8) states the Merchant Shipping Act 2025 point. **This paper needs
a wider form of it**, because Q6 is an Indian commercial-law question and 2025 replaced *four*
of the statutes that surround it:

```
Merchant Shipping Act, 2025      assent 18 Aug 2025   in force 15 Mar 2026   POST-SITTING
Coastal Shipping Act, 2025       assent  9 Aug 2025                          POST-SITTING
Carriage of Goods by Sea Act     replaced during 2025                        POST-SITTING
Bills of Lading Act              replaced during 2025                        POST-SITTING
```

At January 2025 **none of these existed**. The operative Indian statutes are the **Merchant
Shipping Act, 1958**, the **Carriage of Goods by Sea Act, 1925**, the **Indian Bills of Lading
Act, 1856**, the **Marine Insurance Act, 1963**, the **Indian Contract Act, 1872** and the
**Multimodal Transportation of Goods Act, 1993**.

The 2025 replacements are recorded here **only so that a later session which pulls a 2026 answer
backwards onto this line reverses the statute reference instead of inheriting it.** Their exact
citation details are deliberately *not* asserted in this document, because no answer on this
paper may use them and `known_traps.md` §11 forbids inventing provisions of the 2025 Acts. The
assent dates for the Merchant Shipping Act, 2025 and the Coastal Shipping Act, 2025 are taken
from the corpus instrument logs; the other two are recorded as *replaced during 2025* without a
date, which is all this paper needs and all that was verified.

### 2.3 IMSBC amendment 07-23 entered into force **inside the sitting month** — the sharpest date on the paper

**Q9 is a liquefaction question sat in the month a new IMSBC amendment took effect.**

```
amendment 06-21   MSC.500(105)     in force  1 December 2023
amendment 07-23                    in force  1 January 2025      <-- the sitting month
                                   voluntary application from 1 January 2024
```

An answer that describes the IMSBC Code at this sitting must describe it **as amended by 07-23**.
An answer that names 06-21 as current is describing the Code as it stood one month earlier.

**Evidence status — recorded, not glossed.** The True Source corpus holds the IMSBC chain only
as far as `MSC.500(105)` (06-21) and its own instrument log flags everything after that as an
open acquisition item (**RQ-13**, *"amendments after 06-21 … verification required"*). The
adopting resolution for 07-23 was therefore **not read** in this session. The **1 January 2025
in-force date** is carried as an authoritative-secondary claim and flagged
`B_CURRENCY_CHECK` on Q9; the resolution number is **not asserted as verified**. Recording the
gap is the governed behaviour — see [`PASTPAPER_PRODUCTION_PROTOCOL.md`] and playbook §9,
*"never invented, never guessed"*.

This does **not** weaken the answer. Liquefaction is governed by IMSBC sections 4, 7, 8 and
Appendix 2, whose substance 07-23 did not rewrite; the amendment matters for *which edition the
candidate names*, and that is exactly what is stated.

### 2.4 FuelEU Maritime began to apply on 1 January 2025 — and it is not IMO law

**Q7 is a well-to-wake question sat in the month the world's first binding well-to-wake fuel
regime started to apply.** Regulation (EU) 2023/1805 has applied since **1 January 2025**, on a
**well-to-wake** GHG-intensity basis, to energy used by ships calling at EEA ports.

Two errors are available here and both are easy:

1. **Calling it IMO law.** It is a **regional** instrument of the European Union. The IMO
   well-to-wake mandate at this sitting is the *2023 GHG Strategy*, `MEPC.377(80)`, together
   with the **2024 LCA Guidelines**, `MEPC.391(81)` — **guidelines, not a mandatory measure**.
2. **Importing the Net-Zero Framework.** The GFI mechanism was approved at **MEPC 83 in April
   2025** and adopted in **October 2025**. At January 2025 **there was no approved draft
   chapter 5 text**. Any answer describing GFI tiers, remedial units or `US$100`/`US$380` unit
   prices on this paper is describing a document that did not exist. **PROHIBITED.**

`QP2508-Q8` and `QP2602-Q8` are solved MIW answers that legitimately carry the Net-Zero
Framework because their sittings are August 2025 and February 2026. **Neither may be pulled
back onto this line.** They are the specific forward-contamination hazard for this paper and
the sweep is run against them by name.

### 2.5 The LCA guidelines edition changed in 2024, and the superseded one is the famous one

`MEPC.376(80)` — the *2023* LCA Guidelines — is the resolution most MIW material cites, because
`MEPC.377(80)` paragraph 3.2 points at it by name. At January 2025 it is **superseded**: the
**2024 Guidelines on life cycle GHG intensity of marine fuels, resolution `MEPC.391(81)`**,
adopted at MEPC 81 on 22 March 2024, are the current edition.

**Evidence status.** `MEPC.391(81)`'s identity and adoption session are verified through the
existing MIW record for `QP2508-Q8`, which states expressly that the resolution **was not
read**. That limitation is carried forward unchanged on Q7 as `C_ACCEPTED_LIMITATION`: the
answer names the current edition and does not quote it.

### 3.3 The one real reuse on this paper is a limb, and it points backwards

`QP2404-Q5` (April 2024) and `QP2409-Q8` (September 2024) print an identical stem to each other:

> *"a) Briefly discuss Anti fouling convention and state all Documents and records to be
> maintained on a ship to demonstrate Anti fouling convention compliance. (8) b) … alternatives
> to tin-based antifouling paints …"*

**QP2501-Q4(c)** — *"Documents and records to be maintained on a ship to demonstrate convention
compliance. (5)"* — is the **same sub-task**, at 5 marks instead of 8 and stripped of the
"briefly discuss the convention" half. The rest of QP2501-Q4 (cathodic versus anodic protection;
the application process and its supervision) has **no** counterpart in either donor.

- **Direction: BACKWARD.** Both donors are *earlier* sittings (April 2024, September 2024) than
  January 2025. **Forward contamination cannot arise from them.**
- **The class that CAN fire is the opposite one:** an earlier donor does not automatically carry
  a later amendment. Checked: the AFS cybutryne control entered into force **1 January 2023**,
  before *all three* sittings, so the donors and this paper stand on the same AFS text. No
  AFS amendment entered into force between September 2024 and January 2025.
- **Not promoted to tier D.** A shared limb is not the same examiner task; recording it as D
  would tell a candidate the question had been set before, and would create a false family edge
  of exactly the kind `recurrence_model.py` documents. Q4 stays **tier C, limb-supported**, with
  the relationship recorded in `reuse_evidence`.

## 4. CONTAMINATION SWEEP — WHAT IS SWEPT, AND THE POSITIVE CONTROL

With no donors, the classic *donor* contamination sweep has nothing to bite on. That is
precisely when a zero result is worthless, so the sweep is controlled.

**Classes swept:**

1. **Post-sitting instrument import** — any `A.12xx(34)`, the Merchant Shipping Act 2025 and the
   other three 2025 Indian statutes, the IMO Net-Zero Framework / GFI, the Mediterranean ECA
   *taking effect*, `MEPC.385(81)` ECAs as in force, HNS as in force.
2. **Later-sitting MIW answer import** — named targets `QP2508-Q8`, `QP2602-Q8` (Net-Zero),
   `QP2506-Q5`, `QP2508-Q1`, `QP2602-Q1` (LLMC), `QP2511-Q5`, `QP2603-Q6` (ESP/CAP),
   `QP2606-Q9` (FSA).
3. **Authoring-date leakage** — the words *2026*, *August 2026* and the session date must not
   appear in any shipped byte as though they were part of the sitting's world.
4. **Superseded-edition survival** — `MEPC.376(80)` presented as current; IMSBC 06-21 presented
   as current.

**Positive control.** The sweep is proved able to fire before its zero is believed: the
temporal sweep is run against a seeded fixture, and the known-traps checker carries a live
positive in this paper's own subject matter — `known_traps.md` §1, *"strict liability on the
registered owner"*, which a Nairobi answer will trip if it is phrased the way the Convention is
usually described. **Q1 is written in the form `known_traps.md` records as correct — *"makes
the registered owner strictly liable"* — for exactly the reason QP2601-Q4 was.**
