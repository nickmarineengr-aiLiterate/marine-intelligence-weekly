### 4.1 Q2 — two instruments moved in the fourteen months before this sitting, in opposite directions

MSC.560(108), STCW A-VI/1-4    IN FORCE  1 January 2026       <-- eleven months AFTER the sitting

**Both are about the human element. One is law here and one is not.**

- The **MLC 2022 amendments are in force** and a February 2025 answer may rely on them. Their

- **`MSC.560(108)` is adopted and NOT in force.** Mandatory minimum training on the prevention of

- `MSC.486(103)` / `MSC.487(103)` — the high-voltage definition and ETO recognition — entered

### 4.2 Q3 — "Integrated Safety Management System" is not an IMO term, and the answer must say so

**There is no instrument called the ISMS.** The ISM Code defines a **"safety management
system" (SMS)**; SOLAS Chapter IX makes it mandatory; nothing in IMO's vocabulary contains the
word "integrated" in this position. The stem's phrase is **industry and management usage** for
the practice of integrating the SMS with the other management systems a company runs — quality,
environmental, occupational health and safety, energy and security.

This is a **jurisdiction-of-vocabulary** issue rather than a temporal one, but it is recorded
here because the failure mode is identical: an answer that treats ISMS as a defined IMO
instrument will invent requirements the Code does not contain. The governed handling is the one
`PASTPAPER_PRODUCTION_PROTOCOL.md` prescribes for a printed anomaly — **answer what was printed,
state plainly what the term is and is not, and never silently normalise it into "SMS"**.

The temporally load-bearing elements of Q3 are all **stable**: SOLAS IX, the ISM Code as amended
by `MSC.353(92)`, and the ISM certificates (DOC and SMC). The only thing that moved is guidance
— `A.1184(33)`, §3.3.

### 4.3 Q5 — the Act is directly held, and its s.13 does NOT state the qualifications

Q5 is the **best-evidenced question on the paper**. The True Source corpus holds the **official
India Code text of the Admiralty (Jurisdiction and Settlement of Maritime Claims) Act, 2017**
(Act 22 of 2017, in force 1 April 2018), and it was **read directly** this session. Sections
2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 13, 14 and 17 are **P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED** for this answer.

The load-bearing finding is about limb (b). The stem asks for *"the qualifications required for
their appointment under Indian maritime regulations"*. **Section 13 does not state any
qualification.** It provides that the Central Government **shall appoint by notification a list
of assessors with such qualifications and experience in admiralty and maritime matters** — and
the nature of their duties, their fees and ancillary matters — **"in the manner as may be
prescribed"**, `prescribed` being defined by s.2(1)(j) as *prescribed by rules made by the
Central Government*. The Act therefore **creates the mechanism and delegates the criteria.**

An answer that invents a qualification list is fabricating delegated legislation. The correct
answer states the delegation, states what s.13 does provide — including s.13(2), that appointing
assessors is **no bar to a party examining its own expert witnesses** — and identifies the
qualification detail as resting in rules made under s.16, which were **not read** and are
flagged accordingly.

**Temporal position: clean.** The 2017 Act was in force throughout, its repeals under s.17 are of
the colonial statutes of 1840/1861/1890/1891 and the Letters Patent 1865, and the corpus records
an open check (**RQ-34**) on post-2017 amendments which is carried forward as a limitation, not
closed. No 2025 Indian statute touches admiralty jurisdiction.

### 4.4 Q4 — THE HIGH FLAG. The tanker vetting regime changed shape in the year before this sitting

**OCIMF's SIRE programme moved from SIRE (VIQ7) to SIRE 2.0 during 2024**, and by this sitting
SIRE 2.0 — a risk-based, digital, question-pool inspection with a photographic record and
Core/Rotational/Campaign question sets — is the regime a candidate would actually meet.

This is flagged **HIGH and `B_CURRENCY_CHECK`** for a reason that must be stated rather than
hidden: **the corpus does not hold any OCIMF document.** `08-industry-organisations/INDUSTRY_SOURCES.md`
carries OCIMF and SIRE 2.0 at **concept level only**, explicitly classified *"citation/index only
— official web sources listed; no PDFs held."* The transition date is therefore **authoritative
secondary and was not read in an OCIMF source**.

The governed consequence, and the one this answer takes:

> **The answer is written so that it does not depend on the transition date.** The purpose,
> process, the Chief Engineer's role and the machinery-space subject matter of a vetting
> inspection are stable across both generations. Where SIRE 2.0's distinctive features are
> described they are labelled as the current generation and flagged; the answer never asserts a
> go-live date, and never presents a commercial industry scheme as statutory law.

The second half of the flag is the one candidates lose marks on and it is **not** time-sensitive
at all: **vetting is not a statutory inspection.** It is a commercial assurance process run by
oil majors and charterers. It confers no certificate, engages no flag State, and a poor report
withdraws commercial acceptance rather than the ship's entitlement to trade. That distinction is
the spine of the answer.
