**February 2026, Engineering Management, MEO Class I.**

`QP2607_TRUE_SOURCE_DEMAND_MAP.md` and `QP2601_TRUE_SOURCE_DEMAND_MAP.md`, neither of which is
modified by this document.

This is the handoff contract to the corpus-production track: what QP2602 actually needed in order to
be verified, how strong the evidence was, and what acquiring a source would unblock.

> **`reference_shelf` is empty on every QP2602 question, by design.** No corpus object was
> fabricated, no object id invented, no PDF page cited. See `MIW_TRUE_SOURCE_CONTRACT.md` §3 and §5.
> This map records *demand*, not mappings.

## 4. What February changed about the corpus requirements

Three findings that are about the corpus model rather than about any one instrument.

**1. A corpus object must be able to hold a repealed instrument.** Q5 and Q9 are answered on the
Merchant Shipping Act 1958, which was in force at the sitting and repealed five weeks later. A
store that only ever holds *current* law cannot serve a past-paper product at all. It needs the
text, the repeal date, and the repealing provision, and the resolver needs to answer "what was in
force on 24 February 2026?" rather than only "what is in force now?".

**2. A corpus object must never be created for an unadopted instrument.** Q8 depends on the
Net-Zero Framework **not** being law at the sitting date. A corpus object carrying the draft
Chapter 5 text would make the wrong answer look sourced. The January map stated this rule; February
supplies the case that proves it.

**3. Amendment state is a first-class field, not a footnote.** Three separate February claims turn
on it: the LLMC limits (raised 2015, raisable again by tacit acceptance), the CII reduction factors
(extended in April 2025, under review again from 1 January 2026), and the SOLAS ro-ro fire
amendments (in force 1 January 2026, with a separate existing-ship date of 1 January 2028). Text
without amendment state would have produced three wrong answers.
