Marine Insurance Act 1963, no Marine Insurance Act 1906, no York-Antwerp Rules in any edition.

This is not theoretical. `QP2304-Q3` carries **zero `P1_PRIMARY_VERIFIED` claims** — the only
question class on that paper that does — and its entire answer is declared established law and
practice. The same is true of the general-average family, which runs to six sittings.

### 1.3 Merchant Shipping Act 1958 enters the ranking, and it is an ACTIVE DEFECT SOURCE

Absent from the provisional ranking entirely. It should not be, for a reason no other gap has:

> **The corpus holds the WRONG Act.** `06-india-law-and-dg-shipping/` contains the Merchant
> Shipping Act **2025** and the Coastal Shipping Act **2025**, and not the 1958 Act. Every sitting
> before 15 March 2026 — that is **59 of the 61 papers in the window** — is governed by the 1958
> Act. The corpus therefore offers a superficially authoritative Indian statute that is wrong for
> almost the entire corpus.

`QP2304-Q4` had to carry its section numbers as *inherited authoritative secondary* from
`QP2503-Q9` because the 1958 Act could not be read. That is a citation chain with no primary
anchor at the bottom, on 19 questions across 18 sittings in all six years.

## 3. TOP 3 KIMI-SIZED PACKAGES

Chosen to be small, bounded, high-yield and independently verifiable.

### K-1 — Marine Insurance Core
**Marine Insurance Act 1963 (India)**, with the 1906 UK Act as the parallel, plus **York-Antwerp
Rules 1994, 2004 and 2016**.
*Bounded:* two short statutes and three rule-sets; the Acts are public-domain Indian/UK
legislation. *Why sized right:* s.66 (general average), the partial-loss sections and YAR Rule A
carry almost every mark in an 11-family topic. **Carry all three YAR editions** — the edition is
contractual, and a 1994-vs-2016 substitution is a recorded defect class.

### K-2 — Merchant Shipping Act 1958, working parts only
Not the whole Act. **Part IX** (ss. 334–336, unsafe ships and detention), **Part XA**
(ss. 356J–356L, pollution directions and cost recovery), **Part XIII** (ss. 390–404, wreck and
salvage), plus the collision-duty and casualty-inquiry Parts.
*Bounded:* four Parts, publicly published Indian legislation. *Why sized right:* it converts the
largest inherited-citation chain in the corpus into primary-verified text, and closes a live
wrong-Act trap.

### K-3 — Liability Convention Texts
**CLC 1992**, **Bunkers 2001**, **LLMC 1976/1996 Protocol**, **HNS 1996/2010** — the articles
only, not commentary.
*Bounded:* four instruments, article-level. *Why sized right:* the corpus already indexes them but
holds **no wording**, and the status facts it does hold are dated 2026 and are forward-contamination
for every historical paper. Text plus *dated* status would fix both at once.
