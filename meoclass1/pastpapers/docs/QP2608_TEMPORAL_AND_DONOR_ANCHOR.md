### 2.1 The temporal trap that would have wrecked Q3

Every carriage answer in the corpus written before October 2025 stands on the **Indian Carriage of
Goods by Sea Act, 1925**. That Act was **repealed with effect from 10 September 2025** — eleven
months before this sitting. `QP2510-Q2` (October 2025) is the only prior paper written on the
replacement statutes, and its Gazette-verified statutory layer is what Q3 rests on.

An answer to Q3 built from any pre-October-2025 corpus material, or from any textbook in general
circulation, would have been written on a **repealed statute**. This is recorded here because the
same trap will recur on every future carriage question.

## 4. THE FINDING AGAINST THE PRINTED PAPER — Q9

> **The question's premise is one year short of the regulation it names.**

`MARPOL Annex VI regulation 28.7` triggers a plan of corrective actions where a ship is rated
**D for THREE consecutive years, or E in any one year**. The paper posits **two** consecutive D
ratings, which does not engage regulations 28.7 to 28.9 at all.

Checked against the Organization's own published EEXI/CII material before the answer was framed, and
consistent with the verified regulation 28 record already carried on `QP2504-Q6`, `QP2508-Q2`,
`QP2602-Q2`, `QP2401-Q3` and `QP2410-Q8`.

**Handling, under the standing rule that examiner errors are preserved and never silently
normalised:**

1. `text_verbatim` records the question **exactly as printed**.
2. The answer **states the correct trigger** and places the ship one year short of it.
3. The answer **still delivers the plan the examiner asked for** — as the voluntary plan a prudent
   Chief Engineer builds after a second D, drafted in the form regulation 28.8 will require, and
   which becomes the mandatory plan on a third D.

Nothing is refused, nothing is invented, and the candidate is given both the deliverable and the
rule. **This is the single highest-value proposition on the paper and it is flagged
`B_CURRENCY_CHECK` for re-confirmation before publication.**

The assembled-answer sweep, run against a **publish-mode build** rather than against the spec,
found **eight occurrences of production vocabulary addressed to a paying candidate** — statements of
the form *"MIW holds no licensed text of…"*, *"verified in earlier MIW work"*, *"not read during
this session"* — all inside the candidate-facing **Uncertainty** sections of Q3, Q4, Q8 and Q9.

This is the recurrence of a known class: `CURRENT_STATUS` records the same defect on QP2311
(*"no MIW donor at all"* and *"re-check before publication"* shipping to a candidate).

**All eight were rewritten** to preserve the honesty — which is the whole point of an Uncertainty
section — while removing the internal actor. The limitation is now stated as a fact about *the
answer* rather than about *our holdings*, and in two places it was turned into usable exam advice
("quote the substance with confidence and the number with care"; "do not quote one in the
examination").

**The lesson worth keeping: the sweep only works against the PUBLISH build.** The review build
deliberately renders production metadata, so a sweep run against it cannot distinguish designed
disclosure from a leak. Sweep the mode the candidate gets.
