## 4. LATER-STATE REVERSALS — question by question

### Q7 — the sharpest reversal on the paper

The stem asks for **examples** of the adoption-to-entry-into-force delay. Every solved donor in the
entry-into-force family is written for a 2025 or 2026 sitting, and the example each of them reaches
for is the **Hong Kong Convention** — adopted 15 May 2009, **in force 26 June 2025**. At an August
2023 sitting the HKC is the correct example *of a convention still waiting*, and stating that it "took
sixteen years and entered into force in 2025" is a forward trap that reads as authoritative. The
answer therefore uses HKC as a **live, unresolved** example — as it stood in August 2023, with
Bangladesh's and Liberia's accessions of June 2023 having just satisfied the conditions and the
twenty-four-month clock running — and takes its *completed* examples from conventions that had already
entered into force by this sitting (SOLAS 1974, MARPOL 73/78, the Ballast Water Management Convention
2004/2017, the Nairobi Wreck Removal Convention 2007/2015, the Bunkers Convention 2001/2008). The
tacit-acceptance contrast is drawn against MARPOL article 16, which is temporally stable.

### Q8 — the HSSC survey guidelines edition

`A.1156(32)` (Survey Guidelines under the Harmonized System of Survey and Certification, 2021) is the
operative edition at this sitting. `A.1186(33)` was adopted **6 December 2023** and revoked it. Every
solved donor sits after that date, so the number in the donor material is wrong here and is reversed.
The SOLAS Consolidated Edition current at this sitting is **2020**, not 2024.

### Q5 and Q9 — the III obligations list

`A.1157(32)` is operative; `A.1187(33)` (6 December 2023) does not exist yet. `QP2309-Q2` already
carries this reversal correctly for September 2023, and the same reversal is carried here. The **III
Code itself** (`A.1070(28)`, mandatory 1 January 2016) is unaffected — it is the list that was
replaced, not the Code. `A.1155(32)` is likewise the operative PSC Procedures for Q9's port-State limb.

### Q1 — the digital-regulation frame

The IoT donor is April 2024 and its regulatory framing reaches for **EU ETS applied to maritime
(1 January 2024)** and **FuelEU Maritime (adopted 13 September 2023)**. **Both are future at this
sitting** and are removed. What is available and used instead: the **2023 IMO GHG Strategy** of
7 July 2023, one month old and the freshest thing on the paper; **EEXI and operational CII** applicable
from 1 January 2023 with the first reporting year running; the **2022 SEEMP Guidelines**; and the
**Maritime Cyber Risk Management Guidelines** (`MSC-FAL.1/Circ.3`) with the ISM cyber-risk deadline of
1 January 2021 already passed. `MSC-FAL.1/Circ.3/Rev.2` (June 2022) is the operative revision;
`Rev.3` is future.

### Q6 — no reversal required

The donor is earlier and same-year, and the physics of power transformation and the sea-trial
manoeuvre set are not date-dependent. The only currency point is that the **EEDI/EEXI verification
context** for a speed trial is stated as at 2023, and the ITTC recommended-procedure family is cited
generically rather than by edition year.

### Q2, Q3 — no reversal required

Earlier same-year donors on temporally stable subjects. Q2's one currency point is that the **Maritime
Liens and Mortgages Convention 1993** entered into force 5 September 2004 and **India is not a party**;
the Indian route is the Admiralty Act 2017. Q3 is naval architecture.

### Q4 — fresh, therefore no inherited law

The only regulatory anchors are SOLAS chapter II-1 part E (periodically unattended machinery spaces),
the ISM Code element 10 maintenance requirement, and class UMS notation practice — all stable at
this sitting.
