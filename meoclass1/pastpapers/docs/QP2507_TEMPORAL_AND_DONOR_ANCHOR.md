## 1. THE SITTING — AND WHY THIS PAPER IS THE EASY ONE TEMPORALLY

The sitting month is **July 2025**. **No day is printed on the source copy**, exactly as for the
four Batch 2 papers before it.

QP2504 was the hard case: three separate boundaries fell *inside* April 2025 and the relation
between the examination day and each boundary day was unknowable. **July 2025 is the opposite,
and the result is provable rather than assumed:**

> **NO boundary engaged by this paper falls inside July 2025.**
> Every boundary either closed before 1 July 2025 or opened after 31 July 2025.

That was tested against the full standing boundary set (§3), not asserted. Two boundaries sit
very close to the month on either side, and both were checked specifically because proximity is
where this fails:

| Boundary | Date | Relation to July 2025 | Day-dependent? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hong Kong Convention — entry into force** | **26 June 2025** | **4 days before the month opens** | **No** — operative on every day of July |
| **MEPC.385(81) — entry into force** | **1 August 2025** | **1 day after the month closes** | **No** — not in force on any day of July |

The governing rule for this paper is therefore stated once and holds without a hedge:

> **No answer on this paper can depend on which day in July 2025 the candidate sat, because no
> boundary this paper engages moves within the month.**

This is a *stronger* statement than QP2504's. There, day-independence had to be engineered
answer-by-answer around three in-month straddles. Here it is a property of the calendar.

**The corollary matters and is easy to miss.** Because nothing straddles, the danger on this
paper is not the in-month straddle at all — it is **§4, the corpus holding a revoked instrument**,
and **§5, the statute the corpus does hold being the wrong one**. The temporal risk moved from
the calendar into the evidence.
