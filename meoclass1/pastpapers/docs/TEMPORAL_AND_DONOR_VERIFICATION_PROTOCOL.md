# TEMPORAL AND DONOR VERIFICATION PROTOCOL

**Governed by `PRODUCTION_PROTOCOL_INDEX.md`. Read every paper-production session.**

This is where the most expensive mistakes have historically been made. Every rule below exists
because something went wrong once.

---

## 1. SITTING-DATE TRUTH IS MANDATORY

**Every answer must be true as at the date of its own examination sitting — not as at today.**

- Establish the sitting date first. It governs which edition, amendment, resolution and entry
  into force applies.
- A regulation adopted, approved or amended *after* the sitting does not belong in the answer,
  however current it is now.
- Conversely, something in force at the sitting but superseded since is still the correct
  answer for that paper.

**Approved ≠ adopted ≠ in force.** These are three different states with three different
dates. Do not collapse them.

---

## 2. INTAKE TEMPORAL FLAGS ARE PROMPTS, NOT EVIDENCE

An intake temporal flag means *"someone should look at this"*. It does **not** mean the risk
is real.

Multiple intake flags have been proven **wrong** on inspection. Treat every flag as a question
to be answered against a primary source, and record the answer either way — including
"checked, no temporal issue".

**Absence of a flag is not clearance either.** The sweep in §6 is what clears a paper, not the
flag list.

---

## 3. TEMPORAL TRAPS CAN RUN BACKWARDS

The obvious trap is an instrument that changed *after* the sitting. The subtler and more
dangerous one runs the other way: an older paper whose correct answer is **not** the modern
one, where the modern answer looks right and is wrong for that sitting.

Both directions must be checked. A paper that required only temporal adaptation and produced
no new donors is a normal outcome, not a sign the check was skipped.

---

## 4. DONOR REUSE — THE TARGET QUESTION OWNS ITS ANSWER

Reuse tiers describe **how similar two questions are**. They never describe how similar the
two *answers* may be.

**An exact question is NOT an exact answer.** Even at the highest reuse tier, the answer must
be re-anchored to the target paper's sitting date. Sitting-relative prose — "recently
adopted", "the current requirement", "from next year" — must be re-derived every time, not
copied.

Before reusing any donor, compute and record three deltas:

| Delta | Question to answer |
|---|---|
| **Question delta** | What words actually differ? A changed verb can change the whole task. |
| **Marks delta** | Different marks mean a different expected depth and structure. |
| **Temporal delta** | What changed between the donor's sitting and the target's? |

If all three are genuinely nil, say so explicitly in the record. Do not leave it implied.

---

## 5. RECURRENCE TRUTH IS SEPARATE FROM DONOR TRUTH

- **Recurrence** = the observed fact that a question reappeared across sittings.
- **Donor** = the authoring decision to build one answer from another.

They are recorded separately and must not be conflated. A recurrence does not authorise reuse,
and a reuse does not establish a recurrence.

Whole papers have been found reprinted across years. Chronology must be fixed **before**
recurrence is computed, or the map inverts.

---

## 6. THE MANDATORY SWEEPS

Run all three before a paper is considered complete. The sweeps — not the flags — are what
clear a paper.

1. **Assembled-answer sweep.** Read every answer as a candidate would, end to end. Errors that
   are invisible per-field become obvious in assembled prose.
2. **Donor contamination sweep.** Search the paper for any sentence that is only true of a
   *donor's* sitting: dates, "recently", "currently", edition numbers, amendment references.
3. **Future contamination sweep.** Search for anything that entered force after this paper's
   sitting.

Record each sweep as run, with what it found — including "nothing".

---

## 7. STABLE ROUTE, CHANGING TRUTH

A question's **answer route** may be stable across sittings while the **regulatory truth**
inside it changes. This is normal and is not a contradiction.

Keep the two separable: reuse the route where the examiner's task is unchanged, but re-verify
every factual limb against the target sitting date.

---

## 8. THE MOST DANGEROUS SINGLE ERROR

**Using the wrong edition of a source.** It produces an answer that is internally coherent,
confidently written, well-structured and wrong — and nothing downstream will catch it.

Verify the edition against the sitting date before authoring, not after.
