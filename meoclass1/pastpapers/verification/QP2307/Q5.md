## 5. SOURCES AND PROVENANCE

**Regulations and instruments engaged:**

- Convention for the Unification of Certain Rules of Law with respect to Collisions between Vessels, Brussels 1910 (the Collision Convention) - the source of the doctrine; UNAMENDED. Proportionate liability by degree of fault; equal apportionment where the degrees cannot be established or the faults are equal; several liability for the vessels, cargoes and effects, and joint and several liability for loss of life and personal injury; the abolition of all legal presumptions of fault; and the two-year time bar
- Merchant Shipping Act, 1958 (India) - the statute giving effect to those rules at this sitting, and carrying the master's duty to stand by and render assistance after a collision, together with the presumption that follows a failure to do so. NO SECTION IS CITED BY NUMBER
- COLREGs 1972 - the standard of conduct whose breach is the usual foundation of fault. Evidence of fault; NOT a presumption of causation
- Convention on Limitation of Liability for Maritime Claims (LLMC) - tonnage-based limitation, separate funds for claims for loss of life and personal injury and for property claims, and the conduct barring limitation. Identity level; NO FIGURE QUOTED
- Civil Liability Convention 1992 and the Bunkers Convention 2001 - the strict, channelled pollution liability that runs alongside the fault-based collision claim. Named by identity and effect only
- The collision liability (running down) clause in hull and machinery insurance - three-fourths of the liability to the other vessel. Commercial contract, not an instrument
- The Both-to-Blame Collision Clause in bills of lading - the contractual answer to the cargo circularity created by several liability. Commercial contract, not an instrument
- THE PENNSYLVANIA RULE - UNITED STATES LAW ONLY. Named as a contrast and expressly excluded as the applicable position
