## 1. THE COMMENCEMENT QUESTION — RESOLVED

This mattered, and it was not a hypothetical concern.

**The enabling power (verbatim, section 1(2) of the Act):**

> "(2) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the
> Official Gazette, appoint, **and different dates may be appointed for different provisions of this
> Act.**"

So staged commencement was expressly permitted. (Note: the commencement provision is
**section 1(2)**, not section 1(3) as the previous build's working assumption implied.)

**The notification actually made (verbatim, S.O. 1244(E)):**

> "**S.O. 1244(E).—** In exercise of the powers conferred by sub-section (2) of section 1 of the
> Merchant Shipping Act, 2025 (24 of 2025), the Central Government hereby appoints the **15th day of
> March, 2026** as the date on which the **provisions of said Act** shall come into force."

**Conclusion.** The notification appoints **one date** for "the provisions of said Act". It does not
enumerate sections, does not exclude any provision, and does not appoint any second date. **The whole
Act came into force on 15 March 2026.** The power to stage commencement existed and **was not
exercised.**

That is a stronger and more defensible finding than "it probably all commenced", and it is the form
the answer now takes.

> **Caution recorded for the corpus.** At least one widely-read public secondary source was still
> describing the Act as *"not yet in force"* in late March 2026, after commencement. Secondary
> summaries lag; the Gazette does not.

### Correction applied at 7.10

The previous answer said the Act "is drafted to give domestic effect to India's obligations under
**IMO** and **ILO** instruments". A full-text search shows the Act **never names the International
Maritime Organization** (0 occurrences of either spelling). The claim was true in substance but
overstated in form. The answer now cites the **long title's** general treaty-compliance purpose and
the **MLC 2006**, which *is* named in the Act. This is exactly the kind of drift that section-level
verification is for.

## 3. Statement-type separation — still the point of this question

| Category | In this answer |
|---|---|
| **What the Act legally does** | s.324(1) repeal; s.15(1) ownership; s.16 bareboat charter-cum-demise; s.17 recycling registration; s.59 minimum age; long-title treaty-compliance purpose |
| **Government policy expectation** | Tonnage growth, inward investment, ease of doing business — objectives, not guarantees |
| **MIW inference** | The registration reforms remove a genuine legal barrier, so the direction of effect is right; magnitude depends on fiscal treatment, subordinate rules and commercial factors the Act does not control |

The question's own wording ("how it **shall** contribute to trade and tonnage expansion") invites the
candidate to assert economic outcomes as statutory consequences. The answer deliberately does not.
An Act does not guarantee tonnage growth.
