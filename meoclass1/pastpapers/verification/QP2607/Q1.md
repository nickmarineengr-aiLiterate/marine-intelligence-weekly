## 0. The distinction this question turns on

The examiner asked about **iron ore pellets**, not iron ore fines. These are different IMSBC cargoes,
and a candidate who answers "Group A, liquefaction, TML" has answered a different question.

**Revised 2026-08-08 [RT-03].** The v0.1 version of this section presented a clean binary — fines are
Group A, pellets are Group C. That is an oversimplification and has been corrected.

| Bulk Cargo Shipping Name | Group | Governing hazard | Provenance |
|---|---|---|---|
| IRON ORE | C | Not liable to liquefy; high density | `P2_AUTHORITATIVE_SECONDARY` |
| IRON ORE FINES | **A**, *conditionally* | Liquefaction above TML — **but** the schedule carries qualifying criteria (goethite content, particle size distribution) under which fines may be carried as **Group C** | `P2_AUTHORITATIVE_SECONDARY`; thresholds `UNRESOLVED` |
| **IRON ORE PELLETS** | **C** | Not liable to liquefy; not a chemical hazard | `P2_AUTHORITATIVE_SECONDARY` — **not read from the primary schedule** |
| DIRECT REDUCED IRON (B) — lumps, **pellets**, cold-moulded briquettes | **B** | Chemical (hydrogen evolution, self-heating, oxygen depletion) | `P2_AUTHORITATIVE_SECONDARY` |

Two traps, not one. The obvious one is that "pellets" also appears in the DRI (B) schedule name, which
**is** a Group B chemical-hazard cargo. The subtler one is that the fines/pellets split is **not** a
property of the commodity at all — the fines schedule itself admits a Group C route on stated
criteria. So the rule a candidate should carry is neither "fines are A" nor "pellets are C" but:

> **The declared Bulk Cargo Shipping Name and its individual schedule govern. The commodity name
> decides nothing.**

`IRON ORE FINES` was introduced as a Group A individual schedule by **resolution MSC.393(95)**
(adopted June 2015) — IMSBC amendment 03-15 — voluntary from 1 January 2016 and mandatory from
1 January 2017, together with the Modified Proctor/Fagerberg test procedure. Its introduction followed
several bulk-carrier losses attributed to liquefaction of iron ore fines.

**Standing caveat.** MIW holds no licensed IMSBC Code. Every Group classification in this table is
corroborated secondary material, not primary schedule text. None of it is quoted in the answer, and no
characteristics figures (bulk density, stowage factor, angle of repose) are asserted anywhere.

## 2. Statement-type separation (per brief §10.3)

- **Established legal requirement:** IMSBC Code (mandatory via SOLAS VI/VII); SOLAS XII/11 and XII/12;
  SOLAS VI/2 cargo information; ISM Code 1.2.2.2; ORB/records where applicable.
- **IMO methodology, not a shipboard duty:** FSA per MSC-MEPC.2/Circ.12/Rev.2 — a rule-making tool.
- **Industry guidance:** BLU Code (recommendatory in itself, though given effect through SOLAS VI/7
  and national law), P&I loss-prevention practice on shipper declarations.
- **Company/SMS practice:** the ship-level risk assessment, hold inspection and bilge-sounding regimes
  that discharge ISM 1.2.2.2.
- **Engineering judgement:** stiff-ship rolling behaviour with high-density cargo; hold bilge line
  protection against fines ingress.
