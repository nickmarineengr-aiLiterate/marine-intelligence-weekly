## 0. Jurisdiction handling (per brief §9)

The question says "foreign waters" and names no State. **No local authority is named anywhere in the
answer.** The answer states the *duty* — report to the nearest coastal State through its designated
reporting system, via the contact list in the SOPEP appendix — and leaves the identity of that
authority as a function of position. This is both legally correct and the safer exam answer.

One consequence that must be stated rather than assumed: whether the ship is in internal waters, the
territorial sea or the EEZ changes which State's criminal law can bite. It does not change the duty to
report.

## 2. Statement-type separation (per brief §10.3)

- **Established legal requirement:** MARPOL Annex I regs 17 and 37; MARPOL art. 8 and Protocol I;
  MARPOL Annex VI reg 18 (BDN); SOLAS XI-1 + Casualty Investigation Code; Bunkers Convention 2001;
  OPRC 1990; the coastal State's own national law.
- **IMO recommendatory instruments:** res. A.851(20) reporting format; res. LEG.3(91) and A.1056(27)
  fair treatment.
- **Company/SMS practice:** SOPEP action checklists, muster and stop-bunkering procedures, internal
  incident reporting under ISM 9, DPA notification, drug-and-alcohol testing protocols.
- **Commercial/contractual:** P&I club notification, appointment of a local correspondent, instruction
  of lawyers, security/LOU negotiation.
- **Engineering judgement:** which tank to transfer to, whether to stop the transfer pump or shut the
  manifold valve first, scupper plugging sequence, use of the ship's own oil-spill kit versus calling
  a shore contractor.
