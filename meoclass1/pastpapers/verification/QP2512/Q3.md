## The risk this question actually carries, and how it was handled

**It is jurisdictional, not temporal.** The Indian Marine Insurance Act 1963 follows the English
Marine Insurance Act 1906 so closely that **a section number can be transposed between them without
the error being visible in the finished answer**. The checkpoint proposed section numbers for the
uncovered principles; those were treated as a hypothesis to be checked, not as evidence.

**Every section was read in the Indian Act**, not mapped from the English numbering:

| Principle | Section | Read at source |
|---|---|---|
| Marine insurance / indemnity | **s.3** | *"the insurer undertakes to indemnify the assured…against marine losses"* |
| Wagering void | **s.6** | *"Every contract of marine insurance by way of wagering is void"* |
| Insurable interest | **s.7** | *"every person has an insurable interest who is interested in a marine adventure"* |
| When interest must attach | **s.8** | *"must be interested…at the time of the loss"*; *"lost or not lost"* |
| Proximate cause | **s.55** | *"liable for any loss proximately caused by a peril insured against"* |
| Sue and labour | **s.78** | suing and labouring clause, recoverable notwithstanding a total loss |
| Subrogation | **s.79** | total loss — takes over what remains; partial loss — rights but no title |
| Contribution | **s.80** | double insurance, rateable contribution, suit against co-insurers |
| Under-insurance | **s.81** | assured is his own insurer for the balance |

**Also verified: the modern English reform of insurance law does not apply in India**, so the s.19/20
position stated is the Indian one. Carried from `QP2607-Q9`'s ruling and not disturbed.
