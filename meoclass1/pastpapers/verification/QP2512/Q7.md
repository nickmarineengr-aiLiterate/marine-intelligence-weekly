## Provenance — §2.1 applied, and the boundary drawn explicitly

**No instrument governs what a charterer may instruct.** The constraints are the **charterparty**
(a commercial contract between owner and charterer), the owner's obligation to provide a
**seaworthy** ship, and the **ISM Code**. `PASTPAPER_PRODUCTION_PROTOCOL.md` §2.1 governs the
charterparty and engineering limbs, and was applied as follows:

- **No regulation was manufactured** to govern charterer's instructions. The study guide and one
  retrieval card state expressly that none exists.
- **Charterparty terms are described by function, never quoted.** NYPE and the other standard forms
  exist in several editions with materially different wording and are amended by rider clauses in
  every fixture. **No clause number is cited and no charterparty wording is reproduced.** The
  employment and indemnity clause, the speed and consumption warranty and the off-hire clause are
  described by what they do.
- **No off-hire outcome is asserted for any particular failure.** The answer says the result depends
  on the **actual fixture**, including whether the clause operates on a **net-loss-of-time** or a
  **period** basis.
- **The regulatory limb is separable and treated to the higher standard.** The **ISM Code**
  obligations relied on — company responsibility for maintenance of the ship and equipment;
  identification of **critical equipment** whose sudden failure may cause a hazardous situation, with
  measures to promote reliability; **familiarisation** of newly joined personnel; **reporting** of
  non-conformities; and the **master's overriding authority** — are genuine requirements, mandatory
  through **SOLAS chapter IX**, and are what the answer rests on when it routes the Chief Engineer
  to a written assessment to the Company and the DPA.

The provenance summary reflects the true shape: **28 `ENGINEERING_JUDGEMENT`** and 12
