### The expansion — the issuing-authority limb comes back

November folded *"who can issue these certificates in India"* into *"the different certificates in
India"*. December prints it as a **separate question**. The donor already answers it — its route
step 5 is exactly that limb — so this needed **re-weighting, not research**. The step was retitled
*"Who issues them in India, validity and extension"* and given its own share of the marks so that it
cannot be reduced to a clause of the certificate limb, which is what the three-demand November split
did.

### The contraction — and this is the finding that matters

November's stem asked for the certificates *"in India"* **with no qualifier**. The donor's own model
answer states the consequence in terms: the stem asks for the certificates *"without confining them
to the ship"*, and *"the seafarer's own certificate therefore belongs in the answer"*. That deletion
is what brought the **crew's medical certificate** under MLC 2006 Regulation 1.2 and Standard A1.2
inside November's printed task and earned it a **sixth route step**.

**This sitting puts the qualifier back:** certificates *"issued **to a vessel** for maintenance of
**the ship** in good health"*. The donor's own stated condition for including the crew limb is
therefore **not met here**, and the limb has been removed from the answer layer.

**The removal follows from the donor's reasoning rather than contradicting it.** This is
`WORKFLOW_LESSONS.md` *"A compressed stem is a CHANGED TASK, not a shorter one"* running in the
opposite direction: November's compression **widened** the certificate limb; December's expansion
**narrows** it. A session that treated "identical topic, one month apart" as licence to copy would
have shipped a substantial section the examiner did not ask for, and under-weighted one he did.

**The crew is not deleted wholesale.** The stem's *agencies* limb still names *"the ship and the crew
in good health"*, so **DG Shipping remains in the agencies answer** as the authority for seafarer
medical fitness, acting under the **Merchant Shipping Act 1958**.

## Temporal review — December 2025

- **International Health Regulations (2005)**, as amended **2014, 2022 and 2024**. The **2024
  amendments entered into force 19 September 2025** — before *both* sittings, so the donor's
  currency point **carries forward rather than reversing**. Only the interval figure changes.
- **Article 37 is the Ship Declaration of Health** at this sitting, not the Maritime Declaration of
  Health.
- **Currency re-check over the extra month.** The donor's `B_CURRENCY_CHECK` asked whether any
  further IHR amendment entered into force between 19 September 2025 and the November sitting; none
  had. The same question was asked again for the additional month to December 2025: **same answer**.
  Recorded rather than inherited.

**The 34th Assembly boundary — checked, not engaged.** The IHR is a **World Health Assembly**
instrument, not an IMO Assembly one. No `A.12xx(34)` resolution is cited anywhere in this answer.
Negative finding, recorded.

**The Merchant Shipping Act boundary — engaged in the agencies limb, and correct.** DG Shipping acts
under the **Merchant Shipping Act 1958**, the operative Indian statute at this sitting; the Merchant
Shipping Act, 2025 had assent from 18 August 2025 but did not commence until 15 March 2026. The
donor already states the 1958 Act and required no reversal. **The premise was tested separately from
the conclusion**, per `WORKFLOW_LESSONS.md`: the donor does not reason from anything the 2025 Act
does, so there is no premature premise to remove.

**A scope decision is not a currency decision.** The removal of the crew limb is driven by the
printed words of this stem, not by any change in the law — MLC Standard A1.2 is unchanged between
the sittings. Recorded on the object so a later reader does not mistake one for the other.
