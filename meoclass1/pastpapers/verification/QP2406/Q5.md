## 9. ANSWER ROUTE AS BUILT

Archetype `explain`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route - `validate_spec.py` enforces the correspondence, so the candidate cannot be taught one order and shown another.

**1. The functional requirements - ISM Code paragraph 1.4** - limb **(A)**

- List them, do not discuss them - four marks for six items
- 1 A safety and environmental protection policy
- 2 Instructions and procedures for safe operation and environmental protection, in compliance with international and flag State legislation
- 3 Defined levels of authority and lines of communication, shore and shipboard
- 4 Procedures for reporting accidents and non-conformities with the Code
- 5 Procedures to prepare for and respond to emergency situations
- 6 Procedures for internal audits and management reviews
- Note the list is closed and numbered - five or eight shows you have not read 1.4
- Note requirement 6 is what limb (B) then examines: the Code builds audit into the definition of an SMS
- One line of context: adopted by A.741(18), mandatory through SOLAS chapter IX; objectives at 1.2

**2. The objectives of an ISM internal audit** - limb **(B)**

- Verify that activities comply with the SMS - what is written is what is done
- Verify the SMS is effective, which is a separate and harder question
- Confirm continued compliance with the Code and with statutory and flag State requirements
- Identify non-conformities and the conditions that could give rise to them
- Confirm previous corrective actions were implemented and are effective, not merely closed on paper
- Provide objective evidence for the management review
- Drive continual improvement and reinforce crew safety awareness
- The distinction to state: an audit audits the system; an inspection inspects the ship

**3. How the internal audit helps the external audit** - limb **(B)**

- Finds and closes non-conformities first - a closed internal finding with evidence beats an open external one
- Produces exactly the objective evidence the external auditor will call for
- Familiarises the crew with being audited - hesitation is what auditors record, not the answer
- Verifies documentation is controlled and current - superseded manuals are a commonest external finding
- Feeds the management review, showing the loop from finding to decision actually closes
- The negative case: audits that never raise a non-conformity prove the audits are not real, and the external auditor looks harder

**4. The salient issues addressed in an internal audit** - limb **(B)**

- Policy - implemented and understood at all levels
- Company responsibility and authority; the DPA's identity, role and accessibility
- Master's responsibility and overriding authority; the master's review (5.1.5)
- Resources and personnel - manning, qualification, familiarisation, training
- Shipboard operations - plans and instructions exist, are used, and match what the ship does
- Emergency preparedness - drills carried out, recorded and reviewed for lessons
- Reports and analysis of non-conformities, accidents and hazardous occurrences - raised, investigated, closed
- Maintenance and critical equipment identification and testing (paragraph 10)
- Documentation control and currency; certification - DOC and SMC valid and matching

**5. Frequency, and the arithmetic over the SMC cycle** - limb **(B)**

- Paragraph 12.1 - internal safety audits on board and ashore, intervals not exceeding twelve months
- In exceptional circumstances, exceeded by not more than three months - fifteen absolute
- Answer the question asked: over the SMC's five years, at least five internal audits
- Auditors independent of the area audited unless Company size makes it impracticable
- Results brought to the attention of all personnel responsible, with timely corrective action
- Contrast the external cycle: initial, intermediate between the second and third anniversary, renewal at five years; DOC verified annually

**6. Non-conformity and major non-conformity** - limb **(C)**

- 1.1.9 non-conformity - an observed situation where objective evidence indicates non-fulfilment of a specified requirement
- 1.1.10 major non-conformity - an identifiable deviation posing a serious threat to personnel or the ship, or a serious risk to the environment, requiring immediate corrective action
- AND it includes the lack of effective and systematic implementation of a requirement of the Code - the half everyone forgets
- 'Objective evidence' distinguishes a finding from an opinion
- 'Specified requirement' includes the Company's own SMS - so write procedures the ship will actually follow
- Consequence: corrective action for one; suspension or withdrawal of SMC or DOC, and detention, for the other

**7. Accidents and hazardous occurrences** - limb **(C)**

- Note the wording: the stem says 'occurrences', paragraph 9.1 says hazardous situations - treat both
- The Code does not define 'accident' - say so rather than inventing one
- Accident = unintended event that has caused harm - injury, death, damage or pollution
- Hazardous occurrence = event that could have and did not - the near miss
- Non-conformity = not an event at all, but a state of non-compliance
- Give worked examples: drill overdue (NC); fitter's hand injury (accident); fuel spray with no fire (hazardous occurrence); 15 ppm alarm disconnected (major NC); SMS on board but unused (major NC)
- Paragraph 9.1's object is improvement, not punishment - and few near-miss reports means under-reporting, not safety
