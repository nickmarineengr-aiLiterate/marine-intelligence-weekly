## 9. ANSWER ROUTE AS BUILT

Archetype `compare`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route - `validate_spec.py` enforces the correspondence, so the candidate cannot be taught one order and shown another.

**1. The single difference from which the rest follows** - limb **(a)**

- Fix the sense: 'civil law' here means the Romano-Germanic family, not civil versus criminal
- Civil law starts from a comprehensive enacted code; common law from accumulated decided cases
- Precedent is binding at common law (stare decisis), persuasive only in civil law
- Judicial law-making is part of the common law function and no part of the civil law one
- Statutory interpretation is narrow and literal at common law, purposive in civil law
- Present everything that follows as flowing from this one difference

**2. The judge** - limb **(a)**

- Common law judge - neutral umpire; rules on admissibility and law; does not investigate
- Long reasoned judgments, because they will bind later courts and are themselves a source of law
- Appointed late, from senior advocates
- Civil law judge - active investigator; directs the inquiry, questions witnesses, appoints the court's own expert
- Shorter formal judgments applying the code, not intended to create law
- A career judiciary, trained as judges from the start

**3. The lawyer** - limb **(a)**

- Common law advocate drives the case - claims, evidence, witnesses, strategy, discovery
- Examination in chief and cross-examination; oral forensic skill is the valued competence
- Cites cases as binding authority
- Civil law advocate assists the court within an inquiry the judge directs
- Proposes evidence rather than presenting it; questions usually put through the judge
- Written pleading and doctrinal reasoning; cites the code, with commentary and prior decisions as persuasive
- The practical consequence: the same collision is litigated completely differently, which is why forum clauses are negotiated hard

**4. Adversarial and inquisitorial - the models these roles create** - limb **(a)**

- Name both models expressly - this is what 'the roles of the lawyer and Judge' is really asking for
- Adversarial: two parties contest before a passive tribunal; truth from the contest
- Inquisitorial: the court investigates; truth from the inquiry
- Neither exists in pure form - say so, because that is the mark of understanding rather than memorising

**5. How a State becomes bound in the first place** - limb **(b)**

- Consent to be bound - ratification, acceptance, approval or accession - plus entry into force
- Identical for every State; a question of the law of treaties, not of the domestic system
- Whether it then has effect INSIDE the State is a separate question answered by that State's constitution
- A State that has ratified but not implemented is in breach and may not plead its internal law - but no shipowner can sue on the convention in its courts

**6. Monism and dualism** - limb **(b)**

- Monist - one legal order; the treaty becomes domestic law automatically on publication and often outranks ordinary statute
- Dualist - two legal orders; no domestic effect until an implementing Act is passed, and the statute governs if it diverges
- Examples: France, the Netherlands, Japan monist; the United Kingdom, India, Australia, Canada dualist
- THE KEY POINT: monism and dualism do NOT map onto civil law and common law - two independent axes
- The Scandinavian States are civil law and dualist; the United States is common law with self-executing and non-self-executing treaties
- The correlation exists because the common law world inherited a separation-of-powers rule, not a sources-of-law rule

**7. India as the worked example** - limb **(b)**

- Common law system, dualist State
- The executive negotiates, signs and ratifies without parliamentary approval
- Article 253 empowers Parliament to legislate to implement - even on State List subjects, so federalism is no obstacle
- Until then no domestic force, though courts use the convention to construe ambiguous legislation
- The vehicle for shipping is the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958, with rules and DG Shipping circulars supplying detail; DG Shipping authorises ROs
- Dedicated statutes elsewhere - Admiralty Act 2017, Marine Insurance Act 1963
- The consequence to state plainly: the ship complies with the flag State's rules, not with SOLAS directly

**8. The contrasting mechanism in a monist State** - limb **(b)**

- France: on ratification and publication the convention is part of French law and outranks ordinary statute
- No implementing Act needed; the court applies the convention text itself
- Faster - no legislative slot required
- But less adapted - penalties, enforcement and administrative detail still need domestic legislation

**9. Why the difference matters at sea** - limb **(b)**

- Uniformity is the point of maritime conventions and both routes leak
- Dualist leak: delay between ratification and implementation, and implementing statutes that shade the meaning
- Monist leak: the same Article read differently in different States, with no supranational tribunal
- IMO's answer is institutional, not legal - the III Code and the mandatory Member State Audit Scheme
- Close honestly: the difference is managed, not eliminated
