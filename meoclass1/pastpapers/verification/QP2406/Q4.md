## 9. ANSWER ROUTE AS BUILT

Archetype `explain`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route - `validate_spec.py` enforces the correspondence, so the candidate cannot be taught one order and shown another.

**1. What cold corrosion is**

- Sulphur in the fuel burns to SO2, part oxidises to SO3
- SO3 plus combustion water vapour gives sulphuric acid vapour
- It condenses on any surface below the acid dew point - far above the water dew point
- Liquid acid attacks the iron wherever the oil's alkaline reserve does not neutralise it
- Say the name is a misnomer - the liner is below the dew point, not cold

**2. Why the new generation of engines made it worse**

- Longer stroke, lower speed, bigger bore-to-stroke - more surface to cool, greater spread along the stroke
- De-rating and habitual part load - less heat in, so lower wall temperature
- Higher maximum combustion pressure - raises partial pressures and so raises the dew point
- EGR and other NOx measures - recirculate water vapour and acid species into the charge
- More effective cooling - removes heat from exactly the region that needs to stay hot
- The one-sentence answer: wall temperature down, dew point up, margin squeezed from both ends

**3. How it presents, and how to tell it from other wear**

- Location - upper liner, ring-swept region near top dead centre
- Clover-leafing between the lubricator quills - a neutralisation signature, not a mechanical one
- Matt, etched, micro-pitted surface; graphite standing proud in severe cases
- Wear rate rising out of proportion to hours, often after a change of service pattern
- Scrape-down: iron up, residual base number down
- Contrast: abrasive wear wants filtration, scuffing wants film, corrosion wants heat and alkalinity

**4. Limb A - increased jacket cooling water temperature** - limb **A**

- Raised jacket water outlet set point - more of the stroke above the dew point
- Load-dependent control - raise temperature as load FALLS; a fixed high setting is wrong at full load
- Separate or staged circuits so the upper liner is controlled independently of the cover
- Reduced upper-liner cooling by design - the same measure from the other end (see limb B)
- Liner wall temperature sensors so control acts on the variable that matters
- State the limits: boiling and steam pockets, thermal stress, system pressure and venting, cooler duty, less forgiving water treatment

**5. Limb B - new cylinder liner designs** - limb **B**

- Insulating tubes or inserts in the cooling bores - raise upper wall temperature locally
- Re-profiled bore cooling - fewer, shallower or shorter bores in the upper region
- Insulating collar at the top of the liner
- Anti-polishing ring (flame ring) - scrapes carbon off the piston crown top land, which would otherwise polish the liner and wipe the film
- Controlled surface finish - honing pattern, wave-cut or profiled grooves for oil retention and circumferential distribution
- Higher-alloy grey cast irons and surface treatments
- Sensor pockets for liner wall temperature; repositioned and improved lubricator quills
- Unify them: keep the top of the liner hot and keep the oil on it

**6. Limb C - new cylinder lubricators** - limb **C**

- Electronic timed pulse injection at a chosen crank angle - timing and quantity become independent variables, per cylinder
- Oil delivered just before the ring pack sweeps up, and distributed by the rings
- Feed rate proportional to load and to the sulphur of the fuel actually burned, adjusted at every fuel change
- A minimum feed rate always respected for film maintenance
- Load-change dependent dosage during manoeuvring, load changes and running in
- Higher base number oils where the service is corrosion prone - more reserve per unit of oil
- Since the 0.50 per cent limit of 1 January 2020, BN is chosen against fuel sulphur AND engine duty together, decided by scrape-down analysis rather than a table

**7. The other failure mode - too much treatment**

- Unconsumed calcium detergent forms hard ash deposits on crown, top land and ring grooves
- Those deposits are abrasive and polish the honed surface, destroying oil retention
- Ring sticking and blow-by follow, raising liner temperature unevenly
- And the oil is wasted, at real cost
- Under-treatment corrodes, over-treatment polishes - both end as high wear, which is why wear rate alone cannot diagnose

**8. Monitoring - what closes the loop**

- Scrape-down analysis: iron AND residual base number, trended together - the key diagnostic
- Iron high, BN near zero - corrosion, under-dosed. Iron high, BN high - not corrosion, and more oil will worsen it
- Liner wall temperature monitoring where fitted
- Scavenge port inspections - surface condition, film, ring condition, crown and top land deposits
- Periodic wear measurement at fixed reference positions, trended not absolute
- Feed rate records read against fuel sulphur, load history and analysis results
- Every actual number comes from the builder's service letters and the oil supplier for that installation - carry none between ships
