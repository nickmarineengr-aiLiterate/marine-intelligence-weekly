## 9. ANSWER ROUTE AS BUILT

Archetype `explain`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route - `validate_spec.py` enforces the correspondence, so the candidate cannot be taught one order and shown another.

**1. What the Convention is, and which version applies here**

- An ILO instrument, not IMO - International Labour Conference 2006, in force 20 August 2013; the 'fourth pillar'
- Articles; Regulations; Code Part A Standards (mandatory); Code Part B Guidelines (not mandatory)
- Five Titles: 1 minimum requirements, 2 conditions of employment, 3 accommodation and food, 4 health and welfare, 5 compliance and enforcement
- Amended by the Special Tripartite Committee under Article XIII - not by IMO tacit acceptance
- At this sitting the Convention stands as amended through the 2018 set; the 2022 amendments come into force 23 December 2024

**2. Limb (a) - Title 1: minimum requirements for seafarers to work on a ship** - limb **(a)**

- Reg 1.1 minimum age - 16; night work under 18 restricted; hazardous work under 18 prohibited
- Reg 1.2 medical certificate - max 2 years, 1 year under 18, colour vision 6 years; STCW certificate accepted; expiry at sea runs to the next port, up to 3 months
- Reg 1.3 training and qualifications - STCW certification, plus completed training in personal safety on board
- Reg 1.4 recruitment and placement - no fees charged to the seafarer except the statutory medical, seafarer's book and passport; private services licensed and regulated, keeping a register, verifying agreements, and operating a system of protection to compensate monetary loss
- Emphasise the no-fees rule - it is the Convention's answer to debt bondage and is routinely evaded

**3. Limb (b) - Title 2: conditions of employment** - limb **(b)**

- Reg 2.1 the Seafarers' Employment Agreement - written, signed by seafarer and shipowner, opportunity to examine and seek advice, original held by each, copy and English summary available on board for inspection; any collective agreement on board too
- Reg 2.2 wages - at intervals no greater than monthly, monthly account, right of allotment to family
- Reg 2.3 hours - EITHER max work 14 in 24 / 72 in 7 OR min rest 10 in 24 / 77 in 7; rest in no more than two periods, one of at least 6 hours, interval not exceeding 14 hours
- Reg 2.4 leave - minimum 2.5 calendar days per month; forgoing it is prohibited save as the competent authority provides
- Reg 2.5 repatriation - at no cost to the seafarer; maximum service before repatriation less than 12 months
- Reg 2.6 compensation for the ship's loss; 2.7 manning levels; 2.8 career and skill development
- Name Titles 3 and 4 in one sentence to show the whole Convention has been read

**4. Limb (c) - Title 5: flag State responsibilities** - limb **(c)**

- 5.1.1 an effective system of inspection and certification; 5.1.2 Recognized Organizations may be authorised
- 5.1.3 the Maritime Labour Certificate and DMLC at 500 GT on international voyages, 5 years with an intermediate inspection between the second and third anniversary
- DMLC Part I is the flag State's; Part II is the SHIPOWNER'S - the classic trap
- 5.1.4 inspection and enforcement, including the power to prohibit departure
- 5.1.5 on-board complaint procedures with safeguards against victimisation; 5.1.6 casualty inquiry

**5. Limb (c) - port State and labour-supplying responsibilities** - limb **(c)**

- 5.2.1 the certificate and DMLC are prima facie evidence, so the ordinary inspection checks documents
- Clear grounds - documents not produced or invalid, grounds to believe conditions do not conform, or a complaint received - open a more detailed inspection
- Deficiencies must be rectified; serious, repeated or complaint-based deficiencies may bring detention
- 5.2.2 onshore seafarer complaint-handling, seeking resolution at ship level first and reporting to the flag State
- 5.3 labour-supplying - responsibility for recruitment services in the territory and social security for resident seafarers; this is what makes the Reg 1.4 no-fees rule enforceable at source

**6. Limb (c) - the two provisions that make the system work** - limb **(c)**

- Article V(7) no more favourable treatment - a non-ratifying flag gets no advantage, so a ship cannot escape the Convention by choosing a flag
- Article VI substantial equivalence - Part A may be implemented by substantially equivalent measures; what made near-universal ratification possible, and the seam through which divergence enters
- Close honestly: enforcement is strong on documents and weaker on conditions, which is why the complaint procedures matter most in practice

**7. Limb (d) - abandonment, and when it arises** - limb **(d)**

- Regulation 2.5 and Standard A2.5.2, introduced by the 2014 amendments - IN FORCE at this sitting
- Before them the right to repatriation existed with no guarantee that anyone solvent would pay
- Three limbs: fails to cover repatriation; leaves the seafarer without necessary maintenance and support; or otherwise unilaterally severs ties, including failure to pay contractual wages for at least two months
- Subtlety one: the shipowner need not have disappeared
- Subtlety two: two months of unpaid wages is sufficient on its own

**8. Limb (d) - the financial security system** - limb **(d)**

- Financial security, usually from the P&I club, carried by every ship to which the Convention applies
- A certificate posted in a conspicuous place on board with the provider's contact details
- Direct access by the seafarer or a nominated representative - the claim does not go through the shipowner, who is by definition the party who failed
- Covers outstanding wages to a defined limit, repatriation, and essential needs - food, clothing, accommodation, drinking water, essential fuel, medical care
- Provided promptly; the security may not be cancelled without prior notice to the flag State

**9. Limb (d) - why the numbers rise, and what the institutions do** - limb **(d)**

- Causes: single-ship companies and opaque ownership; ageing tonnage in marginal trades; arrest and detention stopping earnings while costs continue; sanctions and trade disruption; weak registries and unlicensed agents; seafarers' weak bargaining position; and better reporting inflating the trend
- ILO owns the Convention and its amendment machinery, and co-hosts the abandonment database
- IMO co-hosts the database and supplies the port State control machinery that detects abandonment
- ITF - not a regulator but the most effective practical actor: its inspectorate boards ships, pursues wage claims and funds food and repatriation
- Flag State notified before security is cancelled and ultimately responsible; port State detects, detains and handles the onshore complaint
- The security provider pays
- Close: the 2014 amendments did not create a right - they created a payer
