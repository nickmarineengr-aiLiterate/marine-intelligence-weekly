## 9. ANSWER ROUTE AS BUILT

Archetype `explain`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route - `validate_spec.py` enforces the correspondence, so the candidate cannot be taught one order and shown another.

**1. Why the oil is analysed, and what the four techniques are for**

- Used oil is the only sample in continuous contact with every working surface
- The four techniques are not four ways of doing one thing
- Is the machine wearing? - spectrometry and particle count
- Is the oil still fit? - FTIR, and BN and AN
- Is anything getting in? - all four, from different directions
- The governing rule: trend against the machine's own history, not the absolute value

**2. A) Elemental (spectrometric) analysis** - limb **A**

- Sample excited in a plasma or across a rotating-disc electrode arc
- Each element emits at characteristic wavelengths; intensity gives concentration in ppm
- Wear metals - iron from liners, rings, gears, shafts; copper, lead, tin from bearings; chromium from rings and plating; aluminium from pistons
- Contaminants - silicon from dirt (abrasive); sodium and boron from coolant; vanadium and nickel from residual fuel
- Additive elements - calcium and magnesium (detergent), zinc and phosphorus (anti-wear)
- Additives falling = depletion; rising = the wrong oil was added
- Silicon reads two ways - dirt ingress in service, or sealant and antifoam residue right after an overhaul

**3. What spectrometry cannot see - and why that governs its use** - limb **A**

- The method needs complete atomisation of the particle in the excitation source
- Large particles are not fully atomised, so response falls away with increasing size
- It therefore reports essentially the fine fraction only
- Normal rubbing wear is fine and is seen well
- Bearing fatigue, spalling, scuffing and gear pitting are coarse and may not be seen
- So a clean spectrometric report is NOT evidence of a healthy machine
- Which is exactly why particle count, ferrography and plug inspection sit alongside it

**4. B) Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy** - limb **B**

- Infrared through a thin film; molecular bonds absorb at characteristic wavenumbers
- All wavelengths gathered at once as an interferogram, Fourier transformed to a spectrum - hence fast
- A DIFFERENTIAL measurement - subtracted from the spectrum of the same oil unused
- Practical consequence: keep a retained sample of every new oil delivery
- Detects oxidation (carbonyl), nitration, sulphation, water (hydroxyl)
- Soot as a broad baseline rise, not a discrete peak
- Also fuel dilution, glycol ingress, additive depletion
- Fast, small sample, no reagents - but semi-quantitative and blind to wear metals and particles

**5. C) Particle count** - limb **C**

- Solid particles counted and sized, usually by optical light obscuration
- Pore blockage where the oil is too dark; microscopy is the referee method
- Reported as counts per millilitre in cumulative size bands, condensed to a cleanliness code
- Decisive for hydraulic and servo systems where clearances are microns
- The direct measure of filtration and centrifuge performance and of ingress
- It sees the large particles spectrometry misses - the two are complementary
- Limits: counts water droplets and air bubbles as particles; cannot say what the particles are

**6. D) Base Number against Acid Number** - limb **D**

- Both titrations, both in mg KOH per gramme - which is why they are confused
- BN = alkaline reserve from overbased detergent additives; it FALLS in service
- AN = acidic constituents from combustion and oxidation; it RISES
- An oil can show both at once - separate titrations of separate species
- Two standard BN methods give systematically different values - never trend them together
- BN governs for cylinder and trunk-piston oils, matched to the sulphur actually burned
- AN governs for turbine, hydraulic, gear and stern tube oils, which have no reserve
- Since the 0.50 per cent limit of 1 January 2020, BN is matched to the fuel in use - and over-alkalinity deposits abrasive calcium

**7. Correct interpretation - reading the four together**

- No single result diagnoses anything; pairs and trends do
- Iron up + residual BN near zero = corrosive wear (in a cylinder, cold corrosion)
- Iron up + BN healthy = NOT corrosion; more oil will make it worse
- Particle count up + wear metals normal = filtration or centrifuge failure, or ingress
- Particle count up + spectrometry clean = coarse debris - the most dangerous case
- Silicon up + iron and chromium up = abrasive dirt ingress
- Sodium and boron up + FTIR glycol and water = coolant leak
- Vanadium and nickel + FTIR fuel + viscosity down = fuel dilution
- Oxidation up + AN up + no wear metals = the oil is degrading, the machine is fine
- Additive elements up = the wrong oil was added
- Two disciplines: trend do not spot-read; and rate of change beats level

**8. Corrective measures**

- The sequence: verify - confirm physically - correct the cause - then correct the oil
- Re-sample before acting - many alarming results are sampling artefacts
- Confirm physically - magnetic plugs, filters cut open, centrifuge sludge, ferrography for debris shape, direct inspection
- Abrasive ingress - find the entry path (filters, seals, breathers), not just more filtration
- Coolant leak - pressure-test coolers, jackets, covers; glycol destroys bearings
- Fuel dilution - injection equipment, timing, combustion; then renew, because diluted oil cannot be recovered
- Water - find the source and run the purifier correctly (gravity disc, throughput, temperature)
- Rising particle count - filters, differential pressure, bypass, purifier operation; then the ingress path
- Low BN in cylinder oil - raise feed rate or move to higher BN, matched to fuel sulphur, and re-check by scrape-down
- High AN and oxidation - find the cause, then renew, and clean the system before recharging
- Additive depletion - renew; additives cannot be topped up
- Record and close the loop in the PMS - unrecorded action destroys the trend
- Standing caution: every limit comes from the builder and the oil supplier for THAT installation

**9. Sampling and trending - without which none of it means anything**

- Same point, same way, same interval; machine at temperature and oil circulating
- From a live line upstream of the filter, not the drain or tank bottom
- Flush the point; use clean dedicated bottles - a dirty bottle condemns a healthy machine
- Retain a sample of every new oil - FTIR needs the reference
- Label fully: ship, machine, point, hours since change and since overhaul, grade, top-ups
- Trend, and read the rate of change
- Plan it in the PMS under the ISM maintenance regime
- Close: analysis does not maintain machinery - it tells you where to look
