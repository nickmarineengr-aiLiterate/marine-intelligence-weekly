## 2. WHAT THE QUESTION ASKS

Two tasks of equal weight. Limb (a) is an enumeration marked on coverage and on correct attribution to the issuing convention - the stem asks twice for the convention reference, so an unattributed list cannot score. Limb (b) is itself two questions: define harmonization under the 1988 Protocol, and then apply regulation I/14 to a certificate that has just expired in a port with no survey capability. The scenario is the discriminator; it separates candidates who know the certification system from candidates who have memorised a certificate list.

**Mark allocation.** 16, set as 8 + 8. Printed marks are unambiguous. Within limb (b) the split is roughly even between the definition of harmonization and the expired-certificate procedure, because the stem gives each its own sentence.

**Examiner expectation.** A grouped, convention-attributed certificate list with the tanker-specific items visible - Form B on the IOPP supplement, the Enhanced Survey Programme, the CLC insurance certificate - and validity periods that show the exceptions rather than reciting five years throughout. Then the four elements of the harmonized system, and a procedure for the expired certificate that reaches the Administration for an extension under regulation I/14, names the three-month ceiling, and does not sail before it is granted.

**Required answer components:**

- The statutory certificates an oil tanker actually carries, grouped by the instrument that requires them
- The validity period of each, and the survey that sustains it
- The tanker-specific certificates a general cargo ship does not carry
- What harmonization under the 1988 SOLAS Protocol actually did - one anniversary date, one five-year cycle, one survey window
- The survey regime the harmonized system runs on, and the three-month window either side of the anniversary date
- The specific scenario: a certificate that has JUST expired, in a port with inadequate survey facilities
- The extension power in SOLAS regulation I/14, its three-month ceiling, and the conditions attached to it

## 9. ANSWER ROUTE AS BUILT

Archetype `legal`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route - `validate_spec.py` enforces the correspondence, so the candidate cannot be taught one order and shown another.

**1. What an oil tanker carries, and under which convention** - limb **(a)**

- Four families - SOLAS, MARPOL, other conventions, insurance - not one undifferentiated list
- The stem asks twice for the convention reference, so attribute every certificate
- State the assumption: oil tanker on international voyages

**2. SOLAS 1974/1988 certificates** - limb **(a)**

- Cargo Ship Safety Construction, Safety Equipment with Record of Equipment Form E, Safety Radio - or the combined Cargo Ship Safety Certificate, Form C
- Safety Management Certificate and the Company's Document of Compliance - chapter IX and the ISM Code
- International Ship Security Certificate - chapter XI-2 and the ISPS Code
- Continuous Synopsis Record XI-1/5 and Minimum Safe Manning Document V/14 - neither expires
- Enhanced Survey Programme under XI-1/2 and the ESP Code - a survey regime, not a certificate, but tanker-specific
- Five years for all the certificates in this group

**3. MARPOL certificates - where the tanker differs most** - limb **(a)**

- IOPP certificate with the Form B supplement - Form B is the oil-tanker form, Form A is not
- NLS certificate or IBC Certificate of Fitness where so certified - Annex II
- ISPP - Annex IV; IAPP with supplement and EIAPP with the NOx Technical File - Annex VI
- International Energy Efficiency Certificate - Annex VI regulation 24, valid for the ship's life
- DCS Statement of Compliance - issued annually
- Annex V garbage attracts no certificate - say so, because candidates invent one

**4. Other conventions** - limb **(a)**

- International Load Line Certificate - LL 1966/88, five years
- International Tonnage Certificate 1969 - no expiry
- Maritime Labour Certificate and DMLC Parts I and II - MLC 2006, 500 GT and above, five years with an intermediate inspection
- AFS certificate - life of the system; BWM certificate - five years
- CLC 1992 insurance certificate above 2,000 tonnes of persistent oil in bulk; Bunkers certificate above 1,000 GT - validity is the insurance period
- Ship Sanitation Control (Exemption) Certificate - IHR 2005, six months, not IMO
- Certificate of Class is NOT statutory - but the same society is usually the Recognized Organization

**5. Harmonization under SOLAS 74/88 - what it actually changed** - limb **(b)**

- The 1988 Protocols to SOLAS and Load Lines, in force 3 February 2000; MARPOL amended to match
- One anniversary date for the ship, governing every harmonized certificate
- A common five-year maximum validity, so they expire together
- A common survey pattern - initial, annual, intermediate, renewal, additional - with a three-month window either side of the anniversary date
- The intermediate survey REPLACES the second or third annual survey; it is not additional
- Flexibility on issue and extension, so operational difficulty does not force uncertificated trading
- The purpose is economic as well as regulatory - one attendance, less off-hire, no drift between certificates

**6. A certificate that has just expired, in a port that cannot survey** - limb **(b)**

- Start with the hard point: an expired statutory certificate is a detainable deficiency
- Survey COMPLETE but certificate not issued or on board - endorsement of the existing certificate, up to five months from expiry
- Certificate expired and the ship NOT in a port where she is to be surveyed - Administration may extend, three months maximum, to complete the voyage to the survey port
- Short-voyage ships otherwise uncovered - a one-month period of grace
- Condition one: on arrival at the survey port she may not leave on the extension - she needs the new certificate first
- Condition two: the new certificate runs five years from the original expiry date, not from the survey

**7. The actions to take, in the order you would take them** - limb **(b)**

- Establish which certificate, the exact expiry, and whether the renewal survey has been completed - that determines which relief applies
- Report to the Company and the DPA as an ISM non-conformity
- Apply through the Recognized Organization to the flag Administration for endorsement, extension or grace - the RO cannot grant the three-month extension itself
- Request an occasional or additional survey where a surveyor can attend, or arrange attendance at the next port
- Notify the port State rather than let a PSCO find it
- Notify P&I, hull underwriters and charterers - cover may be prejudiced and the ship will usually be off-hire
- Test and record the equipment the certificate covers, to make the application credible
- Do not sail until the extension is in hand, and then only to the nominated survey port
