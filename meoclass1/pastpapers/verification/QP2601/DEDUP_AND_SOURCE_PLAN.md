## 3. The two reuse decisions that mattered

**Q3 is the first Tier D in the series, and it is a real test of "write once, relate, appear where
relevant".** QP2607 Q5 and QP2601 Q3 both examine General Average, but:

- **Removed:** the entire Particular Average limb — half the July answer, and not asked in January.
- **Added:** principles of modern salvage law (a printed 4-mark limb with no July counterpart);
  *Artificial* general average; *Contestation*. None exist in July.
- **Retained and re-expressed:** Rule A, the elements of a general average act, MIA 1963 s.66, the
  adjuster's role, and the contractual nature of the Rules.

Only *Adjustment* maps onto a July section, and it was rewritten to answer a narrower prompt. **The
July prose was not copied.** What was reused is the verified proposition set — which is the intended
behaviour, and the reason the delta is recorded in the spec rather than left implicit.

**Q7 is the inverse case, and is more instructive.** The QP2607 Q7 verification — assent 18 August
2025, whole Act in force 15 March 2026 by S.O. 1244(E), 1958 Act repealed by s.324(1) — is reused in
full. But because QP2601 was sat in **January 2026**, those same verified facts establish that the
2025 Act had **not** commenced, so the correct answer is the **1958 Act**. Identical evidence,
opposite conclusion, four months apart. Reusing the July *answer* would have produced a wrong one;
reusing the July *verification* produced the right one.
