## 2. What was verified, and how

MSC.1/Circ.1598 was downloaded from IMO and read in full. Circular number, date (24 January 2019), approving session (MSC 100, 3-7 December 2018), the finalising HTW 5 session and the superseded MSC/Circ.1014 are all from the circular text. The **six module titles were extracted from the document**, not recalled: 1 Fatigue; 2 Fatigue and the Company; 3 Fatigue and the Seafarer; 4 Fatigue Awareness and Training; 5 Fatigue and Ship Design; 6 Fatigue, the Administration and Port State Authorities. The sleep-debt definition, the caffeine passage, the 'rest means rest' passage and the reference to MLC Regulations 2.3, 2.4 and 2.7 were all read in the text.

**A wrong-edition error was caught, and is recorded so it is not repeated.** A freely available IMO STCW.6/Circ.1 copy of section A-VIII/1 was consulted first for the rest hours. It gives a **five-paragraph pre-Manila text**: 10 hours in any 24, no more than two periods one of at least 6 hours, and **'not less than 70 hours of rest ... each seven day period'** - with **no 14-hour interval provision at all**. That is the 1995 Code. A search summary had simultaneously asserted the 77-hour figure while citing that same document. The Manila-amended figures were therefore taken from a competent-authority notice (UK MCA MGN 566) which reproduces the amended regime and confirms entry into force on 1 January 2012: **77 hours** normal weekly rest, **14-hour** maximum interval, and an exception floor of **70 hours** for no more than two consecutive weeks. Both 70 and 77 are real figures doing different jobs, which is exactly why the wrong-edition error is dangerous here.

**Gap:** MIW holds no licensed STCW Convention and Code, so these figures sit at P2. Recorded as a class C accepted limitation and as a corpus need. The STCW comprehensive review was confirmed to be in progress with completion targeted around 2027 - nothing adopted at the question date or at August 2026.

## 3. Sources

- PRIMARY: MSC.1/Circ.1598, 'Guidelines on fatigue', 24 January 2019 - full text read. Approved at MSC 100 (3 to 7 December 2018), finalized by the HTW Sub-Committee at its fifth session (16 to 20 July 2018), superseding MSC/Circ.1014 on Guidance on fatigue mitigation and management (approved at MSC 74, 2001). Six modules, verified from the circular: 1 Fatigue; 2 Fatigue and the Company; 3 Fatigue and the Seafarer; 4 Fatigue Awareness and Training; 5 Fatigue and Ship Design; 6 Fatigue, the Administration and Port State Authorities.
- PRIMARY: MSC.1/Circ.1598 - definition of sleep debt as 'insufficient accumulated sleep over multiple consecutive 24-hour periods'; the causes of fatigue including environmental, health, sleep-disorder and operational factors; the statement that hours of rest are controlled by the prescriptive formula in STCW chapter VIII; and the reference to MLC 2006 Regulations 2.3, 2.4 and 2.7.
- PRIMARY: STCW regulation VIII/1 as quoted in MSC.1/Circ.1598 - each Administration shall establish and enforce rest periods for watchkeeping personnel and those with designated safety, security and pollution-prevention duties in accordance with section A-VIII/1, and require watch systems arranged so that efficiency is not impaired by fatigue.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: UK Maritime and Coastguard Agency MGN 566(M+F), which reproduces the Manila-amended A-VIII/1 regime - the normal weekly rest period of 77 hours, the 10 hours in any 24-hour period, division into no more than two periods one of which is at least 6 hours, intervals between consecutive rest periods not exceeding 14 hours, and the conditions on authorised exceptions (not below 70 hours in any 7-day period, not more than two consecutive weeks, and daily rest divisible into no more than three periods). It confirms the Manila amendments to regulation VIII/1 and section A-VIII/1 came into force on 1 January 2012.
- VERIFIED CORRECTION: an IMO STCW.6/Circ.1 copy of section A-VIII/1 was consulted first and gives a five-paragraph pre-Manila text with '70 hours of rest ... each seven day period' and no 14-hour interval provision. That is the 1995 Code, not the current one. The Manila figures were taken from the competent-authority notice above instead. Recorded so the error is not repeated.
