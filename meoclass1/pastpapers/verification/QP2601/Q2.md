## 1. Question as printed

> Define communication. Enumerate and discuss the different types of communications. Discuss the various barriers to effective communication. You are Carrying out a Main engine decarbonisation and the work is carried out in two groups, one working on the cylinder head platform and the other group working in the bottom platform. Identify the areas in this operation where there can be a hazard due to failure of communication. Also suggest risk mitigation measures to mitigate the hazard of failure of communication.

## 2. What was verified, and how

No external verification was required for the communication theory. The regulatory hooks (ISM elements 6, 7 and 8; SOLAS V/14.3; STCW A-VIII/2) are long-standing and are stated at instrument level.

The material finding here was **a reading of the question, not of a source**. 'Main engine decarbonisation' in this scenario means the carbon-removal overhaul, not GHG decarbonisation - the two working platforms in the printed wording settle it. Q1 of the same paper uses the other sense of the word, so the paper itself creates the ambiguity. Recorded as an examiner trap in the study guide, because misreading it costs the entire scenario limb.

## 3. Sources

- PRIMARY: ISM Code, element 6 (Resources and personnel), including the requirement for effective communication in the working language, and elements 7 (shipboard operations) and 8 (emergency preparedness).
- PRIMARY: SOLAS chapter V regulation 14.3 - a working language shall be established and recorded in the log book.
- PRIMARY: STCW Code section A-VIII/2 (watchkeeping arrangements) and the leadership and teamworking competences in the management-level tables.
- P3 INDUSTRY GUIDANCE: Code of Safe Working Practices for Merchant Seafarers - permit to work, enclosed space entry and lock-out/tag-out practice.
- ENGINEERING JUDGEMENT: identification of the specific hazards of simultaneous work on the cylinder head platform and the bottom platform is standard engine-room risk assessment reasoning.
