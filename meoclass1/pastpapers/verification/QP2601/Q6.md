## 1. Question as printed

> Control of disease vectors is necessary for maintenance of health on board ships. What is the Role of the WHO organisation in this regard and which are the national agencies tasked with the maintenance of the ship and the crew in good health? What are the different certificates issued to a vessel for maintenance of the ship in good health? Who can issue these certificates in India and what is the validity and extension options for such certificates?

## 2. What was verified, and how

**The most thoroughly verified question in the paper.** The consolidated International Health Regulations (2005) as amended in 2014, 2022 and 2024 were downloaded from WHO and read for Articles 20, 22, 24, 27, 37 and 39 and Annexes 3, 5 and 8. Article 39 is taken from the text: maximum six months, extendable by one month where the inspection or control measures cannot be accomplished at the port. Annex 5's 400-metre vector-control programme distance was read.

**Cross-edition finding.** The original 2005 IHR text was downloaded separately and compared. Article 37 in the original is titled **'Maritime Declaration of Health'** - 12 occurrences of that term, 0 of the newer name. In the consolidated amended text it is **'Ship Declaration of Health'** - 6 occurrences, 0 of the old. The renaming is therefore established by direct comparison of two primary texts, not inferred. The 2024 amendments entered into force on 19 September 2025, which is **before** this sitting, so the current name is correct for the question date. Nearly all textbooks and coaching notes still use the old name.

**Gap:** the Indian administrative detail - Port Health Organisation under DGHS, Indian Port Health Rules 1955, and the DG Shipping Order 10 of 2018 Statement of Compliance route - rests on Government and classification-society restatements, not on the rule and order texts. Recorded at P2. The list of authorised Indian ports is deliberately not reproduced, because it changes.
