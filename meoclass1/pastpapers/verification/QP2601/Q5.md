## 2. What was verified, and how

The Performance Standard for Protective Coatings, resolution MSC.215(82), was downloaded from IMO and read. **Section 3.4 was read in full**, and the Coating Technical File contents in the answer follow 3.4.2 item by item, with 3.4.3 (in-service records), 3.4.4 (re-coating) and 3.4.5 (kept on board for the life of the ship) also read. Section 4.1 gives the 15-year target useful life and, importantly, **defines** it as the period over which the system is intended to remain in GOOD condition. Section 2.6 defines GOOD condition as minor spot rusting by cross-reference to resolution A.744(18); 2.8 gives the 90/10 practice.

**Gap:** MIW holds no licensed ESP Code. The PSPC itself defines only GOOD. The FAIR and POOR wording, and the survey consequences of a sub-GOOD rating, come from classification-society and industry restatements which agree with one another, and are recorded at P2 rather than P1. Acquiring a licensed ESP Code would promote this, and is logged as a corpus need.

## 5. Open points carried in the answer

- MIW holds no licensed copy of the ESP Code. The GOOD / FAIR / POOR wording is taken from consistent classification-society and industry restatements and is recorded as authoritative secondary, not primary.
- The often-quoted rule that corrosion rate roughly doubles for each 10 degrees Celsius rise is a general kinetic approximation, not a coating or class standard. It is stated as an approximation and qualified by the oxygen-solubility effect.
