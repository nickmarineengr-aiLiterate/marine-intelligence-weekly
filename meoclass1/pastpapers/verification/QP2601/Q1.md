## 1. Question as printed

> Reducing the speed of main propulsion engines is considered to be the simplest and most practical short-term solution for lowering GHG emissions from ships. What are the drawbacks and dangers of operating main propulsion two stroke marine engines at low speeds for extended periods of time? How can these issues be resolved and the risks associated with low speed operation reduced?

## 2. What was verified, and how

The regulatory framing was the only part needing external verification, and it mattered: the status of the IMO Net-Zero Framework at the question date had to be established rather than assumed. IMO's own meeting record shows that the extraordinary MEPC session convened 14-17 October 2025 to adopt it was **adjourned** for twelve months. So at January 2026 - and still at August 2026 - the Framework was approved but **not adopted and not in force**. EEXI (reg 25) and CII (reg 28) were in force from 1 January 2023 and are stated as such.

The engine behaviour is standard marine-engineering reasoning supported by publicly available engine-designer guidance. No single designer's load thresholds or cylinder-oil figures are quoted as universal; the answer gives approximate bands and says they are approximate.

## 3. Sources

- PRIMARY: MARPOL Annex VI regulation 25 (EEXI) and regulation 28 (operational CII), in force since 1 January 2023, with SEEMP Part III under regulation 26.
- PRIMARY: IMO media briefing and MEPC/ES.2 meeting summary - the extraordinary session of MEPC convened 14 to 17 October 2025 to adopt the IMO Net-Zero Framework was ADJOURNED and will reconvene in twelve months. The Framework is approved (MEPC 83, April 2025) but NOT adopted.
- P3 INDUSTRY GUIDANCE: engine-designer service guidance on part-load and low-load operation of electronically controlled two-stroke engines (low-load tuning, turbocharger cut-out, cylinder oil feed-rate and BN matching). Used for engineering practice, not for legal claims.
- ENGINEERING JUDGEMENT: the causal chains linking reduced scavenge pressure to incomplete combustion, fouling and cold corrosion are standard marine-engineering reasoning and are presented as such.

## 5. Open points carried in the answer

- Specific numerical thresholds for 'low load' vary by engine designer, engine type and tuning. The answer uses approximate bands and says so rather than quoting a single manufacturer's figure as though it were universal.
- Whether a particular ship may use engine power limitation depends on its EEXI technical file and class approval. The answer states the mechanism, not an entitlement.

## 6. Re-verification register

### `B_CURRENCY_CHECK` - The IMO Net-Zero Framework is approved but not adopted, following adjournment of the October 2025 extraordinary MEPC session

The adjourned session was due to reconvene approximately twelve months later, i.e. around October 2026. If it has since adopted the Framework, the currency section of the study guide must be updated - although the January 2026 statement itself remains correct for the question date.

### `C_ACCEPTED_LIMITATION` - Low-load engine behaviour and remedies are stated generically rather than to one engine designer's figures

MIW holds no licensed engine-designer service letter set. The engineering is standard principle plus publicly available guidance, and load bands and cylinder oil recommendations are deliberately given as approximate. Ships must follow their own maker documentation.
