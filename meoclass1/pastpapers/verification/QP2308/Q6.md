## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: **explain**. The principal headings of the model answer ARE these steps.

1. **The power chain, as one idea**
   - Each named power is the previous one less the loss at one interface; the ratio between two is an efficiency
   - Indicated - Brake - Shaft - Delivered - Thrust - Effective
   - Effective power is not delivered to anything: it is the power needed to TOW the ship at that speed
   - Only indicated, brake and shaft power are directly measurable on a ship
   - The first losses are mechanical friction; the last two are hydrodynamic and far the largest
2. **Indicated power - what the engine develops in the cylinder**
   - Power developed by the gas on the piston, before any friction
   - From the indicator diagram - mean indicated pressure, piston area, stroke, working strokes per unit time, all cylinders
   - The only power attributable to ONE cylinder, so it is the basis of load balancing and combustion diagnosis
   - The loss begins at rings, gudgeon pin, crosshead, bearings, valve gear and engine-driven pumps
3. **Brake power, and mechanical efficiency**
   - Power available at the output coupling or flywheel, after engine friction and engine-driven auxiliaries
   - Mechanical efficiency = brake / indicated, typically 0.85 to 0.92
   - Friction power is nearly constant with load, so mechanical efficiency FALLS sharply at low load
   - Measured on a test bed against a dynamometer; inferred rather than measured on board
4. **Shaft power and delivered power - the transmission**
   - Shaft power is measured by torsionmeter from torque and revolutions, normally on the intermediate shaft
   - Delivered power is what actually reaches the propeller, after stern tube, plummer blocks, thrust block and any gearing
   - Transmission efficiency typically 0.97 to 0.99 direct coupled, lower with a gearbox
   - The difference depends on WHERE the torsionmeter is, not only on the ship
   - Without a torsionmeter, shaft power is inferred from brake power and an assumed efficiency - state the assumption
5. **Thrust power and effective power - the propeller and the hull**
   - Thrust power = thrust x speed of advance, and speed of advance is NOT ship speed
   - The propeller works in the hull's wake, described by the wake fraction
   - Effective power = total resistance x ship speed - the power to tow the hull
   - Thrust deduction: the propeller lowers pressure over the after body and increases the hull's resistance
   - Hull efficiency = effective / thrust = (1 - t) / (1 - w), commonly GREATER than 1 on a single-screw ship
6. **The efficiencies that join the stages**
   - Mechanical = brake / indicated, 0.85 to 0.92
   - Transmission = delivered / brake, 0.97 to 0.99 direct coupled
   - Propeller open-water efficiency 0.60 to 0.70 - by far the largest single loss
   - Relative rotative efficiency, close to unity, 0.95 to 1.05
   - Hull efficiency, often above 1
   - Quasi-propulsive coefficient = effective / delivered, 0.65 to 0.75; propulsive coefficient = effective / brake, 0.60 to 0.72
   - Roughly two thirds of cylinder power reaches the water, and most of the loss is at the propeller and hull
7. **Sea trials - what a navigation trial is for, and the conditions it must be run in**
   - To demonstrate the ship handles as designed, satisfy the manoeuvring standard, and produce the wheelhouse poster and manoeuvring booklet
   - IMO Standards for Ship Manoeuvrability, MSC.137(76), adopted 4 December 2002
   - Applies to ships of 100 m and over and all chemical tankers and gas carriers, constructed on or after 1 January 2004
   - Condition one - deep, unrestricted water
   - Condition two - calm environment, negligible current, runs on reciprocal headings
   - Condition three - full load even keel, or trial in ballast with a recorded correction to the loaded condition
   - Condition four - steady approach and steady conditions through the measured run
8. **The essential manoeuvres, and the objective of each**
   - Speed and power trial - the power/revolutions/speed relationship, contract speed, and the design index measurement; also the datum for the ship's whole service life
   - Turning circle both ways - advance, transfer, tactical diameter, drift angle and speed loss; advance not more than 4.5 L, tactical diameter not more than 5 L
   - Zig-zag 10/10 and 20/20 - yaw checking and course keeping; first and second overshoot angles, limits depending on the length-to-speed ratio
   - Initial turning - promptness of response; not more than 2.5 L travelled for a 10 degree heading change
   - Stopping test - track reach, head reach and time; not more than 15 L, and heading is not controllable
   - Spiral, direct and reverse - directional stability; a hysteresis loop measures the instability, and the reverse spiral works where the direct one cannot
   - Pull-out - a quick check of the same property by the residual rate of turn
   - Astern and low-speed trials, steering gear changeover and emergency steering, bow thruster; and man-overboard where programmed
   - Results become the wheelhouse poster, pilot card and manoeuvring booklet; criteria are in ship lengths so all sizes are judged alike
