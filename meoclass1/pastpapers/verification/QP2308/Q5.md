## 2. DECOMPOSITION GATE

**Question intent.** Two tasks separated by a printed full stop, and the examiner is testing the BOUNDARY of delegated authority rather than knowledge of the RO Code. The first task is analytical and asks for reasons, not a list; the second is a register. The discriminator is the first: the corpus shows this examiner's colleagues asking repeatedly what an RO is and how it is monitored, and a candidate with a prepared RO Code answer will deliver it and score badly, because limitations are the one aspect that prepared answer does not contain. The second discriminator is inside the register - whether the candidate knows what is NOT delegated, and whether he keeps class work out of a statutory list.

**Mark allocation.** Sixteen marks with no printed split. The stem sets two tasks of comparable weight - 'state the limitations highlighting them with reasons' and 'list the statutory services' - and the phrase 'with reasons' roughly doubles the work in the first. The answer allots one framing section, six sections to the limitations, and one substantial section carrying both the register and the non-delegable boundary. That gives the limitations more space than the services, which is deliberate: a list can be written quickly and a reasoned limitation cannot.

**Examiner expectation.** That class and statutory work are separated before anything else. That each limitation carries its reason, because the stem says so. That the responsibility-versus-function proposition appears, and that exemptions and equivalents are correctly placed with the Administration. That the RO's lack of enforcement power is stated together with its reporting duty. That the services register is organised by instrument and names real certificates. And that the answer knows what is not delegable - which is the part that shows the boundary is understood rather than the list memorised.

**Freshness risk.** MEDIUM - The RO Code, the enabling provisions and the certificate regime are all stable and need no reversal. The moving parts are two 33rd Assembly instruments: A.1187(33) and A.1188(33) were both adopted 6 December 2023, four months after this sitting, and each revoked the instrument that is operative here. They are small edits with a large consequence, because they are exactly the numbers a reviewer moving between papers would 'correct' forward. Two further currency points were checked and cleared: the MLC 2022 amendments are not in force at this sitting, and the Hong Kong Convention is not in force, so no recycling certificate enters the table.

**Jurisdiction risk.** HIGH, and it is the real subject of the question. Three levels must be kept apart: the RO Code, an IMO instrument binding through the conventions that refer to it; the authorization, which is an act of the flag State's national law and differs from flag to flag; and classification, which is contractual and not statutory at all. An answer that treats class rules or an IACS resolution as part of the statutory chain, or that presents a common delegation practice as a legal requirement, has the levels wrong. Where India is concerned the Administration is the Directorate General of Shipping under the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958.

**Technical ambiguities.** The printed stem's first sentence has no main verb for its first clause - 'Classification societies as Recognized Organisations (RO) and play an important role' does not parse - and the intended sense is 'act as Recognized Organisations and play an important role'. The answer answers the intended sense and the printed text is preserved unaltered. 'Limitations' is itself open: it could mean legal limits on authority, practical limits on what a survey can achieve, or the shortcomings of the delegation model as a policy. The answer covers all three rather than choosing, because each is separately markable, and orders them from the constitutional to the practical. 'Statutory services' is read as the services performed on the Administration's behalf, which excludes class work - the exclusion is stated rather than assumed.

## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: **explain**. The principal headings of the model answer ARE these steps.

1. **The boundary the question turns on**
   - Classification is the society's own work, under its own rules, for the owner, by contract
   - Statutory work is the flag State's work, under convention and national law, done on delegation
   - The society wears the second hat only where a flag delegated, only to that extent, and only for that flag's ships
   - The RO Code - MSC.349(92) and MEPC.237(65), mandatory 1 January 2015 via SOLAS XI-1/1
   - Parts 1 and 2 mandatory, Part 3 recommendatory; recognition and authorization are separate acts
2. **Limitation one - the function is delegated, the responsibility is not**
   - The RO surveys and certifies; the Administration remains responsible for the ship's statutory compliance
   - REASON: the convention obligation runs between Contracting Governments, and a treaty duty cannot be discharged by contracting with a company
   - No other Government has any treaty relationship with the society
   - CONSEQUENCE: the flag must run an oversight programme, may inspect its own ships additionally, and is itself audited under the III Code and the Member State Audit Scheme
3. **Limitation two - the authority is specific, never general**
   - Only what the instrument of authorization confers, function by function and convention by convention, in a formal written agreement
   - REASON one - capability: the Code forbids authorizing beyond capability, assessed by elementary module
   - REASON two - transparency: the list of ROs and the conditions of delegated authority are deposited with the Secretary-General so a port State knows what a certificate represents
   - REASON three - controllability: a specified scope can be narrowed as easily as withdrawn
   - Flag by flag; may not survey a ship not entitled to that flag; must cover withdrawal and cancellation; time-limited and revocable
4. **Limitation three - the RO cannot make, waive or dispense with the law**
   - Exemptions and Exemption Certificates are the Administration's
   - Equivalents are the Administration's, and must be communicated to the Organization
   - Interpretation of national law is the flag's; the RO refers and does not decide
   - Class rules are not statutory requirements and cannot discharge a convention obligation
   - REASON: these are sovereign acts altering the bargain struck with other Contracting Governments
   - Delegating survey delegates the finding of FACT; delegating exemption would delegate the LAW
   - In practice: the RO recommends, the Administration decides
5. **Limitation four - independence, and the conflict the scheme is built around**
   - Independence, a code of ethics, and no activity conflicting with independence
   - May not certify as flag State what it designed, built, supplied or maintains
   - Quality system certified by a body accredited independently of the ROs AND their associations
   - REASON: the organization surveying the ship is paid by the owner of it and competes for his business
   - The dual capacity is itself a conflict to be managed - class and flag State in one visit
   - Commercial pressure is a real limit: an owner can change class, which is why flag oversight and port State control matter
6. **Limitation five - the RO has no enforcement power**
   - It cannot detain, prosecute, penalise or compel
   - It may refuse, suspend or withdraw a certificate within its authorization, impose conditions, and report
   - REASON: detention and prosecution are exercises of state power over persons and property, not transferable by contract
   - The counterpart is the reporting duty - withdrawal or suspension of class, unmet conditions, deficiencies
   - Co-operation with port States in rectifying reported deficiencies
   - A failure to report is a compliance failure, not merely poor service
7. **Limitation six - practical and jurisdictional limits**
   - A survey is a sample at a moment in time - compliance AS FOUND, not a guarantee for the certificate's life
   - The RO depends on the drawings submitted, the access given and the records produced; it has no power to compel disclosure
   - Recognition is not authorization, and neither is universal
   - Subcontracting of statutory work is constrained; work is by the RO's own exclusive surveyors under its control
   - Some services are reserved to the Administration and are not delegable at all
   - The RO cannot bind the flag State in its relations with other Governments
8. **The statutory services undertaken on behalf of the Administration**
   - The enabling provisions: SOLAS I/6, Load Lines article 13, MARPOL Annex I regulation 6 and Annex II regulation 8, Tonnage article 6
   - SOLAS - construction, equipment and radio surveys and certificates; DG document; Certificates of Fitness; ISM and ISPS where delegated; stability, cargo securing and ESP approvals
   - Load Lines - freeboard assignment and the International Load Line Certificate
   - Tonnage 1969 - measurement and the International Tonnage Certificate
   - MARPOL - IOPP, NLS, ISPP, IAPP, EIAPP and IEE certificates, and the associated plan and manual approvals
   - AFS, BWM, MLC and Polar certificates; plus type approval, welder qualification, inclining experiment and plan approval
   - NOT delegated: registry, Minimum Safe Manning Document, STCW seafarer certification, exemptions and equivalents, DMLC Part I, CLC and Bunkers financial-security certificates, casualty investigation, detention and prosecution
   - And class survey and condition of class are the society's OWN work, not statutory services at all
   - Which services a flag actually delegates is that flag's choice, recorded in its authorization and deposited with the Secretary-General

**Memory cue.** TWO HATS - RESPONSIBILITY - SPECIFIC - CANNOT MAKE LAW - INDEPENDENCE - NO ENFORCEMENT - PRACTICAL - SERVICES (1 to 8). Inside steps 2 to 7 hold the same shape: LIMIT - REASON - CONSEQUENCE. Inside step 8 run SOLAS - LOAD LINES - TONNAGE - MARPOL - AFS - BWM - MLC - POLAR.
