## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: **explain**. The principal headings of the model answer ARE these steps.

1. **What the Internet of Things is**
   - A physical asset is instrumented, its data leaves the asset, it is analysed, and the analysis causes an action
   - The operative word is ACTION - measurement that changes no decision is cost without benefit
   - On a ship the things are engines, shafting, pumps, tanks, cargo systems and the hull's measured performance
   - Most of the data already exists inside the alarm and monitoring system
2. **The principles - the five layers**
   - Sensing - pressure, temperature, vibration, torque, flow, oil condition, plus existing control-system outputs
   - Connectivity - shipboard network plus a satellite ship-to-shore link carrying a filtered subset
   - Edge processing on board, because the satellite link is the scarce resource
   - Analytics and storage ashore, where a ship is compared with her own past and her sisters
   - Application and action - dashboards, alerts, work orders, regulatory returns
   - Cutting across all five: interoperability, and security by design
3. **Advantages of loT in the maritime industry**
   - Availability rises - defects seen as trends before they reach an alarm setpoint
   - Maintenance cost falls where it should - condition replaces running hours
   - Fuel and emissions managed daily rather than estimated annually
   - Regulatory reporting becomes a by-product of the same measurement chain
   - Shore expertise and class reach the ship without attending
   - Charter performance disputes settled on evidence; fleet-wide learning overnight
4. **Disadvantages, and the ones that are specific to a ship**
   - General: capital and integration cost, sensor drift producing confident nonsense, alert overload, skill gap, de-skilling
   - Ship-specific: bandwidth at sea is limited, intermittent and expensive
   - Ship-specific: cyber risk across an OT/IT boundary that used to be an air gap - the loss is a main engine, not a file
   - Ship-specific: data ownership between owner, charterer, maker and crew, and discoverability in a dispute
   - Ship-specific: maker fragmentation, and the ship's staff as first-line support in mid-ocean
   - Ship-specific: crew monitoring and privacy
5. **How loT is changing the maritime industry**
   - From calendar to condition - maintenance, hull cleaning and even survey
   - From the ship as an island to the ship as a node - a real shift of authority to the shore centre
   - From assertion to evidence in commerce - charter performance, warranty, emissions reporting
   - From periodic compliance to continuous compliance
   - The regulation is pulling the technology aboard, not the other way round
6. **loT in energy efficiency optimization**
   - The 2023 IMO GHG Strategy was adopted 7 July 2023, one month before this sitting
   - EEXI and operational CII have applied since 1 January 2023; DCS reporting since 2019; SEEMP Part III on board
   - EEXI is a design index settled once; the CII is operational and recalculated yearly - the CII is what the data serves
   - Levers: hull and propeller performance, engine condition and load, trim, routing and just-in-time, auxiliary load, boiler and cargo heating
   - The measurement saves nothing - the saving appears when a decision changes
7. **loT in predictive Maintenance**
   - Reactive, preventive, predictive and prescriptive - predictive is measured condition and trend
   - Sense the parameters that carry a failure signature - vibration, temperature, oil condition, cylinder pressure, differentials
   - Establish a baseline for the individual machine at a defined operating point
   - Detect the deviation and its RATE - the value is in the trend, not the reading
   - Decide, act, and feed back what was actually found on opening up
   - Condition data can support a planned maintenance scheme in place of an opened-up survey
   - Knowing the part three weeks early converts emergency airfreight into routine delivery
8. **What the Chief Engineer has to do about it**
   - Own the data quality - calibration and siting become planned maintenance items
   - Keep the manual competence alive as fallback and as check
   - Cyber risk is an SMS obligation - MSC.428(98), first DOC verification after 1 January 2021; MSC-FAL.1/Circ.3
   - Segregate the machinery network, control removable media and maker remote access, know how to run disconnected
   - Fix in writing what the shore centre may see, advise and not instruct
   - Every alert has an owner and a response, or it is removed
   - Audit claimed savings against the ship's own fuel figures and against what was found on opening up

**Memory cue.** WHAT - LAYERS - ADVANTAGES - DISADVANTAGES - CHANGES - EFFICIENCY - PREDICTIVE - GOVERNANCE (1 to 8). Inside step 2 hold SENSE - CONNECT - EDGE - ANALYSE - ACT. Inside step 7 hold SENSE - BASELINE - DEVIATE - DECIDE, then feed back what you found.
