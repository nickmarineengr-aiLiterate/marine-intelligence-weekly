## 2. DECOMPOSITION GATE

**Question intent.** A composite question with two tasks and a hidden discrimination. The first task looks like recall - differentiate five named surveys - but the examiner has deliberately mixed three periodical surveys with two occasional ones, and the candidate who does not notice writes five paragraphs about one cycle. The second task is a register, and its own hidden discrimination is the phrase 'the IMO Convention under which they are issued', which is untrue of several documents every ship carries. The examiner is testing boundaries in both halves, not list length.

**Mark allocation.** Sixteen marks with no printed split. Three tasks are set - differentiate five surveys, give the purpose of each, and list the certificates with issuing authority and convention - and the first two are really one. The answer allots one framing section, five parallel survey sections, one section on the harmonized system that makes the first three intelligible as a system, and one substantial section carrying the register and the non-IMO documents. The proportions give slightly more to the surveys than to the register, because a list can be produced quickly under pressure and a differentiation cannot.

**Examiner expectation.** That the periodical and occasional surveys are separated. That the purpose of each is given, since the stem asks for purposes explicitly. That only the renewal survey is said to issue a certificate. That the damage survey is described as reported and called, not as spontaneous. That the repair survey is distinguished from it and that approval-and-witness is identified as what is being certified. That the certificate list gives all three columns the stem asks for. And that the Certificate of Class is kept out of a statutory list.

**Freshness risk.** MEDIUM - The survey scheme, the harmonized system and the certificate regime are all stable and need no reversal. The moving parts are two edition references: A.1156(32) is the operative Survey Guidelines here and every donor carries the 33rd Assembly successor adopted four months later; and the current SOLAS Consolidated Edition is 2020 rather than 2024. Two further currency points were checked and cleared by exclusion - the Hong Kong Convention is not in force so no recycling certificate belongs in the register, and the MLC 2022 amendments are not in force so nothing in the MLC entry depends on them.

**Jurisdiction risk.** HIGH, and it is concentrated in the register. Four legal orders appear in a list that looks homogeneous: IMO conventions, under which most of the certificates are issued; national law, under which the Certificate of Registry is issued; other United Nations agencies - the ILO for MLC 2006 and the WHO for the Ship Sanitation Control Certificate; and the classification society's own rules, under which the Certificate of Class is issued and which is not statutory at all. An answer that presents all of them as IMO certificates has the orders confused, and the stem's own wording invites exactly that error.

**Technical ambiguities.** 'Repair survey' is not a defined term in the conventions and is used in class practice for two related things - attendance during a repair following damage, and attendance during planned repair or steel renewal at a docking. The answer covers both and says so rather than choosing. 'Enlist ALL statutory certificates' is impossible to satisfy literally, since the set depends on ship type, size, trade and flag; the answer gives the general set for a conventional cargo ship, marks the ship-type-specific entries, and states the qualification explicitly rather than implying completeness it cannot have.

**Target answer shape.** Eight numbered sections. A short bulleted framing section carrying the periodical-versus-occasional split. Five parallel survey sections, each a bulleted WHEN / PURPOSE / SCOPE / PRODUCES so that five surveys can be compared at a glance and the candidate has a template. A bulleted section on the harmonized system. A final section with a standing note on issuing authority, the register as a rendered THREE-COLUMN TABLE by instrument, a bulleted list of documents NOT issued under an IMO convention, an honest completeness qualification, and a closing single-sentence proposition joining the two halves.

**Primary source plan.** Carry the annual survey and condition of class from the MIW verification record for QP2310 Q2; the annual-versus-periodical distinction from QP2312 Q8 and QP2509 Q9; renewal depth, thickness measurement and substantial corrosion from QP2503 Q2 and QP2507 Q7; and the certificate register structure and the harmonization argument from QP2406 Q1, extended from its oil-tanker set to the general set. Deliberately NOT planned: any quotation of SOLAS or of the Survey Guidelines; any certificate validity period beyond the five-year harmonized period; any class rule number; any ship recycling or MLC 2022 amendment content.

## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: **compare**. The principal headings of the model answer ARE these steps.

1. **The distinction that organises the whole answer**
   - Annual, intermediate and renewal are PERIODICAL - calendar-driven, anchored to the anniversary date, and what keep certificates valid
   - Damage and repair are OCCASIONAL - event-driven, outside the cycle, with no window and no schedule
   - The stem names five as though they were one cycle; two of them are not in it
   - Any of the five may be done as a statutory survey or as a class survey, usually together
   - But the findings go to different places - a statutory finding affects a certificate, a class finding produces a condition of class
2. **Annual survey**
   - WHEN: every year, within three months either side of the anniversary date
   - PURPOSE: to confirm the ship and equipment REMAIN satisfactory and have not been altered without approval
   - SCOPE: general, largely visual, afloat - hull, closing appliances, LSA, FFA, machinery running, documentation; selected items tested
   - PRODUCES: an ENDORSEMENT on the existing certificate, not a new one
   - Missed outside its window, the certificate is invalid
3. **Intermediate survey**
   - WHEN: once per five-year period, in place of the second OR third annual survey
   - PURPOSE: a deeper check part-way through, because a visual annual cannot find deterioration inside tanks and structure
   - SCOPE: annual survey plus internal examination of selected spaces, thickness measurement where judged necessary, more extensive machinery and system testing, ESP programme where applicable
   - PRODUCES: an endorsement in place of the annual endorsement
4. **Renewal survey**
   - WHEN: at intervals not exceeding five years, before the certificate expires; may be done progressively in class practice
   - PURPOSE: to establish fitness for the NEXT five years - the only survey that asks that question
   - SCOPE: structure inside and out with drydocking or approved in-water survey; tanks internally; thickness measurement and substantial corrosion identified and dealt with; machinery, shafting and safety devices opened up; boilers and pressure vessels; full system testing
   - PRODUCES: a NEW certificate - the only one of the five that does
   - The bottom must be examined twice in five years, with a maximum interval
5. **Damage survey**
   - WHEN: whenever damage or a defect affects safety, structural integrity, class or certificate validity - unscheduled, no window
   - PURPOSE: to establish what is damaged, how badly, and what must be done before the ship proceeds
   - SCOPE: the damage and anything it may have affected - which reaches beyond the visible dent
   - The surveyor is CALLED: the owner or master must report a casualty or defect
   - PRODUCES: a report, a condition of class or recommendation with a date, and possibly suspension or a conditional voyage to a repair port
   - It also produces the technical evidence on which a hull and machinery claim turns
6. **Repair survey**
   - WHEN: during and after repair - following damage, planned at docking, or renewing wasted structure found at renewal survey
   - PURPOSE: to confirm the repair was to an approved procedure, with approved materials, by qualified people, to standard
   - SCOPE: procedure approval BEFORE work begins; material, welding procedure and welder approval; attendance at hold points; NDT; testing on completion and sea trials where propulsion or steering is affected
   - PRODUCES: clearance of the condition of class and restoration of the affected certificate
   - A technically excellent unwitnessed repair does NOT clear the condition - approval and witness are what is certified
   - Emergency repairs at sea are temporary, and must be reported and surveyed at the first opportunity
7. **The harmonized system that ties the first three together**
   - Before it, each convention ran its own cycle on its own dates with certificates of different lengths
   - Introduced by the 1988 Protocols to SOLAS and Load Lines, in force 3 February 2000
   - A common five-year period; a single anniversary date; three-month windows
   - A common vocabulary of initial, annual, intermediate, periodical, renewal and additional surveys
   - A short extension to complete a voyage - and the replacement certificate still runs from the ORIGINAL expiry
   - Survey guidance at this sitting is resolution A.1156(32)
   - Damage and repair surveys sit entirely outside this scheme, which is why they have no windows
8. **The statutory certificates, their issuing authority and their convention**
   - Standing note: the issuing authority is the flag Administration, most often through an RO under a specific delegation
   - SOLAS - Safety Construction, Safety Equipment, Safety Radio or the combined Cargo Ship Safety Certificate; Passenger Ship Safety; DG document; Certificates of Fitness; DOC and SMC; ISSC; CSR; Safe Manning; Polar; grain authorization
   - Load Lines 1966 / 1988 Protocol - International Load Line Certificate
   - Tonnage 1969 - International Tonnage Certificate
   - MARPOL - IOPP, NLS, ISPP, IAPP, EIAPP, IEE and the fuel oil consumption Statement of Compliance
   - AFS 2001 and BWM 2004 - anti-fouling and ballast water certificates
   - CLC, Bunkers and Nairobi - financial security certificates issued by a State on evidence of insurance, not survey certificates
   - NOT delegable: Exemption Certificates, the Continuous Synopsis Record, the Minimum Safe Manning Document
   - NOT IMO at all: Certificate of Registry (national law), MLC 2006 (ILO), Ship Sanitation Control Certificate (WHO), and the Certificate of Class (the society's own rules)
   - What a ship carries depends on her type, size, trade and flag

**Memory cue.** SPLIT - ANNUAL - INTERMEDIATE - RENEWAL - DAMAGE - REPAIR - HARMONIZED - CERTIFICATES (1 to 8). Inside steps 2 to 6 hold WHEN - PURPOSE - SCOPE - PRODUCES. What each produces: ENDORSE - ENDORSE - NEW CERTIFICATE - CONDITION OF CLASS - CLEARS THE CONDITION.
