## 2. DECOMPOSITION GATE

**Question intent.** A four-limb technical question that looks like a recall exercise and is not. The examiner is testing whether the candidate understands that all four named devices are recovering from the same small set of propeller losses, because a candidate who has that idea can explain a device he has never seen and can also say why the claimed gains do not add. The second discriminator is scepticism: the stem invites brochure percentages, and an answer that recites them without qualification tells the examiner that the candidate has not thought about where the numbers come from.

**Mark allocation.** Sixteen marks with no printed split on this copy, and none on the January 2023 sitting of the same stem either. The four limbs are named equally and are treated equally - one section each - but they are not the whole question: the stem opens with a proposition about propulsion effectiveness and asks for devices 'currently in use', which licenses the loss-budget section that precedes them and the honesty about the vane wheel that follows. The answer allots one section to the losses, four to the named devices, and three to synthesis, selection and verification.

**Examiner expectation.** That the swirl and the hub vortex are identified as the recoverable losses before any device is named. That the thrust fin mechanism is given correctly - horizontal fin, lift from the swirl, forward component - and that the handedness is explained. That the asymmetry of an asymmetric rudder is placed above and below the shaft line and not port and starboard. That the bulb is tied to the hub vortex specifically. That the vane wheel's turbine-and-propeller action is explained and its service record stated honestly. And that the answer ends on whether a claimed gain can be believed.

**Freshness risk.** LOW - but not zero, and the residual risk runs in an unusual direction. The physics does not date. ## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: **explain**. The principal headings of the model answer ARE these steps.

1. **Where the recoverable energy is**
   - Rotational or swirl energy left in the race - the largest recoverable loss
   - The hub vortex behind the boss - drag, cavitation erosion and pressure pulses
   - Separation and drag on the rudder itself, which the rudder introduces
   - The rudder is the only large appendage standing in that accelerated, swirling flow
   - A rudder device RECOVERS waste; it does not create thrust, so its gain is bounded by the loss
2. **(a) Rudder Thrust Fins**
   - Horizontal wing-like fins each side of the rudder, in the slipstream, above and below the shaft line
   - The swirling inflow meets the fin at an angle of attack and the fin develops lift
   - Because the fin is horizontal, the resolved component of that lift points FORWARD - recovered thrust
   - Fins are HANDED and set at opposite angles above and below, because the swirl reverses
   - Usually mounted on a bulb, which carries the roots; they add wetted surface and some drag
   - Claimed at about 2 to 5 per cent at design condition, falling away off design draught and speed
3. **(b) Asymmetric Rudders**
   - The section differs ABOVE and BELOW the shaft line - twisted leading edge, or offset halves
   - Swirl arrives from one side above the shaft and the other side below it
   - A symmetrical rudder therefore carries an incident angle over its whole span with the helm amidships
   - Consequences: induced drag, separation and rudder CAVITATION at the leading edge and horn gap
   - Twisting aligns each half with its own local flow; course keeping improves too
   - Designed for one propeller and one direction of rotation; a newbuilding or conversion item, not a bolt-on
   - Claimed at about 2 to 6 per cent - and the cavitation and erosion benefit is often the real one
4. **(c) Rudder Bulbs**
   - A streamlined body on the rudder leading edge, aligned with and close abaft the propeller boss
   - Fills the low-pressure space where the hub vortex forms and lets the flow close along a fair body
   - Removes vortex drag, a source of rudder cavitation erosion, and part of the hull pressure pulse
   - Gap and alignment are critical - too far aft or off axis and it achieves little
   - Commonly the structural base for thrust fins; one of the more practicable retrofits
   - Claimed at about 2 to 5 per cent, larger on heavily loaded propellers where the vortex is strongest
5. **(d) Grim Vane Wheels**
   - A freely rotating wheel of LARGER diameter than the propeller, mounted abaft it and connected to nothing
   - Inner span lies inside the race and is driven round as a TURBINE
   - Outer span lies outside the race in undisturbed water and acts as a PROPELLER, producing thrust
   - Absorbs no shaft power at all - it is driven entirely by wasted rotational energy
   - Claims reached about 10 per cent, the highest of the four
   - Service experience was poor: blade fatigue and blade loss on the slender outer span, and bearing problems
   - Rare in service today - the stem says 'currently in use' and the honest answer says so
6. **What the four have in common, and how they combine**
   - All four are recovery devices, not power sources
   - Three attack rotational energy; only the bulb attacks the hub vortex - which is why it combines so well
   - THE GAINS DO NOT ADD - they draw on the same finite pool of wasted energy
   - Every device is specific to a wake field, propeller, rotation direction, draught and speed
   - They are permanent appendages with structural, fatigue and survey consequences
   - The propeller-side family - ducts, boss cap fins, pre-swirl stators - attacks the same losses from ahead
7. **Selection and retrofit in practice**
   - Bulb and fins are realistic dry-dock retrofits; an asymmetric rudder generally is not
   - Judge against the ship's ACTUAL operating profile, not the design point the gain was computed at
   - Do the cheap things first - a fouled hull or rough propeller erases the whole gain
   - Driver at this sitting: EEXI and operational CII since 1 January 2023, and the 2023 GHG Strategy of 7 July 2023
   - Class approval for attachment, scantlings and fatigue; reassess rudder cavitation after modification
   - Exposed appendages are damaged by ice, tugs and contact, and are a docking survey item
8. **Judging whether a claimed gain is real**
   - Quoted figures are model-scale or CFD at design draught, trim, speed, calm water and clean hull
   - Scale effects on wake and boundary layer flatter the model, and wake devices are where that error is largest
   - Combined-device gains are not additive
   - The ship rarely operates at the design point - trim, draught, fouling, weather, part load
   - Verify by continuous hull and propeller performance monitoring on ISO 19030, over enough time to separate the device from fouling
   - Ask for full-scale before-and-after data from a sister ship in a comparable trade
