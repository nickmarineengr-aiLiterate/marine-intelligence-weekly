## 2. DECOMPOSITION GATE

**Question intent.** Three printed limbs testing three different things: knowledge of an instrument, knowledge of a short factual list, and understanding of how the three State roles fit together. The examiner is testing whether the candidate grasps that the III Code is about STATE CAPABILITY rather than about ships. Two discriminators: limb (b) is short, factual and either known or not; and within limb (c) the coastal State part is where most answers collapse, because candidates search for controls over shipping and the Code's coastal obligations are services to it.

**Mark allocation.** Sixteen marks with no printed split on this copy. The three integrated sittings of this identical stem print (6), (4) and (6) against the limbs, and while those marks are DELIBERATELY NOT IMPORTED as printed truth for this copy, they are a fair guide to the examiner's intent and are used as such. The answer allots framing plus three sections to limb (a), one section to limb (b), and three sections to limb (c) - matching the 6/4/6 shape without asserting it.

**Examiner expectation.** That the Code is named with its resolution number and date, and that its mandatory route is explained rather than assumed. That BOTH halves of the objective are given. That the strategy is given as a cycle. That the ambiguity in 'which all IMO instruments' is noticed and both answers supplied. That the five indicators are produced accurately, including the exclusion of constructive total losses. That the coastal State limb is answered as services. And that the answer closes by connecting the three roles rather than listing them.

**Freshness risk.** HIGH - this question carries more reversal risk than any other on the paper. Two Assembly instruments must be reversed, both adopted on the same day four months after the sitting, and all three integrated depth donors sit after that boundary and carry the later numbers correctly for themselves. The trap is compounded because the Code's OWN number, A.1070(28), is not reversed - so a reviewer applying a blanket correction in either direction gets it wrong. The mandating chain and the Audit Scheme are stable and need nothing.

**Jurisdiction risk.** MEDIUM to HIGH. The whole question is about the boundary between three State roles, and the commonest error is to attribute a port State power to a coastal State or a coastal State service to a flag State. Two further boundaries matter: the Code is an IMO instrument that binds through the conventions, while the national legislation it demands is domestic law; and the Code's obligations attach only in respect of instruments the State has actually joined. Where India is concerned the Administration is the Directorate General of Shipping under the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958.

**Technical ambiguities.** The printed limb 'Which all IMO instruments covered in the code.' is genuinely ambiguous - it can mean the Code's six subject areas, which name no convention, or the treaty instruments under which the Code is mandatory. The two answers are different and the answer gives both with an explanation of why, rather than choosing one and risking the other. The stem is also printed as a statement with a full stop rather than as a question; that is preserved and answered as the question it plainly is. 'Strategy recommended to flag state' is read as the Code's strategy paragraph, which addresses the State in all three roles rather than the flag role alone, and the answer says so.

**Target answer shape.** Eight numbered sections mapped onto the three printed limbs. Prose for the framing section, because how the Code binds is an argument. A bulleted two-part objective. A NUMBERED three-step strategy, because it is a cycle and the order matters. Two clearly separated bulleted answers to the instruments question. A bulleted list of five indicators followed by two propositions about what they are for. Three bulleted sections for the three State roles, the coastal one led by the services proposition. A closing paragraph joining the roles and naming the Audit Scheme above them.

## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: **explain**. The principal headings of the model answer ARE these steps.

1. **The Code, and how a recommendatory text came to bind**
   - Resolution A.1070(28), adopted by the Assembly 4 December 2013; revoked A.1054(27)
   - The Code reads in 'should' throughout and was never redrafted into obligation language
   - It binds because the CONVENTIONS were amended to require its use
   - A.1083(28), A.1084(28), A.1085(28) for Load Lines, Tonnage and COLREG
   - MSC.366(93), MSC.373(93), MSC.374(93), MSC.375(93) for SOLAS, STCW, the STCW Code and the 1988 LL Protocol
   - MEPC.246(66) and MEPC.247(66) for MARPOL and its 1997 Protocol; mandatory from 1 January 2016
   - Four parts - common areas, flag, coastal, port - each State part closing on evaluation and review
2. **The objectives of the Code**
   - To enhance global maritime safety and protection of the marine environment
   - AND to assist States in the implementation of instruments of the Organization - the half routinely dropped
   - It is not a new set of technical requirements but a statement of what a State must be able to DO
   - A State is bound only for instruments to which it is a Contracting Government or Party
   - Geography and circumstance decide which of the three roles dominates
   - Promulgate laws and take all other steps to give the instruments full and complete effect
   - Do not transfer damage or hazards from one area to another, or transform one pollution type into another
3. **The strategy recommended to the flag State**
   - Develop an overall strategy covering the flag, port and coastal roles together
   - Establish a methodology to monitor and assess that it delivers implementation and enforcement
   - Continuously review it to achieve, maintain and improve performance and capability
   - DEVELOP - MONITOR - REVIEW: a cycle, not a document written once
   - National legislation and administrative procedures must address the whole field, and be communicated
   - Three initial actions: the ability to legislate; a legal basis for enforcement and verification; sufficient personnel with maritime expertise
4. **Which IMO instruments the Code covers**
   - The printed question is ambiguous and there are TWO correct answers - give both
   - First: the SIX subject areas, where the Code names no convention at all
   - Safety of life at sea; prevention of pollution; training, certification and watchkeeping
   - Load lines; tonnage measurement; preventing collisions at sea
   - MARITIME SECURITY IS NOT ONE OF THE SIX
   - Second: the treaty instruments under which the Code is mandatory, listed at this sitting in A.1157(32)
   - SOLAS 1974 with its 1978 and 1988 Protocols; MARPOL and its 1997 Protocol; STCW 1978 and the STCW Code; LL 1966 and LL PROT 1988; TONNAGE 1969; COLREG 1972
   - Do NOT lead with a count - neither instrument states one and both nine and ten circulate
5. **Key performance indicators for flag State performance**
   - Evaluate performance on a periodic basis, in respect of processes, procedures and resources
   - Port State control detention rates
   - Flag State inspection results
   - Casualty statistics
   - Communication and information processes
   - Annual loss statistics, EXCLUDING constructive total losses
   - Purpose is diagnostic - are staffing, resources and procedures adequate - not a league table
   - For the State's own self-evaluation, not a return to IMO; the external check is the Audit Scheme
   - Further review areas: detentions relative to fleet size, certificate-holder incompetence, responses to PSC deficiency reports, serious casualty investigations, pollution violations, suspensions and withdrawals
6. **Flag State responsibilities**
   - A safety and environmental protection programme, with an audit and inspection programme INDEPENDENT of any body issuing the certificates
   - Ships sufficiently and efficiently manned, on the Principles of Safe Manning
   - Delegation to a recognized organization under a formal written agreement, with specific instructions for ships found unfit to proceed
   - An oversight programme with supplementary surveys and competent supervising staff
   - Prohibit sailing until compliant; inspect periodically; penalties of adequate severity
   - Institute proceedings after investigation IRRESPECTIVE OF WHERE the violation occurred
   - Qualified, trained surveyors and investigators carrying identification documents
   - Impartial and objective casualty investigation, aimed at prevention, reported to the Organization
   - And the part closes on evaluation and review
7. **Coastal State responsibilities**
   - Exercising a right conferred by an instrument incurs the additional obligations that go with it
   - The obligations are overwhelmingly SERVICES TO SHIPPING, not controls over ships
   - Radiocommunication services; meteorological services and warnings; search and rescue; hydrographic services
   - Ships' routeing systems; ship reporting systems; vessel traffic services; aids to navigation
   - A control and monitoring programme within its waters
   - Timely response to pollution incidents, and cooperation in casualty investigation
   - Periodic evaluation of its own performance
8. **Port State responsibilities, and how the three roles interlock**
   - Adequate reception facilities for ship-generated waste
   - Port State control
   - A register of local fuel oil suppliers
   - No more favourable treatment for ships of non-parties
   - A PSC programme consistent with the Assembly's Procedures - at this sitting A.1155(32) - and with the regional MOU
   - PSCOs free of commercial pressure and NOT employed by recognized organizations or classification societies
   - Periodic evaluation of its own performance
   - The three roles overlap deliberately so a failure by one is caught by another - detentions abroad return as the flag's own first indicator
   - Above all three sits the Member State Audit Scheme, auditing the State against this Code

**Memory cue.** HOW IT BINDS - OBJECTIVE - STRATEGY - INSTRUMENTS - INDICATORS - FLAG - COASTAL - PORT (1 to 8). Inside step 3 hold DEVELOP - MONITOR - REVIEW. Inside step 4 hold SAFETY - POLLUTION - TRAINING - LOAD LINES - TONNAGE - COLLISIONS. Inside step 5 hold DETENTIONS - INSPECTIONS - CASUALTIES - COMMUNICATION - LOSSES. Inside step 7 hold RADIO - WEATHER - SAR - CHARTS - ROUTEING - REPORTING - VTS - LIGHTS.
