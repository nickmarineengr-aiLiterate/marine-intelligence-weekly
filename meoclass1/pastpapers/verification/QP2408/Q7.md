## 1. THE PRINTED QUESTION

> A grounded ship has been re-floated and towed by a professional salvor to a place of safety. As per the Salvage Convention the salvor has a right to award. a) List the various criteria which are considered under Article 13 of the Convention for determining the amount of award (6) b) State the duties of the salvor and the shipowner/master as per this Convention (5) c) Assuming LOF-2000 was agreed between the parties, discuss when the Convention is applicable and when the LOF if any dispute arises. (5)

Printed limbs:

- `a)` **(6)** The criteria considered under Article 13 of the Convention in determining the amount of award.
- `b)` **(5)** The duties of the salvor and of the shipowner and master under the Convention.
- `c)` **(5)** Assuming LOF 2000 was agreed between the parties, when the Convention applies and when the LOF applies if a dispute arises.

**Answer route:**

1. **The Convention, and where the stem's facts have got to** _intro_ - International Convention on Salvage, 1989, in force 1996, replacing the 1910 Convention; The stem's facts - grounded, refloated, towed to a place of safety by a professional salvor; A useful result has been achieved, so the right to a reward has accrued
2. **The scheme Article 13 sits inside** _(a)_ - Article 12 - no payment without a useful result. No cure, no pay is retained; Article 13 - the reward, fixed with a view to encouraging salvage operations; The reward is what Article 13 quantifies; the entitlement comes from Article 12
3. **The Article 13 criteria** _(a)_ - Salved value; skill and efforts in preventing or minimising environmental damage; Measure of success; nature and degree of danger; skill and efforts in salving the property and life; Time, expenses and losses; risk of liability; promptness; availability and use of vessels; state of readiness and efficiency of equipment and its value
4. **How the criteria are applied** _(a)_ - They are NOT listed in order of importance - all are taken together; The reward shall not exceed the salved value of the vessel and other property; Payable by the interests in proportion to their respective salved values
5. **Who pays, and what can reduce or extinguish the award** _(a)_ - Ship and cargo interests, each in proportion to its salved value; Article 18 - misconduct, fault or neglect by the salvor may deprive him of all or part; Article 19 - services against express and reasonable prohibition are not payable
6. **The salvor's duties** _(b)_ - Carry out the operations with due care; In performing that duty, exercise due care to prevent or minimise damage to the environment; Seek help from other salvors when circumstances reasonably require, and accept their intervention when reasonably requested by the owner or master
7. **The duties of the owner and the master** _(b)_ - Co-operate fully with the salvor; In doing so, exercise due care to prevent or minimise damage to the environment; Accept redelivery when the vessel is brought to a place of safety and reasonably requested to do so
8. **The mutual character of the duties, the master's authority, and breach** _(b)_ - The environmental duty is imposed on BOTH sides - this is the Convention's innovation; The master may conclude a salvage contract on behalf of the ship's owner; Breach bears on the award and on liability; misconduct engages Article 18
9. **What LOF 2000 is, and what it is not** _(c)_ - Lloyd's Open Form - a standard-form salvage CONTRACT, not an instrument; No cure no pay; the remuneration is not fixed at the time of agreement; Signed quickly, often by the master, so that work can begin at once
10. **When the Convention applies** _(c)_ - It applies as the general law where a State has given it force, and its rules govern by default; Article 6 - the Convention applies to any salvage operation save as otherwise provided in a contract; But its core duties cannot be contracted out of
11. **When the LOF applies** _(c)_ - Where the parties have agreed it, its terms govern the relationship between them; It fixes the forum, the law, the security and the machinery for fixing the sum; It does not create the entitlement - the Convention's principles do
12. **How a dispute is actually resolved under an LOF** _(c)_ - Arbitration in London under the terms of the form, not litigation in the casualty's jurisdiction; The arbitrator fixes the amount applying the Convention's criteria; Appeal within the form's own structure; security provided before release
13. **The relationship, stated as a rule** _(c)_ - The contract governs procedure and terms; the Convention governs the substance; Where the contract is silent, the Convention fills the gap; Where the contract would defeat the Convention's protective provisions, the Convention prevails
14. **Why the arrangement exists at all** _all_ - A casualty needs a decision in minutes and a valuation that takes months; The form lets work start; the Convention ensures the sum is fixed on principled criteria; Together they are why a professional salvor sails towards a ship nobody has yet agreed to pay for
