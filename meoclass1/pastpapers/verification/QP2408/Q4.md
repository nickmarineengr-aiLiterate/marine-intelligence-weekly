**Answer route:**

1. **What a market-based measure is** _(a)_ - An instrument that puts a price on the emission rather than prescribing the equipment; Contrast with technical measures (design) and operational measures (how she is run); The purpose is to make the low-carbon choice the commercially rational one
2. **Where the authority comes from** _(a)_ - The 2023 IMO GHG Strategy, resolution MEPC.377(80), adopted 7 July 2023; It revoked the 2018 Initial Strategy and set the current levels of ambition; Everything discussed at MEPC 81 is being developed under its work plan
3. **The targets the measures have to deliver** _(a)_ - Net-zero GHG emissions from international shipping by or around 2050; At least 20%, striving for 30%, reduction by 2030 against 2008; At least 70%, striving for 80%, by 2040 against 2008
4. **The main objectives of a market-based measure** _(a)_ - Close the price gap between conventional and zero or near-zero fuels; Raise revenue for the transition and for impacts on States; Reward the first mover and penalise the laggard, without prescribing technology
5. **The shape the Strategy fixed - a basket of two elements** _(a)_ - A technical element: a goal-based marine fuel standard on GHG intensity; An economic element: a maritime GHG emissions pricing mechanism; To be finalized and agreed by the Committee by 2025
6. **The types of economic measure under discussion** _(a)_ - A levy or contribution per tonne of GHG emitted; A cap-and-trade emissions trading system; A feebate - a fee on high-intensity fuel funding a rebate on low-intensity fuel
7. **Why the technical and economic elements are coupled** _(a)_ - The fuel standard sets the trajectory; the pricing element pays for it; Well-to-wake accounting, to prevent a shift of emissions to other sectors; Synergies with the existing CII to be considered
8. **Where the process had actually reached at this sitting** _(a)_ - MEPC 81 concluded 22 March 2024, five months before the sitting; The mid-term measures were PROPOSALS UNDER DEVELOPMENT, not an agreed instrument; MEPC 81 did adopt MEPC.384(81) and MEPC.385(81), which are not MBMs
9. **How the measures contribute to reducing GHG emissions** _(a)_ - Price signal, revenue, and a guaranteed demand for compliant fuel; Global and flag-neutral, unlike a regional scheme; Technology-neutral, so it does not bet on one fuel
10. **Challenge one - agreeing the mechanism and the price** _(b)_ - Levy, ETS or feebate are structurally different and produce different winners; The price level is the whole argument, and it had not been settled; Consensus practice at IMO means the least acceptable option constrains the outcome
11. **Challenge two - impacts on States, and the equity question** _(b)_ - The Strategy requires impacts to be assessed before adoption; Particular attention to developing countries, LDCs and SIDS; The clash between no more favourable treatment and common but differentiated responsibilities
12. **Challenge three - fuel, safety and the ship** _(b)_ - A price signal cannot create fuel that is not produced or bunkered; Safety and crew competence for alternative fuels; Verification, data quality and enforcement across the world fleet
13. **Potential impacts on the shipping industry** _(b)_ - Cost, and who bears it - the charterparty allocation problem; Asset values, newbuilding decisions and the risk of stranded tonnage; Competitiveness, trade routes and the risk of regional fragmentation
14. **Alignment with global climate goals** _(b)_ - Explicit reference to the Paris Agreement temperature goal in the Strategy; Well-to-wake accounting aligns shipping with life-cycle climate accounting; But shipping sits outside national inventories, which is why an IMO measure is needed
