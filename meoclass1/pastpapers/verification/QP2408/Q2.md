**Technical ambiguities identified before drafting:**

- 'Signatory states' is loose. Strictly, signature and ratification are different acts and the obligations bind States PARTIES rather than signatories. The answer uses 'Party' where precision matters and does not make an issue of the stem's wording, because the examiner plainly means States bound by the Convention.
- 'Its 2005 Protocol' is printed in the singular. There are in fact TWO 2005 Protocols, one to the Convention and one to the Fixed Platforms Protocol. The answer treats the Convention Protocol as the subject, which is plainly what is meant, and mentions the fixed-platform companion once so that the completeness is visible without derailing the answer.
- 'Key provisions' in limb (a) overlaps 'legal obligations' in limb (b), and a careless answer will write the same material twice. The division taken is that limb (a) carries the OFFENCE-CREATING provisions - what the Convention makes criminal - and limb (b) carries the OBLIGATION-IMPOSING provisions - what a Party must do about them. Article 8bis sits in limb (b) because it is enforcement, and the stem's second limb names enforcement expressly.
- The boarding regime is not an obligation in the same sense as article 10; it is a permission with safeguards. It is placed in limb (b) as the enforcement mechanism, and its consent requirement is stated plainly so that it is not mistaken for a right of interdiction.

**Answer route:**

1. **The gap in the law that the Convention was written to close** _(a)_ - Piracy is defined too narrowly to cover a politically motivated seizure from within; The Achille Lauro, 1985 - the hijackers were passengers, not a second ship; No treaty then obliged a State to prosecute or extradite them
2. **The instruments and their dates** _(a)_ - SUA 1988, adopted at Rome, in force 1992; Fixed Platforms Protocol 1988; 2005 Protocols, in force 2010; Note there are TWO 2005 Protocols - the stem's singular is the Convention Protocol
3. **The primary objectives** _(a)_ - Make specified unlawful acts crimes in every Party's law; Ensure no safe haven - prosecute or extradite; Provide a cooperative framework: jurisdiction, evidence, extradition, information
4. **Where the Convention applies** _(a)_ - Article 4 - keyed to the ship's navigation beyond the outer limit of one State's territorial sea; It is a criminal-jurisdiction treaty, not a construction or equipment standard
5. **Key provisions one - the offences created in 1988** _(a)_ - Article 3 - seizure by force; violence endangering safe navigation; destruction or damage; devices likely to destroy; Interference with navigational facilities; communicating false information; injury or killing connected with these; Article 3.2 - attempt, abetting and threat
6. **Key provisions two - what the 2005 Protocol added** _(a)_ - Article 3bis - the ship as weapon, and transport of WMD-related material; Article 3ter - transporting a person who has committed a listed offence; Article 3quater - attempt, participation and threat for the new offences
7. **How the scheme aims to enhance maritime security** _(a)_ - SUA is criminal law and jurisdiction; ISPS is prevention, access control and security levels; SUA operates after the act and across borders; ISPS operates before it and on board; They are complementary and neither substitutes for the other
8. **Obligation one - criminalize and penalize (article 5)** _(b)_ - Article 5 - each Party shall make the offences punishable by appropriate penalties; Penalties must take into account the grave nature of the offences; Without domestic criminalization the rest of the treaty cannot operate
9. **Obligation two - establish jurisdiction (article 6)** _(b)_ - Article 6 - mandatory bases: flag, territory, nationality of the offender; Optional bases: nationality of victims, stateless residents, compulsion of a State, and others; Plus jurisdiction where the alleged offender is present and is not extradited
10. **Obligation three - custody, preliminary inquiry and notification (article 7)** _(b)_ - Article 7 - take the person into custody or secure their presence, on being satisfied circumstances warrant it; Conduct a preliminary inquiry into the facts; Notify the States with jurisdiction and indicate whether it intends to exercise it
11. **Obligation four - extradite or prosecute (article 10)** _(b)_ - Article 10 - if it does not extradite, the State must submit the case to its competent authorities for prosecution; Without exception whatsoever and whether or not the offence was committed in its territory; This is the provision that removes the safe haven
12. **Obligation five - extradition and mutual legal assistance (articles 11, 12 and 8)** _(b)_ - Article 11 - the offences are deemed extraditable offences in existing treaties, and the Convention may serve as the legal basis; Article 12 - the greatest measure of assistance in criminal proceedings, including evidence; Article 8 - the master may deliver an alleged offender to the authorities of another Party
13. **Obligation six - prevention, cooperation and reporting (articles 13 and 14)** _(b)_ - Article 13 - cooperate in preventing the offences, including by administrative and other measures; Article 14 - report to the Secretary-General of IMO information on the offence and on proceedings; This is what turns individual prosecutions into shared intelligence
14. **The enforcement mechanism at sea - boarding under article 8bis** _(b)_ - Boarding a ship of another Party requires the FLAG STATE'S CONSENT; Standing authorizations may be notified, and safeguards apply to the use of force and to those on board; This is the clearest evidence of the shift from investigating an act to interdicting one
