**Technical ambiguities identified before drafting:**

- 'The present ISO standards' is printed in the PLURAL. Strictly there is one specification for marine fuels, ISO 8217, supported by separate test-method and precision standards. The answer names the specification and explains that it is supported by a family of test-method standards, without asserting the number of any of them, because MIW holds none.
- 'The present' is a date-sensitive word on a question whose answer object must be true at a sitting two years in the past. The answer names the standard and expressly declines to assert which numbered edition was current, stating the reason. This is a declared limitation, not an evasion, and it costs no marks because the marks are for the features and the corrective action.
- 'Corrective action which can be initiated against various adverse observations' is deliberately open. It could be read as laboratory action, as engine-room action, or as commercial action. It is all three, and the answer says so - treatment plant, operating precautions, and the notification and claim route - because a Chief Engineer who purifies harder and tells nobody has taken half the action available.
- The relationship between ISO 8217 and MARPOL is frequently confused. ISO 8217 is a COMMERCIAL SPECIFICATION incorporated by reference into a bunker contract; MARPOL Annex VI regulation 18.3 is a MANDATORY requirement of international law. A fuel can be off-specification without breaching MARPOL, and can breach MARPOL while meeting most of the specification. The answer makes the distinction explicitly.
- Regulation 18.5 as it stood at this sitting speaks of 'fuel oil FOR COMBUSTION PURPOSES'. The 2024 amendments remove that phrase and split the paragraph. The pre-amendment wording is used and the amendment's status is stated separately.

## 7. ANSWER CONSTRUCTION

**Route archetype:** `explain`  ·  **Steps:** 14  ·  **Model answer blocks:** 51  ·  **Study note blocks:** 53  ·  **Retrieval cards:** 8

The answer route is the single canonical spine; the decomposition, memory cue, quick revision, retrieval cards and study notes all derive from it. **Answer route:**

1. **The standard, named and placed** _(a)_ - ISO 8217 is the international specification for marine fuels; It is a commercial specification, not a regulation; MARPOL Annex VI regulation 18.3 is the mandatory floor beneath it
2. **How the specification is organised** _(a)_ - Residual grades - the RM family; Distillate grades - the DM family; Grades distinguished by viscosity and by the parameter set applied
3. **The salient features of the specification** _(a)_ - A table of characteristics with limits, each tied to a test method; Parameters split into those affecting handling, combustion, machinery wear and safety; General requirements: free of used lubricating oil and of material that harms the ship, the crew or the air
4. **The mandatory floor beneath the specification - MARPOL Annex VI regulation 18.3** _(a)_ - Blends of hydrocarbons from petroleum refining; small amounts of performance additives permitted; Free from inorganic acid; No added substance or chemical waste jeopardizing safety, harming machinery or personnel, or adding to air pollution
5. **How a fuel oil analysis report is read** _(a)_ - Sample first, then test, then decide - before the fuel is used if possible; Compare against the ordered grade and against the ship's plant capability; Group the observations - handling, combustion, wear, safety, statutory
6. **Corrective action - density, viscosity and ignition quality** _(a)_ - Density outside the separator's capability - check the plant type and settings; Viscosity - correct the preheating to reach injection viscosity, not a temperature; Poor calculated ignition quality - protect combustion timing and load changes
7. **Corrective action - catalytic fines** _(a)_ - Aluminium plus silicon - abrasive, and they survive combustion; Optimise settling, heating and separation; slow the throughput; Sample after the separator, not only on delivery
8. **Corrective action - water, sediment, stability and compatibility** _(a)_ - Water - drain, settle, purify; suspect the tank and the bunkering if it is high; Total sediment - a stability warning, not just dirt; Never blend incompatible fuels; segregate first, test compatibility before mixing
9. **Corrective action - sulphur and flashpoint** _(a)_ - Sulphur is a statutory parameter, not merely a commercial one; Off-spec sulphur triggers reporting and record obligations; Low flashpoint is a safety matter and may require the fuel to be rejected outright
10. **Corrective action - ash, metals and micro-carbon residue** _(a)_ - Vanadium and sodium - hot corrosion of exhaust valves and turbine components; Used lubricating oil markers - calcium, zinc, phosphorus - a ground for rejection; High carbon residue - deposits, fouling and poorer combustion
11. **The decision that sits above all the corrective actions** _(a)_ - Use with precautions, use with restrictions, segregate, or reject and debunker; Notify owners, charterers, supplier and P&I early, in writing; The MARPOL sample is the evidence on which any claim will turn
12. **What it is, and where it comes from** _(b)_ - Required by MARPOL Annex VI regulation 18.5 for ships subject to regulations 5 and 6; It must contain at least the information in appendix V; It is a statutory document, not a commercial receipt
13. **Its contents, retention and inspection** _(b)_ - The ten appendix V items, including the signed and certified supplier's declaration; Kept readily available for inspection and retained three years; Port State may inspect, copy and require the master to certify the copy
14. **The representative sample, and why the BDN matters** _(b)_ - Sealed and signed by the supplier's representative and the master or officer in charge; Retained under the ship's control until the fuel is substantially consumed; It is the evidence for both statutory defence and commercial claim
