**Technical ambiguities identified before drafting:**

- 'Approve' is not the Code's word. The Code distinguishes RECOGNITION - assessing an organization against Part 2 - from AUTHORIZATION, the delegation of specified functions to an already recognized organization. The answer uses the Code's two words and says why the distinction matters, because a candidate who treats approval as a single act cannot explain why section 8 exists separately from section 2.
- 'Mandatory requirements' could be read as the requirements on the RO only. Part 2 binds BOTH: scope 2.2 records mandatory requirements for the organization to be recognized, mandatory requirements for the RO when performing services, and mandatory requirements that FLAG STATES shall adhere to when authorizing. The answer says so, because limb (a) asks what the flag State must do.
- 'Oversight mechanisms outlined in the RO code' is a restriction, not a topic. Port State control, the III Code and the IMO Member State Audit Scheme are all real flag-performance mechanisms and none of them is in the RO Code. They are not written, because the examiner named the instrument.
- The Code's own scope paragraph 2.2.1 refers to the mandatory requirements for recognition as being in 'part 1' where the structure places them in part 2. The answer follows the STRUCTURE, which is unambiguous from the Contents and from part 2's own heading, and does not reproduce the cross-reference.

**Answer route:**

1. **What the RO Code is, and how it binds** _(a)_ - Adopted by MSC.349(92) and MEPC.237(65); took effect 1 January 2015; Made mandatory through SOLAS chapter XI-1 and the Load Lines Protocol; Three parts: Part 1 general, Part 2 mandatory, Part 3 recommendatory oversight guidance
2. **The power to delegate, and the limit on it** _(a)_ - Part 1 section 4: a flag State MAY delegate to an organization recognized as complying with the Code; 4.2 - the flag State SHALL NOT authorize functions beyond the RO's capabilities; 8.1 - the conventional bases: SOLAS I/6, LL 66 article 13, MARPOL Annexes I and II, TONNAGE 69
3. **Approval step one - the general requirements the organization must satisfy** _(a)_ - Part 2 section 2: rules and regulations, independence, impartiality, integrity, competence, responsibility, transparency; 2.1 - delegation is subject to confirmation of the organization's capability; Independence and revenue dependence are the two that fail organizations in practice
4. **Approval step two - the organizational, resource and process assessment** _(a)_ - Part 2 section 3 management and organization; section 4 resources; section 5 certification processes; Section 6 performance measurement, analysis and improvement, including internal and vertical contract audit; Appendices 1 and 2 - technical staff qualification and the survey and certification functions
5. **Approval step three - certification of the quality management system** _(a)_ - Part 2 section 7.1 - an effective internal QMS no less effective than the ISO 9000 series; 7.2 - periodic assessment and certification by a qualified accredited body recognized by the flag State; 7.3 - IMO monitors the certification and audit process
6. **Approval step four - the act of authorization, and what the flag State must specify** _(a)_ - 8.2 legal basis; 8.3 the nine specifications of scope; 8.4 resources; 8.5 instruments and 8.6 instructions, including what to do when a ship is not fit to proceed to sea; 8.7 records
7. **Approval step five - the written agreement and the notification to IMO** _(a)_ - Appendix 3 - the eight minimum headings of the formal written agreement; Part 1 section 5 - the flag State communicates and deposits with the Secretary-General a list of ROs; and notifies the specific responsibilities and conditions of the authority delegated
8. **What oversight is, and the status of Part 3** _(b)_ - Part 3 sections 1 and 2 - guidance on flag State oversight; expressly NON-MANDATORY; Oversight may include ship inspection, auditing and monitoring activities; The adopting resolution recommends Part 3 as a basis unless national requirements are at least equivalent
9. **Mechanism one - supervision of the delegated duties** _(b)_ - Part 3 section 5.2 - five items the flag State should consider; QMS documentation, internal instructions, RO documentation on the flag's fleet; cooperation with flag State inspection work, and provision of damage and casualty information
10. **Mechanism two - verification and monitoring, and the RO's reporting duties** _(b)_ - Part 3 section 5.3 - a system to ensure the adequacy of statutory certification and services; Immediate notification of a detainable deficiency and of a major non-conformity; Removal from class reported in writing within 30 days
11. **Mechanism three - auditing** _(b)_ - Part 3 section 6.1 - the flag State may rely on audits by an accredited certification body; 6.2 - ethical conduct, fair presentation, due professional care; Part 3 section 4 defines audit, audit criteria, audit evidence and audit findings
12. **Mechanism four - managing the oversight programme** _(b)_ - Part 3 section 7 - objectives and extent, responsibilities, resources, procedures, implementation, records; 7.4 - the programme is itself monitored and reviewed; Additional flag State ship inspections sit inside the programme, not outside it
13. **Mechanism five - the performance indicators** _(b)_ - Part 3 section 7.4.3 - five indicators for evaluating RO performance; Port State performance of ROs; RO internal audit results; third-party QMS audit results; Previous performance monitoring; condition and compliance of the ships the RO certifies
14. **What follows a finding, and why the scheme exists at all** _(b)_ - Corrective action, narrowing or withdrawal of the authorization; The flag State remains responsible in international law however much it delegates; Continued compliance is a condition of authorization, not a courtesy
