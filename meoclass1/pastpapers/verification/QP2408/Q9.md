**Technical ambiguities identified before drafting:**

- 'Components' of the SMS could mean the six FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS at element 1.4 or the twelve PART A ELEMENTS. The answer gives both and shows how they relate, because the functional requirements are what the Code demands an SMS contain, and the elements are how the Code then specifies each of them. Giving only one is defensible; giving both and explaining the relationship is better and costs three lines.
- 'Continuous improvement' is not a defined term in the Code and does not appear as a heading. It is an outcome produced by several elements working together - reporting and analysis at 9, maintenance at 10, and verification, review and evaluation at 12 - and the answer says so rather than pretending there is a clause called continuous improvement.
- 'Evolution ... including technological advancements, cyber risks, and environmental sustainability' invites the claim that the Code has been repeatedly amended to address each. IT HAS NOT. The Code's text has been substantially unchanged since MSC.353(92), and it has accommodated all three largely through the GENERALITY of its existing requirements - with cyber the one exception, where MSC.428(98) directed that the risks be addressed within the existing SMS framework. The honest answer says this, and it is a stronger answer than an invented amendment history.
- 'Environmental sustainability' is the loosest of the three. Environmental protection has been in the Code's title and objectives since adoption, but the decarbonisation instruments - the SEEMP, EEXI, the CII - sit in MARPOL Annex VI and NOT in the ISM Code. The answer distinguishes them, because conflating them is a substantive error.

**Answer route:**

1. **What the Code is, and how it binds** _(a)_ - Adopted by A.741(18); mandatory through SOLAS chapter IX; Amended through MSC.353(92), in force 1 January 2015; It regulates MANAGEMENT, not construction or equipment
2. **What a Safety Management System is, and why the Code works through one** _(a)_ - A structured and documented system enabling Company personnel to implement the policy effectively; The Code sets objectives and requires a system; it does not prescribe the procedures; The system is the instrument through which the Code operates
3. **The six functional requirements - what every SMS must contain** _(a)_ - Policy; instructions and procedures; defined authority and lines of communication; Reporting of accidents and non-conformities; emergency preparedness; Procedures for internal audits and management reviews
4. **The components - Part A, elements 1 to 12** _(a)_ - Policy, company responsibility, designated person, master's authority; Resources and personnel, shipboard operations, emergency preparedness; Reports and analysis, maintenance, documentation, company verification
5. **The certification layer - Part B** _(a)_ - Document of Compliance to the Company, up to five years, annually verified; Safety Management Certificate to the ship; Interim certification, and the consequence of a major non-conformity
6. **The role the SMS plays in implementing the Code** _(a)_ - It converts objectives into procedures that a named person performs; It creates the shore-to-ship link through the Designated Person; It generates the evidence on which compliance is verified
7. **Continuous improvement - the mechanism** _(a)_ - Element 9 - report, investigate and analyse; corrective action to prevent recurrence; Element 12 - internal audit at intervals not exceeding twelve months; Periodic evaluation of effectiveness, and timely corrective action by those responsible
8. **Continuous improvement - why it is the significant part** _(a)_ - Without it the SMS becomes a static manual that describes a ship nobody operates; It is what makes the system self-correcting rather than merely documented; It is the difference between a certified company and a safe one
9. **Why the Code exists, and the shift it represents** _(b)_ - A major ro-ro passenger casualty and an inquiry that found the fault ashore, not only aboard; The move from regulating hardware to regulating management; From compliance by inspection to compliance by demonstrated system
10. **Evolution one - from recommendation to mandatory instrument** _(b)_ - Adopted as an Assembly resolution, A.741(18); Made mandatory through SOLAS chapter IX, phased by ship type; The single largest change in its history
11. **Evolution two - the amendment chain** _(b)_ - MSC.104(73), MSC.179(79), MSC.195(80), MSC.273(85), MSC.353(92); Successive tightening of company responsibility, non-conformity handling and audit; The current text has been in force since 1 January 2015
12. **Evolution three - technological advancement** _(b)_ - Integrated bridge and engine control, remote monitoring, condition-based maintenance; The Code accommodates them through elements 6, 7 and 10 rather than by amendment; Familiarisation and competence become the binding constraint
13. **Evolution four - cyber risk** _(b)_ - MSC.428(98), adopted 16 June 2017 - cyber risk to be addressed in the SMS; Bearing in mind MSC-FAL.1/Circ.3 guidelines; No new element was added; the existing risk-assessment machinery was used
14. **Evolution five - environmental sustainability, and the honest limit** _(b)_ - Environmental protection is in the Code's title and objectives from the start; But decarbonisation instruments sit OUTSIDE the ISM Code, in MARPOL Annex VI; The Code adapts by generality - which is a strength and a weakness
