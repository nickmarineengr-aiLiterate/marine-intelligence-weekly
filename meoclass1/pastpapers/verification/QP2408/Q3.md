**Technical ambiguities identified before drafting:**

- 'The applicable regulation of SOLAS' is printed in the singular and there are TWO. Regulation I/21 creates the general undertaking; regulation XI-1/6 is what makes the Casualty Investigation Code mandatory and is the one the rest of the question depends on. The answer gives both and explains the relationship, because giving only I/21 leaves limb (b) unanchored and giving only XI-1/6 misses the provision it expressly takes into account.
- MARPOL's provision is an ARTICLE, not a regulation. The stem's word 'regulation' is loose. The answer says article 12 and does not make an issue of the wording.
- 'Salient points of casualty investigation code AND the recommended practices' could be read as one demand or two. It is two: the Code's mandatory standards are in Part II, and 'recommended practices' is Part III's own title. An answer that stops at Part II has answered the first half of an eight-mark limb.
- 'Very serious marine casualty' is defined in the Code in a single short sentence, which cannot by itself be worth four marks. The limb is therefore answered by giving the definition exactly, then locating it against marine casualty, marine incident and severe damage to the environment, then explaining what the classification triggers.
- The Code's definition of severe damage to the environment is itself evaluative - 'as evaluated by the State(s) affected, or the flag State' - so a VSMC finding on that branch is not purely objective. The answer says so, because it is the honest reading of the text.

**Answer route:**

1. **SOLAS regulation I/21 - Casualties** _(a)_ - Each Administration undertakes to investigate any casualty to its ships; Where it judges the investigation may assist in determining desirable changes to the regulations; And to supply IMO with pertinent information on the findings
2. **SOLAS regulation XI-1/6 - Additional requirements for the investigation of marine casualties and incidents** _(a)_ - Additional requirements for the investigation of marine casualties and incidents; Taking into account regulation I/21, investigations shall be conducted in accordance with the Casualty Investigation Code; Parts I and II shall be fully complied with; Part III should be taken into account to the greatest possible extent
3. **MARPOL article 12** _(a)_ - The corresponding duty to investigate casualties and report findings to the Organization; Load Lines 1966 article 23 carries the same duty for load line casualties; Together these are the conventional sources the Code recites
4. **What the Code is, and how it is built** _(b)_ - MSC.255(84), adopted 16 May 2008, took effect 1 January 2010; Three parts: I general provisions, II mandatory standards, III recommended practices; Replaces the practice established by A.849(20) as amended by A.884(21)
5. **The purpose - and what a marine safety investigation is not** _(b)_ - A common approach, to prevent future casualties and incidents; It does NOT seek to apportion blame or determine liability; Separate from and independent of any other form of investigation
6. **Mandatory standards - the Authority, and notification** _(b)_ - High seas or EEZ: the flag State notifies other substantially interested States; In territorial waters: flag and coastal State notify each other and then the others; Not delayed for want of complete information; eight-item content list
7. **Mandatory standards - the duty to investigate, and agreement between States** _(b)_ - A marine safety investigation SHALL be conducted into every very serious marine casualty; The flag State is responsible, subject to agreement under chapter 7; States consult to agree which will be the investigating State
8. **Mandatory standards - powers, parallel investigations, cooperation, independence** _(b)_ - National law must let investigators board, interview and acquire evidence; Another substantially interested State may investigate in parallel; timing to be coordinated; Investigators shall be impartial and objective and free from external direction
9. **Mandatory standards - obtaining evidence from seafarers** _(b)_ - Taken at the earliest practical opportunity; the seafarer returned or repatriated as soon as possible; Human rights upheld at all times; Informed of self-incrimination risk, the right to silence and available protections, with access to legal advice
10. **Mandatory standards - draft and final reports** _(b)_ - Draft sent to substantially interested States on request, on a confidentiality guarantee; 30 days or another agreed period for comments; comments considered before finalising; Final report on every VSMC submitted to IMO, and made available to the public
11. **Part III - the recommended practices** _(b)_ - Resources and qualified personnel; investigators appointed on the A.996(25) skills; Principles of investigation, coordination, evidence collection, confidentiality, witness protection; Investigation of casualties other than VSMC, and re-opening an investigation
12. **The definition** _(c)_ - A marine casualty involving the total loss of the ship; or a death; or severe damage to the environment
13. **Reading the definition against its neighbours** _(c)_ - Marine casualty - seven limbs, deliberate acts excluded; Marine incident - endangered, or would endanger if not corrected; Severe damage to the environment - a major deleterious effect, as evaluated by the State affected or the flag State
14. **Why the classification matters** _(c)_ - VSMC triggers the only MANDATORY duty to investigate, at chapter 6; and the mandatory submission of the report to IMO, at chapter 14.1; Below that threshold investigation is judgement, and reporting is conditional
