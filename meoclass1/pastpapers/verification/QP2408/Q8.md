**Technical ambiguities identified before drafting:**

- 'Hybrid turbocharger' and 'electro-assist turbocharger' overlap in industry usage and are sometimes used for the same machine. The distinction taken here - and it is the one that makes the stem's three-way split intelligible - is that the HYBRID is characterised by its GENERATING capability, absorbing surplus turbine power to make electrical power, while the ELECTRO-ASSIST is characterised by its MOTORING capability, adding power to the compressor where exhaust energy is insufficient. Many real machines can do both; the answer says so explicitly rather than pretending the categories are watertight, because a candidate who claims they are will be wrong about some installations.
- 'Power turbine generator' could describe a turbine geared into the propulsion shaft line rather than into a generator. Both arrangements exist and both recover the same energy. The answer takes the stem's word 'generator' as the primary case and notes the shaft-line alternative in one clause.
- Whether a power turbine's output is fed to the ship's main switchboard, to a shaft motor, or into a combined arrangement with a steam turbine driven by an exhaust gas boiler is an installation choice. The answer describes the options rather than asserting one.
- The auxiliary blower is the traditional answer to the low-load air deficit and it is not a development. It is mentioned as the baseline against which electro-assist should be judged, because that comparison is what shows the candidate understands the problem being solved.

**Answer route:**

1. **Why the turbocharger is where the gains now are** _intro_ - The largest single loss from a diesel engine leaves through the exhaust; Modern high-efficiency turbochargers need less of that energy than is available; The surplus is what all three devices exploit
2. **The common thread, and the division that organises the answer** _intro_ - All three put an electrical machine in the exhaust energy path; Two of them harvest surplus energy; one of them adds energy where there is a deficit; Recognising that division is the key to the whole answer
3. **What it is** _(a)_ - A separate exhaust gas turbine fed by gas bypassed around the turbocharger; It drives a generator, usually through reduction gearing; It is an additional machine, not a modification of the turbocharger
4. **How it is arranged and controlled** _(a)_ - Bypass valve, power turbine, gearbox, generator, and the electrical interface; Engaged only above the load at which surplus energy exists; May be combined with an exhaust gas boiler and steam turbine in a combined plant
5. **What it buys, and what it costs** _(a)_ - Electrical power produced with no additional fuel, so auxiliary engines can be stopped; Improves the ship's overall energy efficiency and her design and operational indices; Cost, weight, space, complexity, and no benefit at low load
6. **What it is** _(b)_ - A turbocharger with an electrical machine integrated on its own shaft; No separate turbine and no gas bypass to a second machine; One machine doing two jobs
7. **How it works in both directions** _(b)_ - As a generator, absorbing surplus turbine power at high load; As a motor, driving the compressor when there is not enough exhaust energy; Control decides which, according to load and transient demand
8. **What it buys, and what it costs** _(b)_ - Electrical power without fuel, plus low-load and transient support in one installation; Compact, with no gearbox and no separate turbine casing; The electrical machine runs at turbocharger speed in a hot, dirty environment
9. **What it is** _(c)_ - A turbocharger with an electric motor assisting the compressor; Primarily a motoring device rather than a generating one; Draws power from the ship rather than producing it
10. **The problem it solves** _(c)_ - At low load and during acceleration there is not enough exhaust energy; The result is low scavenge pressure, poor combustion, smoke and thermal loading; Auxiliary blowers do part of this job; the electric motor does it better
11. **What it buys, and what it costs** _(c)_ - Cleaner low-load running, faster response, and better slow steaming behaviour; Supports the derating and low-load operation that speed reduction demands; It consumes electrical power; the benefit is combustion quality, not direct generation
12. **Choosing between them** _all_ - Harvest surplus energy - power turbine and hybrid in generating mode; Supply a deficit - electro-assist and hybrid in motoring mode; Choose by the ship's operating profile, not by the technology
13. **What these developments mean for the operating engineer** _all_ - Exhaust-side cleanliness matters more, not less, because energy is being harvested; New failure modes: bypass valves, gearing, high-speed electrical machines, power electronics; Control interaction with the electrical plant and with the engine's certified configuration
14. **Where this fits in the wider picture** _all_ - Efficiency measured per unit of transport work, and now priced; These recover part of the largest loss without touching the combustion process; The gains are real but incremental, and they are load-profile dependent
