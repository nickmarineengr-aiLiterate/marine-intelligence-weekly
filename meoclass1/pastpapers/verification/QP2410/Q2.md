## Sources and provenance

- COMMERCIAL PRACTICE, settled and uncontroversial: the charterparty as a contract for the use of a ship or of her carrying capacity, distinct from the contract of carriage evidenced by a bill of lading, and distinct from a contract of sale of the goods. The four principal types - voyage charter, time charter, bareboat or demise charter, and the contract of affreightment - together with the trip time charter and consecutive voyage charter as intermediate forms.
- COMMERCIAL PRACTICE: the allocation of cost, time and operational control as the single axis that generates the types. Voyage - owner keeps operational control and bears voyage costs, charterer buys a carriage. Time - owner keeps possession and manning, charterer directs employment and pays for fuel and port charges. Bareboat - charterer takes possession and mans the ship, and is disponent owner for the period.
- COMMERCIAL PRACTICE: the standard clause families found in charterparties of all types - description of the vessel; laycan and cancelling; nomination and safe port or safe berth; laytime and demurrage in voyage charters; freight or hire and its payment; off-hire in time charters; bunkers on delivery and redelivery; employment and indemnity; deviation; cargo exclusions; war risks; ice; strikes; bills of lading; liens and cesser; general average and New Jason; both to blame collision; ISM and ISPS compliance; arbitration and choice of law.
- COMMERCIAL PRACTICE: the widely used standard forms are named ONLY as forms - GENCON for dry cargo voyage chartering, NYPE and BALTIME for time chartering, BARECON for bareboat chartering, SHELLTIME and the common tanker voyage forms in the oil trades - together with the practice of amending a printed form by rider clauses which prevail over the printed text where they conflict. NO CLAUSE NUMBER, NO CLAUSE WORDING AND NO ATTRIBUTION OF ANY PARTICULAR TERM TO ANY PARTICULAR FORM IS MADE. MIW holds none of these wordings.
- ENGINEERING AND OPERATIONAL JUDGEMENT: the Chief Engineer's direct interest in the charterparty - the speed and consumption warranty and how performance claims arise from it; the bunker specification and the quantity and price on delivery and redelivery; off-hire and what a breakdown costs the owner in hire; the fuel-quality and sulphur-content interface between the charterer's supply obligation and the ship's statutory compliance; and the machinery evidence that decides a performance or off-hire dispute - noon reports, logbooks, bunker delivery notes and MRV or DCS data.
