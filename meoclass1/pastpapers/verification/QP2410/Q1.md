## Sources and provenance

- INTERNAL REUSE VERIFIED, from MIW verification record QP2602/Q5 which read the provision: Merchant Shipping Act 1958, section 334 'Unseaworthy ship not to be sent to sea'. Sub-section (1) makes it an offence to send or attempt to send an Indian ship to sea from any port in India 'in such an unseaworthy state that the life of any person is likely to be thereby endangered', unless the person proves he 'used all reasonable means to insure her being sent to sea in a seaworthy state' or that sending her to sea in that state was 'under the circumstances, reasonable and justifiable'. Sub-section (2) applies the offence to a master who 'knowingly takes' such a ship to sea, with only the second defence. Sub-section (3) permits a person charged to give evidence. Sub-section (4) provides that no prosecution shall be instituted except by, or with the consent of, the Central Government. The section describes the state itself: a ship is unseaworthy when 'the materials of which she is made, her construction, qualifications of the master, the number, description and qualifications of the crew including officers, the weight, description and stowage of the cargo and ballast, the condition of her hull and equipment, boilers and machinery are not such as to render her in every respect fit for the proposed voyage or service'.
- INTERNAL REUSE VERIFIED, same record: Merchant Shipping Act 1958, section 335 'Obligation of owner to crew with respect to seaworthiness'. Sub-section (1) implies into every contract of service, express or implied, between the owner of an Indian ship and the master or any seaman, and into every contract of apprenticeship, notwithstanding any agreement to the contrary, an obligation that the owner, the master and every agent charged with loading the ship or preparing her for sea or sending her to sea shall use all reasonable means to ensure the seaworthiness of the ship for the voyage at the time when the voyage commences, and to keep her in a seaworthy state during the voyage. Sub-section (2) empowers the Central Government, at the owner's request or otherwise, to arrange a survey of the hull, equipment or machinery by a surveyor.
- INTERNAL REUSE VERIFIED, same record: Merchant Shipping Act 1958, section 336 'Power to detain unsafe ship and procedure for detention'. Sub-section (1) describes an unsafe ship as one which 'by reason of the defective condition of her hull, equipment or machinery, or by reason of overloading or improper loading, [is] unfit to proceed to sea without serious danger to human life, having regard to the nature of the service for which she is intended'. Clause (a) empowers the Central Government to order provisional detention for survey on complaint or otherwise; clause (b) requires a written statement of the grounds to be served forthwith on the master; clause (c) requires reference to a court of survey, or to a competent person appointed to survey and report, upon which the ship is released or finally detained.
