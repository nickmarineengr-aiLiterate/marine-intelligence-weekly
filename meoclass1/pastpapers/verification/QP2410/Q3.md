standards for ship manoeuvrability are real, well known and constantly paraphrased, and it would
have been simple to state a resolution number, an application threshold and an overshoot-angle

margin or light-running margin**, and **no numerical boundary of the layout diagram** - all are

## Sources and provenance

- ENGINEERING JUDGEMENT, settled naval architecture: directional or course stability as the property of a hull which, when disturbed from a straight course by a transient force and with the rudder returned amidships, settles onto a new straight course rather than continuing to turn. The distinction between straight-line stability, directional stability and positional motion stability, and the fact that stability is judged on the RESIDUAL MOTION after the disturbance ceases, not on the deviation during it.
- ENGINEERING JUDGEMENT, settled naval architecture: the hydrodynamic origin of course instability - a hull at a small drift angle generates a lateral force whose centre of pressure lies FORWARD of the centre of gravity, producing a moment that increases the drift angle; the ship is directionally stable only where the restoring moment from the after body and appendages exceeds that destabilising moment. Hence the role of longitudinal distribution of lateral area.
- ENGINEERING JUDGEMENT: the design conflict that is the substance of limb (A) - directional stability and manoeuvrability are OPPOSED. A long, fine, deeply skegged hull holds a course and turns slowly; a short, full-bodied hull with a cut-up afterbody turns readily and wanders. Length to beam ratio, block coefficient, trim, and the longitudinal distribution of lateral area are the parameters, and the choice is a trade-off made for the ship's intended service.
- ENGINEERING JUDGEMENT: how the attributes are provided - hull form and proportions; skeg, deadwood and after body form; trim by the stern; the rudder as the primary control surface, with area, aspect ratio, section and type (spade, semi-balanced horn, high-lift flap and steerable nozzle) determining lift and stall angle; twin skegs and twin rudders; propeller-rudder interaction and the rudder working in the propeller race; bow and stern thrusters as low-speed devices whose effectiveness falls away with headway; azimuth and podded units; and the autopilot with rate and counter-rudder terms as the closed-loop element that makes a marginally stable ship usable.
- ENGINEERING JUDGEMENT, settled marine engineering: the engine layout and load diagram - power against speed for a slow-speed two-stroke, bounded by the maximum mean effective pressure line, the maximum speed line, the constant-torque or mean-effective-pressure limit at low speed, and the overload region; the layout points L1 to L4 defining the field within which the specific engine is laid out; MCR and the selected service or continuous service rating; and the torque-limit and scavenge-air-pressure-limit curves that constrain acceleration.
- ENGINEERING JUDGEMENT: the propeller demand curve - for a fixed-pitch propeller in free-running conditions the delivered power varies approximately as the CUBE of shaft speed and the torque as the SQUARE, so the demand curve is a cubic through the origin whose position depends on the propeller and on the resistance of the hull. Its migration with fouling, added displacement, shallow water, heavy weather, towing and ice - all of which move the curve to the LEFT, so more torque is demanded at a given speed. The light-running condition of a clean, trial-condition ship moving the curve to the RIGHT.
- ENGINEERING JUDGEMENT: the relation the stem asks for - the propeller is designed to absorb the selected service power at the selected service speed, and the engine is rated above that point. The gap is deliberately made up of MARGINS: a sea margin covering the added resistance of fouling and weather; an engine or operating margin keeping the service point below the maximum continuous rating; and a light-running margin so that the propeller is deliberately slightly light when new and clean, allowing the curve to migrate towards the torque limit through the docking cycle without the engine becoming overloaded. NO PERCENTAGE IS QUOTED for any of these.
- ENGINEERING JUDGEMENT: the consequences of mismatch - a heavy-running or torque-rich engine reaching its torque limit before its rated speed, so the rated power cannot be developed; high thermal loading, high exhaust temperature, fouled turbocharging and scavenge fires; loss of speed and increased consumption; and the converse light-running case, in which the engine overspeeds before it is fully loaded and the ship never reaches her design power.
- REGIME LEVEL ONLY, NO NUMBER ASSERTED: IMO has adopted standards for ship manoeuvrability, applicable to ships above a stated size, tested by defined trial manoeuvres - turning circle, zig-zag, stopping and initial turning ability - and classification societies set requirements for steering gear and may assign optional manoeuvrability notations. THE RESOLUTION NUMBER, THE CIRCULAR NUMBER, THE SIZE THRESHOLD AND EVERY NUMERICAL CRITERION ARE DELIBERATELY NOT ASSERTED: MIW holds neither the standards nor the class rules, and an invented criterion would be a far worse defect than a declared gap.
