## Sources and provenance

- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED, READ AT SOURCE THIS SESSION from the corpus copy of the Hong Kong International Convention for the Safe and Environmentally Sound Recycling of Ships, 2009: ARTICLE 17, Entry into force. The Convention enters into force 24 months after the date on which three conditions are met - not less than 15 States have signed without reservation or deposited the requisite instrument; the combined merchant fleets of those States constitute not less than 40 per cent of the gross tonnage of the world's merchant shipping; and their combined maximum annual ship recycling volume during the preceding 10 years constitutes not less than 3 per cent of the combined gross tonnage of those same States. Article 17.2 and 17.3 govern instruments deposited after the conditions are met and after entry into force respectively.
- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED, same reading: ARTICLE 2, definitions - 'Ship' includes submersibles, floating craft, floating platforms, self-elevating platforms, FSUs and FPSOs, including a vessel stripped of equipment or being towed; 'Ship Recycling' is complete or partial dismantling at a Ship Recycling Facility to recover components and materials for reprocessing and re-use; 'Competent Authority' is the governmental authority designated by a Party for duties relating to Ship Recycling Facilities in its jurisdiction; 'Administration' is the flag State government.
- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED, same reading: ARTICLE 3, Application. The Convention applies to ships entitled to fly the flag of a Party or operating under its authority, and to Ship Recycling Facilities operating under the jurisdiction of a Party. Article 3.2 excludes warships and government non-commercial ships. ARTICLE 3.3 provides that the Convention shall NOT apply to ships of less than 500 GT, or to ships operating throughout their life only in waters subject to the sovereignty or jurisdiction of the flag State - each Party nonetheless ensuring such ships act consistently with the Convention so far as reasonable and practicable. ARTICLE 3.4 requires Parties to apply the requirements to non-Party ships as necessary to ensure NO MORE FAVOURABLE TREATMENT.
- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED, same reading: ANNEX regulation 4 and APPENDIX 1, Controls of Hazardous Materials. Appendix 1 lists asbestos (new installation prohibited for all ships); ozone-depleting substances (new installations prohibited, HCFCs permitted until 1 January 2020); polychlorinated biphenyls (new installation prohibited for all ships); and anti-fouling compounds and systems 'regulated under Annex I to the International Convention on the Control of Harmful Anti-fouling Systems on Ships, 2001 (AFS Convention) IN FORCE AT THE TIME OF APPLICATION OR INTERPRETATION of this Annex' - so later AFS listings are caught dynamically by reference rather than being named in Appendix 1.
- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED, same reading: ANNEX regulation 5, Inventory of Hazardous Materials. Each new ship shall have one on board, verified by the Administration or an authorised organisation, identifying as PART I the Appendix 1 and 2 materials in the ship's structure or equipment with their location and approximate quantities. Regulation 5.2 - existing ships shall comply as far as practicable NOT LATER THAN 5 YEARS AFTER ENTRY INTO FORCE, or before going for recycling if earlier. Regulation 5.3 - Part I maintained and updated throughout the operational life. Regulation 5.4 - prior to recycling the Inventory shall incorporate PART II for operationally generated wastes and PART III for stores, and be verified.
- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED, same reading: ANNEX regulations 8 and 9. Regulation 8 - ships destined to be recycled shall only be recycled at authorised facilities; shall minimise cargo residues, remaining fuel oil and wastes before arrival; a tanker shall arrive with cargo tanks and pump rooms ready for certification as Safe-for-entry or Safe-for-hot work or both; shall provide the facility with all available information; shall complete the regulation 5 Inventory; and shall be certified as ready for recycling before any recycling activity. Regulation 9 - a SHIP-SPECIFIC SHIP RECYCLING PLAN developed by the facility, in a language the authorising Party accepts, covering the establishment, maintenance and monitoring of Safe-for-entry and Safe-for-hot work conditions and the management of the inventoried materials, and either explicitly or tacitly approved by the Competent Authority - tacit approval carrying a 14-DAY REVIEW PERIOD after a written acknowledgement of receipt issued within three working days.
- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED, same reading: ANNEX regulations 10 and 11, surveys and certificates. Four surveys - INITIAL before the ship is put in service or before the certificate is issued; RENEWAL at intervals not exceeding five years; ADDITIONAL after change, replacement or significant repair; and a FINAL SURVEY before the ship is taken out of service and before recycling starts, verifying the regulation 5.4 Inventory, that the Ship Recycling Plan properly reflects it and covers Safe-for-entry and Safe-for-hot work conditions, and that the facility holds a valid authorisation. Regulation 11 - an INTERNATIONAL CERTIFICATE ON INVENTORY OF HAZARDOUS MATERIALS after initial or renewal survey; and, per regulation 8.6 and the final survey, certification as ready for recycling.
- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED, same reading: ANNEX chapter 3, regulations 15 to 25 - the requirements for Ship Recycling Facilities. Regulation 15 - each Party shall legislate, establish an authorisation mechanism, and establish inspection, monitoring and enforcement including POWERS OF ENTRY AND SAMPLING, and designate Competent Authorities. Regulation 16 - authorisation by the Competent Authority including verification of documentation and a SITE INSPECTION, drawn up in the form of Appendix 5, VALID FOR A PERIOD NOT EXCEEDING FIVE YEARS, suspended or withdrawn if inspection is refused. Regulation 18 - the SHIP RECYCLING FACILITY PLAN, adopted by the board or governing body of the Recycling Company, covering policy, implementation, roles and responsibilities, worker information and training, an emergency preparedness and response plan, performance monitoring, record-keeping, and reporting of discharges, emissions, incidents, accidents and occupational disease. Regulation 19 - procedures to establish, maintain and monitor Safe-for-hot work and Safe-for-entry conditions throughout recycling. Regulation 20 - hazardous materials identified, labelled, packaged and REMOVED TO THE MAXIMUM EXTENT POSSIBLE PRIOR TO CUTTING, with wastes kept separate and transferred only to an authorised waste management facility. Regulations 22 and 23 - worker safety and training, and reporting on incidents, accidents, occupational diseases and chronic effects.
- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED, same reading: ARTICLES 10, 11 and 12. Article 10 - violations prohibited by national law, sanctions established under the law of the Administration for a ship and of the Party having jurisdiction for a facility, and 'adequate in severity to discourage violations of this Convention wherever they occur'. Article 11 - all possible efforts to avoid undue detention or delay, with compensation for loss or damage where it occurs. Article 12 - each Party reports to IMO, for dissemination, its list of authorised Ship Recycling Facilities, its Competent Authority contact point, its recognised organisations and nominated surveyors, and an annual list of its ships issued with a Ready for Recycling Certificate.
- INTERNAL REUSE VERIFIED, from MIW verification record QP2511/Q8, whose author read the IMO's own briefing: the Convention was adopted at a diplomatic conference in Hong Kong, China in MAY 2009 and ENTERED INTO FORCE ON 26 JUNE 2025. That date is the single load-bearing fact in this answer not read at source here. Applying Article 17.1 to it, the three conditions were therefore met twenty-four months earlier, in JUNE 2023 - stated in this answer as DERIVED, not as read.
- INTERNAL REUSE VERIFIED, same record: India deposited its instrument of accession with the Secretary-General on 28 NOVEMBER 2019, through the Director General of Shipping, during the thirty-first session of the IMO Assembly, bringing the required number of States to fifteen and contributing materially to the recycling-volume criterion. India gives the Convention domestic effect through the RECYCLING OF SHIPS ACT, 2019 and the RECYCLING OF SHIPS RULES, 2019, with the maritime administration as competent authority for the authorisation of yards.
- P3 INDUSTRY GUIDANCE AND CONTEXT: the EU Ship Recycling Regulation maintains its own list of approved facilities on terms of its own, and non-governmental scrutiny of South Asian yards has continued for years. Used to establish that Convention compliance and European acceptance are distinct standards - a proposition about two regimes, not a claim about any yard.
