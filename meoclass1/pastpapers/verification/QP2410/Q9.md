## What the verification actually turned on

The examiner has **left out the condition that decides the case**. Rule VII requires the ship to be
*"ashore **and in a position of peril**"*, and the printed facts say only *"A large vessel ran
aground"*. Aground is not the same as in peril: a vessel lying safely on soft mud in fine weather
awaiting the tide is not in peril, and her burnt bearings would be the owner's own particular
average.

The correct answer is therefore a **qualified yes**, and the qualification is where the marks are.
The answer identifies peril as the condition the claim turns on and says what facts would decide it,
and it states the converse Rule VII expressly provides: **where the ship is afloat, such damage is
"in no circumstances" allowed**.

## Sources and provenance

- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED, via MIW verification record QP2404/Q6 which read the text: York-Antwerp Rules 2016, Comite Maritime International text. RULE OF INTERPRETATION - 'In the adjustment of general average the following Rules shall apply to the exclusion of any law and practice inconsistent therewith. Except as provided by the Rule Paramount and the numbered Rules, general average shall be adjusted according to the lettered Rules.' RULE PARAMOUNT - 'In no case shall there be any allowance for sacrifice or expenditure unless reasonably made or incurred.'
- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED, same record: York-Antwerp Rules 2016, RULE A.1 - 'There is a general average act when, and only when, any extraordinary sacrifice or expenditure is intentionally and reasonably made or incurred for the common safety for the purpose of preserving from peril the property involved in a common maritime adventure.' RULE A.2 - 'General average sacrifices and expenditures shall be borne by the different contributing interests on the basis hereinafter provided.'
- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED, same record: RULE C.1 - 'Only such losses, damages or expenses which are the direct consequence of the general average act shall be allowed as general average.' Rule C.2 excludes allowance for damage to the environment or in consequence of the escape or release of pollutant substances. RULE C.3 - 'Demurrage, loss of market, and any loss or damage sustained or expense incurred by reason of delay, whether on the voyage or subsequently, and any indirect loss whatsoever, shall not be allowed as general average.'
- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED, same record: RULE D - 'Rights to contribution in general average shall not be affected, though the event which gave rise to the sacrifice or expenditure may have been due to the fault of one of the parties to the common maritime adventure, but this shall not prejudice any remedies or defences which may be open against or to that party in respect of such fault.' RULE E.1 - 'The onus of proof is upon the party claiming in general average to show that the loss or expense claimed is properly allowable as general average.' Rules E.2 and E.3 require particulars of value and notice in writing to the average adjuster, with a twelve-month period after which the adjuster may estimate.
- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED, same record, READ VERBATIM AND DECISIVE FOR LIMB (b): York-Antwerp Rules 2016, RULE VII, Damage to Machinery and Boilers - 'Damage caused to any machinery and boilers of a ship which is ashore and in a position of peril, in endeavouring to refloat, shall be allowed in general average when shown to have arisen from an actual intention to float the ship for the common safety at the risk of such damage; but where a ship is afloat no loss or damage caused by working the propelling machinery and boilers shall in any circumstances be allowed as general average.'
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY, same record: comparison of the published 1994, 2004 and 2016 texts of the York-Antwerp Rules confirms that RULE VII IS MATERIALLY IDENTICAL IN ALL THREE. This matters because the Rules take effect by contractual incorporation in the bill of lading or charterparty rather than as law, so the version in force between the parties may not be the latest; on this point the answer is the same whichever version is incorporated.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY, same record: general average is also recognised in Indian statute through the MARINE INSURANCE ACT, 1963, which contains a statutory definition of a general average loss corresponding to that in the English Marine Insurance Act 1906. NO SECTION NUMBER IS ASSERTED - see unresolved.
- ENGINEERING JUDGEMENT: the mechanism by which working a main engine ahead and astern against a grounded hull overloads the main and crankpin bearings - sustained high torque at low or zero shaft speed, repeated reversals, loss of hydrodynamic oil film at low rotational speed, and restricted cooling water flow if the intakes are silted. This is what makes the master's acceptance of the risk a real and identifiable decision rather than a legal fiction.
