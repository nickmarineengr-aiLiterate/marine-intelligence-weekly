# QP2602 Q6 - verification record

**Paper:** QP2602, February 2026, Engineering Management, MEO Class I.
**Built:** 2026-08-08. **State:** Pilot Review Ready.

**Question.** "General average is based on certain essential features" - discuss (8); whether main
engine bearing damage sustained working the engine ahead and astern to refloat a grounded vessel can
be included in general average, with justification (8).

## Sources actually read

| Source | Class | What it established |
|---|---|---|
| **York-Antwerp Rules 2016** (CMI) - **full text read** | P1 | **Rule A.1** verbatim: "There is a general average act when, and only when, any extraordinary sacrifice or expenditure is intentionally and reasonably made or incurred for the common safety for the purpose of preserving from peril the property involved in a common maritime adventure." **Rule Paramount**: "In no case shall there be any allowance for sacrifice or expenditure unless reasonably made or incurred." **Rule C.1** direct consequence only; C.2 no allowance for environmental damage or pollutant escape; C.3 excludes demurrage, loss of market, delay and indirect loss. **Rule D** fault does not affect rights to contribution, without prejudice to remedies. **Rule E.1** onus on the party claiming; E.3 adjuster may estimate after 12 months. |
| **York-Antwerp Rules 2016 Rule VII** - Damage to Machinery and Boilers - **read verbatim** | P1 | "Damage caused to any machinery and boilers of a ship which is ashore and in a position of peril, in endeavouring to refloat, shall be allowed in general average when shown to have arisen from an actual intention to float the ship for the common safety at the risk of such damage; but where a ship is afloat no loss or damage caused by working the propelling machinery and boilers shall in any circumstances be allowed as general average." |
| York-Antwerp Rules 2016 Rule VIII | P1 | Lightening a ship when ashore - the neighbouring allowance. |
| QP2601 Q3 and QP2607 Q5 | Internal reuse verified | GA doctrine, the intentional-sacrifice principle and the adjuster's role. |

## The decisive finding

**Rule VII is directly dispositive of limb (b)** and every element of the printed scenario maps onto
a phrase in it: *ran aground* to "ashore"; *main engine used ahead and astern to shake the vessel
loose* to "in endeavouring to refloat"; *bearings got burnt* to "damage caused to any machinery".

The answer is therefore **yes, allowable, subject to proof**, and the conditions to be proved are
taken from the rule itself: ashore (satisfied on the facts), **in a position of peril** (not stated
in the question - treated as conditional), and an **actual intention to float the ship for the common
safety at the risk of such damage** (also not stated - treated as conditional and evidential).

The **afloat proviso** is recorded because it is half the rule and the natural oral follow-up.

## Reasoning recorded

- The scenario deliberately omits peril and intention. Assuming them would have produced a
  confident wrong-shaped answer. They are handled as conditions with the evidence needed to prove
  them (log entries, engine records, master's recorded assessment) - which is also what "give proper
  justification" asks for.
- Rule D is included because candidates routinely believe negligent grounding defeats GA.

## Flags

- **C_ACCEPTED_LIMITATION** - the YAR apply by **contractual incorporation**, so the applicable
  edition depends on the bill of lading or charterparty. Rule VII is materially unchanged across the
  1994, 2004 and 2016 editions, so the conclusion does not turn on it; the answer states the
  dependency rather than asserting an edition.
- Marine Insurance Act 1963 (India) s.66 is cited as a pointer to the Indian statutory position and
  was **not** read in the statute; the answer does not rest on its wording.
