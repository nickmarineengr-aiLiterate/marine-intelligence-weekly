**Question.** As per UNCLOS the sovereign rights and jurisdictions applicable to the coastal State

## The decisive reasoning

The scenario is drafted to invite the answer "Indian law, because it happened in India's EEZ". That
is wrong. The EEZ is **not Indian territory**; India's rights there are functional (resources,
artificial islands, marine scientific research, marine environment) and none of them reaches a person
missing from a ship. Because **Art 58** carries **Art 92** into the EEZ, shipboard matters remain
subject to the **exclusive jurisdiction of the flag State**.

**The paper does not state the vessel's flag.** The answer therefore handles both cases explicitly -
Indian flag (MSA 1958 ss.358, 359, 452 and BNS 2023 s.1(5) apply in full) and foreign flag (flag
State law governs investigation and prosecution; India retains SAR responsibility regardless of
flag under Art 98 and the SAR Convention). Choosing one flag and answering only that would have been
the single easiest way to fail this limb.

## Currency findings

- **The Indian Penal Code is no longer the applicable penal statute.** BNS 2023 replaced it on 1 July
  2024. Citing the IPC would be a superseded-instrument error of the same family as the others
  recorded across this series.
- As with Q5, the **MSA 1958 was in force at the sitting** and was repealed with effect from 15 March
  2026.
