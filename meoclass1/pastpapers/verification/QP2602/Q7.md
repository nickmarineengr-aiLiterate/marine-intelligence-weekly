# QP2602 Q7 - verification record

**Paper:** QP2602, February 2026, Engineering Management, MEO Class I.
**Built:** 2026-08-08. **State:** Pilot Review Ready.

**Question.** The procedure of entry into force of an IMO convention after adoption; the provision
and its importance; for a convention of important technical nature the general rules/conditions
observed by States; and the terms (i) Accession (ii) Signature subject to Ratification, acceptance
or approval. (16)

## Sources actually read

| Source | Class | What it established |
|---|---|---|
| IMO conventions page - the treaty process | P1 | Adoption by an IMO organ or a diplomatic conference. Consent expressed by signature alone where provided; by signature **subject to ratification, acceptance or approval**, where signature does not bind and a formal instrument must later be deposited; or by **accession** for a State that did not sign. Records the historical rationale (slow communications, representatives exceeding authority) and the modern one (time to enact domestic legislation and satisfy constitutional requirements). Quotes **VCLT Art 14(2)**: consent "expressed by acceptance or approval under conditions similar to those which apply to ratification". **VCLT Art 15** permits accession where the treaty so provides. |
| IMO conventions page - entry into force and amendment | P1 | Conventions bind only after formal acceptance. Early conventions typically required two-thirds of contracting States, occasionally needing more acceptances for entry into force than for amendment. Conventions of an important technical nature set higher requirements involving both a number of States and a percentage of world merchant fleet gross tonnage. **Tacit acceptance**: for SOLAS 1974, an amendment to the technical annexes is "deemed to have been accepted at the end of two years from the date on which it is communicated to Contracting Governments" unless objected to by **more than one-third of Contracting Governments** or by Governments owning **at least 50 per cent of world gross merchant tonnage**. Amendments now enter into force in about **18 to 24 months**; 1960 SOLAS amendments adopted **1966-1973** never received sufficient acceptances. |
| Convention summaries | P2 | **SOLAS 1974** adopted 1 Nov 1974, in force **25 May 1980**, condition **25 States** whose combined merchant fleets are not less than **50 per cent** of world merchant shipping gross tonnage. **1978 MARPOL Protocol** in force twelve months after **fifteen States** constituting not less than **fifty per cent** of world gross tonnage had become Parties. |

## Reasoning recorded

- **Ratification, acceptance and approval are legally equivalent in effect**, differing only in
  domestic constitutional form. The answer says so explicitly rather than inventing distinctions -
  recorded as an ambiguity in the decomposition gate because candidates commonly invent them.
- **Entry into force of a convention and of an amendment are different processes** with different
  thresholds. The question's structure invites conflating them; the route separates them into steps
  4-5 and step 6.
- Article numbers for the entry-into-force provisions are given **by name and effect** where they
  could not be confirmed against the convention texts, in preference to asserting a number.
