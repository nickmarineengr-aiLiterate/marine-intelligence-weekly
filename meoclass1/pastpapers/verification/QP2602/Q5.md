# QP2602 Q5 - verification record

**Paper:** QP2602, February 2026, Engineering Management, MEO Class I.
**Built:** 2026-08-08. **State:** Pilot Review Ready.

**Question.** Unseaworthy vessels within the meaning of the MSA 1958 as amended; the difference
between unseaworthy and unsafe ships; the obligations of owners to crew with respect to
seaworthiness. (16)

## Sources actually read

| Source | Class | What it established |
|---|---|---|
| **MSA 1958 s.334** "Unseaworthy ship not to be sent to sea" - **read in full** | P1 | s.334(1) offence of sending/attempting to send an Indian ship to sea from an Indian port "in such an unseaworthy state that the life of any person is likely to be thereby endangered", with defences of having "used all reasonable means to insure her being sent to sea in a seaworthy state" or that it was "reasonable and justifiable". s.334(2) master who "knowingly takes" her to sea, second defence only. s.334(3) evidence. s.334(4) prosecution only by or with Central Government consent. Definition: materials, construction, qualifications of the master, number/description/qualifications of the crew including officers, weight/description/stowage of cargo and ballast, condition of hull and equipment, boilers and machinery "not such as to render her in every respect fit for the proposed voyage or service". |
| **MSA 1958 s.335** "Obligation of owner to crew with respect to seaworthiness" - **read in full** | P1 | Implied into every contract of service and apprenticeship, "notwithstanding any agreement to the contrary", that owner, master and every agent charged with loading / preparing / sending to sea "shall use all reasonable means to ensure the seaworthiness of such ship for the voyage at the time when such voyage commences, and to keep her in a seaworthy state during the voyage". s.335(2) Central Government may arrange a survey. |
| **MSA 1958 s.336** "Power to detain unsafe ship and procedure for detention" - **read in full** | P1 | Unsafe ship = one which "by reason of the defective condition of her hull, equipment or machinery, or by reason of overloading or improper loading, [is] unfit to proceed to sea without serious danger to human life, having regard to the nature of the service for which she is intended". Provisional detention for survey; (a) order on complaint or otherwise; (b) written statement of grounds served forthwith on the master; (c) reference to the court of survey or a competent person, then release or final detention. |

## The sitting-date finding

**The MSA 1958 was in force on the examination date.** It was repealed with effect from **15 March
2026**, about five weeks after this paper was sat. The answer is therefore given on the 1958 Act and
must not be re-written onto the 2025 Act. The study guide carries an explicit current note.

This is the second time the QP2607 Q7 Gazette work has paid for itself on a later paper.

## Reasoning recorded

- The Act gives **no definitions-section entry** for "unseaworthy"; the description sits inside
  s.334 itself. Stated rather than glossed over.
- ss.334 and 335 are expressed for an **Indian ship**; s.336 for a ship in a port to which the
  Central Government has specially extended the section. The answer does not overstate the reach of
  any of the three.
