## 1. What the question demands

One limb, sixteen marks, and a five-part printed checklist: the purpose and significance of the Noise Code; its key provisions including the maximum allowable noise limits, the areas of application and the measures to reduce noise; the impact of noise on crew health AND performance; and the compliance requirements for ship design, equipment and operational practices. Each is a separate block of marks and the discriminator throughout is numerical precision.

Required answer components, as decomposed before drafting:

- What the Noise Code is, which resolution adopted it, and what made it mandatory
- The purpose of the Code, in its own words, and its three-part rationale
- Areas of application - which ships, from which dates, and which ship types are excluded
- The maximum allowable noise limits, by space and by ship size
- Noise exposure limits - the Lex(24) criterion, the 85 dB(A) threshold and the peak limit
- Measures to reduce noise on board - at source, on the path, and at the receiver
- The impact of noise on crew health and on performance, and why the Code separates the two
- Compliance requirements for design, equipment and operational practice - the noise survey report, acoustic insulation, warning notices and the SMS link

## 3. The finding that mattered

This is the quietest question on the paper and the finding is that its quietness was verified rather than assumed. MSC.337(91) is unamended at this sitting and nothing in 2024 touched it - which matters precisely because the paper anchor establishes that three other questions on this paper DID move during 2024. The substantive finding is a distinction inside the instrument that most material misses: although the Code is legally treated as a MANDATORY instrument under SOLAS, paragraph 1.1.3 lists provisions that remain recommendatory, and THE WHOLE OF CHAPTER 5 - the noise exposure limits - is on that list, together with 1.3.2, 1.3.3, 3.4.2, 3.4.3, section 6.3, section 7.3 and appendices 2 to 4. So chapter 4 caps the space mandatorily while chapter 5 caps the person recommendatorily. A second finding: the three APPLICATION DATES are in SOLAS regulation II-1/3-12.1 and not in the Code at all, which is why answers that quote only the 1,600 GT threshold are incomplete; and regulation II-1/3-12.2 imposes a real machinery-noise duty on the 2009-to-2015 build window, so older ships are not simply exempt. MSC.338(91) also DELETED the former regulation II-1/36 - the new regulation replaced a general exhortation rather than merely adding to it.

## 9. Answer route as built

**1. What the Noise Code is, and what made it mandatory** - limb `framing`

- annex to resolution MSC.337(91), adopted 30 November 2012
- made mandatory by MSC.338(91) inserting SOLAS regulation II-1/3-12, in force 1 July 2014
- II-1/3-12.3 - ships shall be constructed to reduce noise in accordance with the Code
- the same amendment DELETED the former SOLAS regulation II-1/36
- predecessor was the recommendatory A.468(XII) of 1981
- para 1.1.3 - the Code is mandatory but specified provisions remain recommendatory, INCLUDING ALL OF CHAPTER 5

**2. Purpose and significance** - limb `main`

- para 1.2.1 - safe working conditions: speech communication, hearing audible alarms, clear-headed decisions in control stations
- para 1.2.2 - protection from noise-induced hearing loss
- para 1.2.3 - acceptable comfort in rest and recreation spaces, and conditions for RECUPERATION
- note the ordering - safety first, then health, then habitability
- para 1.1.1 - the Code is a DESIGN standard, verified by sea trials and a Noise Survey Report
- ongoing compliance through training and the SOLAS chapter IX processes

**3. Areas of application** - limb `main`

- para 1.3.1 - new ships of 1,600 GT and above
- SOLAS II-1/3-12.1 - contract on or after 1 July 2014; OR keel on or after 1 January 2015; OR delivery on or after 1 July 2018
- II-1/3-12.2 - ships delivered before 1 July 2018 with keels laid 1 Jan 2009 to 1 Jan 2015: reduce machinery noise, insulate or isolate, refuge from noise, ear protectors
- 1.3.2 and 1.3.3 - MAY be applied to existing ships and to new ships under 1,600 GT (recommendatory)
- 1.3.4 - eleven excluded types: DSC, HSC, fishing vessels, pipe-laying barges, crane barges, MODUs, pleasure yachts not in trade, warships and troopships, non-mechanically propelled, pile drivers, dredgers
- 1.3.5 in port or at sea with seafarers on board; 1.3.8 not passenger spaces except as work spaces
- 1.3.9 major repairs and alterations; 1.3.6 dispensations, NOT for cabins unless exceptional
- 1.3.10 - ship sources only; NOT wind, wave, ice, alarms or public address

**4. Maximum allowable noise limits - chapter 4** - limb `main`

- 4.1.1 - these are MAXIMA, not desirable levels
- 4.1.2 - assessed before the ship is put in service
- machinery spaces 110; machinery control rooms 75; workshops and non-specified work spaces 85
- navigating bridge and chartrooms 65; look-out posts and bridge wings 70; radio rooms 60; radar rooms 65
- cabins and hospitals 60 / 55; messrooms, recreation rooms and offices 65 / 60; open recreation areas 75
- galleys, serveries and pantries 75; normally unoccupied spaces 90
- ONLY accommodation limits differ by ship size - 5 dB(A) tighter at 10,000 GT and above
- 4.1.3 - the 110 dB(A) limit ASSUMES hearing protectors are worn; above 85 dB(A) protectors required

**5. Noise exposure limits - chapter 5** - limb `main`

- 5.1.1 - the chapter 4 limits are designed to deliver an Lex(24) not exceeding 80 dB(A)
- 5.1.2 - above 85 dB(A), protection or time limits
- 5.1.4 - no unprotected exposure to peaks above 135 dB(C)
- 5.3.1 - even WITH protectors, not above 120 dB(A) or Leq(24) 105 dB(A)
- 5.3.5 - unprotected below 85 dB(A) for under eight hours; over eight hours Leq(24) 80 dB(A); a third of each 24 hours below 75 dB(A)
- 5.4 - alternative: no unprotected seafarer above Leq(24) 80 dB(A); four hours continuous or eight hours total in protector spaces
- 5.5 - hearing conservation programme: audiometric tests, instruction, records, periodic analysis
- zones A to E; to work in A-D protectors must bring the ear below 85 dB(A)
- REMEMBER chapter 5 is recommendatory

**6. Measures to reduce noise on board** - limb `main`

- 7.1 - protectors are NOT a substitute for effective noise control; control at source first
- SOURCE - quieter machinery, resilient mounting, balancing and alignment, flexible couplings, silencers, propeller design and clearance
- PATH - acoustic enclosure, insulation and damping, floating floors, avoiding structure-borne short circuits
- PATH - layout: site cabins and hospitals away from casings, machinery, uptakes and mooring machinery
- RECEIVER - machinery control room as refuge; the II-1/3-12.2 refuge from noise; protectors under chapter 7
- RECEIVER - administrative: rotation, four/eight hour limits, scheduling noisy work
- appendix 3 - suggested methods of attenuating noise (recommendatory)

**7. Impact on crew health and on performance** - limb `main`

- HEALTH - noise-induced hearing loss, originating in the cochlea (1.4.24); cumulative, painless, IRREVERSIBLE
- HEALTH - acoustic trauma from peaks above 135 dB(C)
- HEALTH - disturbed sleep, impaired recuperation, fatigue, stress and cardiovascular strain
- PERFORMANCE - masking of speech communication
- PERFORMANCE - masking of audible alarms
- PERFORMANCE - degraded clear-headed decision-making in control stations, navigation and radio spaces
- PERFORMANCE - reduced alertness on the following watch; link to fatigue and MLC Regulation 4.3
- PERFORMANCE - loss of diagnostic machinery sound cues when protectors are worn
- the tie: a noisy ship loses hearing over a career AND misses an alarm tonight

**8. Compliance requirements - design, equipment and operation** - limb `main`

- DESIGN - predict, arrange, specify to chapter 4 limits; chapter 6 acoustic insulation
- chapter 6 - Rw 35 cabin to cabin; Rw 45 public spaces to cabins and hospitals; Rw 30 corridor to cabin; Rw 30 cabin to cabin with communicating door
- 6.2.2 laboratory test to ISO 10140-2; 6.3.2 field R'w tolerance up to 3 dB
- CONSTRUCTION - measurement under chapter 3 conditions; sea trials; Noise Survey Report before service
- 4.3 - one report per ship, reading at each point, points marked on a general arrangement plan, appendix 1 format
- 4.3.3 - the report shall ALWAYS be carried on board and be accessible for the crew
- EQUIPMENT - 7.2.2 protectors to reduce to 85 dB(A) or less, selected by the HML method of ISO 4869-2:1994
- EQUIPMENT - 7.4 warning notices above 85 dB(A); bands 80-85, 85-110, 110-115, above 115
- OPERATION - 5.1.3 an SMS section on hearing protection policy, exposure limits and training, logged in training records
