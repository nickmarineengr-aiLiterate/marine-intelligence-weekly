The danger on this question is diffuse rather than pointed, and that is itself the finding: there is no hard date in the stem, so the natural failure mode is to describe maritime decarbonisation as it stands whenever the answer is written. Three positions were fixed at the sitting date rather than inherited. Second, NO mid-term measure had been adopted; MEPC 82, held 30 September to 4 October 2024, carried that work forward and referred the review of the short-term measures onward to MEPC 83 in 2025. Third, and most valuable: the HONG KONG CONVENTION WAS NOT YET IN FORCE. Its entry-into-force criteria were met in June 2023, when Bangladesh and Liberia became Contracting States, and it enters into force twenty-four months later on 26 JUNE 2025 - SIX MONTHS AFTER this examination. A sitting that falls six months before a convention's entry into force is a concrete, current illustration of the adopted / ratified / in force distinction, and it is exactly what a candidate revising from later material would get wrong.

## 9. Answer route as built

**1. What the Sustainable Development Goals are** - limb `main`

- the core of the 2030 Agenda, UN General Assembly resolution A/RES/70/1
- adopted 25 September 2015, in effect 1 January 2016; successor to the Millennium Development Goals
- 17 Goals and 169 targets, with a global indicator framework
- five areas - People, Planet, Prosperity, Peace, Partnership
- the central pledge to LEAVE NO ONE BEHIND
- balancing the economic, social and environmental dimensions
- integrated and indivisible; global in nature and UNIVERSALLY applicable - the break from the MDGs

**2. The role of the SDGs in promoting global sustainability** - limb `main`

- THEY ARE NOT A TREATY - no legal obligation and no enforcement; so the question is how they work at all
- a common language and framework, giving comparability across sectors and borders
- universality - every State, not only developing States
- integration - forcing recognition of trade-offs and co-benefits
- measurement - 169 targets and an indicator framework turn aspiration into something auditable
- accountability by TRANSPARENCY - High-Level Political Forum and voluntary national reviews, not sanction
- mobilisation of finance and the private sector - ESG, sustainable finance, charterers' requirements: the mechanism with real bite for shipping
- HONEST WEAKNESS - non-binding means uneven delivery and SDG-washing; in this sector the OBLIGATIONS come from IMO conventions

**3. Why the maritime sector matters, and how IMO aligns** - limb `main`

- shipping is the dominant carrier of world trade by volume
- a material source of GHG and air emissions, a vector for invasive species, a source of marine pollution, and a global employer
- a transboundary sector no single State can regulate - which is why it sits with IMO
- IMO has mapped its work to the Goals and identifies SDG 14 as closest to its mandate
- STATE WHICH TWO GOALS YOU HAVE CHOSEN AND WHY - SDG 13 and 14 have dedicated IMO instruments behind them

**4. SDG 13 Climate Action - relevance to shipping** - limb `main`

- SDG 13 - take urgent action to combat climate change and its impacts
- shipping as a SOURCE - most carbon-efficient per tonne-km, but material in absolute terms
- transboundary, so left to IMO rather than national inventories under the Paris Agreement
- shipping as EXPOSED - sea level rise, storm severity, changing ice conditions affect ports and routeing
- the regulatory expression: 2023 IMO GHG Strategy, MEPC.377(80), 7 July 2023, SUPERSEDING the 2018 Initial Strategy
- net-zero by or around, i.e. close to, 2050
- indicative checkpoints for 2030 and 2040 against a 2008 baseline, each a minimum with a striving-for level
- 2030 ambition for zero or near-zero technologies, fuels and energy sources; move to WELL-TO-WAKE accounting
- AT THIS SITTING - EEXI, CII and enhanced SEEMP in force since 1 January 2023; 2023 the first full data year; first ratings issued during 2024
- AT THIS SITTING - mid-term measures UNDER DEVELOPMENT, not adopted; MEPC 82 referred work to MEPC 83 in 2025

**5. SDG 13 - specific actions** - limb `main`

- OPERATIONAL - speed optimisation, weather routeing, just-in-time arrival, trim optimisation, hull and propeller condition management, voyage planning to cut ballast legs
- the SEEMP four-step cycle is the management instrument for all of these
- RETROFIT - waste heat recovery, shaft generators, energy-saving devices, air lubrication, wind assistance, derating, VFDs, LED and HVAC control
- FUELS - LNG with methane slip managed, methanol, ammonia, hydrogen, biofuels, batteries and hybrids, onshore power supply, dual-fuel and fuel-ready designs
- well-to-wake accounting makes the fuel PRODUCTION PATHWAY the decisive question
- FLEET AND COMMERCIAL - fleet renewal, charter party clauses allocating efficiency responsibility, digital performance monitoring, training, R&D participation
- the split incentive between owner and charterer is the largest non-technical obstacle, and is solved contractually

**6. SDG 14 Life Below Water - relevance to shipping** - limb `main`

- SDG 14 - conserve and sustainably use the oceans, seas and marine resources; the Goal closest to IMO's mandate
- target 14.1 - prevent and significantly reduce marine pollution of all kinds
- operational and accidental pollution - oil, NLS, sewage, garbage, plastics
- invasive species via ballast water and biofouling - among the least reversible impacts
- toxic anti-fouling systems
- SOx and NOx deposition, and CO2 driving ocean ACIDIFICATION - where SDG 13 and 14 meet
- underwater radiated noise; ship strikes on cetaceans
- end-of-life recycling contaminating the coastal environment
- instruments - MARPOL Annexes I to VI; BWM Convention 2004 in force 8 September 2017 with D-1 and D-2; AFS 2001; London Convention and Protocol; IMO marine plastic litter action plan
- HONG KONG CONVENTION - criteria met June 2023, ENTERS INTO FORCE 26 JUNE 2025, so NOT YET IN FORCE at this sitting

**7. SDG 14 - specific actions** - limb `main`

- ballast water - operate and maintain the BWMS to D-2 properly, crew training, sampling, honest Ballast Water Record Book
- biofouling - management plan, coating selection, in-water inspection and cleaning with capture; ALSO a major fuel-efficiency measure
- garbage and plastics - segregation, Garbage Record Book accuracy, absolute prohibition on plastics overboard, retention where reception facilities are inadequate, eliminate single-use plastics
- oil and chemicals - Oil Record Book discipline, functioning OWS with no bypass, bunkering procedures and checklists, SOPEP/SMPEP exercised
- sewage and grey water - treatment plants maintained; Annex IV discharge only; voluntary grey water retention in sensitive areas
- air emissions reaching the sea - sulphur limit and ECA compliance, scrubber washwater management, NOx-compliant operation
- anti-fouling systems compliant with AFS, with a valid IAFS Certificate
- underwater radiated noise and ship strikes - reduce propeller cavitation; observe routeing measures, ATBAs and speed restrictions in whale habitats
- responsible recycling - accurate and updated Inventory of Hazardous Materials; select facilities to the Hong Kong Convention standard AHEAD of its entry into force
- observe Special Areas, ECAs and Particularly Sensitive Sea Areas; beyond-compliance participation and transparent reporting

**8. The management-level conclusion** - limb `main`

- for a shipowner the SDGs are now a COMMERCIAL and FINANCING framework, not only an ethical one
- access to capital - sustainable finance frameworks and portfolio emissions disclosure affect the cost of borrowing
- access to cargo - charterers set environmental criteria, so CII rating affects employability
- asset value - a ship that cannot meet tightening carbon intensity faces early obsolescence, making newbuilding fuel choice a balance-sheet decision
- regulatory direction - the SDGs signal where binding IMO regulation is heading, so alignment is cheap regulatory risk management
- licence to operate - crew recruitment and retention, port and coastal State relations, reputation
- CLOSING LINE - the SDGs set the direction, IMO conventions turn direction into obligation, and the SEEMP turns obligation into daily practice
