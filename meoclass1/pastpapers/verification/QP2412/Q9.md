## 1. What the question demands

Three limbs against one named Code, printed 6 + 5 + 5. (a) is really three tasks - the objective, the recommended strategy, and which instruments the Code covers. (b) is precise recall of the flag State performance indicators. (c) asks for flag, coastal and port State responsibilities in five marks.

Printed limbs:

- **a) (6)** The objectives of the Code and the strategy recommended to a flag State to achieve them, and which IMO instruments the Code covers.
- **b) (5)** The key performance indicators for evaluation of flag State performance as per the Code.
- **c) (5)** Flag State, coastal State and port State responsibilities.

Required answer components, as decomposed before drafting:

- Which instrument the question names, its resolution number, and what made it mandatory
- (a) The objective of the Code, stated in the Code's own words
- (a) The strategy the Code recommends a State adopt to meet that objective
- (a) Which IMO instruments the Code covers - the six subject areas within its scope, and the treaty instruments under which its use is mandatory
- (b) The key performance indicators for evaluating flag State performance, at the enlarged five-mark weighting this sitting prints
- (c) Flag State responsibilities under Part 2
- (c) Coastal State responsibilities under Part 3 and port State responsibilities under Part 4

## 9. Answer route as built

**1. The Code, and what made it mandatory** - limb `framing`

- resolution A.1070(28), adopted 4 December 2013
- revoked A.1054(27), the 2011 Code, by operative paragraph 3
- recommendatory in wording; its USE made mandatory by amending the conventions
- A.1083(28), A.1084(28), A.1085(28); MSC.366(93), MSC.373(93), MSC.374(93), MSC.375(93); MEPC.246(66), MEPC.247(66)
- it is the standard against which IMSAS audits a Member State
- four parts: common areas, flag, coastal, port - each State part ending in evaluation and review

**2. The objective of the Code** - limb `a)`

- para 1 - enhance global maritime safety and protection of the marine environment
- para 1 - AND assist States in the implementation of instruments of the Organization
- para 2 - bound only for instruments to which the State is a Contracting Government or Party
- para 2 - geography and circumstance decide whether the flag, coastal or port role dominates
- para 4 - promulgate laws and take all other steps to give full and complete effect
- para 5 - do not transfer damage or hazards, or transform one pollution type into another

**3. The strategy recommended to the flag State** - limb `a)`

- para 3.1 - develop an overall strategy covering the flag, port and coastal roles
- para 3.2 - establish a methodology to monitor and assess that the strategy works
- para 3.3 - continuously review it to achieve, maintain and improve performance and capability
- that is a develop-monitor-review cycle, not a one-off document
- para 9 - communicate the strategy and the national legislation to all concerned
- para 7 - nine areas the legislation and procedures must address
- para 8 - ability to legislate, a legal basis for enforcement, sufficient expert personnel

**4. Which IMO instruments the Code covers** - limb `a)`

- para 6 - SIX subject areas, and only these
- safety of life at sea; prevention of pollution from ships; STCW
- load lines; tonnage measurement; preventing collisions at sea
- maritime security is NOT a subject area of the Code
- the treaty instruments under which the Code is mandatory are listed in A.1187(33)
- SOLAS 1974 and its Protocols, MARPOL and its 1997 Protocol, STCW 1978 and the STCW Code, LL 1966 and LL PROT 1988, TONNAGE 1969, COLREG 1972
- A.1187(33) adopted 6 December 2023, revoking A.1157(32) - twelve months old at this sitting
- its Annex 7 window, 1 January to 1 July 2024, has CLOSED by this sitting

**5. Key performance indicators for flag State evaluation** - limb `b)`

- para 42 - evaluate performance on a periodic basis; this is SELF-evaluation, not a return to IMO
- para 43 - port State control detention rates
- para 43 - flag State inspection results and casualty statistics
- para 43 - communication and information processes
- para 43 - annual loss statistics, EXCLUDING constructive total losses
- para 43 - purpose: to test whether staffing, resources and procedures are adequate
- para 44 - ten areas for regular review; at FIVE marks these must be given, not merely mentioned
- para 44 - fleet loss and accident ratios; detentions against fleet size; certificate-holder incompetence
- para 44 - responses to PSC deficiency reports; very serious and serious casualty investigations
- para 44 - resources committed; inspection results; occupational accidents; pollution violations; suspensions and withdrawals

**6. Flag State responsibilities** - limb `c)`

- para 15 - implement policy through national legislation and guidance
- para 16 - a safety and environmental protection programme with five minimum elements
- para 16.2 - the audit and inspection programme must be independent of the certifying body
- para 17 - sufficiently and efficiently manned, on the Principles of Safe Manning
- paras 18 to 21 - delegation to recognized organizations under a formal written agreement, with an oversight programme
- paras 22 to 27 - prohibit sailing, inspect periodically, penalties of adequate severity
- para 22 - institute proceedings irrespective of where the violation occurred
- paras 38 to 41 - impartial casualty investigation, reported to the Organization

**7. Coastal State and port State responsibilities** - limb `c)`

- paras 45 and 52 - exercising a right under an instrument incurs additional obligations
- para 48 - eight coastal State service areas, and they are SERVICES not controls
- radiocommunication, meteorological services and warnings, search and rescue, hydrographic services
- ships routeing, ship reporting systems, vessel traffic services, aids to navigation
- para 50 - control and monitoring, pollution response mechanisms, cooperation in casualty investigation
- para 56 - three port State obligations: reception facilities, port State control, register of fuel oil suppliers
- para 59 - no more favourable treatment for ships of non-parties
- paras 60 to 62 - PSC under A.1185(33) by authorized, qualified officers free of commercial pressure
- paras 51 and 63 - both must periodically evaluate their own performance
