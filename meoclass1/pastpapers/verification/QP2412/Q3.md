## 3. The finding that mattered

The NIR is structurally unchanged since 1 January 2011, so the finding here is about what is sitting-relative in an otherwise stable subject. Three things are. The White, Grey and Black Lists are reissued annually from a three-year rolling period: the lists operative at this examination are the 2023 performance lists, approved at the 57th Committee meeting and APPLIED FROM 1 JULY 2024, so a January 2024 answer would have been on the previous set. The Russian Federation is SUSPENDED from the Memorandum, which the industry guidance issued at the NIR's introduction does not reflect - that guidance lists it as a full member and describes its national system as feeding THETIS. And the revised Ship Risk Profile aligned with the recast EU Port State Control Directive, including the incorporation of ENVIRONMENTAL CRITERIA, was IN PREPARATION and NOT OPERATIVE; that Directive applies from 2027. Any statement that environmental parameters form part of the Ship Risk Profile is future contamination for a December 2024 paper, and it is the trap anyone updating this object from current Paris MoU material would fall into.

## 9. Answer route as built

**1. What the NIR is, and what it replaced** - limb `a)`

- entered into force 1 January 2011, in parallel with the EU port State control Directive
- replaced the TARGET FACTOR with the SHIP RISK PROFILE
- profile decides both HOW OFTEN and WHAT KIND of inspection
- replaced each State inspecting 25% of individual ships with a SHARED regional commitment to inspect ALL ships
- ranked the ISM company by performance for the first time
- region: European coastal States and the North Atlantic basin from North America to Europe
- at this sitting 27 maritime authorities, with the Russian Federation SUSPENDED

**2. The Ship Risk Profile** - limb `a)`

- three categories - High Risk, Standard Risk, Low Risk; Standard is the RESIDUAL category
- type of ship - chemical tanker, gas carrier, oil tanker, bulk carrier, passenger ship are higher risk
- age of ship - over 12 years attracts higher risk
- flag performance - White, Grey or Black List, and whether the IMO audit is complete
- recognized organization performance
- company performance
- number of deficiencies and number of detentions over 36 months in the region
- RECALCULATED DAILY; a sister ship's inspection can immediately move this ship's profile
- weightings are in Annex 7 Table 1 and are not expected to be reproduced

**3. Company performance - the new parameter** - limb `a)`

- the genuinely novel element of the NIR
- formula over the company's fleet deficiencies and detentions in the last 36 months
- identified by IMO company number
- compared against the average of all vessels inspected in the region
- ranked very low, low, medium or high
- any refusal of access of one of its ships damages the ranking
- intent: reach past the ship to the quality of MANAGEMENT

**4. The selection scheme - windows and priorities** - limb `a)`

- High Risk 5 to 6 months; Standard Risk 10 to 12 months; Low Risk 24 to 36 months
- measured from the last inspection in the region; the span RESTARTS after any inspection
- Priority II - window OPENED, ship MAY be selected
- Priority I - window PASSED, ship WILL be selected and must be inspected
- before the window opens and absent factors, NO priority status - inspection not obligatory
- OVERRIDING factor - Priority I irrespective of the window; e.g. collision, discharge of harmful substances
- UNEXPECTED factor - Priority II irrespective of the window; e.g. failure to report ETA, outstanding deficiencies, cargo problems

**5. Types of inspection** - limb `a)`

- initial - certificates, documents, overall condition
- more detailed - where CLEAR GROUNDS are established
- expanded - defined-scope examination of risk areas
- on a periodic inspection an EXPANDED inspection is mandatory for every High Risk Ship
- and for every bulk carrier, chemical tanker, gas carrier, oil tanker or passenger ship OVER 12 YEARS OLD, whatever its profile
- other Standard and Low Risk Ships get an initial inspection, escalating on clear grounds
- any ADDITIONAL inspection is at least a more detailed inspection

**6. Reporting obligations and THETIS** - limb `a)`

- ETA72 - 72 hours, from ships eligible for expanded inspection
- ETA72 content includes hull configuration, cargo and ballast tank condition, volume and type of cargo for tankers, and the date of the last expanded inspection
- ETA24 - 24 hours, from ALL ships, at any time regardless of the window
- ATA and ATD - actual times of arrival and departure, new under the NIR
- THETIS is the NIR information database, fed through SafeSeaNet
- FAILURE TO REPORT IS ITSELF AN UNEXPECTED FACTOR - that is the enforcement mechanism

**7. Refusal of access - banning** - limb `a)`

- extended from certain ship types to ALL types, and from Black List flags to Grey List flags too
- black-listed flag - banned after the THIRD detention in the last 36 months
- grey-listed flag - banned after the THIRD detention in the last 24 months
- after a SECOND ban, any subsequent detention leads to a ban regardless of flag listing
- jumping detention, or failing to sail to an agreed repair yard, leads to a ban
- lifting - 3 months, then 12 months, then 24 months with more stringent conditions, then PERMANENT
- lists published annually from a three-year rolling period, minimum 30 inspections; the 2023 lists applied from 1 July 2024 are operative here

**8. Pros of the NIR** - limb `b)`

- risk-proportionate use of finite inspection resource
- real reward for quality - LRS inspected every 24 to 36 months instead of annually
- no escape by routing, because the regional commitment covers all ships
- reaches the COMPANY, where substandard operation originates
- transparent and predictable - the operator can calculate the profile in advance
- escalating published banning drives persistent offenders out, and pressures flags and ROs
- harmonisation across member States through common criteria and a common database

**9. Cons of the NIR** - limb `b)`

- collective punishment - a good ship penalised for sister vessels' detentions
- flag and RO parameters largely outside the individual operator's control
- 36-month historic lag - quicker to punish than to reward
- age over 12 years is a crude proxy for condition
- administrative burden of ETA72, ETA24, ATA and ATD
- 5 to 6 month expanded-inspection cycle is heavy for high risk ships
- residual inconsistency in deficiency coding and professional judgement between PSCOs
- severe consequences of banning, with appeal that does not suspend detention
- perverse incentives toward cosmetic preparation rather than genuine improvement

**10. How owners and managers should respond** - limb `b)`

- MEASURE - calculate the profile for every ship, know the window dates, treat movement to Low Risk as a board-level KPI
- CHOOSE - deficiencies and detentions are controllable; flag and RO are not controllable but ARE selectable, so flag and class choice become risk decisions
- PROTECT THE FLEET - one detention degrades every sister ship, so corrective action must be fleet-wide, through ISM root cause analysis, CAPA, internal audit and management review
- PROTECT THE FLEET - PSC record due diligence on acquisition and on taking over management
- PREPARE - self-inspection and mock PSC built on published deficiency statistics; focus on ISM, fire safety, LSA, MLC and pollution prevention
- PREPARE - readiness for expanded inspection scope; officers able to DEMONSTRATE systems, not just produce certificates; prepare for announced Concentrated Inspection Campaigns
- HANDLE THE DAY - rigorous ETA/ATA/ATD reporting, since failure is an unexpected factor
- HANDLE THE DAY - senior officer accompanies the PSCO; rectify before departure; never jump detention or miss the agreed repair yard
- HANDLE THE DAY - appeal to the national authority or through the flag State, but the appeal does NOT lift the detention
- STRATEGIC CLOSE - inspection frequency is now a commercial variable management can move
