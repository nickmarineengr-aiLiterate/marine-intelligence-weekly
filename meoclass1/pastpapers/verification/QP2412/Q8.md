## 9. Answer route as built

**1. What the SEEMP is, and where the four steps live** - limb `framing`

- required by MARPOL Annex VI regulation 26 - NOT regulation 22, renumbered by MEPC.328(76) from 1 November 2022
- ship-specific, operational, kept on board, developed taking account of the Organization's guidelines
- operative guidelines at this sitting - MEPC.395(82), adopted 4 October 2024, revoking MEPC.346(78)
- chapter 4 neighbours: regulation 20 goal, 27 data collection, 28 CII
- Part I under 26.1 - any ship 400 GT and above - carries the four steps
- Part II under 26.2 - any ship 5,000 GT and above - the data collection plan
- Part III under 26.3 and 28.8 - the operational carbon intensity plan
- section 3.6 states the four steps and calls them critical to the continuous cycle; 3.7 safety paramount

**2. Step 1 - Planning** - limb `main`

- 4.1.1 - the MOST CRUCIAL stage; devote sufficient time to it
- determines current status of energy use and carbon intensity, and the improvement achievable
- ship-specific measures - identify what has been done, how effective, what more can be adopted
- not all measures suit all ships, and SOME ARE MUTUALLY EXCLUSIVE
- company-specific measures - yards, charterers, cargo owners, fuel suppliers, ports, traffic management
- human resource development - training ashore and on board
- goal setting - consistent with continuous CII improvement under regulation 28; measurable and easy to understand
- examples: speed optimisation, weather routeing, hull and propeller management, trim, just-in-time arrival

**3. Step 2 - Implementation** - limb `main`

- 4.2.1 - establish a SYSTEM: procedures, defined tasks, assigned responsible personnel
- defined levels of authority and lines of communication; internal audit and management review where relevant
- state how each measure is implemented, who is responsible, and start and end dates
- building the system may be completed at the planning stage
- 4.2.2 - record-keeping supports later self-evaluation
- if a measure CANNOT be implemented, record the reason for internal use
- document events outside the crew's control - berth waiting, port dwell, severe weather - as they affect the rating
- examples: Chief Engineer owns hull and propeller regime; Master owns trim and speed; deferred retrofit recorded with reason

**4. Step 3 - Monitoring** - limb `main`

- 4.3.1 - monitor QUANTITATIVELY by an established method, preferably an international standard
- tool should target the goal indicator - AER, cgDIST, EEOI or another CII
- a ship subject to regulation 28 is likely to use the CII itself
- 4.3.4 - regular intervals; fuel oil consumption by daily reporting such as noon reports
- 4.3.5 - continuous, consistent, reliable data collection is the FOUNDATION; establish a monitoring system at planning stage
- 4.3.6 - as far as possible by shore staff, using logbooks and the oil record book, to limit administrative burden
- 4.3.7 - search and rescue diversion data excluded and recorded separately
- examples: performance system separating fouling from weather; shaft power and mass flow meters; monthly CII tracking

**5. Step 4 - Self-evaluation and improvement** - limb `main`

- 4.4.1 - the FINAL phase; produces feedback for the planning stage of the NEXT cycle
- 4.4.2.1 - evaluate effectiveness of the measures and of their implementation
- 4.4.2.2 - deepen understanding of what works, how and why
- 4.4.2.3 - comprehend the trend of efficiency improvement
- 4.4.2.4 - develop the improved plan for the next cycle
- Part III equivalent - D for three consecutive years or E in one year triggers a plan of corrective actions
- examples: weather routeing selective by trade; polishing interval shortened on warm-water idle trades

**6. Why the cycle is critical, not merely administrative** - limb `main`

- planning without monitoring - savings cannot be claimed against any baseline
- implementation without assignment - the plan is carried for inspection, not used
- monitoring without consistency - effectiveness becomes opinion and weather cannot be separated from operation
- no self-evaluation - the cycle does not close and the same plan is re-issued
- 3.6 - with each iteration some elements change while others remain, which is what keeps it ship-specific and living
- the same four beats reappear in Part III as required CII, attained CII and corrective actions
- contrast with EEDI and EEXI - technical and one-off; the SEEMP is operational and continuous
