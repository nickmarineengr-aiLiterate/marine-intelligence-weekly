## 9. Answer route as built

**1. What a classification society is, and its two distinct roles** - limb `a)`

- publishes rules, assigns and maintains class
- origin in underwriters' need for independent judgement
- classification is private and contractual
- statutory work is delegated under SOLAS XI-1/1
- governed by the RO Code, MSC.349(92) and MEPC.237(65), mandatory 1 January 2015
- the Administration retains responsibility

**2. How class rules are made** - limb `a)`

- service experience and defect returns from the surveyed fleet
- casualty investigation feedback
- research and direct calculation, including FEA and fatigue assessment
- technical committees of owners, builders, makers and surveyors
- IACS Unified Requirements, Unified Interpretations, Procedural Requirements
- Common Structural Rules for Bulk Carriers and Oil Tankers

**3. What the structural, mechanical and electrical rules cover** - limb `a)`

- scantlings, fatigue life, corrosion additions and coatings
- hull girder strength and loading conditions; close-up survey
- propulsion, shafting, gearing, boilers, piping, steering gear
- type approval of components and materials
- generation, distribution and protection; discrimination and short-circuit capability
- essential and emergency services, high voltage, hazardous area equipment

**4. Implementation through the ship's life** - limb `a)`

- plan approval and type approval at design
- material approval, welder and procedure qualification at construction
- NDT and tank testing witnessed
- survey during construction and attendance at trials
- in service: annual, intermediate, renewal, docking, tailshaft and boiler surveys
- continuous survey and approved planned maintenance scheme as alternatives
- condition of class; suspension and withdrawal

**5. Why compliance with class rules is now mentioned in SOLAS chapter II-1** - limb `a)`

- regulation II-1/3-1
- SOLAS never contained scantling rules
- class was commercially indispensable but legally optional
- now designed, constructed AND maintained
- loss of class is a SOLAS non-compliance
- enforceable by flag State and inspectable by port State control
- society must be recognized under XI-1/1 and operate under the RO Code
- national standards of equivalent safety permitted as an alternative
- links to goal-based standards under II-1/3-10 for bulk carriers and oil tankers

**6. The harmonized system, and where these surveys sit** - limb `b)`

- 1988 SOLAS and Load Lines Protocols; extended by MEPC.39(29) and MEPC.132(53)
- common five-year cycle and common anniversary dates
- three months either side window
- surveys combined into one attendance
- initial, annual, intermediate, periodical, renewal, additional; plus bottom inspection
- guidelines at this sitting - A.1186(33) of 6 December 2023, revoking A.1156(32)

**7. Annual versus periodical - the difference and the relevance** - limb `b)`

- annual - each anniversary; certificate examination, visual, certain tests
- annual - confirms no unapproved modifications
- periodical - ONLY Safety Equipment and Safety Radio certificates
- Equipment at second or third anniversary, replacing one annual
- Radio at EVERY anniversary
- periodical is inspection with tests when necessary, plus documentation check
- a five-year certificate needs mid-cycle checkpoints to stay credible
- equipment is consumed in a way structure is not; distress radio cannot degrade unnoticed
