## 9. Answer route as built

**1. What the MLC is, and why its structure is unusual** - limb `a)`

- ILO convention adopted at the 94th (Maritime) Session of the International Labour Conference
- consolidated some sixty-eight earlier maritime labour Conventions and Recommendations
- the FOURTH PILLAR alongside SOLAS, MARPOL and STCW
- designed to be firm on rights but flexible on implementation
- and amendable at industry speed rather than by adopting a new convention

**2. The three-level structure** - limb `a)`

- ARTICLES - treaty framework, general obligations, definitions, scope, amendment, entry into force
- REGULATIONS - the core requirements, each stating a purpose
- CODE - the implementing detail
- Code Part A - STANDARDS, MANDATORY
- Code Part B - GUIDELINES, NOT mandatory but must be given DUE CONSIDERATION
- shared numbering: Regulation x.y, Standard Ax.y, Guideline Bx.y on the same subject
- Article VI(3) substantial equivalence; Title 3 exemptions - both declared in DMLC Part I

**3. The five Titles** - limb `a)`

- Title 1 - minimum requirements for seafarers to work on a ship: age, medical, qualifications, recruitment and placement
- Title 2 - conditions of employment: SEA, wages, hours of work and rest, leave, repatriation, loss of ship, manning, career development
- Title 3 - accommodation, recreational facilities, food and catering
- Title 4 - health protection, medical care, welfare and social security protection
- Title 5 - compliance and enforcement: flag State, port State, labour-supplying responsibilities
- mnemonic - get the job, the terms, live on board, stay well, who checks

**4. The amendment position at this sitting** - limb `a)`

- 2014 amendments in force 18 January 2017 - financial security for repatriation and shipowners' liability; TWO matters added to Appendix A5-I
- 2016 amendments in force 8 January 2019 - harassment and bullying in Guidelines B4.3.1 and B4.3.6
- 2018 amendments in force 26 December 2020 - SEA and wages continue during captivity after piracy or armed robbery
- 2022 amendments approved June 2022 at the 110th ILC session, ENTERING INTO FORCE 23 DECEMBER 2024
- state it BY DATE, not by tense - correct on either side of the boundary
- 2022 content: recruitment financial-security information, prompt repatriation and abandonment, social connectivity and internet access, free food and drinking water

**5. The certification chain - where the DMLC sits** - limb `b)`

- Regulation 5.1.3 - mandatory certification at 500 GT and over on international voyages, and 500 GT and over operating from or between ports in another country
- Regulation 5.1.4 - ALL covered ships are inspected for ALL requirements, certified or not
- Maritime Labour Certificate - maximum FIVE years, intermediate inspection between second and third anniversary
- interim certificate - new ship on delivery, change of flag, new shipowner; maximum SIX months; NOT renewable; no DMLC required for it
- DMLC attached to the Certificate
- A5.1.3(12) - both carried on board, copy posted conspicuously, available to seafarers on request

**6. DMLC Part I - the flag State's declaration** - limb `b)`

- DRAWN UP BY THE COMPETENT AUTHORITY, in the form of the model in Appendix A5-II
- identifies the list of matters to be inspected - Appendix A5-I
- identifies in each area the national requirements, by reference plus concise content
- refers to ship-type specific requirements
- records any substantially equivalent provisions under Article VI(3)
- clearly indicates any Title 3 exemption granted
- Appendix A5-I has SIXTEEN matters at this sitting - the original fourteen plus two financial security matters added by the 2014 amendments

**7. DMLC Part II - the shipowner's declaration** - limb `b)`

- DRAWN UP BY THE SHIPOWNER
- CERTIFIED by the competent authority or a duly authorized recognized organization
- identifies the measures adopted to ensure ongoing compliance BETWEEN INSPECTIONS with the Part I national requirements
- and the measures proposed to ensure CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT
- Part I is WHAT must be complied with; Part II is HOW this ship complies
- Guideline B5.1.3(4) - drafted in clear terms to help inspectors, port State officers and SEAFARERS check implementation
- Appendix B5-I gives an example of both Parts

**8. The welfare measures covered, and how they bite** - limb `b)`

- who may be employed and on what terms - age, medical, qualifications, SEA, recruitment, wages
- fatigue and manning - hours of work or rest, manning levels
- living conditions - accommodation, recreational facilities, food and catering; the 2022 amendments add social connectivity and free food and drinking water
- health and protection against destitution - health and safety, medical care, complaint procedures, the two financial security matters
- flag State inspects and certifies, directly or through an authorized RO
- port State under Regulation 5.2.1 - certificate and DMLC are PRIMA FACIE evidence; clear grounds or complaint trigger a more detailed inspection; detention possible
- A5.1.3(14) - certificate ceases to be valid on incomplete inspection, missing endorsement, change of flag, change of shipowner, or substantial Title 3 changes
