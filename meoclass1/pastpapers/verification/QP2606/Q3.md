## Verification

### What was actually read

The **Comite Maritime International's own tabular comparison** of the York-Antwerp Rules **1994,
2004 and 2016**, read column by column for the Rule of Interpretation, the Rule Paramount, Rules A,
B and C, and Rules VI, XX, XXI, XXII and XXIII.

### The load-bearing finding - Rule A is unchanged

**Rule A is word-for-word identical in the 1994 and 2016 columns.** The 2016 text differs only in
numbering its two paragraphs 1 and 2. The 1994 text reads:

> *"There is a general average act when, and only when, any extraordinary sacrifice or expenditure
> is intentionally and reasonably made or incurred for the common safety for the purpose of
> preserving from peril the property involved in a common maritime adventure."*

**This makes the question far safer than it looks.** The examiner names a superseded edition, but the
definition demanded is simultaneously the 1994 answer and the current one. The answer states this
explicitly rather than leaving it ambiguous.

### Where the editions genuinely differ

| Rule | YAR 1994 | YAR 2016 |
|---|---|---|
| VI salvage | Allowed in general average | Allowed, subject to exceptions where parties have separate liability to salvors (2004 had made it lie where it falls) |
| XX commission | **2 per cent** on GA disbursements allowed | No commission |
| XXI interest | Fixed at **7 per cent per annum** | Rate set by reference to a published benchmark plus four percentage points |
| XXIII time bar | **Does not exist** - the 1994 Rules end at Rule XXII | One year from issue of the adjustment, six years maximum from termination |

The absence of a time bar from YAR 1994 was confirmed structurally as well as textually: the 1994
column ends at **Rule XXII - Treatment of Cash Deposits**.

Also confirmed present in the 1994 column: the **Rule Paramount** (no allowance unless reasonably
made or incurred) and **Rule C paragraph 2** (no allowance for environmental damage).

### The jurisdiction finding on limb (b)

English law diverged from the Indian position under the **Insurance Act 2015**, under which breach
of warranty **suspends** cover rather than discharging the insurer. **The Indian Marine Insurance
Act, 1963 position remains discharge from the date of breach.** Recorded as a jurisdictional
contrast and expressly not applied to the Indian answer. This is the same failure family as the
existing repository caution against citing the UK Marine Insurance Act 1906 in an Indian
examination.
