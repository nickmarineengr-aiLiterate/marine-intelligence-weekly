## Verification

### What was actually read

**Resolution MSC.287(87)**, *Adoption of the International Goal-based Ship Construction Standards
for Bulk Carriers and Oil Tankers*, adopted 20 May 2010, read in full (nine pages): section 1.5,
section 3 STRUCTURE, the Tier I goal with its five sub-goals, all fifteen Tier II functional
requirements, and Tier III section 6.1.

### The load-bearing finding - the tier count

The instrument says two things and both are true:

- **section 1.5** records that the Committee agreed to use a **five-tier system**;
- **section 3 STRUCTURE** states that *"These Standards consist of the following three tiers:
  Tier I, Tier II, Tier III."*

**The framework is five tiers; the instrument contains three.** Tiers IV and V are the class and
national rules and the industry practice beneath the Standards.

> An automated summary of this same resolution asserted a **"four-tier hierarchical framework"**.
> That is neither of the two correct answers. Sixth consecutive paper on which opening the primary
> source caught a substantive error in a summary of it.

### The superseded-edition trap

Conformity is **no longer verified against MSC.296(87)**. International GBS Audit Teams established
by the Secretary-General evaluate submissions against the **Revised guidelines, resolution
MSC.454(100)**, reporting to MSC for approval. Recorded as `B_CURRENCY_CHECK` because no check was
made for a revision later than MSC 100.

### Numbers taken directly from the instrument

Design life **not less than 25 years**; **North Atlantic** environmental conditions; application to
bulk carriers and oil tankers of **150 m and above** contracted **on or after 1 July 2016**;
**fifteen** Tier II functional requirements in **four** lifecycle groups.
