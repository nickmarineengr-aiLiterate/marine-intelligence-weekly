## Verification

### What was actually read

### Circular history, from the circular itself

Original guidelines MSC/Circ.1023-MEPC/Circ.392 (MSC 74, 2001; MEPC 47, 2002), amended by

MEPC 65, 2013); Rev.1 (MSC 94, 2014; MEPC 68, 2015); **Rev.2 (MSC 98, 2017; MEPC 72, 2018)**, which
**supersedes Rev.1**.

### THE CLAIM THAT WAS TESTED AND REJECTED

**The Guidelines do NOT prescribe a mandatory monetary threshold for averting a fatality.**

- **Section 9.2.2**: *"There are several standards for risk acceptance criteria, none as yet
  universally accepted."* Those used within an FSA should be **explicit**.
- **Appendix 7, paragraph 1.3.2**: the tabulated NCAF and GCAF values *"are provided for illustrative
  purposes only"*, the specific values used *"should be explicitly defined"*, and the criteria
  *"are not static, but should be updated every year"*.

A single figure for the cost of averting a fatality circulates widely and is repeated as though IMO
had mandated it. **It has not.** The answer explains what GCAF and NCAF are, records the
recommendation to consider **GCAF first** because NCAF's inclusion of economic benefits can be
misused, and asserts **no monetary figure at all**.

The tabulated values themselves are a table image in the circular and could not be extracted as
text; this is recorded as `C_ACCEPTED_LIMITATION`, and it does not affect the answer, because what
the answer relies on was read in the surrounding text.

### The definitions and outputs, taken directly

- **Purpose (1.1.1)**: *"a structured and systematic methodology, aimed at enhancing maritime safety,
  including protection of life, health, the marine environment and property, by using risk analysis
  and cost-benefit assessment."*
- **Hazard**: *"a potential to threaten human life, health, property or the environment."*
  **Risk**: *"the combination of the frequency and the severity of the consequence."*
- **The five steps (3.1.1.1)**: identification of hazards; risk analysis; risk control options;
  cost-benefit assessment; recommendations for decision-making. **Problem definition precedes them
  and is not one of the five**, and **step 5 interacts with each of the other steps**.
- **Step 1 output**: a list of hazards and associated scenarios including initiating events, and an
  assessment of accident scenarios **prioritized by risk level**.
- **Step 2 output**: identification of the high-risk areas needing to be addressed, and an
  explanation of the risk models.
- **Step 4 output**: costs and benefits for each option overall and for the most influenced
  interested entities, and cost-effectiveness in suitable indices.
- **Step 5**: recommendations **auditable and traceable**, identifying options keeping risk **as low
  as reasonably practicable**, submitted in **SMART** terms.

### A note on the question's own wording

The paper uses **"Risk Analysis"** for step 2, which is the instrument's own correct term. Some
coaching material calls it "assessment of risks", which is not the Guidelines' name for it.
