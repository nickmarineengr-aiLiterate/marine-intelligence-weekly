## Verification

### What was actually read

**MSC-FAL.1/Circ.3/Rev.3**, *Guidelines on maritime cyber risk management*, dated **4 April 2025**,
read directly, together with **ISM Code paragraphs 1.2, 1.4 and 10.3**.

### The load-bearing currency finding - SIX functional elements, not five

**MSC-FAL.1/Circ.3/Rev.3 sets out SIX functional elements:**

> **Govern**, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover

**"Govern" was added by this revision.** Rev.2 carried five. The circular was approved by **MSC 108
(15 to 24 May 2024)** and **FAL 49 (10 to 14 March 2025)** and issued on 4 April 2025.

**Almost all published material - including industry guidance and training material - still states
five elements.** Paragraph 3.5 also states expressly that the elements are **not sequential**: all
should be concurrent and continuous.

Govern requires the risk management strategy, expectations and policies to be established and
monitored, personnel roles defined, business continuity ensured, and **a person or entity designated
as accountable** for cyber security, with the necessary authority, support and expertise.

### The mechanism finding, which carries limb (b)

**Resolution MSC.428(98) did not amend the ISM Code.** It affirms that an approved safety management
system should take cyber risk management into account in accordance with the Code's objectives and
functional requirements, and encourages Administrations to ensure cyber risks are addressed **no
later than the first annual verification of the company's Document of Compliance after 1 January
2021**.

That was possible because **ISM paragraph 1.2.2.2** already requires assessment of **all identified
risks**, and **1.4.2** requires procedures ensuring compliance with **relevant** legislation. Both
are open-ended. **The Code evolves by absorption rather than by amendment** - the most recent
substantive amendment, MSC.353(92), entered into force on 1 January 2015.

### Definitions taken directly from Rev.3

**Computer Based System**, **cyber incident**, **cyber risk management**, and the **IT / OT**
distinction - OT being systems that use data to control or monitor physical processes, which
**should be segmented from IT and protected from internet-facing systems**.
