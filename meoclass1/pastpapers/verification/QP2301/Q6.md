## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: `legal`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route.

1. **a) What a general average act is**
   - Rule A - an extraordinary sacrifice or expenditure
   - Intentionally and reasonably made or incurred
   - For the common safety, to preserve from peril the property in a common maritime adventure
   - The York-Antwerp Rules are incorporated by contract, not imposed as law
   - In India the Marine Insurance Act 1963 also recognises general average loss
2. **a) The essential features**
   - A common danger to the whole adventure - real and substantial, not merely feared
   - The sacrifice or expenditure must be EXTRAORDINARY, not ordinary running cost
   - It must be INTENTIONAL and voluntary - the master's deliberate choice
   - It must be REASONABLE in the circumstances
   - It must be for the COMMON SAFETY, not for one interest alone
   - There must be a SAVED FUND - success, at least in part, or there is nothing to contribute
3. **a) The lettered rules that qualify the definition**
   - Rule of Interpretation - the lettered and numbered Rules govern over any inconsistent practice; numbered Rules prevail over lettered
   - Rule Paramount - no allowance unless reasonably made or incurred
   - Rule C - only loss which is a DIRECT consequence is allowed; delay, demurrage and market loss are excluded
   - Rule D - fault does not affect the right to contribution, leaving remedies intact
   - Rule E - onus of proof lies on the party claiming, with notice to the average adjuster
4. **a) How the loss is shared, and what general average is not**
   - An average adjuster states the adjustment
   - Contribution is rateable on the SAVED VALUES of ship, cargo and freight at destination
   - Security taken before delivery - average bond from the receiver, average guarantee from the cargo insurer
   - Contrast PARTICULAR AVERAGE - accidental, uncompensated, borne where it falls
   - Contrast SALVAGE - services by a third party; and note it cannot be limited under LLMC Article 3
5. **b) The rule that governs the refloating scenario**
   - Rule VII - damage to machinery and boilers
   - Applies where the ship is ASHORE and in a position of PERIL
   - Damage caused in endeavouring to refloat is allowed in general average
   - Only when shown to have arisen from an ACTUAL INTENTION to float the ship for the common safety AT THE RISK OF SUCH DAMAGE
   - But where the ship is AFLOAT, no loss or damage from working propelling machinery and boilers is allowed in any circumstances
6. **b) Applying it - when the damage is allowed, and when it is not**
   - On these facts the ship ran aground, so she is ashore
   - Working ahead and astern to shake her loose is an endeavour to refloat
   - The burnt bearings are damage to machinery caused by that endeavour
   - So the damage IS allowable, subject to proof of the three conditions
   - Proof comes from the log, engine room record, master's and chief engineer's statements
   - It is NOT allowed if she was merely afloat and touching, or if peril was absent, or if the engine was worked for another purpose such as ordinary manoeuvring
   - Rule D preserves the cargo interests' remedies if unseaworthiness caused the grounding

Rule E - the onus of proof is on the party claiming, with notice to the average adjuster.
