## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: `explain`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route.

1. **Where the recoverable energy is**
   - Rotational (swirl) energy left in the propeller race is a real and recoverable loss
   - The hub vortex behind the boss is a second, separate loss
   - The rudder is the only large appendage sitting in that accelerated, swirling flow
   - So a rudder device recovers waste energy; it does not create thrust from nothing
2. **(a) Rudder thrust fins**
   - Horizontal fins projecting each side of the rudder, set in the propeller slipstream
   - The swirling inflow meets the fin at an angle of attack and generates lift
   - Lift on a horizontal fin resolves into a forward component - recovered thrust
   - Fins are handed and set at opposite angles above and below the shaft line
   - Typically claimed at about 2-5 per cent at design condition
3. **(b) Asymmetric rudders**
   - Above the shaft the flow comes from one side, below it from the other
   - A symmetrical section therefore meets an incident angle over most of its span
   - The section is twisted or offset so each half is aligned to its own local flow
   - Reduces separation, drag and rudder cavitation, and improves course keeping
   - Typically claimed at about 2-6 per cent, with the cavitation benefit often the real one
4. **(c) Rudder bulbs**
   - A streamlined bulb on the rudder leading edge, aligned with the propeller boss
   - Fills the low-pressure space behind the hub and suppresses the hub vortex
   - Removes the vortex drag and lets the boundary flow close smoothly
   - Usually combined with fins, the bulb carrying the fin roots
   - Typically claimed at about 2-5 per cent, more on heavily loaded propellers
5. **(d) Grim vane wheels**
   - A larger-diameter wheel, free to rotate, mounted abaft the propeller
   - Inner span lies in the race and is driven round as a turbine
   - Outer span lies outside the race and acts as a propeller, producing thrust
   - Absorbs no shaft power - it is driven entirely by the wasted slipstream energy
   - Claims reached about 10 per cent, but service experience was poor: blade fatigue and loss, and bearing problems, so few remain in service
6. **Judging whether a claimed gain is real**
   - Quoted figures are model-scale or CFD, at design draught, trim and speed
   - Scale effects on wake and on the boundary layer flatter the model result
   - Gains do not add - a bulb, fins and a twisted section compete for the same energy
   - Judge against the ship's real operating profile, not the design point
   - Verify by hull and propeller performance monitoring to ISO 19030, not by the brochure
