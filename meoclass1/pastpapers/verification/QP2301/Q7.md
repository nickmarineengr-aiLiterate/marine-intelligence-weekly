## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: `explain`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route.

1. **A. The mandatory basis in SOLAS and MARPOL**
   - SOLAS regulation I/21 - the Administration undertakes to investigate a casualty to its ships where the investigation may assist in identifying desirable changes to the Regulations, and supplies IMO with the pertinent findings
   - SOLAS regulation I/21 also bars any IMO report from disclosing the identity or nationality of the ship, or fixing or implying responsibility
   - SOLAS regulation XI-1/6 - the operative mandatory hook: investigation SHALL be conducted in accordance with the Casualty Investigation Code
   - MARPOL Article 12 - the Administration undertakes to investigate a casualty to its ships where it has produced a major deleterious effect upon the marine environment
   - UNCLOS Article 94(7) - the flag State duty to hold an inquiry into a high-seas casualty causing loss of life, serious injury or serious damage
2. **B. What the Code is, and how it is structured**
   - The Casualty Investigation Code, resolution MSC.255(84), adopted 16 May 2008
   - Made mandatory through SOLAS XI-1/6, in force 1 January 2010
   - Part I - general provisions and the definitions
   - Part II - MANDATORY standards
   - Part III - RECOMMENDED practices
   - Its sole objective is prevention of future casualties, not blame
3. **B. The salient mandatory standards - Part II**
   - A marine safety investigation SHALL be conducted into every VERY SERIOUS MARINE CASUALTY
   - For other casualties and incidents, the flag State considers whether to investigate
   - The investigation is SEPARATE FROM AND INDEPENDENT OF any other form of investigation
   - It shall not seek to apportion blame or determine liability
   - The flag State conducts it, or agrees with another substantially interested State which will be the lead investigating State
   - States shall co-operate and give access to evidence, witnesses and the ship
   - The investigation shall be unbiased, and the investigator functionally independent
   - Records are protected from disclosure for purposes other than safety investigation
   - The final report is sent to IMO and made publicly available
4. **B. The salient recommended practices - Part III**
   - Investigate other marine casualties and marine incidents where lessons may be learned
   - Notify substantially interested States promptly and invite participation
   - Take evidence early - VDR, logs, charts, machinery data - before it is lost
   - Interview seafarers with due regard to their rights, including against self-incrimination
   - Provide seafarers a fair opportunity to comment on the draft report
   - Publish reports and re-open an investigation if significant new evidence appears
   - Report safety lessons to IMO for wider dissemination
5. **C. Very serious marine casualty**
   - A marine casualty involving the TOTAL LOSS OF THE SHIP, or A DEATH, or SEVERE DAMAGE TO THE ENVIRONMENT
   - Any one of the three limbs is sufficient - they are alternatives, not cumulative
   - It is the ONLY category that makes a safety investigation mandatory
   - Distinguish MARINE CASUALTY, SERIOUS MARINE CASUALTY and MARINE INCIDENT
   - The severity classification decides the obligation, not the investigating State's appetite
