## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: `legal`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route.

1. **a) The problem SCOPIC was written to cure**
   - Salvage is traditionally 'no cure, no pay' - Article 13, Salvage Convention 1989
   - Article 14 gave special compensation where the casualty threatened environmental damage
   - But entitlement had to be proved, and 'fair rate' was held to mean expenses without profit
   - Assessment was slow, uncertain and litigious, and security was slow to arrive
   - A salvor who cannot predict his money or fund his fleet will not respond
2. **a) What SCOPIC is**
   - Special Compensation P&I Clause, a supplementary clause to Lloyd's Open Form
   - First issued 1999; SCOPIC 2020, effective 1 January 2020, is the current version
   - The salvor may invoke it in writing AT ANY TIME, at his option
   - On invocation Article 14 is replaced by the SCOPIC tariff for the whole service
   - Tariff of day rates for personnel, tugs and equipment, plus a 25 per cent bonus
   - Owner must put up initial security within two working days, in a standard sum
   - A Shipowner's Casualty Representative attends, with special representatives for hull and cargo underwriters
   - If the Article 13 award exceeds SCOPIC, the award is discounted by 25 per cent of the difference - the price of holding the option
3. **a) How it answers each of the salvor's difficulties**
   - Certainty of rate - a published tariff replaces an arbitrator's later view
   - No need to prove an environmental threat, or the location of the casualty
   - Prompt security from the P&I club, a solvent and identified payer
   - A profit element through the 25 per cent bonus, which Article 14 was held not to give
   - Cash flow, so the salvor can fund the operation while it runs
   - Reduced litigation, and defined termination rights on both sides
4. **b) The governing principle - Article 97 of UNCLOS**
   - A collision on the high seas is outside any State's territorial jurisdiction
   - Article 97 deliberately narrows who may take PENAL or DISCIPLINARY action
   - It reverses the Lotus principle, following the Brussels penal jurisdiction Convention 1952
   - Article 94 places the general duty of jurisdiction and control on the flag State
   - Article 97 governs punishment only - it does not touch civil liability
5. **b) i) Disciplinary action against the master or crew**
   - Article 97(1) - proceedings only before the authorities of the FLAG STATE, or of the STATE OF WHICH THE PERSON IS A NATIONAL
   - No other State may institute penal or disciplinary proceedings
   - Justification: a seafarer must not answer to the courts of every State he may meet
   - Article 97(3) - no arrest or detention of the ship, even for investigation, except by the flag State
6. **b) ii) Cancellation of a certificate of competency**
   - Article 97(2) - the ISSUING State alone is competent to withdraw the certificate
   - It may do so after due legal process, even if the holder is not its national
   - This is narrower than 97(1): the State of nationality cannot cancel another State's COC
   - It follows from the certificate being that State's own instrument
   - STCW regulation I/2 and I/5 support the issuing Administration's control of its own certificates
7. **b) iii) Payment of compensation**
   - A CIVIL question - Article 97 does not apply, because nobody is being punished
   - Liability is apportioned in proportion to fault under the Collision Convention 1910
   - If the degrees of fault cannot be established, liability is apportioned equally
   - No presumption of fault from breach of the collision regulations under that Convention
   - Brussels Convention 1952 gives the claimant a choice of forum for the civil action
   - The owner may then cap his exposure under the LLMC, and P&I responds
