## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: `explain`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route.

1. **What GISIS is, and the problem it solves**
   - Global Integrated Shipping Information System - a web-based platform maintained by the IMO Secretariat
   - IMO has no inspectorate; it depends on what Member States report
   - Before GISIS, reporting was by paper circular - slow, scattered and hard to search
   - GISIS consolidates the information States are required or invited to report
   - It is an administrative platform, not an instrument, and creates no obligation of itself
2. **The aim of the system**
   - A single point of collection and dissemination for IMO-related information
   - To support Member States in implementing and enforcing IMO instruments
   - To give the Organization the evidence base for reviewing and developing instruments
   - To promote transparency and information sharing between Administrations
   - To reduce duplicated reporting by collecting each item once
3. **The functions - how it works and what it holds**
   - Organised into modules, each corresponding to a reporting obligation or information set
   - Marine casualties and incidents; piracy and armed robbery; port reception facility inadequacies; contact points; recognized organizations; ship and company particulars; port State control; and others - the list is NOT exhaustive
   - Data entered by designated national focal points; the Secretariat administers
   - Tiered access - some modules public, others restricted to Member States
   - Searchable and exportable, which is what turns records into evidence
   - It thereby facilitates data sharing among Member States
4. **The fuel oil module and MARPOL Annex VI**
   - Annex VI regulation 14 caps sulphur content - 0.50% m/m globally since 1 January 2020, 0.10% m/m in emission control areas
   - Regulation 18 - fuel oil availability and quality
   - A ship unable to obtain compliant fuel reports the fact, which is the FONAR mechanism
   - Regulation 18 also provides for notifying the Administration and port State where a supplier has failed to meet requirements
   - The GISIS module collects these reports centrally
   - Regulation 27 fuel oil consumption data reporting is the other, separate Annex VI stream
5. **Usefulness for reporting**
   - One standard channel and format, replacing ad hoc correspondence
   - The ship's own report is preserved as a record supporting its position at a later inspection
   - The port or supplier concerned is identified, so the report is actionable
   - Reports of non-availability and of non-compliant supply are separated
   - The Administration and the port State both receive the same record
6. **Usefulness for analysing**
   - Aggregation converts individual reports into a picture of where compliant fuel is genuinely unavailable
   - Identifies recurring problem ports and repeatedly failing suppliers
   - Supports port State control risk targeting rather than random inspection
   - Gives IMO the evidence base for reviewing fuel availability and the working of regulation 18
   - Distinguishes genuine market non-availability from evasion, which protects the honest operator
   - Useful commercially - bunker planning and supplier selection
7. **The limits of the data**
   - It is a reporting system - its quality depends entirely on States and ships reporting
   - Under-reporting is likely, so absence of reports is not evidence of availability
   - Module membership and structure are administrative and change without any amendment
   - A GISIS record is evidence of a report having been made, not a finding of fact
   - It supplements, and does not replace, the ship's own records and the bunker delivery note
