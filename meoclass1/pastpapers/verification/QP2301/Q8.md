## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: `explain`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route.

1. **A. What the human element means in STCW**
   - The human element is the complex of human factors bearing on safe operation - competence, health, alertness, communication, teamwork, culture and the design of the task
   - STCW 1978 as amended is the instrument; the Manila amendments entered force 1 January 2012 with full implementation by 1 January 2017
   - STCW addresses it not by exhortation but through mandatory standards in Part A of the Code
   - It works on two fronts - demonstrated competence, and the physical limits of the person
2. **A. Competence-based standards**
   - Part A tables specify competence, knowledge understanding and proficiency, methods of demonstrating and criteria for evaluating
   - Certification depends on demonstrated ability, not time served
   - Regulation I/6 - training and assessment; I/8 - quality standards systems; I/11 - revalidation and refresher training
   - Manila added the non-technical competences: leadership and teamwork, managerial skills, and bridge and engine-room resource management
   - Marine environment awareness and security training also brought in
3. **A. Fitness for duty**
   - Regulation I/9 - medical fitness standards and medical certificates
   - Regulation VIII/1 and Code section A-VIII/1 - fitness for duty and hours of rest
   - Minimum 10 hours rest in any 24-hour period and 77 hours in any 7-day period
   - Rest in no more than two periods, one of at least 6 hours, and intervals not exceeding 14 hours
   - Records of daily hours of rest to be maintained and available for inspection
   - Drug and alcohol abuse prevention, with a blood alcohol limit for those on watchkeeping duties
   - Regulation VIII/2 and A-VIII/2 - watchkeeping arrangements and principles
4. **A. Responsibility placed on companies and Administrations**
   - Regulation I/14 - company responsibilities: documented seafarers, familiarisation, effective communication, and ability to co-ordinate in an emergency
   - The company must ensure the ship is manned in compliance with the safe manning document
   - Administrations set standards, approve training, and are subject to independent evaluation
   - The ISM Code reinforces this through resources and personnel and through the safety management system
   - Exceptions to rest hours are tightly bounded and must be recorded
5. **B. The IMO guidance on fatigue**
   - MSC.1/Circ.1598, Guidelines on fatigue, dated 24 January 2019
   - It replaced the earlier fatigue guidance and is a substantial rewrite, not a reissue
   - Structured in modules addressed to the different actors who can influence fatigue
   - Module content covers the nature, causes and consequences of fatigue and its mitigation
   - Separate modules address the seafarer, the master and the company, the Administration, training providers and those who design ships
   - Guidance, not a mandatory instrument - it explains how to comply, not what is required
6. **B. How the guidance and the mandatory rules fit together**
   - The rest-hour rules set an enforceable floor; the guidance addresses the risk that remains
   - MLC 2006 runs in parallel, with hours of work and rest under its own standard
   - Compliance with minimum rest hours does NOT mean a crew is not fatigued
   - Fatigue is invisible to inspection in a way a certificate is not - hence guidance to all actors
   - Practical management: watch schedules, port call planning, workload, and a reporting culture under the safety management system
