## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: `explain`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route.

1. **a) The Convention and the harm it addresses**
   - International Convention on the Control of Harmful Anti-fouling Systems on Ships, 2001
   - In force 17 September 2008
   - Organotins, principally TBT, were highly effective but persistent and bioaccumulative
   - Documented harm to non-target shellfish at very low concentrations
   - Structure: prohibit named substances by Annex 1; prove it by Annex 4 survey and certificate
2. **a) The controls in force at this sitting**
   - Organotins acting as biocides: no application from 1 January 2003
   - From 1 January 2008 either removed, or sealed under a barrier coat
   - Cybutryne added by MEPC.331(76), adopted 17 June 2021, IN FORCE 1 JANUARY 2023
   - No application of a cybutryne system from that date
   - Existing cybutryne systems removed or over-coated at the next scheduled drydocking, and in any event within 60 months of application - a clock that has only just started
3. **a) Documents and records demonstrating compliance**
   - International Anti-fouling System Certificate - ships of 400 GT and above on international voyages
   - Record of Anti-fouling Systems, attached to and forming part of that certificate
   - Declaration on Anti-fouling System - ships of 24 m or more but under 400 GT
   - Supporting evidence: technical data sheets, paint receipts and invoices, applicator and contractor records, and the drydock specification
   - Survey on initial application and on any change of system; MEPC.358(78) guidelines apply
4. **c) Controlled depletion polymer (CDP)**
   - Rosin-based soluble matrix; seawater dissolves the binder and releases biocide
   - Dissolution is not linear - a thickening leached layer slows release with time
   - Simple and cheap; performance falls away towards the end of the interval
   - Typically suited to about 36 months and to lower-activity or slower ships
5. **c) Self-polishing copolymer (SPC)**
   - Biocide is chemically bound into the copolymer backbone
   - Seawater hydrolyses the surface layer at a controlled, near-linear rate
   - Polishes smooth in service, so roughness and release stay predictable
   - Copper acrylate, silyl acrylate and zinc acrylate chemistries; tin-free
   - Suits high-activity ships and intervals to about 60 months
6. **c) Hybrid TBT-free paints**
   - Blend of rosin and copolymer chemistry
   - Intermediate cost, intermediate polishing control, intermediate life
   - The practical compromise where an SPC cannot be justified commercially
7. **c) Fouling release coatings**
   - Non-biocidal - silicone or fluoropolymer elastomer
   - Very low surface energy, so fouling cannot adhere strongly and releases with speed
   - Needs sustained speed and high activity; poor for ships that lie idle
   - Very smooth, so a genuine fuel benefit; but soft, easily damaged and expensive
8. **Choosing between them**
   - Trading pattern, service speed, idle time and drydock interval decide it
   - Newbuilding versus retrofit, and the cost of full removal of the old system
   - Efficiency regulation now weighs on the choice as well as fuel cost
