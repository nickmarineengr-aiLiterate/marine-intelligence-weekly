## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: `legal`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route.

1. **a) What the Convention is, and the bargain it strikes**
   - Convention on Limitation of Liability for Maritime Claims, 1976 - the LLMC
   - Adopted 19 November 1976; in force 1 December 1986
   - 1996 Protocol in force 13 May 2004 raised the limits and added tacit acceptance
   - Amendments adopted under it in 2012 took effect 8 June 2015
   - India gives effect through Part XA of the Merchant Shipping Act 1958
   - Purpose: cap total liability for one occurrence by reference to tonnage
   - The bargain - near-unbreakable, but near-automatic; Article 2 applies whatever the basis of liability
2. **a) The two heads of claim under Article 6**
   - Head one - claims for loss of life or personal injury
   - Head two - any other claims, in practice property and consequential loss
   - Each has its own separate fund, calculated on tonnage
   - The life fund is the larger; unsatisfied life claims then rank rateably against the property fund
   - Passenger claims are separate again, under Article 7, and are not one of the two heads
3. **a) Who may limit, and what cannot be limited at all**
   - Article 1 - shipowners and salvors
   - 'Shipowner' means the owner, charterer, manager and operator of a seagoing ship
   - A salvor is any person rendering services in direct connexion with salvage operations
   - Persons for whose acts the shipowner or salvor is responsible
   - The liability insurer, to the same extent as the assured
   - Article 3 excepts salvage, general average contribution, CLC oil pollution, nuclear damage, and certain claims by servants
4. **b) Plaintiff and Defendant**
   - Plaintiff - the party who brings the action; here, the claimant attacking the cap
   - Defendant - the party sued, who invokes the limit and constitutes the fund
   - In a limitation action the roles can invert procedurally
   - The burden of breaking the limit lies on the claimant
5. **b) Privity - and why it is the old test**
   - Privity means knowledge and concurrence - the owner knew of and assented to the fault
   - The pre-1976 test was the owner's 'actual fault or privity'
   - It put the burden on the OWNER to prove absence of fault
   - Article 4 replaced it: the limit is broken only by the person's own act or omission, committed with intent to cause the loss, or recklessly and with knowledge that such loss would probably result
   - The burden moved to the claimant, and the standard rose to near-unbreakable
6. **b) Unlimited liability**
   - Liability not capped by any fund - the defendant pays the loss in full
   - Route one - the conduct in Article 4 is proved
   - Route two - the claim is an Article 3 excepted claim, outside the Convention
   - Route three - the forum State is not party to the Convention, or the owner fails to constitute the fund
   - The practical consequence is that the exposure becomes effectively uninsurable
