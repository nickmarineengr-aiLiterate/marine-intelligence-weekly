**The operative guidelines are NOT held.** The corpus holds `A.1185(33)`, `A.1186(33)` and
`A.1187(33)` — all 33rd Assembly, all future here — and **neither 32nd-Assembly resolution**. So the
instruments physically present are the prohibited ones and the operative one is absent.

**Rather than assert content from a resolution not held, limb b) was re-anchored on SOLAS chapter I
itself**, which *is* held and was read verbatim for this paper. That decision improved the answer in
three ways:

1. **It is where the survey types are actually created.** The Assembly guidelines give the detailed
   item lists; the survey types, intervals and anniversary windows come from the Convention.
2. **The Convention is the stronger authority.** The HSSC survey guidelines are **recommendatory**;
   SOLAS is not.
3. **It removes the Assembly boundary from the answer's critical path entirely.** Limb b) no longer
   depends on which guidelines edition is operative, so the answer is correct regardless.

`A.1156(32)` is therefore **named** as the operative edition at this sitting, and **no content is
asserted from it**.

## 6. Verification decisions taken

1. **Explain the word "now".** The second demand of limb a) turns on it: SOLAS historically
   contained **no scantling rules**, so class was *technically indispensable but legally optional*
   until II-1/3-1 made compliance a Convention requirement. Most answers describe class and never
   reach this.
2. **Quote II-1/3-1 rather than paraphrase it.** Three features of the actual wording carry marks —
   the three verbs **designed, constructed AND maintained** (so the obligation is continuing); the
   **equivalent national standards** alternative (so the Administration retains authority); and the
   cross-reference to **XI-1/1** (so "recognized" means something).
3. **State the consequence in one line.** *Loss or suspension of class becomes a SOLAS
   non-compliance, inspectable and detainable by port State control.* That is why the reference is
   there.
4. **Give the onward move one sentence, not a paragraph.** II-1/3-10 verifies the society's **own
   rules** against IMO goals — SOLAS first made the rules mandatory, then began auditing them. A
   paragraph on it would be answering a different question.
5. **Answer the rule-formation limb properly.** The stem asks about rule *formation* and most
   answers skip straight to content. Service experience and defect returns, casualty investigation,
   research and direct calculation, technical committees, and harmonisation through unified
   requirements and the Common Structural Rules.
6. **Give limb b) as a table.** The two words are used loosely in the industry and precisely in
   SOLAS, and the marks are entirely in the precision: which certificates, when, the
   displacement relationship, and the depth.
7. **State the three facts candidates get wrong.** Periodical exists **only** on equipment and radio;
   on the construction certificate the mid-term survey is **intermediate**; and the radio certificate
   has **no annual survey at all**.
8. **Answer "relevance" as reasoning, not schedule.** Certificate credibility over a five-year cycle;
   survey depth matched to the **rate and visibility** of deterioration; **radio treated hardest
   because distress communication degrades invisibly**; and harmonisation making the ladder
   practical in **one attendance** — which is the direct answer to *"toward harmonized survey and
   certification system"*.
9. **Close on regulation 11 to join the limbs.** *The surveys verify maintenance; they are not a
   substitute for it* — the same continuing obligation II-1/3-1 imposes in limb a).
10. **Name no classification society and quote no scantling, diminution percentage or acceptance
    criterion.** Society- and edition-specific, none held, and neither adds a mark.
11. **Do not set out the passenger-ship regime.** Annual and periodical are cargo-ship terms; the
    passenger certificate runs on a twelve-month renewal cycle and uses neither word. One line is
    enough.
