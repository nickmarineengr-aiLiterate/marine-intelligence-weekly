## 2. The donor delta — a missing fact

| Candidate | Sitting | Distance | Read | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `QP2503-Q9` | Mar 2025 | +23 | printed stem read | **PREFERRED DONOR** |
| `QP2507-Q9` | Jul 2025 | +27 | printed stem read | **REJECTED** — see below |

Both later stems read:

> *"…resulting in the loss of lives, vessel damage, **and an oil spill**."*

**This paper prints no oil spill.** The casualty therefore contains **three** events — a collision,
loss of life, damage to the vessels — not four. Yet the stem still asks about steps *"for the safety
of the ships **and the marine environment**"*.

**The adjudication.** The environmental limb survives, but it changes character: it is a duty to
**prevent** a discharge from a damaged ship, not a response to one that has occurred. Three
consequences were worked through the answer rather than noted and forgotten:

1. **Route step 1 names the absence explicitly**, so a reader cannot slip into the donor's framing.
2. **The environmental section is written in the preventive voice** — isolate and transfer, SOPEP
   against the *probability* of a release, national machinery on **readiness** rather than mounted.
3. **The Part XA power is presented in its preventive form.** Sections 356J–356L confer power to
   give directions for the **prevention** or containment of pollution — so the statutory machinery
   is genuinely engaged on these facts, and saying so is what allows the limb to be answered at full
   depth without inventing a spill.

**The donors' spill-response prose is not carried** — no booms, skimmers, dispersant authorisation,
shoreline clean-up or cost recovery for a response actually undertaken.

**`QP2507-Q9` was rejected for a second reason as well as distance.** It re-frames the question as
what steps should be initiated *by the coastal state*. This paper asks simply what steps should be
initiated and who should initiate them — a **wider** question reaching the master, the owner, the
Administration and the coastal State alike. Adopting the donor's framing would have narrowed this
paper's question. It was read for its coastal-State material and rejected as the route.

## 4. Temporal reversal — the sharpest instrument reversal on the paper

**Places of refuge.** The donor cites **`A.1184(33)`**, *Guidelines on places of refuge for ships in
need of assistance*, adopted **6 December 2023** and revoking `A.949(23)` from that date. That
adoption is **eight months after this sitting**, on every possible April day.

**The operative guidance at April 2023 is `A.949(23)`** (adopted 5 December 2003), read with
**`A.950(23)`** on Maritime Assistance Services. `A.1184(33)` appears **nowhere** in this object.

`known_traps.md` **Entry 24** records `A.949(23)` as *stale* and `A.1184(33)` as *current*. That is
correct for the present and **inverted here by 32 months**. Direction was checked on consumption
rather than the register being read off.

**The substance is unaffected either way**, and the answer says so: there is **no right** to a place
of refuge, the **coastal State decides**, and the decision is a case-by-case objective assessment
weighing the risk of keeping the casualty at sea against the risk of bringing her in. That was true
under both editions.

**Three further register inversions bear on this question**, each checked:

| Register says | Operative here |
|---|---|
| MS Act 2025 supersedes the 1958 Act (Entry 6) | **MS Act 1958** — the 2025 Act is 35 months future |
| DG Shipping renamed DGMA (Entry 6) | **DG Shipping** — the rename is ~37 months future, and Entry 6 expressly leaves point-in-time exam content as DG Shipping |
| `A.1184(33)` current (Entry 24) | **`A.949(23)`** |

**Checked and found stable across the gap:** the Casualty Investigation Code `MSC.255(84)`,
mandatory via SOLAS XI-1/6 since 1 January 2010 and unamended between April 2023 and the donor's
sitting; the Admiralty Act 2017, in force since 1 April 2018; and the LLMC 2012 increases, in force
8 June 2015 by resolution **`LEG.5(99)`** — not `LEG.3(91)`, which `known_traps.md` Entry 32 records
as an unrelated instrument on the fair treatment of seafarers.

**Donor sweep.** `QP2503-Q9` was swept before use for: `A.1184(33)`, 33rd Assembly, 34th Assembly,
Merchant Shipping Act 2025, DGMA, Hong Kong Convention, MEPC 80–83, 2024, 2025, 2026. **The only
substantive hit was `A.1184(33)`**, which is the reversal above. The remainder of the donor's
statutory machinery pre-dates both sittings by decades.

## 6. Verification decisions taken

1. **Decompose the casualty before listing anything.** Each element opens a different part of the
   Act, so the decomposition is what makes the answer's order the examiner's order.
2. **Answer the "who" in a dedicated closing paragraph.** The stem asks two things and most answers
   leave the second implied. Master; owner and DPA; Director-General; Coast Guard; Central
   Government; class and Administration; coastal State — each named against what he does.
3. **Put muster and search before the reports.** Lives have been lost. Reporting first is a common
   and revealing ordering error.
4. **Keep the two investigations apart, in a table.** One may cancel a certificate; the other
   exists to prevent recurrence and **expressly not** to apportion blame. Note also *which limb*
   of the very-serious-marine-casualty definition is engaged here: the **death**, since there has
   been no environmental damage at all, let alone severe damage.
5. **State no number of lives, no maritime zone, no contingency-plan provision.** The stem says
   *"few lives"* and *"off the coast of India"*; the National Oil Spill Disaster Contingency Plan is
   not held. All three would be invention rather than inference. The other vessel's flag is likewise
   treated conditionally.
