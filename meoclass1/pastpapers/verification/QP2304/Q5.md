## 4. Two evidence streams, compared provision by provision

**Stream 1 — PRIMARY, read here.** The Admiralty Act 2017 is held in the corpus at
`06-india-law-and-dg-shipping/Admiralty-Act-2017/_base/` and the following were read **verbatim**:

- **s.8** — on a judicial sale the vessel vests in the purchaser *free from all encumbrances, liens,
  attachments, registered mortgages and charges of the same nature*;
- **s.9(1)(a)–(e)** — the inter se priority of maritime liens;
- **s.9(2)** — the lien continues *notwithstanding any change of ownership or of registration or of
  flag*, and is extinguished after **one year**, with a proviso of **two years** for the wages
  claim, unless arrest or seizure has led to a forced sale;
- **s.9(3)** — time runs from discharge for wages and from accrual for the rest, *continuously
  without any suspension or interruption*, excluding any period under arrest;
- **s.9(4)** — the oil / hazardous-and-noxious-substance and nuclear exclusions;
- **s.10** — liens, then registered mortgages and charges, then all other claims; equal ranking
  within a category; various salvages in **inverse order of time**.

**Stream 2 — INTERNAL_REUSE_VERIFIED.** The 1993 Convention articles, carried from `QP2404-Q3`'s
recorded direct reading of the certified UN text.

**The two were compared provision by provision and correspond exactly:**

| Convention | Indian Act | Correspond? |
|---|---|---|
| art. 4 — the five liens and their order | s. 9(1)(a)–(e) | **Yes**, same five, same order |
| art. 8 — follows the vessel notwithstanding change of ownership, registration or flag | s. 9(2) | **Yes**, near-identical wording |
| art. 9 — one-year extinction, running from discharge or accrual | s. 9(2), 9(3) | **Yes**, with the Indian two-year wages proviso |
| art. 4(2) — oil, HNS and nuclear exclusions | s. 9(4) | **Yes** |

**This is a genuine cross-check and not a restatement.** The two texts were established
independently — one read here from the corpus, one read in another session from the UN deposit —

**`known_traps.md` Entry 21 confirmed against primary text.** Entry 21 records that the s.9(1)

the corrected order is what this answer uses, and it is stated explicitly in the study notes

## 5. Verification decisions taken

**5.1 Guarantee the order of settlement is reached.** Five demands, sixteen marks, and the order of
settlement is both the last demand and the most concrete, most markable item in the question. Every
definition is held to one short section so that the seven-step distribution is reached with time in
hand. This is the same failure mode as Q3 — a leisurely opening consuming the scoreable close — and
it was designed against in the same way.

**5.2 Give both ordered lists as numbered lists.** The five liens and the seven-step distribution.
A numbered list is faster to write under exam conditions than prose and far easier to mark.

**5.3 Hold two apparently contradictory salvage statements together.** Salvage ranks **third** in
the list of liens, *and* a salvage lien **jumps** every lien that attached before the salvage
operations, *and* several salvages rank in **inverse order of time**. All three are true. The answer
states them together with the single justification that makes them memorable — **without the salvor
there would have been no ship for the earlier claimants to claim against** — rather than leaving
them as three rules to be learned separately.

**5.4 State India's non-party position, and why it changes nothing.** India has not acceded to the
1993 Convention. A candidate who says India is a party is wrong; a candidate who therefore concludes
that the Convention scheme does not apply in India is **also** wrong. The correct position — that
the Admiralty Act 2017 reproduces the scheme domestically — is a distinguishing mark, and the Indian
statute is given its **own closing section** rather than being mixed into the Convention answer, so
the examiner can see the two instruments are known to be distinct.

**5.5 Quote no monetary figure or SDR value.** Maritime liens are not capped by the Convention and
this question does not reach limitation of liability; any figure would belong to a different
instrument.

**5.6 Describe the wreck-removal priority as optional, and do not assert it for India.** Article
12(3) gives an **optional** national priority for wreck-removal costs. Whether India has exercised
that option is not established from held sources, so the Indian order is given from s.10 instead.
