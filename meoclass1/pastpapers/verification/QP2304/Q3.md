## 3. The same-year donor — tested, not assumed

`QP2312-Q3` (December 2023) is **eight months** from this sitting and is the closest average
question in the corpus. In a batch whose standing fact is that *every donor is later than its
recipient*, a same-year object is exactly what an author wants to find.

**It was read and rejected as the route donor.** Its printed stem asks for:

- (a) the **types of losses** in marine insurance;
- the definition of a **General Average Act as per York Antwerp Rules 1994**;
- the **circumstances** under which general average can be declared;
- (b) the difference between **express and implied warranties**, with a hull and machinery example.

**One limb of four overlaps this question.** This paper asks for particular average, general
average, the circumstances of declaration, and average adjusters — and `QP2312-Q3` contains
nothing at all on the adjuster, which is a quarter of this question.

**It was retained for two specific purposes**, and both are recorded in `reuse_evidence`:

1. as a cross-check on the **declaration limb**, where it does overlap; and
2. as **same-year evidence on the which-edition question** — see §4.

The general point is recorded because it will recur across this batch: **proximity is not
donorship.** A same-year object that asks a different question donates nothing, and testing that
is cheaper than discovering it after the answer is written.

## 4. Temporal review — a boundary that was expected and does not exist

The batch anchor makes the **Merchant Shipping Act 1958 / 2025** boundary the standing statutory
trap for every 2023 paper. `TEMPORAL_AND_DONOR_VERIFICATION_PROTOCOL.md` §2 requires the answer to
a flag to be recorded **either way**, including *checked, no temporal issue*. So:

**The boundary does not bite here.** General average is **contractual**, not statutory, and the
Indian statutory limb is **section 66 of the Marine Insurance Act, 1963** — a different statute,
which the Merchant Shipping Act 2025 neither repeals nor amends. There is nothing to reverse.

**The York-Antwerp Rules are temporally inert across the whole donor gap.** All three editions in
circulation — **1994, 2004 and 2016** — existed at this sitting, the most recent having been adopted
by the CMI in May 2016, nearly seven years before. No edition falls between this sitting and the
donor's.

**And the same-year object confirms the edition position independently.** `QP2312-Q3`'s printed
stem, eight months later, expressly names the **York Antwerp Rules 1994** — an Indian examiner in
2023 setting a question on the 1994 edition *seven years after* the 2016 edition appeared. That is
direct, same-year, printed evidence that **currency does not determine which edition governs**, and
it is why this answer says in terms that the contract fixes the edition and that the newest edition
never applies automatically.

**Casualty illustrations were date-checked before use.** *Maersk Honam* is March 2018 (five years
before the sitting) and *Ever Given* is March 2021 (two years before). Both are safe. No casualty
after April 2023 is used anywhere.

**Donor sweep.** A token sweep of `QP2509-Q3` was run before reading any of it into this answer,
for: Merchant Shipping Act 2025, 2024, 2025, 2026, 34th Assembly, Hong Kong Convention, *current*,

## 5. Verification decisions taken

1. **Answer "circumstances" as conditions, not casualties.** This is the single most commonly
   misread word in the question. The route gives the **seven Rule A elements as a numbered test**,
   each with the way a claim fails on it, and only then names the typical occasions — jettison,
   firefighting damage, voluntary stranding, refloating damage, port of refuge — as illustration.
2. **Guarantee the adjuster is reached.** Four demands and sixteen marks means the definitions must
   be tight. The route holds each definition to one section and gives the adjuster a section of his
   own, in six ordered moves.
3. **Add a section the donor does not have.** A short section on **security before release** — lien,
   average bond, average guarantee, deposit — was added between the declaration test and the
   adjuster. It is not in the printed stem, but it is how general average is actually experienced by
   cargo, it is where the adjuster's second function sits, and it makes the transition from
   declaration to adjustment intelligible.
4. **Quote Rule A and nothing else.** Rule A is short, settled and identical across all three
   editions in circulation at this sitting. The numbered Rules differ in wording between editions
   and no edition text is held, so they are described **by subject** rather than quoted.
5. **State the two adjuster negatives explicitly.** He does **not** declare general average — the
   shipowner does — and he is **not** the shipowner's advocate despite being appointed and paid by
   him. Both are single-sentence marks and both are commonly got wrong.
6. **Quote no contributory-value formula, percentage or figure.** Values are ascertained case by
   case at the termination of the adventure. The same restraint is applied to the level of salvage
   security demanded in any particular casualty.
