## 6. Verification decisions taken

1. **Route the two limbs from different donors and reverse them separately.** They carry
   incommensurable reversal loads — one high and concentrated, one nil.
2. **Lead limb a) with the division of labour, not a list of conventions.** *IMO regulates the
   seafarer as the operator of a ship; the ILO regulates him as a worker; and hours of rest is where
   the two meet.* Everything else hangs on that sentence, and without it the limb reads as recall.
3. **Honour the trailing phrase.** *"such as maritime labour and welfare"* gets the MLC Titles real
   content — a table with substance in the labour and welfare rows — rather than a mention.
4. **State the MLC amendment position explicitly, using the adopted / in-force distinction.**
   "Adopted but not yet in force" is a phrase that earns marks in itself. It is also the **same
   distinction Q1 on this paper turns on**, in a different organisation, and the study notes say so:
   two questions on one paper testing one idea is not a coincidence.
5. **Give limb B full treatment despite its smaller share.** It is one named circular with six
   modules and it is the easiest part of the question to score completely; omitting it or reducing
   it to two lines is the commonest failure.
6. **Join the two limbs at the end.** The mandatory rest formula and the guidance about it are the
   same subject seen twice, and the closing point — *compliance with the formula is not the same as
   being rested*, so **an always-exactly-compliant rest record is a warning rather than a
   reassurance** — is where the question's two halves actually meet.
7. **State no count of consolidated ILO instruments.** Figures in the sixties are commonly quoted
   and vary between sources; the answer says *"a large body of earlier ILO maritime instruments"*.
8. **Assert no ratification count for the MLC or STCW, and cite no Indian implementing instrument.**
   Both counts are status facts that date; India's MLC implementation sits under the MS Act 1958 and
   DG Shipping circulars, neither held, and the printed question sets no Indian limb.
