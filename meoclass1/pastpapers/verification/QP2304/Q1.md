| Read | What it establishes |

## 4. Temporal reversal — the discriminating fact of the question

**The donor is 29 months later and its amendment prose is wrong here.** `QP2509-Q1` states that the
operative edition is **amendment 07-23, mandatory from 1 January 2025**. That is **21 months after
this sitting**. It also devotes an unresolved item and a re-verification flag to **amendment 08-25**,
adopted at MSC 110 in June 2025 — **26 months after this sitting**. Neither appears anywhere in this
object.

**What was established from primary text instead**, by reading the two adopting resolutions:

| Resolution | Adopted | Deemed accepted | In force | Voluntary from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `MSC.462(101)` — amendment **05-19** | 13 June 2019 | 1 July 2020 | **1 January 2021** | 1 January 2020 |
| `MSC.500(105)` — amendment **06-21** | 28 April 2022 | **1 June 2023** | **1 December 2023** | **1 January 2023** |

**The sitting falls inside two windows at once.** April 2023 is:

- **after** 05-19 entered into force, so that amendment is the **mandatory** text;
- **after** the 1 January 2023 opening of the 06-21 **voluntary** window;
- **before** the 1 June 2023 deemed acceptance of 06-21;
- **before** the 1 December 2023 entry into force of 06-21.

So at this examination amendment 06-21 is **adopted, not yet accepted, not in force, and
voluntarily applicable** — four true statements at once. The answer states all four and collapses
none of them into "in force". This is the `adopted ≠ accepted ≠ in force` distinction landing
precisely inside the sitting window, and it is the most examinable temporal fact on the paper.

**The substantive answer is protected from the boundary.** Both bauxite schedules were introduced
by **05-19**, which *is* mandatory here. A candidate answering from the mandatory text answers
correctly whichever side of 1 December 2023 the reader stands on. The boundary is stated because it
is true and examinable, **not** because the marks turn on it — and that distinction is itself
recorded, because a temporal fact that changes no mark is easy to over-weight.

**The 10% indicative limit.** It belongs to `CCC.1/Circ.2` of 20 October 2015 and was superseded by
`Rev.1` in 2017. It appears in this answer only as dated history and is expressly marked superseded
in three places — the answer, the study notes and `temporal_review`. Its appearance as an operative
limit would be a regression.

## 5. Verification decisions taken

1. **State the amendment position in four parts, not one.** Tested against the resolution text; the
   four statements are each supported by a numbered operative clause.
2. **Quote the schedule's hazard paragraph closely.** It is the only place where the Code itself
   distinguishes liquefaction from dynamic separation, and paraphrase loses the distinction.
3. **State bulk density and stowage factor; do not state TML.** The first two are printed in the
   schedule read; the third is not a property of the commodity.
4. **Do not restate the Modified Proctor/Fagerberg saturation figures.** They sit in a test
   procedure annexed to a circular not held, and in an IMSBC appendix not read for this paper. The
   answer requires TML to be determined by an approved test procedure without asserting the
   procedure's internal numbers. This is a *narrowing* of the donor, which did state them from the
   circular it had read.
5. **Carry the casualty narrative as declared internal reuse.** Not re-read here; labelled as such.
