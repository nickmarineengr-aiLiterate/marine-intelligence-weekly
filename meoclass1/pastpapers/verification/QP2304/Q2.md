## 4. The forward-contamination trap, and why it is the worst class

`known_traps.md` **Entry 12** records, correctly for the present:

> *"the 2010 HNS Protocol is not yet in force (confirmed entry into force **29 November 2027**)"*

and the corpus's own liability-and-compensation instrument log records, also correctly for the
present:

> *"12 contracting States as of April 2026 … All three EIF conditions met **2026-05-29** → enters
> into force **2027-11-29**."*

**Both statements are true. Both are prohibited here.** The entry-into-force date is **55 months
after this sitting**, and the conditions were not met until **37 months after it**. A candidate
sitting this paper in April 2023 could not have known either, and no examiner could have marked
them.

This is the worst class of forward contamination precisely because **it does not look like an
error**. A confident sentence giving a specific entry-into-force date reads as diligence. It would
survive a casual review, and it would be wrong.

**Three exclusions follow, and each is recorded in the answer:**

| Excluded | Ground |
|---|---|
| Any entry-into-force **date** | Post-sitting; no such date existed in April 2023 |
| Any **count of Contracting States** | The April 2023 tally is not established from held sources; the corpus figure is an April 2026 one |
| Any **numerical threshold** from the Protocol's entry-into-force article | The Protocol text is not held by the corpus |

**On the third point the answer narrows the anchor deliberately.** The paper anchor as first
drafted said Q2 would "set out the article 21 conditions". On review that was tightened: the
Protocol text is not held, so asserting the specific figures — a number of States, a tonnage
qualification, a contributing-cargo quantity — would be reciting recalled numbers as though they
were read. **The answer instead states the three *kinds* of condition and puts a figure on none of
them**, and the anchor was amended to match. Recorded here because an anchor and an answer that
disagree is itself a defect.

## 5. Establishing the status by entailment

The status statement — *adopted, not in force, conditions unmet at April 2023* — was not taken on
trust. It is established by **entailment**:

> `QP2503-Q6` records that the Convention was not in force at **March 2025**. An instrument that is
> not in force in March 2025 cannot have been in force in April 2023.

The entailment runs in the safe direction: a *later* record of non-entry-into-force necessarily
fixes the *earlier* position. Had the donor recorded the Convention as in force, no such inference
would have been available and the claim would have had to be established independently.

The corpus log independently confirms the direction, since it records the conditions as met only in
2026 — but that document is used here **only** to confirm the direction of the entailment, and none
of its figures or dates is reproduced in the answer.

## 6. Verification decisions taken

1. **Preserve the examiner's premise; adjudicate it; contradict nothing.** The stem asserts the
   Convention is "expected shortly to come into force". The answer records that as the examiner's
   position, states the true position at the sitting, and offers no prediction. Neither repeating
   the prediction as fact nor correcting the examiner would score.
2. **Answer limb (b) legally, then technically.** The stem says "types of pollution", which reads as
   a technical question and is a legal one. The four heads of article 1.6 are given first, and the
   four chemical risk types — fire and explosion, toxicity, contamination, reactivity — are named
   alongside so both readings are satisfied.
3. **Keep "probably" in limb (c).** The examiner hedged deliberately; the requirement depends on the
   cargo of the day, and explaining the dependency *is* the mark. CLC applicability is stated
   conditionally throughout — she is a CLC ship *on a voyage carrying persistent oil in bulk*, never
   by nature.
4. **Apply `known_traps.md` Entry 12 in its non-temporal half.** CLC reaches only **persistent
   hydrocarbon mineral oil**; a persistent oil of animal or vegetable origin is an HNS. That half of
   the entry is not date-dependent and is used; the entry-into-force half is excluded.
5. **Reproduce no article 9 or article 14 monetary figure.** The Protocol text is not held and the
   printed limbs do not require them; the answer describes the shape — a bulk scale, a higher
   packaged scale, an overall Fund ceiling — and puts no number on any of them.
