## 4. Known-trap adjudication — the live positive control

`known_traps.md` §1 auto-scans the phrase **"strict liability on the registered owner"** because
it is *wrong for the Bunkers Convention*, whose Article 1(3) definition of "ship-owner" includes
the bareboat charterer, manager and operator jointly and severally, and which puts only the
**insurance** duty on the registered owner alone.

**That phrase is correct for Nairobi.** Article 10 does make the registered owner strictly
liable. The trap file records this collision expressly — QP2601-Q4 tripped it with a sentence
about Nairobi that was *correct*, and the resolution taken there was to **keep the scanner at
full strength and change the wording**, so that no protection was given up.

**This answer follows that precedent.** Every occurrence is written as
*"makes the registered owner strictly liable"*. The scanned phrase does not appear.

This is the second paper to meet the collision. The trap file states that the decision to move
the entry to `GREP: SKIP` must **not** be made on one paper's evidence; this record notes the
second data point and makes no change.

Other traps checked: §11 (MS Act 2025 — not cited), §13 (source provenance — the spec records an
unverified third-party scan), §14 (host branding — absent).
