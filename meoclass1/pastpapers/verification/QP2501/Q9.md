## 4. The evidence gap — and the citation that was deliberately not made

The corpus IMSBC instrument log states, in its own words:

> *"Known gap: amendments after 06-21 (the 07-23 cycle and any 2025 adoption) — `unknown —
> verification required` (RQ-13). The chain above is NOT claimed complete to 2026 until that
> check is done."*

This is the corpus declining to certify precisely the amendment this question turns on. Two
decisions follow, both deliberate:

1. **The adopting resolution number for 07-23 is not cited anywhere** — not in `sources`, not in
   `regulations`, not in the answer, not in a flashcard. A resolution number recalled from memory
   would be plausible, specific and unfalsifiable to a reader, which is exactly the profile of
   the invented citation the protocol forbids. The amendment is referred to throughout by its
   **amendment designation**, `07-23`, which is how the Code and the industry identify it.
2. **The in-force date is carried as authoritative secondary with a `B_CURRENCY_CHECK`**, not as
   a verified fact — even though it is the single most examinable date on the question.

A second `B_CURRENCY_CHECK` records that the *absence* of any later amendment cannot be confirmed
from a corpus whose chain is open. That is a negative claim without a live positive control, and
it is flagged rather than asserted.

**What the corpus does supply and what was used:** the base text `MSC.268(85)` and six amendment
resolutions; the group definitions at section 1.7; and the document-side boundary that *"the
shipper's declaration is document-side, master may do complementary can test"* — which is the
source of the can-test treatment in §3 of the answer and card C9.

## Addendum - 2026-08-12 laptop integration review

**Temporally critical claim independently corroborated.** The sharpest date on this paper - that
**IMSBC Code amendment 07-23 entered into force on 1 January 2025**, in the sitting month itself,
with voluntary application permitted from 1 January 2024 - was re-checked during laptop review
against four independent industry sources (UK P&I, Lloyd's Register class news, IRClass technical
circular, Britannia P&I). All four agree, and all four attribute the amendment to resolution
**MSC.539(107)**, adopted at MSC 107 in June 2023.

The same sources confirm that 07-23 is the amendment that **introduced the definition of "dynamic
separation"** and the cargo group "cargoes which may undergo dynamic separation" into the Code. That
materially supports this answer's treatment of the January 2015 bauxite casualty as dynamic
separation rather than classic liquefaction, because the concept entered the Code by that route.

**The flag is retained, not closed.** The corroboration is multiple and consistent but remains
**authoritative secondary** - MSC.539(107) itself was not read, and the corpus does not hold it. The
provenance class is therefore unchanged; what has changed is that the claim is now corroborated
rather than single-sourced.
