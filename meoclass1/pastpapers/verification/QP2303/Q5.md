2. **A) Indicated power to brake power - the engine's own losses** _(A.)_ - Indicated power from the indicator diagram - work on the piston per cycle; The only power in the chain measured inside the engine; Losses: friction at rings, crosshead, crankpin and main bearings - much the largest; Engine-driven auxiliaries - camshaft, pumps, governor, scavenge arrangement; Pumping and windage within the engine; The ratio is mechanical efficiency; It is not constant - friction is steady while output varies, so the engine is worst at low load
3. **A) Brake power to shaft power to delivered power - the transmission** _(A.)_ - Thrust block, intermediate and line shaft bearings, and the stern tube - usually the largest single element; Reduction gearing where fitted; the electrical chain in a diesel-electric plant; Shaft power measured by torsionmeter - torque × angular velocity; The last point in the chain where power can be measured directly; Delivered power is what reaches the propeller and cannot be measured - it is inferred; The ratio is transmission or shaft efficiency, and it is sensitive to alignment
4. **A) Delivered power to thrust power to effective power - the propeller and the hull** _(A.)_ - Open-water efficiency - accelerated water astern, rotational slipstream energy, blade friction. Normally the biggest loss in the chain; Thrust power = thrust × SPEED OF ADVANCE, not ship speed; Relative rotative efficiency - the propeller behind the hull is not the propeller in open water; Wake fraction: the hull drags water along, so the propeller works in slower water - it HELPS; Thrust deduction fraction: the working propeller raises the hull's resistance - it HURTS; Together they give hull efficiency, which can exceed unity, and the reason is explainable; Effective power = resistance × ship speed - the destination
5. **A) The efficiencies pulled together - the propulsive coefficient** _(A.)_ - Quasi-propulsive coefficient = effective ÷ delivered - hull and propeller only; Propulsive coefficient = effective ÷ shaft or brake - the whole installation; The second is the first multiplied by the transmission efficiency; No typical value is offered for any efficiency - all are installation specific; The diagnostic use: shaft power up for the same speed means hull or propeller; Fuel up for the same shaft power means the engine
6. **B) What sea trials are for, and the conditions that make them valid** _(B).)_ - To verify by measurement what has until then been predicted; Four purposes at once: contractual, statutory, operational and baseline; Deep, unrestricted water - shallow water changes resistance, squat and turning; Low sea state and wind, recorded; the specified loading condition and trim; A steady approach before the manoeuvre begins; Reciprocal headings, so current and tide cancel in the mean; Clean hull and propeller, and calibrated instruments
7. **B) The navigation trials, and what each establishes** _(B).)_ - Progressive speed trial - the power-speed curve, the contract speed, and the service datum; Endurance and consumption - specific fuel consumption against the guarantee; Turning circle - advance, transfer, tactical diameter, drift angle, speed loss. Both sides; Zig-zag - overshoot angles, giving yaw-checking and course-changing ability; Spiral, reverse spiral and pull-out - course stability and the width of the loop; Crash stop - head reach, track reach and time to dead in the water; Astern and minimum steerage trials - astern power, consecutive starts, minimum manoeuvring speed; Steering gear trial - hard over to hard over, main and auxiliary, changeover and emergency steering; Anchoring trial - windlass capability and brake holding power
