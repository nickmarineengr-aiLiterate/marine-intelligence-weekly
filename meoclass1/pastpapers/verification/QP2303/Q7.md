**Answer route:**

1. **Where the power comes from, and what an inspection is before clear grounds exist** _(a))_ - The power is in the conventions - SOLAS I/19, MARPOL 5 and 6, Load Lines 21, STCW X and I/4, MLC Title 5.2; IMO's Procedures tell an officer how to exercise it and supply the vocabulary - they do not create it; The adopting resolution only invites Governments to implement them; The initial inspection is limited: certificates and documents, and the general condition of the ship; If the certificates are in order and nothing contradicts them, the inspection ends there; Clear grounds is therefore the boundary between an hour's visit and a day alongside
2. **'Clear grounds' - the definition, and the two words that carry it** _(a))_ - Evidence that ship, equipment or crew does not correspond substantially with convention requirements; OR that master or crew are not familiar with essential shipboard procedures for safety or pollution prevention; EVIDENCE excludes suspicion, reputation, rumour and the flag - something must be observed; SUBSTANTIALLY excludes the trivial, the cosmetic and the isolated minor defect; Crew unfamiliarity is a free-standing head - a materially perfect ship can be opened up on it alone
3. **The circumstances that create clear grounds - with examples** _(a))_ - Documents - certificates absent, expired, invalid, mismatched or apparently falsified; an Oil Record Book that will not reconcile; Equipment - required equipment absent, or present but bypassed, blanked, corroded or plainly unmaintained; Condition - heavy corrosion, unsafe machinery spaces, unsanitary accommodation; a report from a pilot or terminal; People - crew unable to start the emergency generator or change over the steering gear; rest hours inconsistent with the watch pattern; ISM-related findings are a class apart - certificates missing or mismatched, uncontrolled or superseded documentation, a master who cannot explain his authority; They point at the system, so nothing else on board can be relied on - which widens the inspection
4. **What follows once clear grounds exist** _(a))_ - A more detailed inspection, scoped by the grounds - not a general re-survey; Rectification, mostly before departure or within a stated period; Detention only on the test: clearly hazardous to safety, health or the environment, or so numerous as to show systemic failure; Notification to the flag State in sufficient detail, and to the recognized organization that issued the certificates; Proceeding to a repair port on conditions where the defect cannot be remedied where she lies; The standing limits: avoid undue detention or delay, with compensation under MARPOL article 7(2); Operated regionally - the Indian Ocean MOU, DG Shipping, and the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958
5. **The three terms that must be separated** _(b))_ - Define all three by what they act upon; Correction acts on the non-conformity itself - fix the thing that is wrong; Corrective action acts on the cause of a DETECTED non-conformity - stop it happening again; Preventive action acts on the cause of a POTENTIAL non-conformity - stop it happening at all; The difference reduces to one word: DETECTED against POTENTIAL; Correction is NOT corrective action - the commonest failure in the subject; They are not degrees of the same thing
6. **Corrective action - what it actually involves** _(b))_ - The ISM safety management system must provide for reporting, investigation and analysis, and for corrective action; Contain and correct - and do not stop there; Establish what happened before forming a view about why; Find the cause and go past the first plausible one - 'he opened the wrong valve' is where the enquiry stopped; Decide the action against the cause, not against the event; assign an owner and a date; VERIFY EFFECTIVENESS - not was it done, but did it work. The step most often omitted; Feed it back through management review and to the fleet; Inputs: audit non-conformities, PSC deficiencies and detentions, class conditions, accidents, defect reports
7. **Preventive action - where it comes from** _(b))_ - Near misses and hazardous occurrences - the most valuable input, and the first casualty of a blame culture; Risk assessment before a job, a modification or an unusual operation; Audit observations that are not non-conformities but point at a weakness; Trend analysis - alarm counts, defect backlogs, repeated minor findings, deteriorating oil analysis; Fleet and industry experience - a sister ship's casualty, a circular, a service letter, a published report; Other ships' PSC records - a fleet-wide deficiency is a warning to the uninspected; It is the harder discipline: it competes against visibly urgent work, and when it succeeds nothing happens
8. **The two worked through one defect, and how the limbs connect** _(b))_ - A sea water cooler tube fails and floods the lubricating oil system; Correction - plug or renew the tube, renew the oil. The cause is untouched; Corrective action - find why it wasted, change the interval, protection or treatment, and verify at the next opening; Preventive action - inspect every other cooler of the same material and duty, here and on the sisters, before one leaks; A PSC deficiency is corrective action for this ship; and preventive action for every ship not yet inspected; A company running on corrective action alone will be detained at the rate its worst ship deserves
