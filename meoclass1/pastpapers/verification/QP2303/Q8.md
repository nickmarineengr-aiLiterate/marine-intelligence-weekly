**Answer route:**

1. **What IACS is and how it is organised** _(a))_ - A non-governmental association of classification societies with consultative status at IMO; It makes no law and issues no certificate - its output reaches ships through Members' Rules or through Administrations relying on it; Council - governing body, one Representative per Member; strategy, budget, admission, adopts Resolutions; General Policy Group - executive deputy to Council, the Members' Permanent Representatives, runs the work programme; Panels and Expert Groups - the technical bodies that draft and revise the Resolutions; Permanent Secretariat in London; a quality oversight function over Members' quality systems; No membership figure and no panel count is stated - both have changed and neither is needed
2. **UR, UI and PR - what each one acts upon** _(a))_ - All three are IACS Resolutions; they differ in what they act upon; UR acts on the society's own Rules - a minimum technical requirement each Member builds in; A UR reaches a ship through class rules, never directly; UI acts on an IMO convention - a common interpretation where a provision is vague or left to the Administration; A UI interprets; it does not create a class requirement; PR acts on the society itself - survey conduct, transfer of class, reporting, outstanding conditions; The test: ship, convention, society
3. **Does it signify to owners and operators - and why** _(a))_ - Yes, substantially - and the question wants reasons, not a yes; A UR is a floor - a predictable standard whichever Member classes the ship; But a new UR applied to existing ships is an unbudgeted fleet cost; No rule-shopping between Members for a materially softer technical rule; A UI reduces delay - the cost of a disputed interpretation is the ship alongside at port State control; A PR has commercial bite - transfer of class does not shed outstanding conditions or overdue surveys; The limit: IACS Resolutions bind Members, not owners, and a non-IACS society is outside the system
4. **What a Recognized Organization is** _(b))_ - An organization to which a flag Administration has delegated statutory survey and certification work; Most commonly, but not necessarily, a classification society; The delegation is of function, not of responsibility - the Administration remains answerable; Class and RO are different capacities - private and contractual against public and statutory; A society is an RO only for the flag that delegated, and only to the extent delegated; The same surveyor may act in both capacities in one attendance; Recognition and authorization are two separate acts - assessment, then specified delegation
5. **The RO Code and its salient features** _(b))_ - Adopted by MSC.349(92) of 21 June 2013 and by MEPC.237(65); A harmonised, transparent and independent standard replacing scattered Assembly criteria; Mandatory from 1 January 2015 by MSC.350(92), MSC.356(92) and MEPC.238(65); Amending SOLAS, MARPOL Annexes I and II and the 1988 Load Line Protocol; SOLAS XI-1/1 is the hook; Part 1 general, Part 2 mandatory requirements, Part 3 oversight guidelines; Parts 1 and 2 bind; Part 3 is recommendatory - the most examinable structural fact; On the organization: legal personality, competence, ethics, independence, resources, subcontracting control, complaints and appeals, vertical contract audit, and a quality system certified by an accredited independent body; On the flag State: rest it on its conventional basis, SPECIFY the functions, make a formal written agreement, and notify the Secretary-General
6. **How Administrations monitor Recognized Organizations** _(b))_ - Before: assess against Part 2, confirm capability, and reduce the delegation to a written agreement with scope and limits; During, mechanism one - ACCESS to the quality system, internal instructions, fleet documentation and casualty statistics; Mechanism two - a system to ensure adequacy: reporting, additional flag State inspections, consultation between the authorized organizations; Mechanism three - auditing, including vertical contract audits and witnessed surveys, with reliance permitted on accredited independent audits; Mechanism four - a managed programme with objectives, resources, records and periodic review; Performance indicators: port State detentions, internal audits, third-party quality audits, previous monitoring, and the condition of the ships certified; The authorization is function-specific, so it can be narrowed as readily as withdrawn; failure to report is a compliance failure; The Administration is itself audited under the Member State Audit Scheme against the III Code, A.1070(28), and the obligations at A.1157(32); The work may be delegated; the responsibility cannot
