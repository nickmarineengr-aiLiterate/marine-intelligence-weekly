**Answer route:**

1. **What decarbonisation means in shipping** - The progressive reduction, towards elimination, of greenhouse gas emissions from the operation of ships; Principally CO2, with methane and nitrous oxide, increasingly assessed across the whole fuel life cycle; A climate measure, not an air-quality measure - SOx and NOx are local, CO2 is global; CO2 cannot be scrubbed out - it is the intended product of burning carbon; So the only routes are burn less, burn less carbon, or burn none; An efficiency problem before it is a fuel problem - which is why both short-term measures are efficiency measures; International shipping was excluded from national commitments, which is why IMO carries the mandate
2. **The instrument that frames it at this sitting** - The Initial IMO Strategy on reduction of GHG emissions from ships, resolution MEPC.304(72), adopted 2018; A strategy, not a regulation - nothing in it binds a ship; What binds a ship is what is written into MARPOL Annex VI as a result of it; It carries its own programme of follow-up actions - short-term, mid-term and long-term; The measures' own founding resolution expressly recalls the adoption of the Initial Strategy in its preamble; So the two short-term measures are, in terms, delivered under that Strategy
3. **The levels of ambition** - Three levels, all against a 2008 baseline; One - peaking: GHG emissions should peak as soon as possible and decline thereafter; Two - carbon intensity: CO2 per transport work down at least 40 per cent by 2030, pursuing 70 per cent by 2050; Three - total annual GHG: at least 50 per cent lower by 2050; Phase-out as soon as possible in this century, consistent with the Paris temperature goals; Level two is a RATIO; level three is a QUANTITY; A fleet can meet the intensity ambition and go backwards on the absolute one by carrying more
4. **The pathway - short-term, mid-term and long-term** - Short-term - agreed and in force: the design index, the data collection system, and EEXI and CII with the enhanced SEEMP; Mid-term - CANDIDATE measures under consideration, nothing adopted at this sitting; An implementation programme for alternative low-carbon and zero-carbon fuels; Further operational efficiency measures; new and innovative emission reduction mechanisms, possibly including market-based measures; R&D and capacity building; Long-term - directional: zero-carbon or fossil-free fuels, and further innovative mechanisms; Two running principles: no more favourable treatment, and assessment of impacts on States; The honest position: the mid-term mechanism was unresolved at this sitting
5. **The first short-term measure - the Energy Efficiency Existing Ship Index** - Both measures were introduced by MEPC.328(76), in force 1 November 2022, applying from 1 January 2023; Technical, one-off, design-based - it extends the new-ship design discipline to existing ships; It computes the ship's theoretical CO2 per tonne-mile at a reference condition - a calculation, not a measurement; Attained EEXI must not exceed required EEXI, derived from the reference line with a reduction factor. No factor quoted; Demonstrated once, at the first periodical survey on or after 1 January 2023, endorsed on the IEEC; Compliance route one: engine or shaft power limitation, sealed and verifiable, with an overridable power reserve the master may release for safety, recorded when used; Route two: technical improvement - energy-saving devices, propeller and hull work, waste heat recovery. Route three: a lower-carbon fuel; The honest criticism: it says what the ship is CAPABLE of, not what she does
6. **The second short-term measure - the Carbon Intensity Indicator** - Operational, annual, continuing - it measures what the ship actually did; The attained annual operational CII: CO2 from fuel actually consumed, over capacity and distance actually sailed; The data comes from the fuel oil consumption data collection system, which already existed; Compared with a required CII from a reference line with an annual reduction factor that tightens each year; Result: a rating A to E, documented and visible to charterers and financiers; D for three consecutive years, or E for one year - a plan of corrective actions in the SEEMP, approved; The enhanced SEEMP carries the implementation plan, the calculation methodology and the corrective action plan, and is verified; Improving it: speed above all, then hull and propeller condition, trim, weather routeing, port efficiency, shore power; The honest criticism: it divides by capacity and distance, not by cargo carried - and the regulation provides for review
