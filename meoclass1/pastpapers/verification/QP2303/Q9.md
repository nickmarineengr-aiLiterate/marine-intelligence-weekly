Carried as the constraint that governs how the technique may be used, not as a qualification appended to it.
- ENGINEERING AND ANALYTICAL-CHEMISTRY REFERENCE - <b>Fourier Transform Infrared spectroscopy</b>: infrared energy is passed through a thin film of oil, molecular bonds absorb at wavenumbers characteristic of the bond, the interferogram is Fourier transformed into a spectrum, and that spectrum is compared against the spectrum of the same oil unused. **Answer route:**

1. **Why the oil is analysed, and what the four techniques are for** - A running machine is a sealed box; the oil is the only thing that comes out of it; It has been in contact with every working surface inside; Is the machine wearing? - spectrometry and particle count; Is the oil still fit? - FTIR, and BN with AN; Is anything getting in? - all four, from different directions; The governing rule: trend against that machine's own history, not the absolute value
2. **A) Elemental (spectrometric) analysis** _(A))_ - Sample excited in a plasma or across a rotating-disc electrode arc; Each element emits at characteristic wavelengths; intensity gives concentration in ppm; Wear metals - iron from liners, rings, gears, shafting; copper, lead, tin from bearings; chromium from rings and plating; aluminium from pistons; Contaminants - silicon from dirt and abrasive; sodium and boron from coolant; vanadium and nickel from residual fuel; Additive elements - calcium and magnesium (detergent), zinc and phosphorus (anti-wear); Additives falling = depletion; rising = the wrong oil was added; Silicon reads two ways - dirt ingress in service, or sealant and antifoam residue right after an overhaul
3. **What spectrometry cannot see, and why that governs its use** _(A))_ - The method needs complete atomisation of the particle in the excitation source; Coarse particles are not fully atomised, so response falls away with increasing size; It therefore reports essentially the fine fraction only; Normal rubbing wear is fine and is seen well; Bearing fatigue, spalling, scuffing and gear pitting are coarse and may not be seen; So a clean spectrometric report is NOT evidence of a healthy machine; Which is exactly why particle count, ferrography and plug inspection sit alongside it
4. **B) Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy** _(B))_ - Infrared through a thin film; molecular bonds absorb at characteristic wavenumbers; All wavelengths gathered at once as an interferogram and Fourier transformed to a spectrum; A DIFFERENTIAL measurement - compared against the spectrum of the same oil unused; Practical consequence: retain a sealed sample of every oil delivery; Detects oxidation, nitration, sulphation, water and glycol; Soot appears as a broad baseline rise, not a discrete peak; Also fuel dilution and additive depletion; Fast, small sample, no reagents - but semi-quantitative and blind to wear metals and particles
5. **C) Particle count** _(C))_ - Solid particles counted and sized, usually by optical light obscuration; Pore blockage where the oil is too dark; microscopy is the referee method; Reported as counts per millilitre in cumulative size bands, condensed to a cleanliness code; Decisive for hydraulic, servo and control systems where clearances are microns; The direct measure of filtration and centrifuge performance and of ingress; It sees the coarse particles spectrometry misses - the two are complementary; Limits: counts water droplets and air bubbles as particles; never says what they are
6. **D) Base Number against Acid Number** _(D))_ - Both titrations, both in mg KOH per gramme - which is why they are confused; BN = alkaline reserve from overbased detergent additives; it FALLS in service; AN = acidic constituents from combustion and oxidation; it RISES; An oil can carry both at once - separate titrations of separate species; Two standard BN methods give systematically different values - never trend them together; BN governs cylinder and trunk-piston oils, matched to the sulphur actually burned; AN governs turbine, hydraulic, gear and stern tube oils, which carry no reserve; Since the 0.50 per cent limit of 1 January 2020, BN is matched to the fuel in use - and over-alkalinity deposits abrasive calcium ash
7. **Correct interpretation - reading the four results together** - No single result diagnoses anything; pairs and trends do; Iron up + residual BN near exhaustion = corrosive wear (in a cylinder, cold corrosion); Iron up + BN healthy = NOT corrosion; more oil will make it worse; Particle count up + wear metals normal = filtration or centrifuge failure, or ingress; Particle count up + spectrometry clean = coarse debris - the most dangerous case; Silicon up + iron and chromium up = abrasive dirt ingress; Sodium and boron up + glycol and water on FTIR = coolant leak; Vanadium and nickel + fuel on FTIR + viscosity down = fuel dilution; Oxidation up + AN up + no wear metals = the oil is degrading, the machine is fine; Additive elements up = the wrong oil was added; Two disciplines: trend, do not spot-read; and rate of change beats level
8. **Corrective measures - what is actually done** - The sequence: verify - confirm physically - correct the cause - then correct the oil; Re-sample before acting - a large share of alarming results are sampling artefacts; Confirm physically - magnetic plugs, filters cut open, centrifuge sludge, ferrography for debris shape, direct inspection; Abrasive ingress - find the entry path (filters, seals, breathers), not just more filtration; Coolant leak - pressure-test coolers, jackets, covers; glycol destroys bearing metal; Fuel dilution - injection equipment, timing, combustion; then renew, because diluted oil cannot be recovered; Water - find the source and run the purifier correctly (gravity disc, throughput, temperature); Rising particle count - filters, differential pressure, bypass, purifier operation; then the ingress path; Low residual BN in cylinder oil - raise feed rate or move to a higher-BN oil matched to fuel sulphur, and confirm by scrape-down; High AN and oxidation - find the cause, then renew, cleaning the system before recharging; Additive depletion - renew; additives cannot be topped up; Record and close the loop in the PMS - unrecorded action destroys the trend; Standing caution: every limit comes from the builder and the oil supplier for THAT installation
9. **Sampling and trending - without which none of it means anything** - Same point, same way, same interval; machine at temperature and oil circulating; From a live line upstream of the filter, never the drain or the tank bottom; Flush the point; use clean dedicated bottles - a dirty bottle condemns a healthy machine; Retain a sample of every new oil - FTIR needs its reference; Label fully: ship, machine, point, hours since change and since overhaul, grade, top-ups; Trend, and read the rate of change rather than the level; Plan it in the PMS under the ISM maintenance regime at chapter 10; Close: analysis does not maintain machinery - it tells you where to look
