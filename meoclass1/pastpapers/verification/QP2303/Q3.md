**Answer route:**

1. **What 'perils of the sea' actually means** - The Rules for Construction of Policy in the Schedule to the Marine Insurance Act, 1963; Fortuitous accidents or casualties of the seas; NOT the ordinary action of the winds and waves; Test one - fortuity: accidental, not inevitable, not the natural consequence of going to sea; Test two - of the sea, not merely at sea: the sea must be the cause; Covered: stranding, grounding, sinking, foundering, collision, striking a submerged object, extraordinary heavy weather, accidental entry of seawater, capsizing; Excluded: wear and tear, ordinary leakage and breakage, inherent vice, corrosion, vermin; And machinery damage not proximately caused by a maritime peril - the hinge of the whole question
2. **The maritime perils that are covered but are NOT perils of the sea** - Perils of the seas is only the FIRST item in the perils clause; Fire and explosion - listed separately precisely because the sea did not cause it; Violent theft by persons from outside the vessel; jettison; piracy; Contact with land conveyance, dock or harbour equipment or installation; Earthquake, volcanic eruption or lightning; Accidents in loading, discharging or shifting cargo or fuel
3. **How an insured covers a peril that is not an insured peril - with examples** - Extend the policy by an additional clause for extra premium - example: additional perils cover, buying back the defective part itself; Buy a separate policy - examples: war and strikes, loss of hire, increased value and hull interest, freight; Cover it mutually through a P&I club - examples: pollution, cargo claims, crew injury and repatriation, wreck removal, fixed and floating objects; Adjust the terms rather than the perils - higher sum insured, lower deductible, removal of a restrictive warranty; Limit one: an inevitable loss is not insurable - no fortuity, no risk to transfer; Limit two: a known existing defect is a repair bill, not a risk - and section 20 disclosure puts the whole policy at stake
4. **The perils whose cover is qualified by due diligence - the Inchmaree group** - The Inchmaree clause, named from the case in which a donkey-engine casualty was held not to be a peril of the seas; Bursting of boilers; breakage of shafts; latent defect in machinery or hull; Negligence of Master, Officers, Crew or Pilots; negligence of repairers or charterers not assured under the policy; barratry; The proviso - provided the loss has not resulted from want of due diligence by the assured, owners, managers or superintendents; The Master, Officers and Crew are NOT in the diligence list - their negligence is the insured peril; Latent defect cover pays the resulting damage, not the renewal of the defective part; A defect a reasonable inspection would have found is not latent - and is probably want of diligence too
5. **Where the onus of proof lies, and why it matters** - The assured proves the loss was proximately caused by a peril insured against; The insurer proves any exception, exclusion or proviso on which it relies; The due-diligence provision is a proviso limiting liability, not a condition precedent; Therefore THE ONUS OF PROVING WANT OF DUE DILIGENCE LIES ON THE UNDERWRITERS; The direct answer to the stem: boilers, shafts, latent defect, negligence, repairers, barratry; In practice fought on documents - planned maintenance, defect reports, superintendent's reports, conditions of class, SMS records
6. **The Indian frame, and what it means on board** - The governing statute is the Marine Insurance Act, 1963 - NOT the UK Act of 1906; Section 19 utmost good faith; section 20 disclosure; section 66 general average loss; The perils are in the policy, not the statute - Indian hull policies are ordinarily on London market forms; The shipping statute is the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958, administered by DG Shipping; Your negligence as an engineer is insured; shore management's failure to act is what puts the claim at risk; On a casualty: notify, preserve the failed component, photograph, and do not dismantle beyond necessity before the surveyor attends
