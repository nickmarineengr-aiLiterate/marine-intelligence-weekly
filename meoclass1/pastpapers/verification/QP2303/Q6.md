**Answer route:**

1. **(a) Where the 80 per cent claim comes from, and what kind of statement it is** _((a))_ - It is not a study finding that four accidents in five are caused by defective people; It is an artefact of how investigations attribute causes; A person is present at the end of almost every chain, so the last human act is always available; Properly read: in about 80 per cent of accidents a human action can be identified in the chain; In a system operated by people that is close to a tautology; So it is a stopping rule, not a finding; Reading it as a finding produces blame and more procedure, and neither prevents the next accident
2. **(a) What 'a complex hierarchical socio-technical system' means for a ship** _((a))_ - Socio-technical - performance is the joint output of people, technology, procedures and environment; Complex - many tightly coupled parts interacting unforeseeably; a blackout is never one failure; Hierarchical - regulator and flag, class, yard, company, master, watchkeeper; Upper-layer decisions create the conditions in which the lowest layer acts; Manning, watch pattern, budget, spare gear, alarm design and procedure wording are all set ashore; Those decisions leave latent conditions which do no harm alone; An accident is when a latent condition meets an active failure and the gaps in the defences line up
3. **(a) What 'human error' therefore means here, and what follows** _((a))_ - It denotes the last human action in the chain, where the investigation happened to stop; A label applied at a boundary of enquiry, not a description of a cause; Therefore a symptom, not a cause; The right question: why did the action make sense to a competent person at the time?; Errors are not eliminated by instructing people not to make them; They are reduced by changing conditions - interfaces, procedures, manning, rest, reporting; Which is why the Casualty Investigation Code, MSC.255(84) of 16 May 2008 seeks circumstances and causes, not blame
4. **(b) Human Element - the domain** _((b))_ - The subject area - people as an integral part of the maritime system. The broadest of the three; Framed by IMO in the Human Element Vision, Principles and Goals, resolution A.947(23); A complex, multi-dimensional issue affecting safety, security and the environment; NOT confined to seafarers - it spans shore management, regulators, recognized organizations, shipyards, designers and legislators; Its regulatory expression is the ISM Code, STCW and MLC 2006; It answers: who and what is in scope?
5. **(b) Human Error - the outcome** _((b))_ - An event - a planned action failing to achieve its outcome, or an inadequate plan; The narrowest of the three, and retrospective; Skill-based slips and lapses - right intention, failed execution; remedy is design; Rule-based mistakes - a good rule in the wrong situation, or a bad rule applied correctly; Knowledge-based mistakes - a novel situation reasoned through wrongly under pressure; Violations are deliberate and are NOT errors - routine, situational or exceptional; A routine violation is a statement about the procedure, not about the person; It answers: what happened?
6. **(b) Human Factors - the discipline** _((b))_ - The scientific discipline and its application; The interaction between people, equipment, procedures, organisation and environment; And designing systems to fit the people who operate them; The only one of the three that is proactive and prospective; Workload, fatigue and circadian rhythm; controls, displays and alarm management; Automation and mode awareness; anthropometry and workspace; Procedure design; communication and team behaviour; It answers: how do we study this, and what do we change?
7. **(b) The three distinguished through one incident** _((b))_ - One constructed incident - a fatigued engineer opens the wrong valve and floods a space; The error is the act - a skill-based slip; The factors are the conditions - identical unlabelled valves, the lighting, the watch pattern; The element is the territory - manning, training, budget, reporting culture; So: element is the field, factors the method, error the event; Error is what you see; factors is how you explain and prevent it; element is where both live; And each implies a different response - a warning, a redesign, or a change of manning
