**Technical ambiguities identified before drafting:**

- 'Targets' in the stem is loose. The Strategy distinguishes LEVELS OF AMBITION from INDICATIVE CHECKPOINTS, and the two use different measures - intensity per transport work against total annual emissions. The answer separates them under their own headings.
- 'Midterm and long-term measures proposed' invites a list of long-term measures. There is none: the Strategy defines long-term measures only by timing, beyond 2030 and to be developed in the 2028 review. Inventing a list would be fabrication.
- The mid-term basket is often described as one measure. Section 4.5 requires it to comprise both a technical and an economic element.

## 5. SOURCES CONSULTED

- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED - READ AT SOURCE IN FULL: resolution MEPC.377(80), 2023 IMO Strategy on Reduction of GHG Emissions from Ships, adopted 7 July 2023, from the MIW True Source corpus. Operative paragraph 5 agrees to keep the Strategy under review with a view to adopting a revised Strategy in 2028; operative paragraph 6 provides that the 2023 Strategy revokes the 2018 Initial IMO GHG Strategy (resolution MEPC.304(72)) as from that date.
- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED - MEPC.377(80) section 2, Vision: IMO remains committed to reducing GHG emissions from international shipping and, as a matter of urgency, aims to phase them out as soon as possible, while promoting a just and equitable transition.
- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED - MEPC.377(80) section 3.3, Levels of ambition: (.1) carbon intensity of the ship to decline through further improvement of energy efficiency for new ships, reviewing with the aim of strengthening the energy efficiency design requirements; (.2) carbon intensity of international shipping to decline - to reduce CO2 emissions per transport work, as an average across international shipping, by at least 40% by 2030, compared to 2008; (.3) uptake of zero or near-zero GHG emission technologies, fuels and/or energy sources to represent at least 5%, striving for 10%, of the energy used by international shipping by 2030; and (.4) to peak GHG emissions as soon as possible and to reach net-zero GHG emissions by or around, i.e. close to, 2050, taking into account different national circumstances, whilst pursuing efforts towards phasing them out consistent with the long-term temperature goal in Article 2 of the Paris Agreement.
- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED - MEPC.377(80) section 3.4, Indicative checkpoints: to reduce total annual GHG emissions from international shipping by at least 20%, striving for 30%, by 2030 compared to 2008; and by at least 70%, striving for 80%, by 2040 compared to 2008.
- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED - MEPC.377(80) section 3.2: the levels of ambition and indicative checkpoints should take into account the well-to-wake GHG emissions of marine fuels as addressed in the LCA guidelines, resolution MEPC.376(80), with the objective of preventing a shift of emissions to other sectors.
- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED - MEPC.377(80) section 3.5, Guiding principles: non-discrimination and no more favourable treatment as enshrined in MARPOL; common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities in the light of different national circumstances, as enshrined in the UNFCCC, its Kyoto Protocol and the Paris Agreement; the requirement for all ships to give full and complete effect regardless of flag; the need to consider impacts on States, in particular LDCs and SIDS; and the need for evidence-based decision-making balanced with the precautionary approach.
- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED - MEPC.377(80) section 4.1, Timelines: short-term measures are those finalized and agreed between 2018 and 2023; the basket of mid-term measures should be finalized and agreed by the Committee by 2025; other candidate mid-term measures could be finalized and agreed between 2023 and 2030; and possible long-term measures could be measures finalized and agreed beyond 2030, to be developed as part of the 2028 review.
- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED - MEPC.377(80) section 4.5, the basket of candidate mid-term measures: a basket comprised of both (.1) a technical element, namely a goal-based marine fuel standard regulating the phased reduction of the marine fuel's GHG intensity; and (.2) an economic element, on the basis of a maritime GHG emissions pricing mechanism. The measures should promote the energy transition, provide the world fleet with a needed incentive, and contribute to a level playing field and a just and equitable transition. Section 4.8 requires potential synergies with existing measures such as the Carbon Intensity Indicator to be considered.
- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED - MEPC.377(80) section 6.2, the milestone table: MEPC 80 (Summer 2023) initiation of the comprehensive impact assessment and of Phase III of the Work Plan; MEPC 81 (Spring 2024) interim report and finalization of the basket of measures; MEPC 82 (Autumn 2024) finalized report; MEPC 83 (Spring 2025) approval of measures, with the review of the short-term measure to be completed by 1 January 2026; an extraordinary MEPC session about six months after MEPC 83 (Autumn 2025) for adoption of measures; and entry into force of measures 16 months after adoption (2027). MEPC 86 (Summer 2027) initiates the review of the 2023 Strategy and MEPC 88 (Autumn 2028) finalizes it with a view to adopting a 2028 Strategy.
- P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED - MEPC.377(80) section 1.8: the Fourth IMO GHG Study 2020 estimated that GHG emissions from shipping in 2018 accounted for some 2.89% of global anthropogenic GHG emissions.
