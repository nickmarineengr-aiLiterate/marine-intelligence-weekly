**Technical ambiguities identified before drafting:**

- 'Human error' in the printed statement is used loosely and the whole of limb (a) turns on that looseness. The answer distinguishes the popular usage - a careless individual - from the technical one - the last identifiable human action in a causal chain - and says which the statement actually supports.
- 'In your opinion' in limb (b) is a genuine invitation to take a position rather than hedge. The answer takes one: the three terms sit at different levels of the same system, and treating them as synonyms produces the wrong intervention.
- 'Suitable examples' could be answered with three unconnected illustrations. A single incident read in three registers is stronger, because it demonstrates that the terms describe different aspects of one situation rather than different situations.
- Violations sit awkwardly inside a question about error. They are included because omitting them leaves the taxonomy incomplete and because the routine violation is the clearest evidence that a procedure rather than a person is at fault - but they are expressly labelled as NOT errors.
