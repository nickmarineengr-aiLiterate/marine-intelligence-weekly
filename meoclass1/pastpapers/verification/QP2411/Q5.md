## 5. SOURCES CONSULTED

- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY - the International Convention on Salvage, 1989, adopted at London on 28 April 1989, in force 14 July 1996, replacing the Brussels Convention of 1910. MIW holds no copy; identity, objectives, scheme and the Article 13 and 14 content are stated from authoritative restatement and no text is quoted.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY - Article 13, the criteria for fixing the reward: the ten listed criteria, the rule that the reward is paid by vessel and property interests in proportion to their respective salved values, and the cap that the reward shall not exceed the salved value of the vessel and other property. Stated by identity; no text quoted.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY - Article 14, special compensation: entitlement where the vessel or its cargo threatened damage to the environment and the Article 13 reward is less than the special compensation assessable; measure equal to the salvor's expenses; uplift of up to 30 per cent where damage to the environment was prevented or minimised, which a tribunal may increase further but not beyond 100 per cent; expenses defined as out-of-pocket expenses reasonably incurred plus a fair rate for equipment and personnel actually and reasonably used; payable only to the extent it exceeds the Article 13 reward; and forfeiture in whole or part for salvor negligence which failed to prevent or minimise environmental damage. Stated by identity; no text quoted.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY - Article 12, the no cure, no pay rule that salvage operations which have had a useful result give right to a reward and that no payment is due if they have had no useful result; Article 8, the duties of the salvor and of the owner and master, which include the duty to exercise due care to prevent or minimise damage to the environment; and Article 1, which defines damage to the environment in terms of substantial physical damage to human health or to marine life or resources caused by pollution, contamination, fire, explosion or similar major incidents. Stated by identity; no text quoted.
- P3 INDUSTRY GUIDANCE - Lloyd's Open Form as the standard no-cure-no-pay salvage contract under which the Convention regime is most often operated, and the SCOPIC clause as the industry's contractual response to the difficulty of assessing special compensation. Described as industry contract, expressly NOT as part of the Convention. No edition year is asserted, because MIW holds no copy and the clause has been revised more than once.
- P3 INDUSTRY GUIDANCE / HISTORICAL - the Amoco Cadiz and Torrey Canyon casualties as the class of incident that exposed the commercial failure the 1989 Convention was written to cure, and the judicial construction of the Article 14 'fair rate' as covering expenses without a profit element, which is what made SCOPIC necessary. Historical and case record; no citation is quoted.
