## 5. SOURCES CONSULTED

- ENGINEERING AND ANALYTICAL-CHEMISTRY REFERENCE - atomic emission spectrometry: the sample is excited in a plasma or across a rotating-disc electrode arc, each element emits light at characteristic wavelengths, and the intensity at each wavelength gives the concentration of that element in parts per million. Explained mechanistically; declared under production protocol section 2.1.
- ENGINEERING AND ANALYTICAL-CHEMISTRY REFERENCE - the particle-size limitation of emission spectrometry: large particles are not fully atomised in the excitation source, so the response falls away progressively with increasing particle size and the method effectively reports the fine fraction only. This is the single most important interpretive fact about the technique and it is explained by mechanism rather than by a quoted cut-off.
- ENGINEERING AND ANALYTICAL-CHEMISTRY REFERENCE - Fourier Transform Infrared spectroscopy: an infrared beam is passed through a thin film of oil, molecular bonds absorb at characteristic wavenumbers, the interferogram is transformed to a spectrum, and the spectrum is subtracted from that of the same oil unused so that only the change is measured. The approximate characteristic positions - carbonyl for oxidation, nitro compounds for nitration, sulphate for sulphation, hydroxyl for water, and a broad baseline rise for soot - are properties of the bonds themselves and are given as approximate.
- ENGINEERING AND ANALYTICAL-CHEMISTRY REFERENCE - particle counting by automatic optical light-obscuration, by pore-blockage, or by microscopic count; the reporting of results as counts per millilitre in cumulative size bands and their expression as a three-part ISO cleanliness code; and the known interferences - water droplets and entrained air counted as particles, and dark or sooty oils defeating optical instruments. The ISO standard is named by identity only and is not held by MIW; no code table or band value is quoted.
- ENGINEERING AND ANALYTICAL-CHEMISTRY REFERENCE - Base Number as the alkaline reserve conferred by overbased detergent additives, and Acid Number as the quantity of base required to neutralise the acidic constituents present, both expressed in milligrammes of potassium hydroxide per gramme of oil. That the two can coexist in the same sample, being determined by different titrations, is explained mechanistically.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY - that two distinct standard titration methods for Base Number are in use, one employing a strongly acidic titrant and one a weaker acidic titrant, and that they give systematically different results on the same oil, the stronger method returning the higher value. Named by identity only; MIW holds no test-method standard and no procedure is quoted. Recorded because comparing results obtained by different methods is a real and common practical error.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY - the 0.50 per cent m/m global sulphur limit for fuel oil used outside emission control areas, in force since 1 January 2020 under MARPOL Annex VI. Used solely to explain why cylinder oil base numbers are now selected against the fuel actually burned. No Annex VI provision is quoted and no regulation number is asserted, because this answer needs only the fact of the limit.
- ENGINEERING JUDGEMENT - the analysis at steps 1 and 7: that the four techniques answer three separate questions, that spectrometry and particle counting are complementary rather than alternative, and the account of how a Chief Engineer sequences and acts on the results. Reasoned from the mechanisms above and declared as judgement.
