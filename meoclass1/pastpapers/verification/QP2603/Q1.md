## Sources actually used

- PRIMARY: IMO conventions page - the treaty process. Adoption is by the relevant IMO organ or by a diplomatic conference. A State may express consent by <b>signature</b> alone where the treaty so provides; most multilateral treaties instead allow signature <b>subject to ratification, acceptance or approval</b>, where signature does not bind and the State must subsequently deposit a formal instrument with the depositary. The page records that the practice originated when communications were slow, protecting representatives from exceeding their authority, and that States now use it to allow time to enact the necessary domestic legislation and satisfy constitutional requirements. It cites <b>Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties Article 14(2)</b>: 'the consent of a State to be bound by a treaty is expressed by acceptance or approval under conditions similar to those which apply to ratification'. <b>Accession</b> is the means by which a State becomes party to a treaty it did not sign within the signature period, permitted by <b>Vienna Convention Article 15</b> where the treaty so provides or the negotiating States so agreed, and effected by depositing an instrument of accession.
- PRIMARY: IMO conventions page - entry into force and amendment. A convention becomes binding only after governments formally accept it. Early conventions typically required acceptance by two-thirds of contracting States, which sometimes meant more acceptances were needed for entry into force than for amendments, causing delay. Conventions of an important technical nature generally set higher requirements involving both a number of States and a percentage of world merchant fleet gross tonnage. The <b>tacit acceptance procedure</b> was devised to accelerate amendment: an amendment is deemed to have been accepted unless objections are received before a deadline. For SOLAS 1974, an amendment to the technical annexes is 'deemed to have been accepted at the end of two years from the date on which it is communicated to Contracting Governments' unless objected to by <b>more than one-third of Contracting Governments, or by Contracting Governments owning at least 50 per cent of world gross merchant tonnage</b>. The page records that amendments now enter into force within about 18 to 24 months, against the 1960 SOLAS experience in which amendments adopted between 1966 and 1973 never received sufficient acceptances to enter into force at all.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: convention summaries confirming the entry into force conditions actually used. <b>SOLAS 1974</b> was adopted on 1 November 1974 and entered into force on 25 May 1980, its condition being acceptance by not less than <b>25 States whose combined merchant fleets constitute not less than 50 per cent of the gross tonnage of the world's merchant shipping</b>. The <b>1978 MARPOL Protocol</b> provided for entry into force twelve months after not less than <b>fifteen States, the combined merchant fleets of which constitute not less than fifty per cent of the gross tonnage of the world's merchant shipping</b>, had become Parties.
