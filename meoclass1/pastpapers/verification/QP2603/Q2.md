## Printed question, as transcribed

> With reference to cargo securing of containers in Container vessels:
> i) Discuss the use of roll design assessment software to avoid excessive roll motion in container ship.
> ii) Discuss the use of cargo securing Manual (CSM) and it's content and state regulations and codes associated with Cargo Securing Manual.

*Marks note:* Two limbs are printed, i) and ii), with no marks against either. The whole question carries the paper's equal-marks value of 16. No split has been invented; March prints per-limb marks on Q8 only.

## Findings

**No prior sitting is recorded on the source copy for this question** - the only
March question with an entirely empty recurrence table, and the classification against the
three built MIW papers is NONE.

**The phrase "roll design assessment software" is not an IMO term of art.** No instrument,
circular or classification rule uses it. Rather than guess at a proprietary product, the
answer states the reading it adopts - the direct stability assessment and onboard
decision-support tooling contemplated by the Second Generation Intact Stability Criteria -
and records that a candidate reading it as a lashing-force program would not be wrong about
the onboard half. This is recorded as a C-class accepted limitation rather than resolved.

**Annex 14 application dates were read rather than assumed.** MSC.1/Circ.1352/Rev.1 was
adopted 15 December 2014 and applies in full to container ships keel-laid **on or after 1
January 2015**; for existing ships only sections 4.4, 7.1, 7.3 and 8 apply. A flat "all
container ships must have a Cargo Safe Access Plan" would have been more categorical than
the instrument, and the semantic-integrity rule forbids that in a derived layer - so the
flashcard and the critical-numbers entry both carry the date and the split.

**The Second Generation Intact Stability Criteria are INTERIM.** MSC.1/Circ.1627 is
guidance, not a mandatory SOLAS requirement, at this sitting. The answer says so explicitly
and a B-class currency flag is raised, because that status is the fact most likely to move.

## Verification status

The Cargo Securing Manual requirement traced to SOLAS chapter VI regulation 5 and chapter VII regulation 5 and to the IMO Revised Guidelines MSC.1/Circ.1353/Rev.2; CSS Code Annex 13 and Annex 14 and the Cargo Safe Access Plan confirmed against MSC.1/Circ.1352/Rev.1 including its 1 January 2015 application date; the second generation intact stability criteria and their five failure modes confirmed against MSC.1/Circ.1627

## Sources actually used

- PRIMARY: MSC.1/Circ.1353/Rev.2, Revised Guidelines for the Preparation of the Cargo Securing Manual - the section structure of the manual (chapters 1 to 5 and the annexes), the requirement for Administration approval, and the harmonisation of the manual's evaluation annex with Annex 13 of the CSS Code.
- PRIMARY: MSC.1/Circ.1352/Rev.1, Amendments to the Code of Safe Practice for Cargo Stowage and Securing - Annex 14, Guidance on providing safe working conditions for securing of containers on deck. Adopted 15 December 2014. Applies to container ships the keels of which were laid on or after 1 January 2015; sections 4.4, 7.1, 7.3 and 8 apply to existing container ships keel-laid before that date. Requires an approved Cargo Safe Access Plan for all areas where containers are secured.
- PRIMARY: MSC.1/Circ.1627, Interim Guidelines on the Second Generation Intact Stability Criteria - the five dynamic stability failure modes (parametric rolling, pure loss of stability, excessive acceleration, dead ship condition, surf-riding and broaching) and the three assessment levels, level 3 being direct stability assessment by numerical simulation, producing operational limitations and operational guidance.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: Code of Safe Practice for Cargo Stowage and Securing (CSS Code), resolution A.714(17) as amended - the parent code for the CSM, with Annex 13 the method for assessing the efficiency of securing arrangements for semi-standardised and non-standardised cargo. MIW holds no licensed copy of the CSS Code; the annex structure is taken from the IMO circulars that amend it and from classification-society restatements.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: classification-society guidance on parametric rolling in container ships - the mechanism of stability variation between wave crest and trough arising from large bow flare and a wide flat stern, and the roughly two-to-one ratio of roll period to encounter period.
- P3 INDUSTRY GUIDANCE: MSC.1/Circ.1228, Revised guidance to the master for avoiding dangerous situations in adverse weather and sea conditions - the speed and heading changes used to break out of synchronous and parametric roll.

## Open / unresolved

- MIW holds no licensed copy of SOLAS or of the CSS Code. The regulation numbers and the annex structure are cited from IMO circulars, classification-society restatements and Administration guidance rather than from the convention and code texts themselves.
- The second generation intact stability criteria remain INTERIM guidelines at the sitting date and are not a mandatory SOLAS requirement. Whether and when they become mandatory was not established and the answer does not assert it.
- The phrase 'roll design assessment software' is not an IMO term of art. The answer treats it as the direct stability assessment and onboard decision-support tooling that the second generation criteria contemplate, and says so rather than assuming a single named product.
