## Printed question, as transcribed

> a) Discuss the differences between Enhanced Survey Program and Condition Assessment Program Survey.
> b) State the documents and records needed for carrying out Enhanced survey program.
> c) State information found in safe access plan for enhanced survey program.

*Marks note:* Three limbs are printed, a) to c), with no marks against any of them. The whole question carries the paper's equal-marks value of 16. No split has been invented. The printed capitalisation is internally inconsistent - 'Enhanced Survey Program' in a), 'Enhanced survey program' in b) and 'enhanced survey program' in c) - and is transcribed as printed.

## Findings

**The Code's identity was verified from the IMO resolution record.** The 2011 ESP
Code is resolution **A.1049(27)**, adopted **30 November 2011**, made mandatory by **SOLAS
regulation XI-1/2**, with Annex A covering bulk carriers and Annex B oil tankers, and
amended through MSC.371(93), MSC.381(94), MSC.405(96), MSC.412(97) and MSC.461(101), the
last in force 1 January 2021. It is closely aligned with IACS UR Z10.

**The most valuable finding is a distinction, not a fact: CAP is not CAS.** The
**Condition Assessment Programme** named in the printed question is a **voluntary**
classification-society service. The **Condition Assessment Scheme** is a **mandatory**
regime under MARPOL Annex I for single-hull oil tankers. The abbreviations are routinely
conflated, and the confusion **inverts the answer** to a question whose whole point is the
mandatory/voluntary contrast. This is recorded in the study guide, in a flashcard and in
`major_trap`.

**Two figures were withheld on purpose.** Gross tonnage thresholds for ESP application are
stated inconsistently across secondary sources, so the answer names the ship types and
asserts no tonnage. CAP rating scales, scope options and validity periods differ between
societies, so the common structure is described and the variation stated.

**The documentation and safe-access content is C-class.** MIW holds no licensed copy of the
ESP Code, so limbs (b) and (c) are assembled from classification-society restatements of
the Code's survey programme requirements. They are consistent across societies but are not
quoted from the instrument, and the record says so rather than implying otherwise.

**Merchant Shipping Act 2025 check - CLEAR.** International survey regime; no Indian
statutory content.

## Sources actually used

- PRIMARY: IMO resolution A.1049(27), International Code on the Enhanced Programme of Inspections during Surveys of Bulk Carriers and Oil Tankers, 2011 (2011 ESP Code), adopted 30 November 2011. Made mandatory under SOLAS regulation XI-1/2. Annex A parts A and B cover bulk carriers of single-side skin and double-side skin construction; Annex B parts A and B cover oil tankers of double-hull and non-double-hull construction.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: the ESP Code amendment chain - resolutions MSC.371(93), MSC.381(94), MSC.405(96), MSC.412(97) and MSC.461(101), the last entering into force on 1 January 2021 - and the Code's close alignment with IACS Unified Requirement Z10 on hull surveys.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: classification-society descriptions of the Condition Assessment Programme as a VOLUNTARY, additional service performed at the owner's request, producing a numerical rating of actual condition measured against a new-build standard, with selectable scope covering hull, machinery and cargo systems, and driven by charterer, vetting, sale-and-purchase and finance requirements rather than by statute.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: the Condition Assessment SCHEME under MARPOL Annex I - a statutory scheme for single-hull oil tankers, and a different instrument entirely from the Condition Assessment PROGRAMME. Recorded because the abbreviations are routinely conflated.
- INTERNAL REUSE VERIFIED: QP2601 Q5 for the Coating Technical File and the assessment of coating condition as an indicator of tank corrosion status; QP2607 Q3 for the role of the recognized organization acting for the Administration and the status of IACS unified requirements.

## Open / unresolved

- MIW holds no licensed copy of the 2011 ESP Code or of SOLAS. The Code's identity, adoption date, mandatory basis and annex structure are verified from the IMO resolution record, but the detailed documentation list and the safe access content are assembled from classification-society restatements of the Code's survey programme requirements rather than read in the Code's own annexes.
- Gross tonnage thresholds for application are stated differently across secondary sources. The answer identifies the ship types the Code applies to and does not assert a tonnage figure.
- Condition Assessment Programme rating scales, validity periods and scope options differ between classification societies. The answer describes the common structure and says that the detail is society-specific rather than quoting one society's scheme as though it were universal.
