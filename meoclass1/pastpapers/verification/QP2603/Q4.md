## Printed question, as transcribed

> Control of disease vectors is necessary for maintenance of health on board ships. What is the Role of the WHO organisation in this regard and which are the national agencies tasked with the maintenance of the ship and the crew in good health? What are the different certificates issued to a vessel for maintenance of the ship in good health? Who can issue these certificates in India and what is the validity and extension options for such certificates?

*Marks note:* Four tasks are set - the role of WHO, the national agencies, the certificates, and issue, validity and extension in India - without printed marks against any of them. The whole question carries the paper's equal-marks value of 16.

## Sources actually used

- PRIMARY: International Health Regulations (2005), as amended in 2014, 2022 and 2024 - consolidated WHO text, read in full for the relevant Articles. Article 39 (ship sanitation certificates: six months, extendable by one month, model in Annex 3, issue conditions); Article 20(3) and 20(4)-(5) (lists of authorized ports, WHO certification of ports, certification guidelines); Article 22 (role of competent authorities); Article 24(1)(c) (conveyance operators to keep conveyances free of sources of infection and contamination including vectors and reservoirs); Article 27 (affected conveyances - disinfect, decontaminate, disinsect, derat); Article 37 (Ship Declaration of Health, model in Annex 8); Annex 5 (specific measures for vector-borne diseases, including the 400 metre programme distance and recording of vectors on the SSCC).
- PRIMARY: International Health Regulations (2005), original 2005 text - read for direct comparison. Article 37 is titled 'Maritime Declaration of Health' in the original (12 occurrences of that term, none of 'Ship Declaration of Health'); the consolidated amended text uses 'Ship Declaration of Health' (6 occurrences, none of the former). This establishes the renaming as a real change rather than an inference.
- PRIMARY: WHO - the 2024 amendments entered into force on 19 September 2025 in accordance with Article 59.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: Directorate General of Health Services and Port Health Organisation material on the functions of the PHO in India, and the Indian Port Health Rules 1955 read with the IHR 2005.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: DG Shipping Order 10 of 2018 (revised) concerning SSCC/SSCEC and the Statement of Compliance route for coastal trade where no Port Health Officer is available.
