## Printed question, as transcribed

> With respect to the geo-political situations, war situations ongoing in the Persian gulf:
> a) Explain War risk cover in Marine insurance, and also describe the key aspects which are included in this war risk cover.
> b) Describe some of the measures which the insurers and P&I clubs take to protect themselves from high claims during war situations.

*Marks note:* Two limbs are printed, a) and b), with no marks against either. The whole question carries the paper's equal-marks value of 16. No split has been invented.

## Findings

**The statutory anchor was read at source.** Section 2(e) of the Indian Marine
Insurance Act, 1963 defines "maritime perils" to include *war perils, ... captures,
seizures, restraints and detainments of princes and peoples*. This settles the opening
proposition of the answer: war risk is **excluded by the policy wording, not by the
statute**, which is the reverse of what most candidates assume.

**Jurisdiction.** The governing statute for an Indian examination is the Act of 1963, not
the English Act of 1906 - the same point QP2607 Q9 established and QB9_C got wrong. The
market forms named (Institute War and Strikes Clauses, Joint War Committee Listed Areas)
are London market instruments and are described as such, without implying the English Act
applies.

**Deliberately not reproduced: Listed Areas and premium rates.** Both moved repeatedly
through the period around this sitting. Any figure printed in a study answer would be wrong
within weeks, so the answer describes the mechanism and says the current list must be
checked. This is a decision, not an omission.

**The examiner's premise was checked but carries no scoring weight.** Trade reporting
through the first quarter of 2026 describes an active Gulf and Strait of Hormuz war-risk
situation, which confirms the question was set against a live backdrop. Trade press sits at
the bottom of the source hierarchy and is recorded as CONTEXT ONLY; no claim in the model
answer rests on it.

**Merchant Shipping Act 2025 check - CLEAR.** The Marine Insurance Act 1963 is separate
legislation and is not among the enactments repealed by s.324(1) of the 2025 Act.

## Verification status

The statutory definition of maritime perils read in section 2(e) of the Indian Marine Insurance Act 1963, confirming that war perils, captures, seizures, restraints and detainments are maritime perils under the Act; the Joint War Committee Listed Areas mechanism, additional war risk premium and the seven days' notice of cancellation confirmed against market and P&I club sources

## Sources actually used

- PRIMARY: The Marine Insurance Act, 1963 (India), section 2(e) - 'maritime perils' means the perils consequent on, or incidental to, the navigation of the sea, that is to say, perils of the sea, fire, WAR PERILS, pirates, rovers, thieves, CAPTURES, SEIZURES, RESTRAINTS AND DETAINMENTS of princes and peoples, jettisons, barratry and any other perils which are either of the like kind or may be designated by the policy. Read on the India Code text of the Act. This establishes that war perils are insurable maritime perils under Indian law; their exclusion from the ordinary policy is a matter of the policy wording, not of the statute.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: International Underwriting Association - the Joint War Committee publishes a list of areas of perceived enhanced risk in relation to hull war, strikes, terrorism and related perils, assessed against an enhanced risk benchmark by an independent consultant.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: P&I club published guidance on war risks cover - the exclusion of war risks from the Club Rules, the separate excess war risks P&I cover written above the ship's proper value, and the cancellation provisions.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: market explainers on additional war risk premium - annual war cover is effectively suspended for a Listed Area transit, with prior notification to underwriters and a separately negotiated additional premium reinstating it for that transit; cancellation on seven days' notice under London market wordings and forty-eight hours under some United States wordings.
- P3 INDUSTRY GUIDANCE: Institute War and Strikes Clauses (Hulls-Time) and Institute War Clauses (Cargo) as the standard market forms in which war cover is written back, together with the historical free of capture and seizure exclusion in the ordinary policy.
- CONTEXT ONLY (trade press): reporting through the first quarter of 2026 on the expansion of Joint War Committee Listed Areas in the Gulf and on war risk premium movement around the Strait of Hormuz. Used only to confirm that the examiner's premise describes a live situation at the sitting; no scoring claim rests on it.

## Open / unresolved

- MIW holds no licensed copy of the Institute War and Strikes Clauses or of any P&I club rule book. The clause content, the detainment period giving rise to a constructive total loss, and the excess war cover limits are stated at the level of published market and club explanatory material, not from the wordings themselves.
- The Joint War Committee Listed Areas and the additional premium rates applying at the sitting date are deliberately NOT reproduced. Both change continuously - sometimes weekly - and any figure printed in a study answer would be wrong within months.
- The precise limit of the International Group excess war risks P&I cover was not established from a club rule book and is therefore described by its structure rather than by a figure.
