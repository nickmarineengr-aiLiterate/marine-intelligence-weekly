## Printed question, as transcribed

> With reference to LNG bunkering procedure onboard vessels fuelled with LNG fuel. Please explain the following processes.
> a. Gassing up.
> b. Cooling down.
> c. Vapour Management
> d. Emergency release coupling procedure.

*Marks note:* Four processes are named, a. to d., with no marks against any of them. The whole question carries the paper's equal-marks value of 16. No split has been invented. The stem is printed as a sentence fragment ending in a full stop and is transcribed exactly as printed.

## Findings

**The emergency release architecture is the primary-verified part.** ISO 20519
requires that the emergency release system *shall consist of an emergency release coupling
including interlocked isolating valves to minimize loss of LNG or NG when the ERC is
activated*, and that *the delivery facility and receiving vessel ESD systems shall be
interconnected with a ship/shore or ship/ship ESD link*. The vapour return line is defined
in the same standard as the connection allowing excess vapour to be returned to the
bunkering facility, **removing any need to vent to atmosphere** - which is why the answer
ranks venting last rather than listing it as an equal option.

**Numbers were deliberately not fixed.** The hydrocarbon and carbon dioxide percentages
that end gassing up, and the permitted rate of temperature fall during cooldown, are set by
the containment system designer, the class rules and the ship's own manual, and differ
between membrane and independent tank types. Asserting a single figure would be wrong on
most ships. They are described as parameters to be read off the ship's documents, and
recorded as a C-class limitation.

**Edition caveat.** ISO 20519 has been issued in more than one edition and MIW holds no
licensed copy. The requirements relied on are those consistently reported across editions,
and **no clause numbers are cited** - deliberately, because citing a clause from an edition
not held would be exactly the correct-instrument-wrong-edition failure this series has now
caught on three papers.

**Merchant Shipping Act 2025 check - CLEAR.** Entirely international and operational; no
Indian statutory content.

## Verification status

The bunkering framework confirmed against ISO 20519 and the IGF Code; the emergency release system architecture - an emergency release coupling with interlocked isolating valves, and an interconnected ship-to-ship or ship-to-shore ESD link - confirmed against ISO 20519; the vapour return line function confirmed from the same source and from classification-society best-practice guidance

## Sources actually used

- PRIMARY: ISO 20519, Ships and marine technology - Specification for bunkering of liquefied natural gas fuelled vessels. Read for the emergency shutdown and emergency release requirements: the emergency release system shall consist of an emergency release coupling including interlocked isolating valves to minimize loss of LNG or natural gas when the coupling is activated; and the delivery facility and receiving vessel ESD systems shall be interconnected by a ship-to-ship or ship-to-shore ESD link so that both operate in a coordinated manner. Also read for the vapour return line as the connection allowing excess vapour generated during bunkering to be returned to the bunkering facility, removing the need to vent to atmosphere. Note: ISO 20519 has been issued in more than one edition; the requirements relied on here are common to them.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: IGF Code - the International Code of Safety for Ships using Gases or other Low-flashpoint Fuels, adopted by resolution MSC.391(95) and given effect through SOLAS chapter II-1 part G, as the mandatory design, arrangement and operational framework for gas-fuelled ships. MIW holds no licensed copy of the IGF Code; its scope is taken from IMO and classification-society material.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: classification-society best-practice guidance on LNG bunkering (2025) covering the bunkering sequence, safety and hazardous zones, pre-transfer checklists and the roles of the bunkering and receiving vessels.
- P3 INDUSTRY GUIDANCE: industry society guidance on gas as a marine fuel and on LNG transfer operations - the purpose of gassing up in displacing inert gas before liquid is introduced, controlled spray cooldown to limit thermal stress, and boil-off gas handling by consumption, reliquefaction or a gas combustion unit.
- ENGINEERING JUDGEMENT: the ordering of the ESD-1 then ESD-2 sequence, and the description of the practical steps at the coupling, are presented as the standard arrangement rather than as a quotation from any single wording, because the detail is set by the equipment maker's design and by the agreed bunkering plan.

## Open / unresolved

- MIW holds no licensed copy of the IGF Code or of the IGC Code. The framework statements rest on IMO summaries and classification-society material rather than on the code texts.
- Numerical completion criteria for gassing up (methane or carbon dioxide percentage at the outlet) and the permitted cooldown rate are set by the tank designer, the class rules and the ship's own manual, and vary by containment system. Representative values are described as such and no single figure is asserted as universal.
- ISO 20519 exists in more than one edition and MIW holds no licensed copy. The requirements relied on are those consistently reported across editions; specific clause numbers are not cited.
