## Printed question, as transcribed

> Discuss the Hong Kong Convention for the Safe and Environmentally Sound Recycling of Ships. What is its significance for India, and how does India's shipbreaking industry stand to benefit or be impacted by its implementation? (16)

*Marks note:* Set as a single undivided question carrying a printed (16). Three tasks are set inside the running stem - the Convention itself, its significance for India, and the benefit or impact on India's shipbreaking industry - with no marks printed against them individually.

## Findings

**Convention dates verified on the IMO's own briefings.** Adopted at a diplomatic
conference in Hong Kong, China in **May 2009**; entered into force **26 June 2025**; at
entry into force there were **24 Parties representing 57.15 per cent** of world shipping by
tonnage, including the recycling States Bangladesh, India, Pakistan and Turkiye. The
Article 17 conditions - **15 States, 40 per cent of world gross tonnage, and 3 per cent
combined maximum annual recycling volume** over the preceding ten years, then twenty-four
months - were confirmed separately.

**India's accession verified.** The Director General of Shipping deposited India's
instrument with the IMO Secretary-General on **28 November 2019**, during the thirty-first
session of the IMO Assembly, taking the count to the required fifteen States.

**THIS IS THE ONE MARCH QUESTION WHERE THE 15 MARCH 2026 COMMENCEMENT COULD HAVE BITTEN,
AND IT DOES NOT.** India's ship recycling law is the **Recycling of Ships Act, 2019** with
the Recycling of Ships Rules, 2019 - **separate legislation**, not part of the Merchant
Shipping Act. The QP2607 Q7 Gazette reading established that **s.324(1) of the Merchant
Shipping Act, 2025 repeals the Merchant Shipping Act 1958 and the Coasting Vessels Act 1838
only**. That repeal list is closed and does not reach the 2019 Act. The 2025 Act touches
recycling at **s.17** alone, providing for temporary registration of a vessel sought to be
recycled. The answer states this expressly and carries it as a flashcard, because a paper
sat in the very month the 2025 Act commenced invites the assumption that everything Indian
and maritime was swept into it. A **B-class currency flag** is nonetheless raised, because
the subordinate Merchant Shipping Rules 2026 were still in draft and could in principle
carry consequential provisions.

**The overstatement this topic invites was avoided.** The Convention does **not** prohibit
beaching, and Convention compliance is **not** equivalent to acceptance under the EU Ship
Recycling Regulation, which maintains its own list of approved facilities on stricter terms.
Both are stated explicitly.

**No share figure is asserted.** Published figures for India's share of world ship recycling
differ widely and none could be verified, so Alang-Sosiya is named and no percentage is
given. The industry effects in step 6 are presented as reasoned **mechanisms**, not as
measured outcomes, because no price or capacity data was obtained.

## Sources actually used

- PRIMARY: IMO press briefing, 'New era for ship recycling as Hong Kong Convention enters into force' - adopted at a diplomatic conference in Hong Kong, China in May 2009; entered into force 26 June 2025; 24 Parties representing 57.15 per cent of world shipping by tonnage; Parties include the flag States Japan, Liberia, Marshall Islands and Panama and the recycling States Bangladesh, India, Pakistan and Turkiye; the Convention prohibits or restricts asbestos, polychlorinated biphenyls, ozone-depleting substances, and anti-fouling compounds and systems containing organotin compounds or cybutryne.
- PRIMARY: IMO press briefing on India's accession - the Director General of Shipping deposited India's instrument of accession with the Secretary-General on 28 November 2019, during the thirty-first session of the IMO Assembly, bringing the required number of States to fifteen and contributing materially to the recycling volume criterion.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: the Article 17 entry-into-force conditions - twenty-four months after not less than 15 States have consented to be bound, whose combined merchant fleets constitute not less than 40 per cent of world gross tonnage, and whose combined maximum annual ship recycling volume during the preceding ten years constitutes not less than 3 per cent of the combined gross tonnage of those States.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: classification-society guidance on Hong Kong Convention certification - the Inventory of Hazardous Materials in three parts, the International Certificate on Inventory of Hazardous Materials, the final survey and the International Ready for Recycling Certificate, application to ships of 500 gross tonnage and above on international voyages, and the compliance date of 26 June 2030 for existing ships.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: Indian maritime administration material on ship recycling - the Recycling of Ships Act, 2019 and the Recycling of Ships Rules, 2019 as the domestic implementing instruments, with the administration as competent authority for authorisation of ship recycling facilities.
- INTERNAL REUSE VERIFIED: QP2607 Q7, in which the Merchant Shipping Act 2025 was read in the Gazette - s.324(1) repeals the Merchant Shipping Act 1958 and the Coasting Vessels Act 1838 only, so the Recycling of Ships Act 2019 is untouched; s.17 provides for temporary registration of a vessel sought to be recycled.
- P3 INDUSTRY GUIDANCE and CONTEXT: the EU Ship Recycling Regulation and its separate list of approved facilities, and continuing non-governmental scrutiny of South Asian yards - used to establish that Convention compliance and European acceptance are distinct standards.

## Open / unresolved

- India's share of world ship recycling by tonnage is quoted very differently by different sources and no authoritative current figure was established. The answer describes India as one of the principal recycling States and names Alang-Sosiya without asserting a percentage.
- The current number of Indian yards holding authorisation under the Recycling of Ships Act 2019, and the number listed under the EU Ship Recycling Regulation, change continuously and are not reproduced.
- MIW holds no licensed copy of the Hong Kong Convention text. Article and appendix content rests on the IMO's own briefing material and on classification-society guidance rather than on the Convention text itself.
- Whether the Convention's entry into force has in fact changed the price per light displacement tonne obtained at Indian yards is a commercial outcome that was not established from data and is discussed as a mechanism, not as a measured result.
