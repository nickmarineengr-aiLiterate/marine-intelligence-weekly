**Technical ambiguities adjudicated.**

- Whether a P&I club is an insurance company - it is not; it is a mutual, non-profit association whose members are simultaneously the insured and the insurers
- Whether a member's liability is capped at the advance call - it is not, which is what supplementary, release and overspill calls exist to express
- Whether protection and indemnity are formally separate classes in modern club rules - they are not; the split is historical and describes the origin of two groups of risk
- Whether the club insures the entered ship - it does not; damage to the ship herself is the hull and machinery underwriter's subject
- Whether freight, demurrage and defence is part of ordinary P&I cover - it is not; it is a separate class
- Whether the club pays 'averages' - it does not; average is a hull and machinery term meaning loss or damage, and the club indemnifies third-party liabilities. The one genuine overlap is cargo's proportion of general average unrecoverable because of a breach of the contract of carriage
- Whether limb c) asks about the club's conduct or the owner's - the OWNER's, which is what distinguishes this paper from the three donors
- Whether a member can recover before paying - the general rule is 'pay to be paid', subject to the direct rights of action certain compulsory-insurance regimes confer on claimants
- Whether loss of class merely needs reporting - it must be reported at once, and under the rules cover may cease automatically

**Target answer shape.** Three printed limb headings. Under (a), one section on what a club is with two numbered distinguishing features and a paragraph on the pooling and reinsurance structure, then one section with a five-row call table, a bulleted basis of assessment and the policy year. Under (b), one short historical section explaining where the two words came from, then a bulleted protection list, a bulleted indemnity list, and a bulleted exclusions list. Under (c), three numbered sections - entry, the continuing conditions, and conduct after a casualty - closing on a single sentence that compresses the whole minimum.

They are not corpus candidates and the True Source corpus holds none of them - its reference resolver contains 320 entries, every one MARPOL Annex VI, and it holds no marine insurance instrument of any kind. NO CLUB IS NAMED, NO CLUB RULE IS CITED OR NUMBERED, AND NO CLUB RULE IS QUOTED. The Indian statutes are likewise named at identity level for this sitting.

**Sources consulted.**

- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: a protection and indemnity club is a MUTUAL, non-profit association of shipowners, operators, managers and demise charterers which provides its members with cover against third-party liabilities arising out of the operation of their ships. Its members are simultaneously the insured and the insurers, it is governed by its own Rules, and its board is drawn from the membership.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: the principal clubs act together through a group arrangement under which claims above each club's own retention are POOLED between them, and above the pool the group buys a common excess-loss reinsurance programme in the commercial market, with a further layer of member liability above that.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: funds are raised by CALLS rather than by a fixed premium - an advance call paid at the beginning of the policy year, often in instalments and sometimes with a deferred portion; a supplementary or additional call where the year's claims exceed the estimate; a release call payable by a member leaving the club to buy out his exposure to future calls on open years; and an overspill call where a claim exceeds the top of the reinsurance programme.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: a member's contribution is assessed on entered tonnage, ship type and trade, the member's claims record, the deductibles chosen, and any general increase applied by the club across its membership; laid-up returns may be allowed for extended lay-up.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: the policy year for protection and indemnity cover conventionally runs from noon on 20 February to noon on 20 February.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: the PROTECTION side of the cover derives from the mutual hull clubs and covers the owner's liabilities as operator of the ship - crew and other personal injury, illness, death and repatriation, passenger and third-party injury, the proportion of collision liability not recoverable under the hull policy and excess collision liability, damage to fixed and floating objects, wreck removal, pollution liability and clean-up, life salvage, stowaways and persons rescued at sea, quarantine and diversion expenses, certain fines, and the legal costs of all of these.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: the INDEMNITY side arose to cover liabilities the hull clubs would not, and covers cargo and contractual liabilities - loss, shortage, damage, delay, contamination and mis-delivery of cargo carried, liability arising under the contract of carriage, cargo's proportion of general average not recoverable because of a breach of the contract of carriage, damage to property on board other than cargo, and liabilities under towage and other contracts approved by the club.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: modern club rules do not divide their cover into two formally separate classes labelled protection and indemnity; the terms are historical and describe the origin of the two groups of risk rather than a present-day boundary in the rules.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: principal exclusions - damage to the entered ship herself and her machinery, which is the hull and machinery underwriter's subject; war risks, for which separate cover is arranged; freight, demurrage and defence, which is a separate class of cover; liabilities arising from unapproved contracts or from carriage on terms less favourable than the applicable carriage regime; and wilful misconduct.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: the conditions on which cover is granted and continued - application with full disclosure, issue of a certificate of entry recording the terms, maintenance of class with an approved classification society and prompt reporting of conditions or withdrawal of class, maintenance of statutory certification, submission to condition survey where required, payment of calls, compliance with the club's rules and any trading or other warranties, notification of material changes, and prompt notice and co-operation after a casualty.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: the 'pay to be paid' principle, under which a member must first discharge the liability before recovering from the club, subject to the statutory rights of direct action that certain compulsory-insurance regimes confer on claimants.
- PRIMARY (identity and status, from the corpus central amendment register): the civil liability and fund regimes and the bunkers convention are in force; the wreck removal convention has been in force since 14 April 2015; and the maritime labour convention financial security provisions introduced by the 2014 amendments are in force, the 2022 amendments entering into force on 23 December 2024 and therefore being FUTURE at this sitting.
