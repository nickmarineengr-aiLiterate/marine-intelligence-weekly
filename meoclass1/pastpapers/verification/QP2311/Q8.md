## 2. DECOMPOSITION GATE - COMPLETED BEFORE RESEARCH

**Completed:** 2026-08-14, before research and drafting

**Question intent.** Sixteen marks in one unlimbed block setting two tasks joined by 'including': the developments in marine diesel engines generally, and the retrofit methods adopted to enable slow and ultra-slow steaming. The word 'including' makes the retrofits the larger task. The discriminating feature is whether the candidate can explain WHAT GOES WRONG at prolonged low load and present each retrofit as an answer to a named failure, rather than reciting a catalogue. A 2023 answer must additionally know that slow steaming has become a regulatory matter - EEXI, CII and power limitation - and not only a commercial one.

**Mark allocation.** 16 in a single block with no printed apportionment. Roughly a third to the developments, delivered as a seven-row table so it cannot sprawl, and the balance to the retrofits. A section on the failure modes of prolonged low load is inserted BETWEEN the two halves, because it is what converts the retrofit list from a catalogue into an argument and it costs only a bulleted list. The retrofits are then grouped by the failure they answer - air, combustion, wear, propulsion, heat - with the regulatory power limitation retrofit as a separate section and a closing judgement on which ship needs which.

**Examiner expectation.** That the candidate knows why slow steaming saves fuel and why it damages an engine; can name the developments without turning the answer into an emissions essay; understands that electronic control is what made software tuning possible and therefore split the retrofit menu in two; can pair each retrofit with the failure it addresses; remembers cylinder lubrication, the heat balance and the propeller, which are the three most commonly omitted; and knows that EEXI power limitation makes these retrofits necessary rather than optional.

**Freshness risk.** MEDIUM - and it runs in two directions. The regulatory content is recent rather than absent: EEXI and CII are eleven months old and in transition at this sitting, and the 2023 GHG Strategy is four months old, so a paper from earlier in 2023 would answer differently. The other direction is technology maturity: ammonia and hydrogen are under development and NOT in commercial service at November 2023, and a later editor would upgrade them. Both are pinned to the sitting.

**Jurisdiction risk.** LOW. The subject is engineering and the regulation is MARPOL. No Indian instrument is engaged; the domestic statute at this sitting is the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958 and nothing in the answer needs it.

**Technical ambiguities adjudicated.**

- Whether the first failure at low load is combustion or air supply - it is AIR: the turbocharger is off its matched point, and most retrofits attack that
- Whether turbocharger cut-out is available on any engine - only on an engine with two or more turbochargers
- Whether low-load tuning is a hardware change - not on an electronically controlled engine, where timing lives in software; on a mechanically controlled engine the equivalent requires hardware
- Whether de-rating and power limitation are the same - they are not; de-rating permanently re-tunes the engine to a lower rated output, while power limitation restricts the power that may be delivered and must be capable of override
- Whether power limitation may be irreversible - it may not; it must be capable of being overridden to release a power reserve where safety or saving life at sea requires, with the override recorded
- Whether cold corrosion and high-temperature corrosion are the same - they are not; cold corrosion is sulphuric acid condensing on a cool liner, while vanadium and sodium corrosion attacks hot exhaust valves and turbine parts
- Whether slow steaming reduces the boiler load - it INCREASES it, because less waste heat means the auxiliary boiler fires more and part of the saving is given back
- Whether the propeller is unaffected - it is not; it was matched to a speed the ship no longer runs at, and re-pitching or replacement moves the barred speed range
- Whether ammonia and hydrogen engines are in service at this sitting - they are not; they are under development

**Target answer shape.** Ten numbered sections, no limb headings, because the paper prints none. One on why the question exists and why it is regulatory by 2023. One seven-row table of developments. One bulleted section on what goes wrong at prolonged low load, which is the hinge of the whole answer. Then five retrofit sections grouped by the failure they answer - air, combustion, lubrication and wear, propulsion train, heat balance. One section on engine and shaft power limitation as the regulatory retrofit unique to this period. And a closing section pairing operational measures with a four-part judgement on which ship needs which retrofit.
