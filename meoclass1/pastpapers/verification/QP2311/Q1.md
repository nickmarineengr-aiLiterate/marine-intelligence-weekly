**Technical ambiguities adjudicated.**

- Whether continuous survey extends the five-year interval - it does not; it distributes it, and every item is still examined within the five years
- Whether continuous survey is statutory or class - it is a classification arrangement accepted in discharge of the statutory machinery survey
- Whether continuous survey and the planned maintenance system are the same thing - they are not; one says when class must see the item, the other when the ship overhauls it, and an approved PMS is the bridge between them
- Whether doing the work discharges the item - it does not; crediting by the surveyor does
- Whether a Chief Engineer's survey delegates judgement - it delegates examination and recording only, and the surveyor confirms
- Whether remote survey is a general substitute for attendance - it is accepted for a defined subset of items only
- Whether condition monitoring can displace opening-up - only where class approves a condition-based arrangement, and the data must have a trustworthy baseline
- Whether the question is about hull - it is not; it is expressly about machinery, and enhanced survey programme and docking material is out of scope

**Target answer shape.** Two printed limb headings. Under (a), five numbered sections: what the scheme is and where it sits; its significance as a bulleted list; its objectives as a numbered list; the methodology as an eight-row table ending on the overdue item and the condition of class; and the technology section closing expressly on limits. Under (b), an enumerated list of eleven challenges followed by an eleven-row two-column table pairing each with its solution, and one closing paragraph identifying the common root - that these are planning and information failures rather than engineering ones.

**Sources consulted.**

- PRIMARY (identity and status): SOLAS 1974 chapter I regulation 6 - survey and inspection, and the recognition of nominated surveyors and recognized organizations. Instrument identity and amendment status taken from the corpus central amendment register.
- PRIMARY (identity and status): SOLAS 1974 chapter I regulation 10 - surveys of the hull, machinery and equipment of cargo ships, requiring a renewal survey at intervals not exceeding five years, with intermediate and annual surveys inside the cycle. This five-year interval is the arithmetic the continuous scheme is built on.
- PRIMARY (identity and status): SOLAS 1974 chapter I regulation 11 - maintenance of condition after survey; no change may be made without sanction of the Administration.
- PRIMARY (identity and status): A.1156(32), Survey Guidelines under the Harmonized System of Survey and Certification, 2021 - THE OPERATIVE GUIDELINES AT THIS SITTING. The 33rd Assembly successor was adopted on 6 December 2023, one month after this sitting, and is not cited.
- PRIMARY (identity and status): the ISM Code as amended through MSC.353(92), in force 1 January 2015 - section 10, maintenance of the ship and equipment, which is the safety-management route by which the maintenance evidence supporting a continuous scheme is generated. Chain and dates from the corpus amendment register.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: continuous survey of machinery is a classification-society arrangement under which the items that would otherwise be examined together at the machinery renewal survey are divided into groups and examined in rotation, so that every item is examined within the five-year cycle and roughly a fifth of the items fall due each year.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: classification societies permit, subject to approval, certain continuous survey items to be examined and recorded by the Chief Engineer rather than by an attending surveyor, with the surveyor confirming the records; and permit an approved planned maintenance scheme to be operated in place of, or in support of, the continuous arrangement, subject to periodical audit.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: an item that passes its due date without being examined or postponed becomes overdue and attracts a condition of class or equivalent recommendation, which must be discharged by a stated date; class is at risk if it is not, and the ship's insurance and charterparty position follows class.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: classification societies operated remote survey techniques - streamed video, photographic and documentary evidence assessed ashore - at scale from 2020 onward, and by this sitting they are an established route for a defined subset of survey items rather than an experiment.
- P3 INDUSTRY GUIDANCE: condition monitoring practice - vibration analysis, lubricating oil analysis, thermography, performance and thermodynamic monitoring - and its use in condition-based maintenance notations offered by classification societies.
- ENGINEERING JUDGEMENT: the pairing of each enumerated challenge with a proportionate solution, and the ordering of the challenges from the commercially most damaging outward.
- INTERNAL REUSE VERIFIED: QP2310-Q2 (October 2023) for the annual survey and the meaning and effect of a condition of class; QP2308-Q8 (August 2023) for the survey-type vocabulary. Both read in full; neither supplied any wording used here.
