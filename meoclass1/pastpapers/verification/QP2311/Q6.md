**Freshness risk.** LOW - the Code's operative text has been stable since 1 January 2015 and no later Code amendment exists to import. **Jurisdiction risk.** LOW. The ISM Code is international and applies through SOLAS chapter IX. The Company's own standards are contractual rather than national. The domestic statute at this sitting is the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958; the 2025 Act commenced 15 March 2026 and is prohibited.

**Technical ambiguities adjudicated.**

- Whether the planned maintenance system and the class survey regime are the same thing - they are not; the PMS is the Company's ISM-driven overhaul regime and the survey regime is class's, and they meet only where class approves the scheme and credits maintenance against survey items
- Whether replacing the failed part is corrective action - it is a CORRECTION; corrective action removes the cause, and the Code requires measures intended to prevent recurrence
- Whether closing an action means checking it was done - it means checking it was done AND that it worked; an ineffective action is reopened
- Whether a maintenance record is paperwork or evidence - it is the evidence for class credit, port State control, vetting, insurance, charter disputes and interval optimisation
- Whether as-left condition alone is sufficient - it is not; without AS-FOUND condition there is no basis for changing an interval
- Whether critical equipment is a class term - it is an ISM Code requirement: equipment whose sudden operational failure may result in a hazardous situation, attracting specific measures including regular testing of standby arrangements
- Whether critical equipment tests may sit outside the PMS - they may not; the Code requires them to be integrated into the ship's operational maintenance routine
- Whether running hours is always the right basis for an interval - it is not; a standby item maintained on running hours may never come due, which is why the standby-testing obligation exists
- Whether deferring a job and extending an interval are the same act - they are not; they are different decisions with different approvals and different records

**Target answer shape.** An opening section mapping the four printed terms onto ISM Code section 10, with a four-row table, and one sentence fixing the PMS-against-class-survey boundary.
