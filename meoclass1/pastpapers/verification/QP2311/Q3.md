**Technical ambiguities adjudicated.**

- Whether the bill of lading IS the contract of carriage - between owner and charterer it is evidence of it, the charterparty being the contract; in a third-party holder's hands it is the contract sued upon
- Whether a sea waybill is a document of title - it is not, and goods under it are delivered against identification rather than presentation
- Whether a letter of indemnity cures a clean bill issued for damaged cargo - it does not; it is unenforceable against the party deceived and it prejudices the owner's P&I cover
- Whether the master must sign whatever a time charterer presents - the obligation to sign as presented does not extend to bills that are false, misdated or inconsistent with the mate's receipts
- Whether a voyage charter has an on-hire survey - it does not, properly so called: no hire is paid and the ship is never delivered; what is done is a pre-loading condition and cargo-worthiness survey
- Whether an on-hire survey is a class or statutory survey - it is neither; it is a commercial survey between the parties and it credits nothing
- Whether limb d) asks about one survey or three - the limb prints a singular noun after a plural numeral; the intended sense is plainly 'which of the three surveys', and the stem is preserved rather than corrected
- Whether the bareboat charterer becomes the owner - he does not; he takes possession and control for the period and redelivers in like condition, fair wear and tear excepted

**Sources consulted.**

- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: a bill of lading performs three functions - it is a receipt for goods received or shipped, stating their quantity, marks and apparent order and condition; it is evidence of the contract of carriage; and it is a document of title to the goods, transferable by endorsement and delivery where it is made out to order.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: where a charterparty exists, the bill of lading is evidence of the contract of carriage but is not itself the contract as between owner and charterer, the charterparty being that contract. In the hands of a third-party holder who is not the charterer, the bill of lading contains or evidences the contract on which that holder sues.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: the principal distinctions between a shipped or on-board bill and a received-for-shipment bill; between a clean bill and a claused or foul bill; and between an order bill, a straight bill naming a consignee, and a bearer bill. - AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY (identity only): the Hague-Visby Rules, given effect in India by the Carriage of Goods by Sea Act, 1925 as amended in 1993; and the Indian Bills of Lading Act, 1856, which is the statute in force at this sitting. No article, rule or section is quoted.

- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: the mate's receipt records the quantity and apparent order and condition as ascertained by the ship, and any clausing on it must be carried through onto the bill of lading.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: standard forms are NAMED ONLY in this answer - the voyage charter and its associated bill, the time charter forms, and the bareboat form. No clause of any form is quoted, numbered or paraphrased as though read.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: under a bareboat or demise charter the charterer takes possession and control of the ship, mans, operates, maintains and insures her for the charter period, and redelivers her in like condition, fair wear and tear excepted. Under a time charter the owner retains crewing and maintenance while the charterer directs employment and pays hire and bunkers.
