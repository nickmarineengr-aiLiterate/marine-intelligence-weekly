**Question intent.** Sixteen marks printed as one unlimbed block concealing THREE tasks: name the present ISO standards for marine fuel and give their salient features; state the CONTENT of a fuel oil analysis report; and give corrective action against EACH adverse observation. The word 'each' makes the third task the largest. The discriminating features are naming the correct EDITION of the specification at this sitting, naming ISO 4259 and knowing what it does, giving the report's structure rather than only its results table, and knowing which findings are treatable and which mean the fuel is not burnt at all.

**Mark allocation.** 16 in a single block with no printed apportionment, so the candidate must apportion. Roughly a third to the standards and their features, a fifth to the report content, and the balance to corrective action, which is where the word 'each' puts the weight. The corrective action task is delivered as a three-column table - observation, what it threatens, what you do - because seventeen findings cannot be delivered as prose inside an examination. A separate short section is placed BEFORE the corrective action table covering sample identity, precision, segregation and notification, because that is the sequence a Chief Engineer is actually judged on and no donor supplies it.

**Examiner expectation.** That the candidate names ISO 8217 WITH its edition; knows the general requirements clause and that sulphur defers to statute; knows that the specification governs the fuel as delivered and not at the engine inlet; can structure an analysis report rather than list results; and can pair each adverse finding with a proportionate action, distinguishing the three findings where the fuel must not be burnt at all from those that are managed by treatment, temperature, throughput and load.

**Freshness risk.** HIGH - the highest on this paper, and it is a single date. The specification's seventh edition was published in 2024, eight months after this sitting, and the donor question sits after that publication and may legitimately call it current. Importing it would hand a candidate an edition that did not exist. A second, smaller edge is the corpus resolver's annotation that the fuel oil quality paragraphs were replaced by a 2024 resolution, which is also future here.

**Jurisdiction risk.** LOW. The specification is international and the regulation is MARPOL. No Indian instrument is engaged. The contractual layer - notice periods for a quality claim - is contract law rather than national law, and the answer says the applicable period is read from the contract rather than asserting one.

**Technical ambiguities adjudicated.**

- Which edition of the specification is current at this sitting - the sixth, of 2017; the seventh is eight months future
- Whether the specification sets the sulphur limit - it does not; it requires the purchaser to specify sulphur in accordance with the relevant statutory requirements, so MARPOL governs
- Whether the flash point minimum is an ISO requirement - it is a SOLAS requirement, which is why a low flash point is a safety breach and not a commercial dispute
- Whether an on-specification fuel is safe to burn as delivered - not necessarily; the specification is a DELIVERY specification and the engine-inlet requirement for abrasive particulate is far lower
- Whether a marginal result proves an off-specification fuel - it does not, once the method's reproducibility under ISO 4259 is applied
- Whether the fuel may be burnt while a claim is being pursued - it should not be; burning it destroys the evidence and usually the claim
- Whether a low flash point can be corrected by blending - it cannot, and attempting it is a safety breach
- Whether a non-availability report permits burning non-compliant fuel - it does not; it records a supply failure and is not a defence for burning fuel already known to be non-compliant
- Whether high sodium is a fuel quality problem or a water problem - very often it is seawater, which is why purification and a chloride test come before any additive

**Target answer shape.** Six numbered sections, no limb headings, because the paper prints none. One on the standards and the edition with a five-row standards table. One on salient features as a seven-item numbered list plus an eight-row table grouping parameters by the failure they threaten. One on the content of an analysis report in six numbered parts. One short but load-bearing section on the four steps that precede any corrective action, with the regulation 18 scaffolding behind them. One large three-column corrective action table covering seventeen adverse observations. And a closing judgement section separating findings that are treated from findings that are refused.
