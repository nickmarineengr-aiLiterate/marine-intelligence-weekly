## 2. DECOMPOSITION GATE - COMPLETED BEFORE RESEARCH

**Completed:** 2026-08-14, before research and drafting

**Question intent.** Three limbs of 5, 5 and 6 marks. Limb a) wants the definition, the formula and the mechanism by which mean piston speed governs fuel burn - the power identity and the propeller cube law - together with the qualification that specific consumption rises off the matched point so lower is not automatically cheaper. Limb b) wants two things and names them: the proportionality to rpm at fixed stroke and the reason long-stroke engines exist, and the effect of piston speed on combustion in BOTH directions. Limb c) wants an ordered, defensible shipboard methodology on a ship whose data is not to be trusted. The discriminating feature is whether the candidate knows that the optimum is a band rather than the lowest attainable speed.

**Mark allocation.** 5 + 5 + 6, all printed. Limb a) gets a formula table and two short mechanism sections. Limb b) gets one section on rpm and one on combustion, the latter built as a two-column HIGH against LOW table because the low-speed case is where the marks are in a slow-steaming era and is what most answers omit. Limb c) carries the most marks and prints 'formulate a methodology', so it is written as five numbered stages in a defensible order, with measurement proved before the engine is judged and the constraints stated before the decision.

**Examiner expectation.** That the candidate writes 2LN and not LN; can show why power is a pressure term times a speed term; can convert a speed reduction into a power reduction by the cube law AND knows that consumption per mile follows roughly the square; can explain combustion degradation in both directions from the design point; and can produce a methodology that starts with calibration and fuel measurement rather than with the engine. A Chief Engineer who separates hull, propeller, engine condition and fuel quality before pronouncing on the engine is demonstrating the judgement the limb is testing.

- Derive the power identity from the definition of mean effective pressure, so the significance for fuel follows rather than being claimed
- State the propeller cube law WITH its assumptions - fixed pitch, unchanged hull and weather
- Distinguish fuel per hour from fuel per mile explicitly, because that distinction is the whole commercial point
- Describe the SFOC curve as passing through a minimum in the upper-middle load range, as a region and not a percentage figure
- Give typical mean piston speed ranges as orders of magnitude only, and assert no maker, engine type, power, speed or SFOC value
- Establish the SEEMP and CII position at this sitting from the corpus amendment register, reversing the register's present-tense supersession marking on MEPC.346(78)
- State that 2023 is the first CII data year and that it is STILL RUNNING at this sitting
- Order the limb c) methodology so that instruments and fuel measurement are proved first, and hull, propeller, engine condition and fuel quality are separated before the engine is judged
- Name the retrofit hardware only as the exit route, leaving it to Q8 on this same paper

**Jurisdiction risk.** LOW. The question is entirely technical and international. No Indian instrument is engaged; the domestic statute at this sitting is the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958 and it is not cited because nothing in the answer needs it.

**Technical ambiguities adjudicated.**

- Whether mean piston speed is 2LN or LN - it is 2LN, because the piston covers the stroke twice per revolution
- Whether it is a mean or a maximum - a mean; the peak near mid-stroke is roughly half as much again
- Whether rpm and mean piston speed are interchangeable - only within one engine, because the relation depends on the stroke
- Whether fuel consumption follows the cube law - power per hour does, approximately; consumption per mile follows roughly the square because the voyage lengthens
- Whether the lowest mean piston speed gives the lowest fuel cost - it does not; SFOC rises off the matched point and deposits, fouling and maintenance cost rise with it
- Whether combustion degrades only at high piston speed - it degrades in BOTH directions from the design point, and the low-speed case is the one that matters in a slow-steaming era
- Whether the propeller law applies to a controllable-pitch installation - not in the same way; shaft speed may be held while pitch varies, so mean piston speed is fixed while load moves
- Whether a consumption problem on an old ship is an engine problem - often it is not; hull fouling, propeller roughness, drifted pump timing, a fouled turbocharger and fuel quality all present the same way

**Target answer shape.** Three printed limb headings. Under (a), a definition section with a two-row formula table and a significance section built on the power identity and the cube law, closing on why lower is not always better. Under (b), one bulleted section on the rpm relation including the long-stroke argument, and one section on combustion built as a HIGH against LOW table with a unifying paragraph on design-point matching. Under (c), five numbered stages - baseline and calibration, structured test and data reduction, separation of hull, propeller, engine and fuel, the real constraints, and the decision with its review - closing on the judgement that the optimum is a negotiated band.

**Verification statement.**

ENGINEERING, NOT REGULATORY, AND THE PROVENANCE IS DECLARED ACCORDINGLY. The physics is P1 in the sense that matters for an engineering answer: mean piston speed is defined by the geometry of the crank mechanism as twice the stroke times the rotational speed, and the identity that power equals the product of mean effective pressure, piston area, mean piston speed and cylinder number follows from the definition of mean effective pressure itself. Both are derived in the answer rather than asserted. The propeller cube law is stated as the approximation it is, with its assumptions named. THE NUMERICAL RANGES ARE THE WEAK POINT AND ARE MARKED AS SUCH. Typical mean piston speeds for low-speed two-stroke, medium-speed four-stroke and high-speed engines are given as orders of magnitude drawn from general engineering practice; NO MAKER, ENGINE TYPE, POWER, RPM OR SFOC VALUE IS PRESENTED AS SOURCED, because the True Source corpus holds no engine-maker documentation and none was consulted. The regulatory frame of limb c) - SEEMP Part III mandatory from 1 January 2023, the 2022 SEEMP guidelines MEPC.346(78) operative at this sitting, and 2023 as the first CII data year still running - is P1 by identity and status from the corpus amendment register, with the register's own supersession marking correctly reversed for this sitting.

**Sources consulted.**

- PRIMARY (derivation): mean piston speed is defined by the crank geometry. In one revolution the piston travels twice the stroke, so mean piston speed = 2 x stroke x rotational speed. Stated in metres per second with rotational speed in revolutions per second, or 2LN/60 with N in revolutions per minute.
- PRIMARY (derivation): from the definition of mean effective pressure, indicated power = mean effective pressure x swept volume x cycles per second x number of cylinders. Substituting swept volume as piston area x stroke and grouping the stroke with the speed yields power proportional to mean effective pressure x piston area x mean piston speed x number of cylinders. Power is therefore the product of a PRESSURE term and a SPEED term, and mean piston speed is that speed term.
- PRIMARY (identity and status): MARPOL Annex VI chapter 4 - the SEEMP, with Part III mandatory from 1 January 2023 for ships to which the CII applies. Annex VI identity and the 1 November 2022 entry into force of MEPC.328(76) taken from the corpus record and TSCR-3.
- PRIMARY (identity and status): MEPC.346(78), the 2022 SEEMP Guidelines, are the OPERATIVE guidelines at this sitting. The corpus amendment register marks them superseded by MEPC.395(82); that supersession is a 2024 event and is future here. The register is present-tense and its direction is reversed for this sitting.
- PRIMARY (identity and status): the CII rating regime applies from 1 January 2023, so 2023 is the FIRST CII data year and it is still running at this November sitting. No ship has yet been issued a rating.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: the propeller law for a fixed-pitch propeller in unchanged hull and weather conditions - delivered power varies approximately as the cube of shaft speed and torque as the square. It is an approximation whose assumptions are stated in the answer.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: specific fuel oil consumption varies with engine load and passes through a minimum in the upper-middle part of the load range for a conventionally tuned engine, rising at both very high and low loads. The rise at low load is attributable to reduced compression and scavenge pressure, turbocharger mismatch and poorer atomisation.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: mechanical friction losses in the running gear increase with mean piston speed, so mechanical efficiency falls as mean piston speed rises; wear rate and cylinder oil demand rise with it too.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: the time available for injection, ignition delay and complete combustion in each cycle is inversely proportional to rotational speed, and therefore inversely proportional to mean piston speed at fixed stroke.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: typical mean piston speeds are of the order of 8 to 8.5 metres per second for large low-speed two-stroke engines at their rated output, 9 to 11 for medium-speed four-stroke engines and higher again for high-speed engines. These are orders of magnitude from general engineering practice, not maker data.
- P3 INDUSTRY GUIDANCE: shipboard engine performance measurement practice - steady-state test points, correction to reference ambient conditions, indicator and electronic pressure measurement, flowmeter and sounding reconciliation, and hull and propeller performance monitoring.
- ENGINEERING JUDGEMENT: the ordering of the limb c) methodology so that instrumentation and fuel measurement are proved before any engine conclusion is drawn, and the placing of hull, propeller and engine condition ahead of the choice of operating point.
- INTERNAL REUSE VERIFIED: QP2503-Q8 and QP2307-Q7 for hull roughness management and condition-based propeller maintenance; QP2306-Q9 for the resistance vocabulary. Read for consistency; no prose taken.

- The position of the SFOC minimum is described as a region in the upper-middle load range rather than as a percentage of MCR, because it depends on engine type, tuning and turbocharger matching and no held source records it for any particular engine.
- No numerical value is given for the load below which auxiliary blowers cut in, for a barred speed range, or for a minimum continuous load. These are ship-specific and are read off the ship's own documentation, which is what limb c) instructs.
- The propeller cube law is an approximation for a fixed-pitch propeller in unchanged hull and weather conditions. Its assumptions are stated in the answer and no claim is made for its accuracy on a fouled hull, in a seaway, or with a controllable-pitch propeller off its design combinator curve.
- No charterparty speed-and-consumption warranty form is cited. The commercial constraint is described in general terms because no standard form is held.
