## 2. DECOMPOSITION GATE - COMPLETED BEFORE RESEARCH

**Completed:** 2026-08-14, before research and drafting

**Question intent.** A printed preamble followed by three limbs at 6, 6 and 4 which have very little to do with one another. The preamble looks like an invitation to write the standard AFS Convention and coating-taxonomy essay, and that is the trap: the taxonomy is needed only as vocabulary for limb (a). Limb a) wants a comparison of roughness BEHAVIOUR by coating type. Limb b) wants the mechanism of a foul release coating and then what incorporated oil adds to it, with its limits. Limb c) wants occupational health during application - and it is about the whole coating system, including the removal of the old scheme, rather than about tin alternatives specifically. The discriminating features are the physical-against-biological roughness distinction, the modulus-and-surface-energy relationship, and organising limb c) by the hierarchy of control rather than as a PPE list.

**Mark allocation.** 6 + 6 + 4, all printed. The preamble is deliberately compressed into two paragraphs and one four-row table so that it cannot consume the page. Limb a) gets a section on what roughness is and the physical-against-biological distinction, then a four-row comparison table whose columns are 'at application' and 'through the interval', which is the whole point of the limb. Limb b) gets a mechanism section first, then the enhancement, then a limits section carrying half the marks. Limb c) at four marks gets a seven-row hazard table and six numbered controls in hierarchy order, closing on the regulatory frame named rather than quoted.

**Examiner expectation.** That the candidate knows the four tin-free families and can say what each does to the SURFACE over time; distinguishes physical from biological roughness and knows the smoothest coating is not automatically the best; can state that foul release works on adhesion rather than toxicity and needs both low surface energy and low modulus; can explain oil incorporation as a mobile surface layer that lowers the release threshold, AND can state its limits; and organises the safety limb by the hierarchy of control with PPE last, naming the removal of the old coating as the most toxic operation and high-pressure injection injury as the acute hazard.

**Primary source plan.**

- Read QP2306-Q5, the same-year June 2023 member, in full FIRST - and record that it shares only the preamble, so no family edge is created
- Read QP2503-Q8 and QP2307-Q7 for hull roughness management and QP2306-Q9 for frictional resistance vocabulary
- Confirm from the corpus that the AFS Convention is NOT held and set the regulatory content at authoritative-secondary level accordingly
- State the cybutryne position precisely as at this sitting - applied from 1 January 2023, ten months old, transition still running
- Quote no annex or regulation of the Convention and attribute no paragraph
- Name no paint manufacturer, product, brand or formulation anywhere
- Present every numerical figure expressly as an order of magnitude, and assert no service life, polishing rate, surface energy, modulus, threshold speed or roughness-to-power coefficient
- Use the release relationship qualitatively only, to explain why both modulus and surface energy must be low
- Leave the biocidal-status question of oil-amended coatings OPEN and take no position
- Quote no occupational exposure limit, and refer to the enclosed-space recommendations descriptively rather than by resolution number

**Freshness risk.** MEDIUM - and it sits entirely on one date. The cybutryne control applied from 1 January 2023 and at this sitting is TEN MONTHS OLD and still in transition, with the sixty-month arithmetic running. Every donor-era paper in the corpus is 2024 or later and would treat the transition as complete or historical. That is the reversal, and it runs on the freshness of the amendment rather than on its absence.

**Jurisdiction risk.** LOW. The Convention is international and the coatings technology is not national. Limb c) touches national occupational hygiene regimes, and that is handled by quoting no exposure limit at all and by noting that for work done in dock the yard's regime is usually the controlling one. The domestic statute at this sitting is the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958.

**Technical ambiguities adjudicated.**

- Whether the printed preamble governs limb (c) - it governs (a) and (b) fully and (c) only contextually, because application health is about the whole coating system including removal of the old scheme
- Whether the smoothest coating is the best - it is not; a fouled smooth surface is worse than a clean rougher one, and biological roughness costs far more than physical roughness of the same height
- Whether over-coating reduces roughness - it does not; it compounds it, and only removal to bare steel resets it
- Whether a foul release coating is biocidal - it is not; it works on adhesion, not toxicity
- Whether low surface energy alone is enough for foul release - it is not; the release force scales with the square root of modulus times surface energy, so both must be low
- Whether oil incorporation makes the coating tougher - it does not; it lowers the release threshold and improves static performance, and does nothing for mechanical fragility
- Whether the oil enhancement is permanent - it is not; the oil is a finite reservoir and the benefit decays across the docking interval
- Whether an oil-amended foul release system is regulated as biocidal - OPEN. The answer records it as a question and takes no position
- Whether PPE is the primary control - it is the LAST line of the hierarchy, after elimination, engineering and administrative controls

**Target answer shape.** One short setting section carrying the Convention, the cybutryne date and a four-row family table, deliberately compressed. Then three printed limb headings. Under (a), a section on what roughness is with the physical-against-biological distinction, then a four-row comparison table with 'at application' and 'through the interval' as columns, closing on repair, over-coating and the cleaning regime. Under (b), a mechanism section built on modulus and surface energy, then a bulleted section on what the oil does, then a limits section carrying half the marks and leaving the regulatory question open. Under (c), a seven-row hazard table and six numbered controls in hierarchy order, closing on the regulatory frame and a judgement that ties the three limbs together.

- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: the four families of tin-free anti-fouling system - controlled depletion polymer, self-polishing copolymer, tin-free hybrid, and non-biocidal foul release - and their distinguishing mechanisms of dissolution, hydrolysis-driven polishing, blended behaviour, and low-adhesion release.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: average hull roughness is the accepted measure of the physical roughness of a ship's underwater surface, obtained by traversing a stylus gauge over the hull and averaging the maximum peak-to-trough height measured over successive short intervals, and reported in micrometres.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: added surface roughness increases frictional resistance and therefore the power required at a given speed, and biological fouling causes a substantially larger penalty than physical roughness of the same nominal magnitude.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: a foul release coating works by presenting a surface of low surface energy and low elastic modulus, so that organisms adhere weakly and are removed by hydrodynamic shear once the ship exceeds a threshold speed. The force required to release an adhered organism scales with the square root of the product of elastic modulus and surface energy, which is why both must be low.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: silicone oils blended into but not chemically bound to the polydimethylsiloxane matrix migrate toward the surface and form a mobile liquid layer, which lowers the effective surface energy further, interferes with the curing and anchoring of biological adhesives, and reduces the shear stress and therefore the ship speed required to release fouling.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: incorporated oil is a finite reservoir which depletes as it leaches, so the enhancement decays across the docking interval; and unbound oil at the surface complicates repair, over-coating and adhesion of subsequent schemes.
