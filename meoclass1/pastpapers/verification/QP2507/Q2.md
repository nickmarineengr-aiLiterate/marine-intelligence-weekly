## 9. ANSWER ROUTE AS BUILT

Archetype `explain`. The route is the single canonical sequence; the model answer's
principal headings **are** these steps, and `validate_spec.py` enforces that
correspondence.

1. **The problem that makes life-cycle accounting necessary**
   - Every mandatory measure in force at this sitting - EEXI, CII, the Data Collection System - counts emissions at the funnel
   - A zero-carbon fuel at the funnel is not necessarily a low-carbon fuel: ammonia and hydrogen carry the emissions of their production pathway
   - Counting only at the funnel therefore permits CARBON LEAKAGE - the emission is moved ashore rather than removed
   - It also mis-ranks fuels: LNG loses on methane slip, biofuels win or lose on feedstock and land use, and none of that is visible at the funnel
2. **What life cycle analysis of a marine fuel is** *(limb (a))*
   - A systematic, quantified assessment of the greenhouse gas emissions of a fuel across its entire life, from resource to use
   - The output is a GHG INTENSITY: mass of carbon dioxide equivalent per unit of energy delivered, in grams of CO2 equivalent per megajoule
   - Carbon dioxide equivalent because methane and nitrous oxide are converted at their global warming potentials
   - The IMO basis at this sitting: the 2023 IMO GHG Strategy, resolution MEPC.377(80), requires the ambitions and checkpoints to take account of WELL-TO-WAKE emissions as addressed in the LCA guidelines
   - The operative guidelines are the 2024 Guidelines on Life Cycle GHG Intensity of Marine Fuels, resolution MEPC.391(81), adopted 22 March 2024, whose paragraph 5 REVOKES the LCA Guidelines adopted by MEPC.376(80)
   - Both are GUIDELINES - recommendatory, not mandatory on a ship
3. **The four methodological stages of an assessment** *(limb (a))*
   - GOAL AND SCOPE DEFINITION - why the study is being done, the functional unit (one megajoule of energy delivered), and the system boundary
   - INVENTORY ANALYSIS - quantify every input and output across the boundary: energy, feedstock, emissions, co-products
   - IMPACT ASSESSMENT - convert the inventory into impact categories, principally global warming potential expressed as carbon dioxide equivalent
   - INTERPRETATION - test sensitivity and uncertainty, check completeness and consistency, and state the conclusions and their limits
   - The stages are iterative: an inventory result routinely sends the analyst back to the boundary
4. **The physical stages of a marine fuel pathway** *(limb (a))*
   - FEEDSTOCK - extraction, recovery or cultivation, including any land-use change for a biofuel and any fugitive methane from gas production
   - PROCESSING AND CONVERSION - refining, liquefaction, synthesis or electrolysis, including the electricity mix used and any carbon capture
   - TRANSPORT, STORAGE AND DISTRIBUTION - moving the fuel to the bunkering port, with boil-off and losses
   - BUNKERING - transfer to the ship
   - ONBOARD CONVERSION - combustion or fuel-cell conversion in service, including methane slip, nitrous oxide and pilot fuel
   - Plus the accounting questions that cut across them: allocation between co-products, end-of-life, and the treatment of biogenic carbon
5. **Well-to-Tank, Tank-to-Wake and Well-to-Wake** *(limb (b))*
   - WELL-TO-TANK is everything upstream - feedstock, production, distribution and bunkering - ending when the fuel is in the ship's tank
   - TANK-TO-WAKE is everything onboard - conversion in the engine or fuel cell, including slip and pilot fuel
   - WELL-TO-WAKE is the sum of the two, and is the complete figure
   - So WtT is a COMPONENT of WtW, not an alternative to it - the choice the question poses is really between the whole and one of its parts
   - The two parts differ in who controls them: the shipowner controls tank-to-wake and buys well-to-tank from someone else
6. **Why well-to-wake is the feasible basis** *(limb (b))*
   - It is the only basis that closes carbon leakage - the emission cannot be moved out of the boundary, only out of existence
   - It is the basis the 2023 IMO GHG Strategy adopted for its ambitions and checkpoints, and MEPC.391(81) exists to make it operable
   - Only WtW ranks the candidate fuels correctly: green, blue and grey ammonia are the same fuel at the funnel and entirely different fuels over the life cycle
   - It captures both ends of the methane problem - upstream leakage and onboard slip - which neither half does alone
   - Well-to-tank alone is not a usable metric for a ship: it says nothing about the ship's own operation
   - Tank-to-wake alone is measurable and enforceable but incentivises the wrong thing, which is why CII being a funnel measure is a known limitation rather than a model
   - At MEPC 83 in April 2025 the Committee APPROVED a draft new MARPOL Annex VI chapter carrying a well-to-wake GHG Fuel Intensity mechanism - approved only, NOT adopted and not law
   - The first binding well-to-wake regime is regional, not IMO: FuelEU Maritime has applied since 1 January 2025 to energy used by ships calling at European ports
7. **What makes it hard, and how the difficulty is managed** *(limb (b))*
   - The shipowner does not control, observe or audit the upstream half of his own fuel's emissions
   - Upstream data varies with feedstock, plant, electricity grid mix and season, and is commercially sensitive
   - Allocation between co-products, and the treatment of land-use change, are methodological choices that materially change the answer
   - Verification, certification and the risk of double counting across regimes all add administrative burden
   - The practical answer is a two-part system: CONSERVATIVE DEFAULT VALUES for the upstream half, which any supplier may improve upon by CERTIFYING an actual value, combined with MEASURED consumption onboard
   - That is why the guidelines matter as much as the strategy: without an agreed method for the well-to-tank half, a well-to-wake obligation could not be administered
