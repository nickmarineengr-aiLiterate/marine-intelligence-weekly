   - CAREER - interrupted sea time around maternity in a system whose revalidation depends on recent sea service; attrition at the first-command and first-chief-engineer stage
4. **The IMO's role - the programme, the mechanism, and its limits** *(limb b)*
   - the Women in Maritime Programme, established in the late 1980s in the technical co-operation work - fellowships, training places, institutional support
   - the regional Women in Maritime Associations, the programme's most durable structure
   - agenda-setting - the World Maritime Day theme for 2019 and the International Day for Women in Maritime on 18 May
   - measurement - the joint IMO and WISTA International Women in Maritime Survey
   - gender mainstreaming in the Strategic Plan and the technical co-operation programme
   - STCW is already gender-neutral on its face, which locates the barrier at access, conditions and retention rather than certification
   - THE LIMIT - almost all of it is promotional and capacity-building rather than mandatory; the hardest law in this field is not an IMO instrument at all
5. **The hard legal layer, and exactly where it stood at this sitting** *(limb b)*
   - the MLC 2006 is an ILO instrument, amended by the Special Tripartite Committee under Article XIII, not by IMO tacit acceptance
   - the 2022 amendments are IN FORCE from 23 December 2024 - seven months before this sitting
   - Standard A4.3(1)(b) now requires all necessary APPROPRIATELY-SIZED personal protective equipment - Part A, therefore mandatory and inspectable
   - PPE sized for the average male body does not protect a smaller person: this is a safety defect, not a comfort complaint
   - the 2016 amendments, in force 8 January 2019, strengthened protection against shipboard harassment and bullying
   - the 2025 amendments were APPROVED by the International Labour Conference at its 113th Session in June 2025 - one month before this sitting - and are NOT IN FORCE
   - APPROVED IS NOT IN FORCE: state the approval, refuse the operative status, and do not describe their content
6. **India's initiatives to reduce the gender gap** *(limb b)*
   - REGULATORY - the Directorate General of Shipping as competent authority under the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958, with MLC obligations taking domestic effect through the Merchant Shipping (Maritime Labour) Rules
   - TRAINING - DG Shipping-approved institutes open to women across deck, engine and electro-technical streams, and the Indian Maritime University under the Act of 2008
   - PLACEMENT - the real bottleneck is the CADET BERTH, not admission to training; administrative measures address berth availability, shipboard conditions and harassment redressal
   - POLICY - national maritime policy commitments to women's participation, and support for the IMO's regional Women in Maritime association
   - THE CLOSING JUDGEMENT - the regulatory barrier has largely gone; what remains is berth allocation, shipboard conditions and retention, which are decided by owners and by senior officers on board rather than by regulators
