## 2. WHAT THE QUESTION ASKS

Two eight-mark limbs of different kinds. Limb (a) is a process question about how a ship becomes and stays classed, with an explicit sub-demand about how the society ENSURES compliance - that is, its enforcement mechanism, not merely its activities. Limb (b) is a distinction question disguised as a scenario: it asks what happens when the class relationship is doubled, and then asks who does the statutory work - which is the trap, because the answer is that the statutory work was never a class function at all.

**Mark allocation.** 16, printed as 8 + 8. Even split, and the two limbs are genuinely equal in demand: limb (a) needs breadth across the ship's life, limb (b) needs one sharp distinction argued properly plus the practical management detail. Three sections to limb (a) and three to limb (b) gives each mark a home.

**Examiner expectation.** For (a): that class is a private certification against the society's own rules; the sequence from application and plan approval through material certification, construction survey, assignment with notations, the maintenance survey cycle, damage reporting and conditions of class; and that the enforcement lever is condition, suspension and withdrawal rather than detention. Then the legal hook - SOLAS II-1/3-1 adopting class rules as the construction standard, and XI-1/1 with the RO Code as the authorisation route. For (b): what dual class is and is not, why an owner takes it, the joint-class agreement and the division of survey scope, mutual acceptance of records, and above all that statutory surveys and audits belong to the flag Administration and are performed by whichever organisation it has authorised - not by both societies, and not automatically by either.

**Required answer components:**

- What classification actually is, and the sharp line between class and statutory work
- The requirements a ship must satisfy to be assigned class - plan approval, survey during construction, and the maintenance-of-class regime
- Where class rules acquire legal force - SOLAS II-1/3-1, and the authorisation route in SOLAS XI-1/1 and the RO Code
- What dual class means and why an owner would take it
- How dual class is managed in practice - the split of scope, the survey arrangement and the IACS procedural layer
- Who conducts the statutory surveys and audits when two societies are involved, and why that is a flag State question and not a class question

**Ambiguities adjudicated:**

- 'Requirements for the classification of a ship' could mean the technical requirements the ship must meet or the procedural requirements for obtaining class. The mark allocation and the second half of the limb ('the role of classification societies in ensuring compliance') indicate the procedural reading, so the answer runs the process and states the enforcement mechanism.
- 'A ship is classes by two classification societies' is printed for 'classed', identically to the March 2025 sitting. Preserved verbatim; the answer treats it as 'classed'.
- 'Who is responsible for conducting statutory surveys and audits in such cases' presupposes that dual class changes the answer. It does not, and saying so plainly is the point of the limb rather than an evasion of it.
- 'Audits' is printed alongside 'surveys' and must be answered separately - ISM and ISPS verification are delegated separately and produce different documents.

**Target answer shape.** Six numbered sections under two printed limb headings. Limb (a): one section drawing the class/statutory line, one running the eight-stage classification process, one on the two legal hooks and the enforcement mechanism. Limb (b): one on what dual class is and why it is taken, one on how it is managed, one on the statutory question with the II-1/3-1 textual point and a stated conclusion.

## 9. ANSWER ROUTE AS BUILT

Archetype `explain`. The route is the single canonical sequence; the model answer's
principal headings **are** these steps, and `validate_spec.py` enforces that
correspondence.

1. **What classification is, and the line between class and statutory work** *(limb a)*
   - class is a private contractual certification against the society's own published rules
   - the society's sanction is condition, suspension and withdrawal - never detention
   - class is commercially compulsory even though it is not law - insurers, charterers, financiers and flag all require it
   - statutory work belongs to the flag Administration and reaches a society only by delegation
2. **The requirements a ship must satisfy to be assigned class** *(limb a)*
   - application and selection of the applicable rule set, including CSR for bulk carriers and oil tankers
   - plan approval of structure, machinery, piping and electrical installations before construction
   - approval and certification of materials and components at approved works under survey
   - survey during construction against the approved plans, with NDT, tank testing and trials
   - assignment of class and the Certificate of Class, with notations recording the basis of build
   - maintenance of class by annual, intermediate and renewal surveys, docking, tailshaft and machinery surveys
   - the owner's duty to report damage, defects and repairs affecting class
   - conditions of class as the time-bound instrument of enforcement short of suspension
3. **Where class rules acquire legal force, and how the society ensures compliance** *(limb a)*
   - SOLAS II-1/3-1 - ships shall be designed, constructed AND MAINTAINED to the requirements of a recognised society, or to equivalent national standards
   - SOLAS I/6 - survey is the Administration's function, which it may entrust to nominated surveyors or recognized organizations
   - SOLAS XI-1/1 - that authorisation must follow the RO Code, MSC.349(92), parts 1 and 2 mandatory
   - the society acts in two capacities - as class under its own rules, and as RO in the Administration's name
   - the RO's minimum powers under I/6 - require repairs, survey at a port State's request, notify the Administration
4. **What dual class means, and why an owner takes it** *(limb b)*
   - entered with two societies at once; both rule sets must be satisfied and two Certificates of Class held
   - distinguish sharply from transfer of class and from the ordinary class/flag split
   - drivers - national register requirements, charterer or oil-major preference, yard or financier requirement, second-hand purchase, market access
   - costs - two rule sets, two survey programmes, two record sets, and the risk of divergent views
5. **How the arrangement is managed in practice** *(limb b)*
   - a joint-class agreement settled in advance between the two societies and the owner
   - where the rule sets differ the stricter governs, because both must be satisfied
   - division of the survey scope - by system, or by alternating the cycle - with each accepting the other's survey
   - mutual exchange and acceptance of survey reports, thickness measurements and conditions of class
   - one coherent survey status on board, whichever society raised the item
6. **Who conducts the statutory surveys and audits, and why this is a flag question** *(limb b)*
   - statutory surveys and audits are the Administration's; only it can say who performs them
   - dual class confers no statutory authority - a society may class the ship and hold no delegation from her flag
   - SOLAS II-1/3-1 speaks of A classification society - singular and indefinite; one recognised society satisfies it
   - statutory certification is not duplicated - one Cargo Ship Safety Construction Certificate, not two
   - ISM and ISPS audits are separately delegated and produce the DOC and SMC and the ISSC
   - the same surveyor may do both on one attendance, under two different authorities - which is what obscures the distinction
