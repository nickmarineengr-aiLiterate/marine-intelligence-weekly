## 9. ANSWER ROUTE AS BUILT

Archetype `legal`. The route is the single canonical sequence; the model answer's
principal headings **are** these steps, and `validate_spec.py` enforces that
correspondence.

1. **What a standard charter party is** *(limb (a))*
   - a charter party is the contract by which an owner places the ship, or her capacity, at a charterer's disposal
   - a STANDARD charter party is a pre-printed industry form published by a recognised body, its wording negotiated between representative interests and settled by long use
   - in practice the fixture is the printed form AS AMENDED by a rider of additional and amending clauses
   - VOYAGE charter - a stated voyage with a stated cargo; freight, laytime and demurrage are its commercial heart
   - TIME charter - the ship at the charterer's disposal for a period, the charterer directing employment and paying hire and ordinarily bunkers and port charges
   - BAREBOAT or DEMISE charter - the charterer takes possession, mans and operates her as if owner; this is the form that decides limbs (c) and (d)
2. **The advantages of using a standard form** *(limb (a))*
   - CERTAINTY OF MEANING - the wording has been litigated and arbitrated for decades, which is the largest advantage and cannot be bought another way
   - SPEED AND COST - a fixture concludes on a form and a short rider in hours; bespoke drafting per voyage would make the market unworkable
   - BALANCE - drafted or approved by bodies representing both owners and charterers, so the printed risk allocation starts from a recognised middle
   - COMPATIBILITY - brokers, clubs, underwriters, banks, arbitrators and courts all know the forms, and published clause sets bolt on
   - COMPLETENESS - off-hire, lien, general average, arbitration and notices are all covered rather than omitted and litigated
   - FEWER AND CHEAPER DISPUTES - argument is usually confined to the rider clause the parties actually negotiated
   - THE CAUTION: the form is a starting point, and riders routinely reverse the printed allocation of risk
3. **What the cover is, and what it reaches** *(limb b))*
   - the hull policy is the owner's and the owner's P&I entry does not answer for the charterer's liabilities - charterer's cover fills that gap, written as a charterer's entry or by the fixed-premium market
   - DAMAGE TO THE CHARTERED SHIP HERSELF is ordinarily the charterer's largest exposure - unsafe port or berth, damaging cargo, and off-specification or contaminated bunkers he supplied
   - CARGO LIABILITY where he is the contracting carrier, and cargo claims passed to him through the charterparty indemnity chain
   - POLLUTION arising from his own operations, including a bunkering he arranged
   - THIRD-PARTY LIABILITIES - damage to fixed and floating objects, and contributions to wreck removal
   - personal injury, illness and death claims attributable to his act or order; general average and salvage contributions on his bunkers and freight; loss of or damage to his bunkers; fines and penalties so far as insurable
   - DEFENCE COVER for charterparty disputes - hire and off-hire, laytime and demurrage, speed and consumption, bunker quality
   - THE TWO LIMITS: it insures LIABILITIES not commercial losses, so unpaid hire and an unprofitable fixture are outside it; and it does not answer for wilful misconduct or trading in breach of warranty
4. **The answer turns on who the carrier is** *(limb c))*
   - YES - but not because he is a charterer. He is liable to the cargo holder only if he is the CARRIER under the bill
   - the bill of lading is a contract between holder and carrier, and the holder is a STRANGER to the charterparty
   - OWNER'S BILL - signed by the master, or by an agent for and on behalf of the master: the shipowner is the carrier
   - CHARTERER'S BILL - signed by the charterer or his agent in his own name as carrier: the charterer is the carrier and is liable to the holder
   - BAREBOAT CHARTER - the demise charterer possesses and operates her as if owner, so bills are ordinarily his contracts
   - identity-of-carrier and demise provisions on the form may identify the shipowner as carrier whoever signed; their effect is a matter of construction
   - where he IS the carrier his liability is the Hague or Hague-Visby liability - due diligence to make her seaworthy, and proper care of the cargo - with the carrier's defences and package limitation, given domestic effect in India by the Indian Carriage of Goods by Sea Act, 1925
5. **And the indemnity chain can put the claim back on him anyway** *(limb c))*
   - even on an OWNER'S bill, where the shipowner is sued, the loss frequently ends with the charterer
   - EMPLOYMENT AND INDEMNITY - the time charterer directs employment and requires bills to be signed as presented, and the owner is entitled to be indemnified against the consequences of complying
   - INTER-CLUB APPORTIONMENT - time charters on the New York Produce Exchange form customarily apportion cargo claims between owner and charterer BY THE CAUSE of the loss, mechanically, without litigating fault
   - CONCLUSION: liable to the holder if he is the carrier; and liable to the owner through the charterparty even if he is not
6. **Channelling, and the one convention whose definition reaches a charterer** *(limb d))*
   - LARGELY NO, and the reason is a deliberate design feature of the conventions - but there is one exception and it must be named precisely
   - CLC 1992 - MAKES THE REGISTERED OWNER STRICTLY LIABLE, and Article III(4) CHANNELS liability to him, barring claims against a protected list that expressly includes any charterer howsoever described, INCLUDING A BAREBOAT CHARTERER
   - the point of channelling is that the claimant gets a single identifiable compulsorily insured defendant and need not litigate a chain of commercial parties
   - THE EXCEPTION IS NARROW: protection is lost only for that person's own act or omission committed WITH INTENT, or RECKLESSLY AND WITH KNOWLEDGE that such damage would probably result - ordinary negligence comes nowhere near it
   - BUNKERS 2001 - does NOT channel. Article 1(3) defines 'shipowner' WIDELY as the registered owner, bareboat charterer, manager and operator, jointly and severally liable, so A BAREBOAT CHARTERER IS LIABLE and a time or voyage charterer is not
   - the compulsory insurance duty under Bunkers still falls on the REGISTERED OWNER alone, so the certificate is his though the liability is shared
   - THE PATTERN: Nairobi likewise makes the registered owner strictly liable, and HNS 2010 would channel similarly but WAS NOT IN FORCE at this sitting
   - THE QUALIFICATION: channelling bars the pollution claimant, not the owner's recourse - the charterer can still be liable to the owner under the charterparty for an unsafe port or bad bunkers
