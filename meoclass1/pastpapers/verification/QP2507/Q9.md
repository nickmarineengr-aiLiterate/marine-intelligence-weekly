## 2. WHAT THE QUESTION ASKS

A scenario question with two express demands and one decisive qualifier. The demands are WHAT steps and WHO is responsible for initiating them; the object is twofold, the safety of the ships and the marine environment; and the qualifier is BY THE COASTAL STATE. The qualifier is the whole difficulty: the vessel is Indian-flagged and the casualty is off India, so India is simultaneously flag State and coastal State, and it is entirely possible to write a complete and accurate flag-State answer that does not address the question asked. The examiner rewrote this question from an earlier sitting precisely to insert that qualifier.

**Mark allocation.** 16, undivided. The two named objects - ships and environment - take roughly equal weight and are the largest blocks; the jurisdictional basis and the who-is-responsible allocation are each worth real marks because the stem's own words demand them; the investigation limb is kept proportionate rather than expansive, because it is primarily a flag-State function on a question that has asked about the coastal State. Seven sections weight it that way.

**Examiner expectation.** That the candidate notices the narrowing and answers it: the coastal State's jurisdictional basis in India's maritime zones, reaching both ships regardless of flag; the Part XA directions power with action in default and cost recovery as the legal engine; detention of an unsafe ship; the place-of-refuge decision as an exclusively coastal-State call under A.1184(33); the national pollution response through the Indian Coast Guard and the NOSDCP; navigational safety measures; and a named allocation of responsibility. Then, correctly bounded, the investigation layer - the national inquiry and the mandatory marine safety investigation triggered by the loss of life - with an honest statement of which parts are flag-State functions and where the coastal State's standing lies.

**Required answer components:**

- What has actually happened in legal terms, and which State the examiner is asking about
- The coastal State's jurisdiction - why it may act at all, and over whom
- What the ship must do, and what the coastal State requires of her
- The coastal State's steps for the safety of the ships
- The coastal State's steps for the marine environment
- Investigation and inquiry - which part belongs to the coastal State and which to the flag State
- Who is responsible for initiating each step, and what funds it

**Ambiguities adjudicated:**

- 'By the coastal state' is the decisive qualifier and it is easy to read past, because India is also the flag State here. Every step in the answer was tested against the question 'would this power exist if the ship were foreign flagged'.
- The printed scenario does not state the OTHER vessel's flag. Both possibilities are addressed: coastal-State jurisdiction reaches her either way, but the investigation architecture and India's standing in it differ.
- 'The Merchant Shipping Act' is printed without a year. At this sitting it can only mean the Act of 1958, and the answer says so expressly rather than relying on the reader to infer it.
- 'Steps ... to ensure the safety of the ships and the marine environment' names two objects, and the answer must not collapse them - the ships limb is about compulsion and movement, the environment limb about response and cost recovery.
- The investigation limb is engaged but is primarily a flag-State function. It is included because the casualty plainly requires it and because the coastal State has real standing in it, but it is kept proportionate rather than allowed to dominate a coastal-State answer.

**Target answer shape.** Seven numbered sections. One separating the casualty into its four legal events and identifying which State the stem asks about. One establishing the coastal State's jurisdictional basis. One on what the ship must do and what the coastal State requires of her. Then the two objects the stem names, each as an attributed list of coastal-State steps. One bounding the investigation between coastal and flag State. One answering 'who is responsible' as a named allocation table with a one-sentence summary and a closing caution about the two hats.

## 9. ANSWER ROUTE AS BUILT

Archetype `legal`. The route is the single canonical sequence; the model answer's
principal headings **are** these steps, and `validate_spec.py` enforces that
correspondence.

1. **What has happened, and which State the examiner is asking about**
   - Four legal events in one casualty: a collision, loss of life, damage to the vessels, and an oil spill - each with its own machinery
   - Loss of life makes this a VERY SERIOUS MARINE CASUALTY
   - THE STEM IS NARROWED: it asks what steps should be initiated BY THE COASTAL STATE, not what steps should be taken generally
   - India is here BOTH the flag State of the casualty vessel AND the coastal State - and the examiner has asked about one of them
   - The governing statute is the MERCHANT SHIPPING ACT, 1958; the Act of 2025 had not received assent at this sitting
   - The Act is enabling as much as prohibitory - most of what follows is a power to DIRECT, and a corresponding duty to comply
2. **The coastal State's jurisdiction - why it may act at all**
   - The casualty is in India's maritime zones, established by the Maritime Zones of India Act, 1976
   - Coastal-State jurisdiction is TERRITORIAL, not flag-based, so it reaches BOTH ships - including the other vessel, whatever her flag
   - The Merchant Shipping Act, 1958 supplies the domestic powers, principally Part XA and the unsafe-ships power
   - Those powers are exercised by the Central Government and the Director-General of Shipping
   - Coastal-State measures must be proportionate to the harm threatened to the coastline and related interests
3. **What the ship must do, and what the coastal State requires of her**
   - The order on board is LIFE, then SHIP, then ENVIRONMENT, then EVIDENCE
   - Muster and account for all persons and search for the missing - the overriding duty where life is lost
   - Render assistance to the other vessel and persons in danger, stand by, and exchange particulars - the Act's collision Part, not discretionary
   - Assess damage and stability, contain flooding, stop the source of the spill and deploy the SOPEP
   - Report outward in parallel - Indian Coast Guard and MRCC, DG Shipping through the Mercantile Marine Department, owner and DPA, port and coastal authorities - plus a MARPOL Protocol I report and a SOLAS chapter V danger message
   - Preserve evidence: voyage data recorder, charts, logs, engine and alarm records
4. **The coastal State's steps for the safety of the ships**
   - Take charge of and coordinate the casualty response
   - GIVE DIRECTIONS to the master, owner or salvor under Part XA - to move, to act or refrain, or to be salved - exercisable in respect of EITHER ship
   - DETAIN an unsafe ship under the unseaworthy-and-unsafe-ships power, sections 334 to 336, on the Director-General's authority
   - DECIDE ON A PLACE OF REFUGE - exclusively the coastal State's call, under resolution A.1184(33) of 6 December 2023, on an objective analysis of the risks of granting against refusing
   - Secure navigational safety - mark the casualty, issue warnings, establish an exclusion or safety zone
   - Require survey and approve repairs before the ship proceeds; oversee salvage, and apply the wreck and salvage Part, sections 390 to 404, where wreck arises
5. **The coastal State's steps for the marine environment**
   - THE LEGAL ENGINE IS PART XA, sections 356J to 356L - directions to owner, master or salvor for prevention or containment; action in default; and RECOVERY OF THE COST from the owner
   - Activate the National Oil Spill Disaster Contingency Plan, with the INDIAN COAST GUARD responsible for the response at sea in India's maritime zones
   - The plan is TIERED - local resources, then national, then international mobilisation
   - Response operations - surveillance to establish extent and drift, containment booms, mechanical recovery, dispersant only where authorised, shoreline protection and clean-up, waste reception
   - Protect coastal and related interests with State pollution control boards, fisheries and environment departments and port authorities
   - Recovery of cost is backed by the owner's P&I cover and the compulsory insurance behind the CLC or Bunkers certificate, with direct action against the insurer
6. **Investigation - which part is the coastal State's, and which is not**
   - TWO PARALLEL SYSTEMS with different purposes, and they must not be merged
   - The national inquiry under the MS Act 1958 investigations and inquiries Part - report, preliminary inquiry, then a formal investigation before a court with assessors which may find fault and cancel or suspend a certificate
   - The marine safety investigation under the Casualty Investigation Code, MSC.255(84), mandatory through SOLAS XI-1/6, exists to PREVENT RECURRENCE and not to apportion blame
   - It is MANDATORY here because a very serious marine casualty includes one involving A DEATH - engaged by the death, not automatically by the spill
   - BOTH are primarily FLAG STATE functions, and here India happens to be the flag State too - say so rather than blurring it
   - The coastal State's own standing: a State whose environment or coastal interests are seriously harmed is a SUBSTANTIALLY INTERESTED STATE entitled to participate, and if the other vessel is foreign flagged that is the basis of India's participation in her investigation
7. **Who is responsible for initiating each step**
   - The MASTER initiates everything in the first hour - muster, assistance, damage control, SOPEP and the first reports
   - The OWNER AND COMPANY initiate the commercial and technical response through the ISM safety management system
   - The CENTRAL GOVERNMENT, through the DIRECTOR-GENERAL OF SHIPPING, directs, detains, acts in default and recovers the cost
   - The INDIAN COAST GUARD executes the pollution response at sea under the national plan
   - The COASTAL STATE decides on a place of refuge
   - The ADMINISTRATION holds the preliminary inquiry and any formal investigation; the FLAG STATE conducts the marine safety investigation
   - Closing caution: India wears both hats here, but they are different powers with different bases - and if the other vessel is foreign flagged the distinction becomes immediately practical
