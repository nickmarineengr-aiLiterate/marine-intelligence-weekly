## 9. ANSWER ROUTE AS BUILT

Archetype `explain`. The route is the single canonical sequence; the model answer's
principal headings **are** these steps, and `validate_spec.py` enforces that
correspondence.

1. **The mechanism - why a solid starts behaving as a liquid** *(limb (a))*
   - A fine-particled cargo is a solid only because of inter-granular friction and capillary forces between wet particles
   - Ship vibration and rolling progressively COMPACT the cargo, reducing the void volume between particles
   - The water occupying those voids cannot escape at the same rate, so PORE WATER PRESSURE rises
   - Rising pore pressure reduces the effective stress - the contact force - between particles in equal measure
   - When effective stress approaches zero the particles are supported by water rather than by each other, and the mass flows: it has liquefied
   - Nothing was added to the cargo. The same material has changed state because the water inside it moved
2. **The conditions and the contributing factors** *(limb (a))*
   - THREE CONDITIONS MUST COINCIDE: a cargo of a type that can liquefy, a moisture content above the transportable moisture limit, and sufficient ship motion and vibration
   - The cargo type is established from the DECLARED Bulk Cargo Shipping Name and its current individual schedule - never from the commodity name used in conversation
   - Fine particle size and a proportion of clay or very fine material are what make the mechanism possible
   - Contributing factors on the supply side: open stockpiles, rain during storage or loading, wet mining and washing processes, barge transfer in a seaway, poor drainage, and monsoon-season loading
   - Contributing factors on the ship side: engine and propeller vibration, rolling and pitching, long voyages, and heavy weather
   - Sampling error is a factor in its own right - a stockpile is not homogeneous, and a certificate is only as good as the sample behind it
3. **TML and FMP - the numbers that decide acceptance** *(limb (a))*
   - FLOW MOISTURE POINT - the moisture content at which the cargo begins to lose shear strength and behave as a fluid under test conditions
   - TRANSPORTABLE MOISTURE LIMIT - the maximum moisture content considered safe for carriage; for most cargoes it is ninety per cent of the flow moisture point
   - Not every cargo uses that relationship - some schedules specify their own determination, so the individual schedule governs
   - Laboratory determination is by the flow table test, the penetration test or the Proctor-Fagerberg test, set out in Appendix 2
   - Two certificates matter: the TML certificate, valid for a limited period before loading, and the MOISTURE CONTENT certificate, taken much closer to loading because moisture changes and TML does not
   - The CAN TEST is the master's complementary check on board - it can raise an alarm but it is not a substitute for the certified laboratory test
4. **Liquefaction is not dynamic separation** *(limb (a))*
   - In liquefaction the cargo mass itself flows
   - In DYNAMIC SEPARATION the moisture migrates upward and forms a liquid SLURRY above a still-solid mass - a wet base with a free surface, without the bulk of the cargo liquefying
   - The consequence for the ship is similar and equally dangerous: a free surface where none was expected, and a cargo surface that can slop and shift
   - The distinction matters because the casualty most often cited as a liquefaction case is now generally understood as dynamic separation, and because the Code addresses the two through different schedules and provisions
   - A candidate who knows the difference demonstrates that the mechanism, not the label, has been understood
5. **The dangers to the ship** *(limb (b))*
   - A FREE SURFACE appears in a hold designed on the assumption that there is none, and the virtual rise in the centre of gravity reduces GM
   - The liquefied mass SHIFTS to the low side as the ship rolls and does NOT return - the roll becomes asymmetric and a progressive list develops
   - Cargo can re-solidify at the angle of list, locking the ship in a heeled condition and removing the reserve she needs for the next sea
   - Successive shifts compound: each list increases the shift, which increases the list
   - Capsize can follow with very little warning and very little time - these are among the most sudden losses in the bulk trades
   - Secondary effects: loss of steering and manoeuvrability, structural damage from cargo movement against bulkheads, inability to launch survival craft from a heeled ship, and extreme danger to anyone in or near the hold
6. **What the casualties show** *(limb (b))*
   - 2010 - three bulk carriers carrying nickel ore from Indonesia and the Philippines were lost within weeks of each other with heavy loss of life; that cluster drove the IMSBC work on cargoes which may liquefy
   - 2013 - Harita Bauxite and Trans Summer, both associated with cargo behaviour of this kind
   - JANUARY 2015 - Bulk Jupiter, carrying bauxite, sank in the South China Sea with the loss of eighteen of her nineteen crew
   - BULK JUPITER IS THE INSTRUCTIVE ONE AND IT IS NOT A SIMPLE LIQUEFACTION CASE: the mechanism is now generally understood as DYNAMIC SEPARATION, and the regulatory response was a new schedule and express treatment of that phenomenon
   - 2017 and 2019 - Emerald Star and Nur Allya, both carrying nickel ore, both lost
   - THE COMMON THREAD: fine, moist ores loaded in tropical rain-affected ports, certificates that did not reflect the cargo actually loaded, and losses that were sudden rather than progressive
7. **Prevention and the regulatory framework** *(limb (c))*
   - THE INSTRUMENT: the IMSBC Code, mandatory through SOLAS, and at this sitting AS AMENDED BY AMENDMENT 07-23, which has been mandatory since 1 January 2025 and had been available for voluntary application from 1 January 2024, superseding amendment 06-21
   - BEFORE LOADING: the shipper must declare the cargo under its Bulk Cargo Shipping Name and supply the information required by section 4, with certificates of TML and of moisture content; the ship checks the declaration against the individual schedule and the section 7.3 conditions
   - ACCEPTANCE RULE: a cargo that may liquefy is not accepted unless its moisture content is below the TML, or the ship is specially constructed or fitted for the purpose, or the cargo is carried under the specific alternative conditions the Code allows
   - DURING LOADING: inspect the cargo and the barges; watch for splatter, free water, a flat or sloping surface that will not hold; carry out the complementary can test; suspend loading in rain and cover the hatches; trim as the schedule requires; and REFUSE OR STOP if the cargo does not match its declaration
   - ON PASSAGE: monitor bilges and sound wells for water and pump as permitted; watch the ship's roll behaviour for the asymmetry that signals a shift; avoid heavy weather by routeing; reduce motion by course and speed; and NEVER enter a hold to inspect
   - IF LIQUEFACTION IS SUSPECTED: minimise ship motion, seek shelter or a port of refuge, inform the company, the flag State and the nearest coastal State, and prepare the crew - the condition is not recoverable at sea
   - AT DISCHARGE: expect the cargo to have compacted or set at an angle, brief on the hazards of working a shifted cargo, and record and report what was found so that the loading terminal and the charterer are held to the certificate they issued
   - SUPPORTING FRAMEWORK: SOLAS chapter VI cargo information, SOLAS chapter XII for bulk carriers, and the BLU Code for the loading and unloading process itself
