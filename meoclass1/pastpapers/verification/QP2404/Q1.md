## 4. Temporal delta - an edition boundary was found

The intake flag said `STABLE / LOW`. That **understates** the risk, and the flag is recorded as
answered rather than accepted: digitalisation is the fastest-moving topic on this paper and any
current source describes a much later state of the art.

**The boundary found:** `MSC-FAL.1/Circ.3/Rev.3` is dated **4 April 2025** - eleven months
**after** this sitting - and on its release **Rev.2 (7 June 2022) was withdrawn**. Rev.2 is
therefore the revision current at April 2024.

**How it is resolved.** The answer relies on **MSC.428(98)**, which is mandatory and has not
moved, and **does not put a revision number in front of the candidate at all**. The revision
history is recorded in the study-notes source map and in `sources` so a future session can see the
reasoning, and the study notes tell the candidate to cite the mandatory resolution rather than a
circular revision. This removes the defect without hiding the finding.

Also checked and applied: EEXI/CII (1 January 2023) and EU ETS (1 January 2024) are **in** - both
in force at the sitting, EU ETS by only three months, which makes it a strong current point. No
development in autonomous shipping or artificial intelligence later than April 2024 is described
as existing; autonomy appears as a direction of development, which is what it was.

**No vendor, platform or product is named anywhere**, which also removes a whole class of temporal
drift, since products date faster than instruments do.
