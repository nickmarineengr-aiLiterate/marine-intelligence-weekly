- PRIMARY: MSC.255(84) chapter 6.1 - a marine safety investigation shall be conducted into every very serious marine casualty; 6.2 - flag State responsibility; chapter 14.1 - the final report of every very serious marine casualty investigation is submitted to the Organization.
- PRIMARY: MSC.255(84) preamble - resolution MSC.257(84) adopted amendments to SOLAS chapter XI-1 making Parts I and II of the Code mandatory, taking effect on 1 January 2010 upon entry into force of the amendments to regulation XI-1/6.
- PRIMARY: MSC.255(84) chapter 7 - participation in another State's investigation fulfils the flag State's obligations under the Code, SOLAS regulation I/21 and UNCLOS article 94(7).
- PRIMARY: MARPOL Article 12 - Casualties to ships: each Administration undertakes to investigate any casualty to its ships subject to the Convention where the casualty has produced a major deleterious effect upon the marine environment, and to supply the Organization with information on the findings.
- VERIFIED NEGATIVE: the term 'serious marine casualty' does not appear anywhere in MSC.255(84) - only 'very serious marine casualty' is defined. Searched across the full Code text.

## Addendum - 2026-08-12 candidate-feedback audit: SOLAS regulation I/21(b)

**Trigger.** A candidate supplied handwritten study notes asserting that the casualty report sent
to IMO "should be in the anonymous form and should not disclose identity or nationality of the
ships concerned or fix a responsibility upon any ship or person". The claim was treated as a
discovery lead only and independently verified before any use.

**Primary verification.** SOLAS 1974 as amended, chapter I part C regulation 21 (Casualties), read
verbatim from the SOLAS Consolidated Edition 2018:

- **(a)** "Each Administration undertakes to conduct an investigation of any casualty occurring to
  any of its ships subject to the provisions of the present Convention when it judges that such an
  investigation may assist in determining what changes in the present Regulations might be
  desirable." The regulation carries a footnote directing the reader to regulation XI-1/6.
- **(b)** "Each Contracting Government undertakes to supply the Organization with pertinent
  information concerning the findings of such investigations. No reports or recommendations of the
  Organization based upon such information shall disclose the identity or nationality of the ships
  concerned or in any manner fix or imply responsibility upon any ship or person."

**Adjudication.** The candidate was right that a non-disclosure rule exists and that the answer did
not carry it - paragraph (b) was genuinely absent from this answer, which stated only paragraph
(a). The candidate was **wrong about where the duty sits**: the obligation binds *reports and
recommendations of the Organization*, not the investigating Administration's own report. The note's
formulation would have had a flag State anonymising its own findings, which regulation 21 does not
require.

**Action.** Paragraph (b) added to the limb (a) bullet, stated correctly, with an explicit note that
the non-disclosure rule binds IMO's output rather than the Administration's report. Source and
regulation entries added; P1_PRIMARY_VERIFIED incremented by one. No other claim in this answer was
altered.
