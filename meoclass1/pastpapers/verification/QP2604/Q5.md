## Verification

### What was actually read

**Resolution MEPC.378(80)** in full where it matters: the operative clauses, the section 2
definitions, section 10 (Biofouling Management Plan) including all thirteen items of paragraph
10.3, and section 11 (Biofouling Record Book) including the eight entry categories of 11.4.

### The load-bearing status finding

Taken from the operative clauses themselves, not from commentary:

- clause 1 **ADOPTS** the 2023 Guidelines;
- clause 2 **REQUESTS** Member States to take urgent action in applying them;
- clause 3 **AGREES** to keep them under review;
- clause 4 **REVOKES** resolution MEPC.207(62).

**The instrument is recommendatory. It requests; it does not require.** There is no MARPOL annex
on biofouling. A candidate treating the Biofouling Record Book as the equivalent of the Oil Record
Book has made a legal error, and this is recorded as the question's `major_trap`.

### Content taken directly from the instrument

- **10.1** a ship-specific BFMP is recommended for every ship; **10.2** it should contribute to
  maintaining a **fouling rating of 1 or less**.
- **10.3.1** names the responsible officer or position and gives **chief engineer** as its own
  example - so citing the chief engineer's role is citing the instrument, not guessing.
- **11.2** the BFRB is recommended to be retained on board for the **life of the ship**, physical
  or electronic, stand-alone or integrated.
- **11.4** the eight entry categories, which are the whole of limb (d).

### Deliberately not quantified

**No fuel or resistance penalty percentage is given.** Published figures vary by an order of
magnitude with fouling rating, hull form, speed and measurement method, and MEPC.378(80) sets none.
A number would be false precision.

**No national arrival standard is quoted.** Several States operate their own biofouling
requirements; these are live external instruments revised independently of IMO. This is the
**fourth** instance in the set of the live-external-list problem - see the demand map.

## Sources relied on

- PRIMARY: IMO resolution MEPC.378(80), adopted 7 July 2023 at MEPC 80 - '2023 Guidelines for the control and management of ships' biofouling to minimize the transfer of invasive aquatic species'. Operative clause 1 ADOPTS the Guidelines; clause 2 REQUESTS Member States to take urgent action in applying them; clause 3 AGREES to keep them under review; clause 4 REVOKES resolution MEPC.207(62), the 2011 Guidelines. The instrument is RECOMMENDATORY - it requests, it does not require.
- PRIMARY: MEPC.378(80) section 2 definitions - biofouling is the accumulation of aquatic organisms on surfaces and structures immersed in or exposed to the aquatic environment, and can include pathogens; microfouling is caused by bacteria, fungi, microalgae and protozoans and creates a biofilm, also called a slime layer; macrofouling is visible plants and animals such as barnacles, tubeworms, mussels, algal fronds, bryozoans and sea squirts; an anti-fouling system (AFS) is a coating, paint, surface treatment, surface or device used to control or prevent attachment of organisms; a marine growth prevention system (MGPS) is an AFS used to prevent accumulation in niche areas or other surfaces; invasive aquatic species are non-native species which may pose threats to human, animal and plant life, economic and cultural activities and the aquatic environment; fouling rating is a number allocated to a defined inspection area from a visual assessment; capture is the containment, collection and removal of biofouling material and waste substances detached during cleaning; biofouling pressure is the accumulation rate, which differs regionally and seasonally.
- PRIMARY: MEPC.378(80) paragraph 10.1 - it is recommended that every ship have a ship-specific BFMP under the responsibility of shipowners, ship operators and shipmasters; 10.2 - an effective BFMP should contribute to maintaining a recommended fouling rating of 1 or less, as described in chapter 8.
- PRIMARY: MEPC.378(80) paragraph 10.3, items .1 to .13 - the BFMP should include the officer or position responsible (the Guidelines give chief engineer as the example); details of the AFS installed and where; recommended operating conditions suited to the AFS including temperature, salinity and speed; expected AFC efficacy through its lifetime; description of monitoring of biofouling risk parameters; the cleaning regime if any; details of hull and niche areas where biofouling may accumulate; the schedule for fixed inspections; procedures for reactive cleaning triggered by inspection results; a contingency action plan based on specific triggers; the regime for repair, maintenance and renewal of the AFS per the manufacturer's instructions; the process for monitoring and maintaining the MGPS; and details of the documentation and reports required. Paragraph 10.7 requires the BFMP to be reviewed after inspections and cleaning and updated if the management actions are ineffective or deficient. Paragraph 10.8 - a form of BFMP is set out in appendix 3.
- PRIMARY: MEPC.378(80) paragraph 11.1 to 11.5 - record-keeping in a BFRB is the responsibility of shipowners, ship operators and/or shipmasters; it is recommended that the BFRB be retained on board for the LIFE OF THE SHIP; it may be maintained physically or electronically and may be stand-alone or integrated into existing manuals or planned maintenance systems; and paragraph 11.4 lists the eight categories of entry, reproduced in the answer. A form of BFRB is set out in appendix 4.
- PRIMARY: MEPC.378(80) paragraph 1.8 - the related frameworks are the BWM Convention 2004 and the AFS Convention 2001; paragraph 1.9 - biofouling management supports hydrodynamic performance and energy efficiency, linked to the SEEMP guidelines (MEPC.346(78)) and the 2023 IMO GHG Strategy (MEPC.377(80)); paragraph 1.10 - the GEF-UNDP-IMO GloFouling Partnerships Project.
- PRIMARY: MEPC.378(80) section 9 - proactive and reactive cleaning; 9.12 captured biological waste and waste substances to be disposed of safely and in an environmentally sound manner; 9.13 and 9.14 - a cleaning report per appendix 2, a copy available on board, and the activity entered in the BFRB.
- P2_AUTHORITATIVE_SECONDARY: classification-society and industry guidance on hull performance monitoring, foul-release coatings and robotic in-water inspection and cleaning with capture. Used for limb (b) technology description only. No numeric fuel-penalty figure is quoted from it.

## Left open

- No numeric fuel or resistance penalty is quoted. Published figures for the added power demand caused by biofouling vary widely with fouling rating, hull form, speed and measurement method, and MEPC.378(80) itself sets none. A specific percentage would be a false precision, so the answer states the mechanism and its direction instead.
- National and sub-national biofouling requirements (for example the arrival standards applied by some States) are live regulatory instruments that change independently of IMO. The answer describes their existence and effect in principle and names no current list or threshold.
