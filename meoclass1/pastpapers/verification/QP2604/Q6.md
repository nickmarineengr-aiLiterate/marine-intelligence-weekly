## Verification

### Reuse decision

Tier D against **QP2601 Q6** (January) and **QP2603 Q4** (March), which are an exact pair.

### Why the similarity score was not used to classify

The normalised similarity is **0.1527**, and that number is **misleading**. April runs to 764
characters against January's 453, and the difflib ratio is strongly length-sensitive, so a question
that expands its wording scores low even when it asks the same things.

Classification was therefore made on a **task-by-task comparison**, and the four tasks map
one-to-one:

| | January (single running stem) | April (four printed limbs) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | role of the WHO | a) role of the WHO |
| 2 | national agencies | b) national agencies **+ the procedure followed in India** |
| 3 | certificates issued | c) certifications issued |
| 4 | who issues in India, validity, extension | d) **competent authority**, validity, **extension beyond expiry** |

**Note also that the source copy's recurrence table for this question lists only this sitting** and
does not list January or March. The relationship was found by the systematic sweep. That is the
exact mirror image of the Q2 finding on the same paper: on Q2 the table over-claimed, here it
under-claimed.

### The printed marks anomaly

Four limbs, each printed **(5)**. They sum to **20** against a question that instruction 2 values
at **16**. Every other question on this paper sums to exactly 16.

**Recorded as printed, not normalised.** The machine-readable subpart marks are left `null`:
writing 5 into each would assert a 20-mark total the paper's own instructions contradict, and
normalising to 4 would invent figures the examiner never printed. See the spec's
`subpart_marks_note`.

### Currency - checked, not assumed

This question carries **no Merchant Shipping Act dependency**. It rests on the IHR 2005 as amended
and the **Indian Port Health Rules 1955**, made under the **Indian Ports Act 1908**, so the
commencement of the Merchant Shipping Act 2025 on 15 March 2026 does not touch it. This is the
third paper on which that has been verified rather than presumed.

## Sources relied on

- PRIMARY: International Health Regulations (2005), as amended in 2014, 2022 and 2024 - consolidated WHO text, read in full for the relevant Articles. Article 39 (ship sanitation certificates: six months, extendable by one month, model in Annex 3, issue conditions); Article 20(3) and 20(4)-(5) (lists of authorized ports, WHO certification of ports, certification guidelines); Article 22 (role of competent authorities); Article 24(1)(c) (conveyance operators to keep conveyances free of sources of infection and contamination including vectors and reservoirs); Article 27 (affected conveyances - disinfect, decontaminate, disinsect, derat); Article 37 (Ship Declaration of Health, model in Annex 8); Annex 5 (specific measures for vector-borne diseases, including the 400 metre programme distance and recording of vectors on the SSCC).
- PRIMARY: International Health Regulations (2005), original 2005 text - read for direct comparison. Article 37 is titled 'Maritime Declaration of Health' in the original (12 occurrences of that term, none of 'Ship Declaration of Health'); the consolidated amended text uses 'Ship Declaration of Health' (6 occurrences, none of the former). This establishes the renaming as a real change rather than an inference.
- PRIMARY: WHO - the 2024 amendments entered into force on 19 September 2025 in accordance with Article 59.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: Directorate General of Health Services and Port Health Organisation material on the functions of the PHO in India, and the Indian Port Health Rules 1955 read with the IHR 2005.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: DG Shipping Order 10 of 2018 (revised) concerning SSCC/SSCEC and the Statement of Compliance route for coastal trade where no Port Health Officer is available.
