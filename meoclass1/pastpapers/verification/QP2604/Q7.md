## Verification

### THE HEADLINE TEMPORAL FINDING OF THE APRIL PAPER

Tier D against **QP2601 Q7** (January 2026). Two independent changes compound, and together they
mean the answer to limb (c) is **not** the answer January gave.

### 1. The governing statute changed

| | January 2026 | April 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| sitting relative to commencement | **before** | **after** |
| operative Indian flag State statute | Merchant Shipping Act, **1958** | Merchant Shipping Act, **2025** |

The Merchant Shipping Act, 2025 received assent on 18 August 2025 and was brought into force in
full on **15 March 2026** by S.O. 1244(E), repealing the 1958 Act by **section 324(1)** - all read
in the Gazette during the QP2607 work, now paying for itself a **fourth** time.

A statute sweep of the January object found the 1958 Act asserted in the model answer, the study
guide, `recall_15s`, `major_trap`, an `answer_route` core point, a retrieval card, `regulations`
and `search_aliases`. **Every one of those inverts for April.**

This is **not** sitting-relative prose of the March kind. March's ten re-anchors were arithmetic
over facts that had not moved. Here the underlying legal truth is different at the two sittings,
so the correct treatment under the learning-method rules is to record a currentness change and
**re-author**, not to clone an inconsistent route.

### 2. The question itself widened

January asked *"What mechanism is used in India to comply"*. April asks to **ANALYZE** the
**LEGISLATIVE AND CONSTITUTIONAL** mechanisms **SINCE ITS RATIFICATION**. Two substantive
additions: the constitutional dimension, which January did not ask for at all, and a span anchored
on ratification.

### What was verified for the re-authored limb (c)

- India **signed UNCLOS on 10 December 1982** and **deposited its instrument of ratification on
  29 June 1995**; the Convention entered into force on **16 November 1994**. Taken from the United
  Nations treaty status record.
- India is **dualist**: ratification binds the State but does not create domestic rights and duties.
- **Constitution Article 253** - Parliament may legislate to implement a treaty notwithstanding the
  distribution of legislative powers. **Union List** entries 14, 21, 25 and 57. **Article 297** -
  maritime-zone resources vest in the Union, limits by law. **Article 51(c)** - Directive Principle.
  All stated **by effect, not quoted**.
- The **Maritime Zones Act 1976** was enacted *before* ratification, in anticipation of the Third
  UN Conference - a good point on a question asking about the position "since its ratification".

### Limbs (a) and (b)

Reused unchanged. UNCLOS Articles 91, 92, 94(1)-(7) and 217 have not moved, and the sweep found no
sitting-relative prose in them.

### Left open

The subordinate rules under the 2025 Act were still being brought forward. The answer names the
statute, the administration and the mechanism rather than enumerating rules. It also does **not**
claim that every rule made under the repealed 1958 Act has been remade - repeal of a parent Act
does not automatically invalidate subordinate rules made under it, and the savings position was not
independently established.

## Sources relied on

- PRIMARY: United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea - Article 91 (nationality of ships, conditions for registration, genuine link), Article 92 (exclusive flag State jurisdiction on the high seas, ships sailing under two flags), Article 94 (duties of the flag State, paragraphs 1 to 7), Article 217 (enforcement by flag States in respect of marine pollution), and Article 211 (rules established through the competent international organization).
- PRIMARY: IMO Casualty Investigation Code (MSC.255(84)) footnotes citing UNCLOS Article 94 and Article 94(7) as the basis of the flag State inquiry duty - read in the Code text.
- INTERNAL REUSE, PRIMARY-VERIFIED IN QP2607: the RO Code and the Administration's monitoring obligations over recognized organizations.
- P2 AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: DG Shipping and Mercantile Marine Department material on the Indian administrative structure - registration, survey, certification and examination functions.
- PRIMARY: IMO Instruments Implementation Code (III Code) and the IMO Member State Audit Scheme, mandatory since 1 January 2016.
- PRIMARY: United Nations treaty status record for the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea - India signed on 10 December 1982 and deposited its instrument of ratification on 29 June 1995; the Convention was adopted at Montego Bay on 10 December 1982 and entered into force on 16 November 1994.
- PRIMARY (Constitution of India): Article 253 - Parliament may make law for the whole or any part of India for implementing any treaty, agreement or convention, notwithstanding the distribution of legislative powers; Seventh Schedule Union List entries 14 (treaties), 21 (piracy and crimes on the high seas), 25 (maritime shipping and navigation) and 57 (fishing beyond territorial waters); Article 297 - resources of the territorial waters, continental shelf and exclusive economic zone vest in the Union, with limits to be specified by law; Article 51(c) - Directive Principle to foster respect for international law and treaty obligations. Stated by effect, not quoted.
- INTERNAL REUSE, PRIMARY-VERIFIED IN QP2607: The Merchant Shipping Act, 2025 (Act No. 24 of 2025) received assent on 18 August 2025; S.O. 1244(E) of 10 March 2026 brought the whole Act into force on 15 March 2026; section 324(1) repeals the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958. All read in the Gazette during QP2607 verification. At the April 2026 sitting the 2025 Act is therefore the operative statute.
- S: The Territorial Waters, Continental Shelf, Exclusive Economic Zone and other Maritime Zones Act, 1976 - India declared its maritime zones by legislation enacted in anticipation of the Third UN Conference on the Law of the Sea, before ratification in 1995.
