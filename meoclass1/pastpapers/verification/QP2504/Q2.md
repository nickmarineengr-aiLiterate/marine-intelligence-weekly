## 2. DECOMPOSITION GATE - COMPLETED BEFORE RESEARCH

**Completed:** 2026-08-12, before research and drafting

**Question intent.** Three limbs at 4 + 6 + 6 which move from definition to conduct to procedure. Limb (a) is a pure recall test of a defined term and the circumstances that satisfy it. Limb (b) is a judgement test dressed as a procedure question - the examiner wants to see whether a Chief Engineer who disagrees with a detention behaves professionally or obstructively, and the correct answer is counter-intuitive to many candidates. Limb (c) is a systems test: who does what, and through which channel, once the dispute becomes formal.

**Mark allocation.** 16, printed as 4 + 6 + 6. The 4-mark limb is definitional and must be answered tightly - a long answer there steals time from the twelve marks that follow. Limbs (b) and (c) are equal and both are procedural, but they are different procedures: (b) is what happens on board and immediately, (c) is what happens afterwards and ashore. The answer keeps that boundary sharp because blurring it is how candidates write limb (b) twice.

**Examiner expectation.** For (a): the definition in something close to the Procedures' own words, and at least four or five of the nine enumerated grounds. For (b): that the candidate does NOT refuse to cooperate, does not sail, and does not argue with the officer on deck - but records, requests written particulars, notifies the company, the flag State and class, and works the deficiency. For (c): that the candidate knows the appeal does not suspend the detention, that the review route is a regional MOU mechanism rather than an IMO one, and that the flag State is the ship's principal advocate.

**Primary source plan.**

- Take the clear-grounds definition verbatim from A.1185(33) paragraph 1.7.2
- Take all nine enumerated grounds from paragraph 2.4.2 and note that they are expressly not exhaustive
- Take the limiting rule from 2.4.1 - what an inspection is confined to WITHOUT clear grounds - because that is what gives the term its force
- Take the more-detailed-inspection trigger from 2.5.1
- Take the right of appeal and the non-suspension rule verbatim from 2.3.11
- Take the detention definition from 1.7.4 and the notification duties from 3.5.2
- Take the rectification and release route, including the repair-yard provision, from 3.7.1 to 3.7.6
- Read chapter 5 and establish that it is NOT a detention review panel, so the IMO/MOU boundary can be drawn correctly
- Do NOT assert any MOU procedural rule, time limit or panel composition - none is held
- Exclude every A.1xxx(34) instrument: the 34th Assembly postdates this sitting by eight months

**Freshness risk.** LOW - the substantive content is stable. The one real risk is EDITION DRIFT IN THE WRONG DIRECTION: the 34th Assembly replaced the Assembly instrument set after this sitting, so an answer inherited from a 2026 paper would cite an A.1xxx(34) edition that did not exist in April 2025. The guard is to name A.1185(33) and its 6 December 2023 adoption date explicitly.

**Jurisdiction risk.** MEDIUM. The question is written from an Indian syllabus and the phrase 'Detention Review Panel' is a regional MOU term, not an IMO one. India participates in the Indian Ocean MOU, and the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958 with the DG Shipping circulars is the domestic vehicle - the 1958 Act governs at this sitting and the 2025 Act is post-sitting and prohibited. The answer must state the IMO layer from primary text and the regional layer as regional, without pretending the corpus holds the latter.

**Technical ambiguities adjudicated.**

- Whether clear grounds are needed to board at all - they are not; they are needed to go BEYOND certificates, documents and an overall impression
- Whether the nine grounds in 2.4.2 are exhaustive - they are expressly not, 'include but are not limited to'
- Whether an appeal suspends a detention - it does not, and 2.3.11 says so in terms
- Whether the Detention Review Panel is an IMO body - it is not; A.1185(33) chapter 5 is a reporting and evaluation mechanism, not an individual-detention review
- Whether a detained ship can move at all - it can, under 3.7.3, to the nearest appropriate repair yard on agreed conditions
- Whether the Chief Engineer or the master should refuse to sign the deficiency report - signing acknowledges receipt, not agreement, and refusing gains nothing
- Whether class or flag leads the challenge - the flag State is the ship's principal advocate; class supports on technical questions concerning its own certificates

**Target answer shape.** Six numbered sections under three printed limb headings. Limb (a) gets two short sections - the definition, then the grounds as a numbered list, because the marks there are in the enumeration. Limb (b) gets two - what is done in the first hours on board, then how the disagreement is put on the record formally. Limb (c) gets two - the review route with the IMO/MOU boundary drawn expressly, then a table of the three parties' roles, because three actors across one process is exactly what a table renders better than prose.

**Verification statement.**

P1 PRIMARY VERIFIED for the whole IMO layer against resolution A.1185(33), Procedures for Port State Control, 2023, adopted 6 December 2023, held in the True Source corpus and read directly. Provisions read in full: the definitions at 1.7.2 (clear grounds), 1.7.3 (deficiency), 1.7.4 (detention) and 1.7.5 (initial inspection); paragraph 1.2.4 on applying only accepted conventions in force and 1.2.5 to 1.2.6 on design arrangements, exemptions and alternative design and arrangements; section 2.4 in full, including the limiting rule at 2.4.1 and the nine enumerated clear grounds at 2.4.2; section 2.5.1 on more detailed inspections; the general procedural guidelines at 2.3.8 to 2.3.13, including the right of appeal at 2.3.11 and the express statement that appeal does not suspend the detention; chapter 3 in full - 3.3.2 and 3.3.3 on notification of the flag State and the recognized organization, 3.4 on remedial action, 3.5.1 and 3.5.2 on detention guidance and written notification, 3.6.1 to 3.6.4 on suspension of inspection, and 3.7.1 to 3.7.6 on rectification and release including the nearest-appropriate-repair-yard route at 3.7.3; and chapter 5, section 5.1.1 to 5.1.3, the Review Procedures. EVIDENCE GAP RECORDED AND MATERIAL: the printed stem asks about 'the Detention Review Panel'. That body is NOT created by A.1185(33) - chapter 5 of the Procedures is an Organization-level reporting and evaluation mechanism, not a panel that reviews an individual detention. The Detention Review Panel is a REGIONAL MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING mechanism, and neither the Indian Ocean MOU nor the Tokyo MOU instruments are held by the corpus, nor are the DG Shipping circulars (the corpus records DGS-circulars as a placeholder). Every statement about the Panel's own procedure is therefore carried at P2/P3 with the limitation stated in the answer itself, and the IMO/MOU boundary is drawn expressly rather than blurred.

**Sources consulted.**

- PRIMARY: A.1185(33) paragraph 1.7.2 - 'Clear grounds: Evidence that the ship, its equipment, or its crew do not correspond substantially with the requirements of the relevant conventions or that the master or crew members are not familiar with essential shipboard procedures relating to the safety of ships or the prevention of pollution. Examples of clear grounds are included in section 2.4.'
- PRIMARY: A.1185(33) paragraph 1.7.4 - detention is intervention action taken by the port State to ensure the ship will not sail until it can proceed to sea without presenting a danger to the ship or persons on board, or an unreasonable threat of harm to the marine environment, whether or not it affects the normal schedule of departure
- PRIMARY: A.1185(33) paragraph 2.4.1 - absent clear grounds, an inspection is LIMITED to verifying valid certificates and other relevant documentation and the PSCO forming an impression of the overall condition of the ship, its equipment and its crew
- PRIMARY: A.1185(33) paragraph 2.4.2 - the nine enumerated clear grounds, expressly 'include but are not limited to': absence of principal equipment or arrangements; evidence that a certificate is invalid; required certificates and documents absent, incomplete, not maintained or falsely maintained; serious hull or structural deterioration threatening structural, watertight or weathertight integrity; serious deficiencies in safety, pollution prevention or navigational equipment; master or crew not familiar with essential shipboard operations, or such operations not carried out; key crew members unable to communicate; emission of false distress alerts not properly cancelled; and receipt of a report or complaint that the ship appears substandard
- PRIMARY: A.1185(33) paragraph 2.5.1 - a more detailed inspection is carried out if the ship does not carry valid certificates, or where the PSCO has clear grounds for believing the condition does not correspond substantially with the particulars of the certificates, or that the master or crew are not familiar with essential shipboard procedures
- PRIMARY: A.1185(33) paragraph 1.2.6 - where an Exemption Certificate with the correct reference is held, or approved alternative design and arrangements documentation is carried, port State authorities should interpret this as meaning the ship complies
- PRIMARY: A.1185(33) paragraph 2.3.8 - involving the flag State, the RO and the owner can result in a safer ship, avoid subsequent arguments about the circumstances of the detention, and prove advantageous in litigation involving 'undue delay'
- PRIMARY: A.1185(33) paragraph 2.3.11 - 'The company or its representative have a right of appeal against a detention taken by the authority of a port State. The appeal should not cause the detention to be suspended. The PSCO should properly inform the master of the right of appeal.'
- PRIMARY: A.1185(33) paragraph 2.3.10 - detention reports to the flag State should be in sufficient detail for an assessment of the severity of the deficiencies giving rise to the detention
- PRIMARY: A.1185(33) paragraph 3.5.2 - on a detention the PSCO immediately notifies the flag Administration IN WRITING with the report of inspection, and the RO which issued the certificates is likewise notified where appropriate; both are also notified in writing of the release
- PRIMARY: A.1185(33) paragraph 3.6.1 to 3.6.4 - suspension of inspection where the overall condition is obviously substandard, following recording of detainable deficiencies and detention
- PRIMARY: A.1185(33) paragraph 3.7.1 - the PSCO should endeavour to secure the rectification of all deficiencies detected
- PRIMARY: A.1185(33) paragraph 3.7.3 - where the deficiencies causing detention cannot be remedied at the port of inspection, the authority may allow the ship to proceed to the nearest appropriate repair yard chosen by the master and agreed by the authority, subject to conditions agreed between the port State and the flag State, which may include flag State confirmation that remedial action has been taken
- PRIMARY: A.1185(33) chapter 5, paragraphs 5.1.1 to 5.1.3 - the Organization summarises deficiency reports and disseminates them; the appropriate committee periodically evaluates the summary to identify measures for more consistent and effective application of IMO instruments; recommendations are incorporated into the relevant instrument
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: the Detention Review Panel is a mechanism of the REGIONAL port State control memoranda of understanding, not of A.1185(33). India participates in the Indian Ocean MOU. The Panel considers a written application from the company or flag State that a detention was improperly imposed, reviews the inspection record and the parties' submissions, and reports its findings to the detaining authority and the MOU committee. The MOU instruments are not held by the corpus and the answer states that limitation.
- CONTEXT: SOLAS regulation I/19 and XI-1/4, and MARPOL articles 5 and 6, are the treaty powers under which control is exercised; UNCLOS article 226 constrains delay to a foreign vessel

## 6. OPEN ITEMS AND EVIDENCE GAPS

- THE CENTRAL EVIDENCE GAP OF THIS QUESTION. The Detention Review Panel is not created by A.1185(33) and the corpus holds no regional memorandum of understanding instrument and no DG Shipping circular (the corpus records DGS-circulars as a placeholder). The Panel's composition, its filing deadlines, its procedural rules and the form of its decision are therefore NOT asserted from any held source. The answer explains the Panel's function and the parties' roles, states expressly that the detailed procedure is set by the regional MOU rather than by IMO, and quotes no time limit or membership rule it cannot source.
- No numeric time limit is given for lodging a review application. Those limits are MOU-specific and differ between regimes, and quoting one wrongly is worse than stating that the applicable MOU fixes it.
- The Indian implementation - the DG Shipping circulars and the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958 rules governing PSC in Indian ports - is named as the vehicle but not quoted, because no DG Shipping instrument is held.
- Detention statistics, target factors and MOU blacklist or greylist positions are commercial and regional performance data, not regulatory facts, and are not held or quoted.
