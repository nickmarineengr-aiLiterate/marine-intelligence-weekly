## 2. DECOMPOSITION GATE - COMPLETED BEFORE RESEARCH

**Completed:** 2026-08-12, before research and drafting

**Question intent.** Two eight-mark limbs on one new SOLAS chapter. Limb (a) is definitional and jurisdictional - what the chapter is FOR, which ships and operations it catches, and what 'industrial personnel' means as a term of art. Limb (b) is operational and administrative - what the chapter actually requires by way of safety measures and certification, and what the flag Administration does. The examiner is testing whether the candidate has read a chapter that was nine months old at the sitting, or is improvising from the phrase 'offshore wind'.

**Mark allocation.** 16, printed as 8 + 8. The split is even and the demands are genuinely comparable, but limb (a) contains three separately markable objects - objectives, application, definition - while limb (b) contains two - safety measures and certification, with the flag role threaded through both. The answer therefore gives limb (a) three route steps and limb (b) two, rather than splitting the page down the middle by word count.

**Examiner expectation.** That the candidate can state the entry-into-force date, the 500 GT and 'more than 12' thresholds, the 1 July 2024 construction date, the definition of industrial personnel in something close to the chapter's own words, and the name of the certificate. Above all, that the candidate knows industrial personnel are NOT passengers and can say what follows from that. Naming resolution MSC.527(106) for the Code, and knowing that the chapter itself came in by a different resolution, is the mark of someone who has opened the book.

**Primary source plan.**

- Read SOLAS chapter XV in full from the held 2024 consolidated edition - regulations 1 to 5, the printed footnotes and the unified interpretation
- Take the definition of industrial personnel verbatim from regulation 1.1 and the definition of offshore industrial activities verbatim from regulation 1.3
- Take the application thresholds from regulation 3.1 and the three retrofit ladders from regulations 3.2, 3.3 and 3.4 separately - they are not the same rule
- Take the requirement structure from regulation 5.1 items .1, .2 and .3, which is the whole of the certification answer
- Take the certificate NAME from the unified interpretation, since regulation 5 does not print it
- Establish the two adopting resolutions separately - MSC.521(106) for the chapter and MSC.527(106) for the Code
- Confirm the aggregate counting rule at regulation 2.3 and the 12-passenger cap inside it
- Do NOT assert any IP Code content beyond what chapter XV itself references - the Code is not held
- Confirm no post-1-July-2024 amendment operates on chapter XV before citing the edition as current

**Freshness risk.** MEDIUM - the chapter is new but stable: in force nine months at the sitting, with no amendment to it adopted since. The perishable content is not the law but the TRANSITION - the regulation 3.2 and 3.3 survey windows are open at this sitting and will have closed for the whole fleet by later sittings, so an answer inherited from a 2026 paper would describe the ladder in the past tense and be wrong here.

**Jurisdiction risk.** LOW. The chapter is international and sets no Indian limb. India is a Party to SOLAS and the chapter applies through the flag Administration in the ordinary way. The Merchant Shipping Act, 1958 is the implementing vehicle in India at this sitting and the 2025 Act is not engaged; neither is load-bearing on this answer and neither is cited.

**Technical ambiguities adjudicated.**

- Whether industrial personnel are passengers - they are expressly NOT, by regulation 2.2, and the whole architecture of the chapter follows from that
- Whether the '12' in 'more than 12 industrial personnel' counts only industrial personnel - it does not; regulation 2.3 makes it the aggregate of industrial personnel, special personnel and passengers
- Whether a pre-2024 ship escapes the chapter - it does not; regulation 3.2 or 3.4 catches it, and which one depends on whether it was previously authorized
- Whether chapter XV displaces the ordinary cargo ship regime - it does not; regulation 4 preserves it and modifies it
- Whether the chapter applies to passenger ships - it does not; it applies to cargo ships and high-speed cargo craft
- Whether the transfer of personnel to the offshore facility is regulated by the chapter - the chapter's own scope is the ship; personnel transfer is dealt with in the IP Code, which is not held

**Target answer shape.** Five numbered sections under two printed limb headings. Limb (a): one section on what the chapter is and why it exists, one on application including the retrofit ladder, one on the definition and the not-passengers consequence. Limb (b): one section on the substantive safety requirements built on regulation 5.1's three items, one on certification, survey and the flag Administration's role ending on port State control. A small table for the application ladder, because four different trigger points in prose are lost.

## 6. OPEN ITEMS AND EVIDENCE GAPS

- The IP Code (resolution MSC.527(106)) is NOT held by the True Source corpus. Every statement in this answer about the Code is drawn from what SOLAS chapter XV itself says about it - the regulation numbers chapter XV cites, the deeming rules and the survey and certification hook. No IP Code chapter is summarised or quoted, and the detailed content of parts I to V of the Code is deliberately not asserted.
- The precise scope of the phrase 'special personnel' is fixed by the 2008 SPS Code (resolution MSC.266(84)), which the chapter cites by footnote. The SPS Code is not held, so the answer uses the term as chapter XV uses it and does not define it from any other source.
- No figure is given for the number of ships certified under chapter XV, or for offshore wind installation activity. Those are commercial statistics, not regulatory facts, and are not held.
