**Question intent.** Three limbs at 5 + 5 + 6, moving from what the institution IS, to what it COVERS, to how it WORKS when something goes wrong. The examiner is testing whether the candidate understands that a P&I club is a mutual rather than an insurer, that it covers LIABILITY rather than the member's own loss, and - in the largest limb - that claims handling is a procedure the ship participates in rather than something that happens ashore. **Jurisdiction risk.** MEDIUM. P&I is an international commercial institution but the question is set on an Indian syllabus, so the statutory frame must be Indian and must be on the right side of the statute boundary. The Marine Insurance Act, 1963 and the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958 govern at this sitting; the Merchant Shipping Act, 2025 is post-sitting and prohibited. The Admiralty Act 2017 supplies the arrest context and, at section 4(1)(s), the statute's own recognition of the mutual insurance call.

**Technical ambiguities adjudicated.**

- Whether a club is an insurance company - it is not; it is a mutual association owned by its members, with calls rather than premiums and no external shareholders
- Whether the club covers the ship - it does not; hull and machinery damage, and the member's own loss of hire, are not club risks
- Whether protection and indemnity are two policies - they are one entry, but the two words historically divide the cover and the question demands the division be made
- Whether cargo damage is protection or indemnity - INDEMNITY, and getting this the wrong way round is the commonest error
- Whether the club pays the claimant directly - in principle no, because of pay to be paid, though that is relaxed in practice and displaced by the direct-action compulsory-insurance regimes
- Whether a supplementary call is a penalty - it is not; it is the mutual mechanism operating as designed when a year's claims exceed the advance calls
- Whether the club or the member conducts the claim - the club takes conduct of substantial claims, and the member's duty is to notify, preserve evidence and not admit liability
- Whether a letter of undertaking is a payment - it is not; it is security substituting for the ship so that the ship can sail

**Target answer shape.** Seven numbered sections under three printed limb headings. Limb (a) gets two short sections - what the mutual is, then the funding tiers - and both must be tight, because five marks is not eight. Limb (b) gets two - protection, then indemnity - with a table splitting the two words, because that split IS the question. Limb (c) gets three - how the club handles a claim, the filing procedure as a numbered sequence, and what the ship and the Chief Engineer actually contribute - because it is the largest limb and the one the candidate will be examined on orally.

- INDUSTRY GUIDANCE: the club is a MUTUAL - the members are the owners of the club and there are no external shareholders, so an underwriting surplus is retained for the members and a deficit is met by them. It is not a company selling a product to a customer; it is a group of shipowners sharing each other's third-party liabilities.
- INDUSTRY GUIDANCE: funding operates in tiers - an ADVANCE CALL levied on entry for the policy year, based on the member's entered tonnage, ship type, trade, crew nationality and claims record; SUPPLEMENTARY CALLS if the year's claims exceed the advance calls and investment income; and a RELEASE CALL payable by a member leaving, which buys out the exposure to future supplementary calls on the open years.
- INDUSTRY GUIDANCE: above the individual club sit two further layers - the INTERNATIONAL GROUP POOLING AGREEMENT, by which the participating clubs share claims above each club's own retention, and the GROUP'S COLLECTIVE REINSURANCE placed above the pool, with a further layer of overspill cover. The effect is that a very large single claim is met by the whole group rather than by one club.
- INDUSTRY GUIDANCE: PROTECTION covers liabilities connected with the operation of the ship other than cargo - crew personal injury, illness, death and repatriation; injury to and death of other persons on board and third parties; the proportion of collision liability not covered by the hull policy; damage to fixed and floating objects such as piers, buoys and cables; wreck removal; pollution liability beyond the hull cover; stowaways, refugees and persons rescued at sea; quarantine expenses; and fines.
- INDUSTRY GUIDANCE: INDEMNITY covers the member's liability to CARGO - loss, damage, shortage and contamination arising from breach of the contract of carriage - together with cargo's proportion of general average unrecoverable from cargo interests, and liabilities arising under approved charterparty and bill of lading terms.
