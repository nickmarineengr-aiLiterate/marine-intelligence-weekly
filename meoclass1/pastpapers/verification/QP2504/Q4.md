**Question intent.** One unlimbed 16-mark question setting THREE distinct tasks, and the printed comma splice hides the boundary between the first two. The examiner wants: what the Convention is for; what a flag State must do under it; and what paper the ship must carry. The discriminating feature is that the third task is not really answered from OPRC at all - it is answered from MARPOL Annex I regulation 37, and a candidate who knows that has understood how the two instruments interlock.

**Mark allocation.** 16 printed as a single block with no sub-division. The flag State responsibilities and the documents are the substantial tasks. The answer therefore gives the purpose two short sections, the flag State duties one substantial section, and the documents one substantial section with a table, closing on the OPRC-MARPOL relationship which is where the real understanding is shown.

**Examiner expectation.** That the candidate can state the three words in the Convention's own title and explain that it is about PREPAREDNESS AND RESPONSE, not prevention and not liability; can distinguish what a State does as flag State from what it does as coastal State; and can name the SOPEP correctly, give its thresholds, and list its four mandatory contents. Knowing that the SOPEP obligation reaches the ship through MARPOL Annex I regulation 37 rather than directly through OPRC is the mark of someone who has read both.

- Take the four mandatory contents verbatim from 37.2.1 to 37.2.4 and note the words 'at least'
- Take the combination rule and the resulting plan title from 37.3
- Take the 5,000 dwt shore-based damage stability access requirement from 37.4
- Confirm from the corpus amendment record that no amendment in force at this sitting touches regulation 37

- Record explicitly that OPRC itself is NOT held, and confine the OPRC account to authoritative secondary level with no verbatim quotation

**Freshness risk.** LOW - the Convention is unamended and nearly thirty years old, and the regulation that carries the document duty is unamended at this sitting. The one live temporal edge is MEPC.384(81), which amends the Protocol I that regulation 37.2.1 cross-refers to and which is adopted but NOT in force at April 2025; an answer inherited from a 2026 sitting could import it wrongly.

**Jurisdiction risk.** MEDIUM. The question is set on an Indian syllabus and article 6 national systems are the point at which the answer becomes national. India's national system - the National Oil Spill Disaster Contingency Plan and the Coast Guard's role as the designated authority - is described in outline as the SHAPE an article 6 system takes, without citing an instrument, because no Indian contingency-plan document is held. The implementing statute at this sitting is the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958; the 2025 Act is post-sitting and prohibited.

**Technical ambiguities adjudicated.**

- Whether OPRC prevents pollution - it does not; it is a preparedness, response and co-operation instrument and prevention is MARPOL's job
- Whether OPRC deals with liability or compensation - it does not; that is the CLC/FUND and Bunkers regime
- Whether the SOPEP requirement comes from OPRC or MARPOL - OPRC obliges the Party to require it, MARPOL Annex I regulation 37 imposes it on the ship
- Whether the thresholds are the same for tankers and other ships - they are not: 150 GT for oil tankers, 400 GT for other ships
- Whether the four listed contents are exhaustive - they are not; regulation 37.2 says the plan shall consist AT LEAST of them
- Whether a combined plan is still a SOPEP - when combined with the Annex II plan its title becomes 'Shipboard marine pollution emergency plan'
- Whether the Oil Record Book is an OPRC document - it is not; it is MARPOL Annex I regulation 17 and should be labelled as such if mentioned
- Whether flag State duties and coastal State duties are the same under OPRC - they are not, and conflating them is the commonest structural error in this answer

**Target answer shape.** Six numbered sections, no limb headings, because the paper prints none. Two short sections on identity and objectives to honour 'Briefly'. One section on the Convention's architecture so that the flag State duties have somewhere to sit. One substantial section on flag State responsibilities, expressly separating them from coastal State duties. One substantial section on the documents, built around regulation 37 with a table of the four mandatory contents. A closing section on how OPRC and MARPOL interlock, which is the highest-value paragraph in the answer.

**Sources consulted.**

- PRIMARY: MARPOL Annex I regulation 37.1, read from the held official text of resolution MEPC.117(52) - 'Every oil tanker of 150 gross tonnage and above and every ship other than an oil tanker of 400 gross tonnage and above shall carry on board a shipboard oil pollution emergency plan approved by the Administration.'
- PRIMARY: MARPOL Annex I regulation 37.2 - the plan shall be prepared based on guidelines developed by the Organization and written in the working language of the master and officers, and shall consist AT LEAST of the four items following
- PRIMARY: MARPOL Annex I regulation 37.2.1 - the procedure to be followed by the master or other persons having charge of the ship to report an oil pollution incident, as required in article 8 and Protocol I of the Convention, based on the guidelines developed by the Organization
- PRIMARY: MARPOL Annex I regulation 37.2.2 - the list of authorities or persons to be contacted in the event of an oil pollution incident
- PRIMARY: MARPOL Annex I regulation 37.2.3 - a detailed description of the action to be taken immediately by persons on board to reduce or control the discharge of oil following the incident
- PRIMARY: MARPOL Annex I regulation 37.2.4 - the procedures and point of contact on the ship for co-ordinating shipboard action with national and local authorities in combating the pollution
- PRIMARY: MARPOL Annex I regulation 37.3 - where regulation 17 of Annex II also applies, the plan may be combined with the shipboard marine pollution emergency plan for noxious liquid substances, and in that case the title shall be 'Shipboard marine pollution emergency plan'
- PRIMARY: MARPOL Annex I regulation 37.4 - all oil tankers of 5,000 tons deadweight or more shall have prompt access to computerized, shore-based damage stability and residual structural strength calculation programs
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: the International Convention on Oil Pollution Preparedness, Response and Co-operation was adopted on 30 November 1990 and entered into force on 13 May 1995. It was developed in the aftermath of the Exxon Valdez grounding of 1989, which exposed the absence of any international framework for co-ordinated response.
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: OPRC article 3 requires each Party to require ships flying its flag to have on board an oil pollution emergency plan as required by and in accordance with provisions adopted by the Organization, and extends parallel requirements to offshore units, sea ports and oil handling facilities under the Party's jurisdiction
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: OPRC article 4 requires Parties to ensure that masters and persons in charge report without delay any event involving a discharge or probable discharge of oil, to the nearest coastal State
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: OPRC article 5 governs the action a Party takes on receiving an oil pollution report - assessment, and notification of States whose interests are affected together with the Organization
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: OPRC article 6 requires each Party to establish a national system for responding promptly and effectively, including a national contingency plan and a designated competent national authority and operational contact point, and to establish a minimum level of pre-positioned response equipment, a programme of exercises and training, and detailed plans and communication capabilities
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: OPRC articles 7, 8, 9 and 10 provide for co-operation in response on request, research and development, technical co-operation and assistance to developing countries, and the promotion of bilateral and regional agreements; article 12 places the institutional and information-clearing role with IMO
- AUTHORITATIVE SECONDARY: the OPRC-HNS Protocol, 2000 extends the same preparedness, response and co-operation architecture to hazardous and noxious substances
- CONTEXT: UNCLOS article 199 obliges States to develop and promote contingency plans for responding to pollution incidents in the marine environment; article 194 is the general obligation on which OPRC's response architecture rests
