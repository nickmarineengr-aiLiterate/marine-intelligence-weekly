## Findings worth recording

**1. The Bunkers Convention has no limitation figures at all.** Article 6 is the entire
limitation provision and it merely refers out. This is stated in the answer as the substantive
*weakness* of the regime, with its three consequences drawn out: the amount varies between
States; the pollution claim shares a **general** LLMC fund with wreck removal, cargo and injury
claims rather than a ring-fenced pollution fund; and there is **no second tier**. Verifying this
from the text rather than from summary mattered - the proposition is widely misstated.

**2. The channelling principle is reversed between the two conventions.** CLC article III.4
protects the bareboat charterer, manager and operator; Bunkers article 1.3 read with 3.2 makes
those very parties jointly and severally liable. The answer and the study guide both flag that
candidates who know CLC well are the ones most likely to carry the channelling rule across and
reverse the answer.

**3. Two limbs are genuinely identical and the answer says so.** The time limits (three years /
six years) and the three defences are the same in both conventions. Stating identity explicitly
is treated as a scoring point rather than a gap, because it demonstrates the comparison was
performed rather than assumed.
