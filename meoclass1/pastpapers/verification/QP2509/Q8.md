**Both directions were available and both are errors.** Omitting the 2025 set entirely is a
temporal error, because it had been adopted before the candidate sat the paper and this limb asks
about the *regimes* - that is, about the state of the regime. Applying it as an operative
requirement is the opposite error. The answer names the set, states its adoption date, and marks
it as not in force on every surface where it appears.

### 2.1 Why this object names the 2025 amendments where `QP2510-Q9` quarantines them

`QP2510-Q9` (October 2025) faced the identical instrument boundary one month later and excluded
the 2025 amendments from its model answer **completely and correctly**. This object names them.
The two decisions are not in conflict, and the reason must be recorded so that a later editor
does not "harmonise" them:

- `QP2510-Q9` asks for the MLC's **key provisions and requirements**. The 2025 set imposes no
  requirement at that sitting, so it has no place in the answer at all.
- `QP2509-Q8` asks for a **broad overview of the relevant IMO, ILO regimes**. That is a question
  about the state of the regime, and at September 2025 that state included a set of amendments
  adopted and awaiting force.

**The wording of the stem decides it.** Recorded in `temporal_review.notes` on the question
itself so the distinction survives.

## 6. Reasoning recorded

- **Limb (a) is not a second STCW question.** The stem says *IMO, ILO regimes* and then names
  *maritime labour and welfare*, which is a signpost to MLC 2006. An answer that never reaches the
  MLC has missed the centre of the limb. The route enforces the split by putting the IMO side at
  step 2 and the ILO side at steps 3 to 5.
- **The organising sentence is the analytical mark.** *IMO regulates the seafarer as the operator
  of a ship; ILO regulates the seafarer as a worker* - and they meet at **hours of rest**, which is
  simultaneously a condition of employment and a condition of safety. Stated at step 1 and carried
  into `understand_first`.
- **Fatigue is kept out of limb (a)** except as *fitness for duty*. This is the inherited reasoning
  from `QP2508-Q4`: if fatigue fills the first limb, the second limb has nothing new to say and
  half the marks evaporate.
- **Limb B) must name the circular.** "The IMO guidance" is one document, and naming it, dating it
  and giving its six modules is most of the limb.
- **The 2022 additions are integrated into their Titles, not appended.** Inherited from
  `QP2510-Q9` section 5 and for the same reason: a paragraph headed *"the 2022 amendments"* teaches
  a candidate to treat as optional what is simply the law.
