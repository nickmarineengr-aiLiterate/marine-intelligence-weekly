## Two findings worth recording

**1. A widely repeated "exclusion" that is not in the Convention.** Secondary summaries - including
the Convention's own public-facing overview page - state that HNS "does not apply to loss or
damage as covered by the Bunkers Convention". **That proposition appears nowhere in the treaty
text.** Article 4.3 contains exactly two exclusions, and a full-text search shows the word
**"bunker" does not occur anywhere in the Convention**. Bunker oil is outside HNS for a
different and more useful reason: **article 1.5(a) covers only substances carried *as cargo***,
and a ship's own fuel is not cargo. The answer states the real mechanism, because it also
disposes of residues, stores and ballast, which the false exclusion does not.

This is a direct vindication of `PASTPAPER_PRODUCTION_PROTOCOL.md` §2 - a summary may point you
at a source but may never *be* the source.

**2. HNS reaches fire and explosion damage from a persistent oil cargo.** Derived from the two
texts read together, and stated in the object as such. Article 4.3(a) excludes only "pollution
damage **as defined in** the CLC"; CLC article I.6 confines that to damage **by contamination**.
Non-contamination damage from an oil cargo is therefore not excluded, and persistent oil in bulk
is itself HNS under article 1.5(a)(i). This is the second half of the limb (c) argument and is
what makes the answer more than a description of three conventions.
