## 1. The demand, and why it is five questions

The printed stem carries **five** separate demands in sixteen marks:

1. What is a maritime lien?
2. How does this lien travel with the property?
3. Explain "in rem" and "in personam".
4. Which convention deals with maritime lien?
5. Give the order of settlement of various debtors' claims after legal action as per this
   convention.

Roughly three marks each. The fifth is the most concrete and the most preparable, and it is
written in the answer as a **numbered list** rather than narrated, because that is how it will be
marked.

## 2. India's party status - NOT established, and NOT asserted

`QP2509_TEMPORAL_AND_DONOR_ANCHOR.md` section 3.6 flagged this:

> *"Verify India's party status before asserting it applies to an Indian-flag scenario - this has
> not been established and must not be assumed from the fact that the question asks about it."*

The session prompt repeated the instruction.

**It remains unestablished.** The United Nations depositary document for Chapter XI.D.4 was
retrieved and read, but it is the **certified multilingual text of the Convention**, not the
participation list - a term probe of its 92,000 characters for "India" returns nothing, which
proves nothing either way because the document does not list participants at all.

**The resolution is that the question does not need it.** The stem asks *which convention deals
with maritime lien* and *the order of settlement as per this convention*. Both are answerable at
**convention level**, and that is how the answer is written.

The Indian dimension is supplied instead by **domestic law in its own right** - the Admiralty
(Jurisdiction and Settlement of Maritime Claims) Act, 2017 - which is what an Indian court would
actually apply, and which does not depend on treaty status at all. The two are set out **side by
side and expressly kept separate**, with a closing parenthetical in the model answer saying so.

Recorded in `unresolved` and as a `C_ACCEPTED_LIMITATION`, with an express instruction that a
reviewer should treat the later appearance of *either* "India is a party" or "India is not a
party" as a regression unless sourced to the UN participation record.

## 4. The order of settlement, derived from the text rather than from commentary

This is the fifth demand and it is assembled from **article 12 read with article 5**, not taken
from a textbook list:

| # | Claim | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Costs and expenses of arrest or seizure and of the sale - **expressly including upkeep of vessel and crew, and article 4(1)(a) wages and sums incurred from the time of arrest** | art. 12(2) |
| 2 | *Optionally*, wreck-removal costs where a stranded or sunken vessel was removed by a public authority for **safe navigation or protection of the marine environment**, and the State has so provided - **before all other claims secured by a maritime lien** | art. 12(3) |
| 3 | The **article 4 maritime liens**, in the article 5 order, subject to both salvage rules | arts. 4, 5 |
| 4 | **Shipbuilder's or shiprepairer's right of retention** - he must surrender possession but is satisfied out of proceeds **after** the article 4 lienholders | arts. 7, 12(4) |
| 5 | **Registered mortgages, "hypotheques" and charges** complying with article 1 | art. 5(1) |
| 6 | **Other national maritime liens**, which rank after article 4 liens *and* after registered mortgages | art. 6(c) |
| 7 | **Residue to the owner**, freely transferable | art. 12(2), 12(6) |

Two points are frequently omitted from published lists and are stated expressly here: that
**item 1 outranks the maritime liens themselves**, and that **item 2 is permissive rather than
automatic**.

## 5. The Indian statute - two real divergences

Read in the Gazette text rather than summarised. Section 9(1) reproduces the Convention's five
liens **in the same order**, but:

| Provision | Divergence from the Convention |
|---|---|
| s.9(1)(c) | salvage **"including special compensation relating thereto"** - the Convention says only "reward for the salvage of the vessel" |
| s.9(1)(d) | adds **"and any other statutory dues related to the vessel"** |
| s.9(1)(e) | "loss or damage" rather than the Convention's "physical loss or damage", and the exclusion names cargo and containers but not passengers' effects |
| **s.9(2) proviso** | **TWO years for wage claims** under limb (a), running from the date the wage, repatriation cost or social insurance contribution fell due - against the Convention's **one year** for all liens. **More generous to seafarers than the Convention.** |
| s.10 | a **three-tier** priority - liens, then registered mortgages and charges, then all other claims - with equal ranking within a category and **salvage in inverse order of time** |

The **two-year wage proviso** is the strongest single Indian point in the answer and is given its
own retrieval card (**C9**).

**The commencement date is not stated.** The Act provides for commencement on a date appointed by
the Central Government and the appointing notification was not read; only the **assent date of
9 August 2017**, which appears in the Gazette text itself, is given.

## 7. Reasoning recorded

- **The lien and the action in rem are one mechanism, not two topics.** The answer states the link
  expressly - *the maritime lien is what gives the claimant a right in rem against the ship* -
  which joins the middle three demands instead of leaving them as unrelated definitions.
- **The "secret lien" point is given commercial consequence**, not just a label: there is no
  register of maritime liens, so a buyer with a clean registry search can still take a ship
  encumbered by claims ranking ahead of the mortgage that financed the purchase. That is also why a
  forced sale is commercially valuable and why arrest has teeth.
- **What is NOT a lien is included deliberately.** Cargo claims are excluded in terms by article
  4(1)(e), and necessaries men - bunkers, repairs, agency - have maritime **claims** but no lien,
  so they rank behind mortgages. The maritime claim / maritime lien distinction is what the whole
  priority list is about.
- **Both salvage rules are stated.** Salvage jumps ahead of earlier liens, *and* salvage ranks in
  inverse order of time. Candidates commonly give one and not the other, or apply inverse order to
  every category.
