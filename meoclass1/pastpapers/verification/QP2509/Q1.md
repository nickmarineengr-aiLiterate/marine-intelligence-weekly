## 1. The question is a quotation

The printed stem reads:

> *"...the specific care and action be taken, **taking into account the provisions of relevant IMO
> instruments**, when **handling and carrying bauxite in bulk**."*

**CCC.1/Circ.2/Rev.1**, paragraph 7, reads:

> *"...requesting that extreme care and appropriate action be taken, **taking into account the
> provisions of relevant IMO instruments when handling and carrying Bauxite in bulk**."*

The same sentence closes the original **CCC.1/Circ.2** at its paragraph 5. **The examiner has
taken the question from the circular.**

This is not trivia. It fixes what the answer is expected to contain - the casualty, the
mechanism, the instruments and the practical care - and it makes the two circulars the natural
spine of the answer rather than an optional citation. It is recorded in the study guide so the
candidate understands why the question is phrased as it is.

## 2. The anchor's TO VERIFY item is DISCHARGED, and better than expected

`QP2509_TEMPORAL_AND_DONOR_ANCHOR.md` section 3.1 left this open:

> *"TO VERIFY at authoring, in the Code text itself: the exact schedule structure for bauxite...
> the moisture and particle-size criteria that separate them... **Do not** state the group
> classification from secondary summaries - the search material consulted this session was
> inconsistent about it."*

The warning was justified: two authoritative P&I and class sources (Gard, IRClass) confirmed the
schedules but **declined to publish the numeric criteria**, and the only numbers on offer came
from search-engine summaries, which is not a source.

**The criteria were then found in a primary IMO document.** `CCC.1/Circ.2/Rev.1` **Annex 1**
carries the *Test Procedure for Determining the TML for Bauxite* as finalized by CCC 4, and
section 1.6.1 states it numerically:

> *".1 more than 30% of fine particles less than 1 mm (D30 < 1 mm); and
> .2 more than 40% of particles less than 2.5 mm (D40 < 2.5 mm)."*

and the TML determination:

> *"...the critical moisture content at 80% degree of saturation... where the Optimum Moisture
> Content (OMC) of the Bauxite tested occur at saturation levels greater than or equal to 90%"*,
> and at **70%** where the OMC occurs below 90%.

**So the numbers are stated in the answer with primary provenance**, not omitted. The anchor's
instruction was to avoid *secondary summaries*, not to avoid the figures; a primary source was
found and the figures are attributed to it.

**One precision retained.** The criteria are quoted from the **test procedure as finalized by CCC 4
and annexed to Rev.1**, not from the adopted schedule text in amendment 05-19, which was not read.
The answer attributes them accordingly and does not assert the wording of the adopted schedule.

## 4. Temporal findings

### 4.1 The operative edition

**Amendment 07-23, mandatory from 1 January 2025** - eight months before the sitting. Verified in
the corpus at `QP2607-Q1` and consistent across sources.

### 4.2 Amendment 08-25 is adopted but had NO application

Adopted at **MSC 110, June 2025** - three months *before* this sitting - but it applies
**voluntarily only from 1 January 2026** and becomes **mandatory 1 January 2027**. At this sitting
it had no application **at all, not even voluntary**. Nothing from it is relied on. This is the
third adopted-but-not-operative instrument on this paper, after the MLC 2025 amendments (Q8) and
the MEPC 83 package (Q2).

### 4.3 The 10% indicative limit is SUPERSEDED - a trap in wide circulation

The **indicative moisture limit of 10%** is still widely quoted, including in current industry
writing. It comes from **CCC.1/Circ.2 of 2015**, an interim measure issued while the research was
running and **before any Group A bauxite schedule existed**.

**Rev.1 supersedes it in terms** (paragraph 8) and records that the original's advice "has been
superseded by the outcome of the research undertaken" (paragraph 3). **Rev.1 carries no indicative
moisture limit at all.** Since 1 January 2021 the position is governed by the schedules and by a
**cargo-specific TML determined by test**.

The answer mentions the 10% figure **only** as part of the historical sequence and marks it
expressly as superseded. It has its own retrieval card (**C5**) and a study-guide section. A
reviewer should treat any restatement of it as an operative limit as a regression.

### 4.4 The interim circulars are history, not operative requirement

Both circulars were awareness and interim-classification measures. Once amendment 05-19 became
mandatory the **schedules govern**. The answer states the sequence and is careful not to present
Rev.1 as the operative carriage requirement at this sitting - a distinction that is easy to lose
because Rev.1 is still a live document.

### 4.5 Intake flag corrected

Intake recorded `STABLE / LOW`. Corrected to `TEMPORAL REVIEW COMPLETE / MEDIUM` with classes
`instrument_amendment_boundary` and `superseded_guidance`.

### 4.6 No Indian statutory limb

Entirely IMSBC Code and SOLAS. The Merchant Shipping Act boundary of 15 March 2026 is not engaged.

## 5. Reasoning recorded

- **The organising sentence is that bauxite was reclassified because of a casualty.** The causes
  the stem asks for are the reasons the old classification was wrong; the care it asks for is what
  the new classification requires. Stated in `understand_first`.
- **Group C is dangerous here for what it switches OFF.** A Group C cargo has no TML, so there is
  no moisture certificate, no moisture limit, and **no lawful basis for a master to refuse wet
  cargo**. The *Bulk Jupiter* cargo was carried in accordance with its schedule; the schedule was
  the defect. This is the point that shows compliance and safety are not the same thing.
- **Dynamic separation must be named and explained mechanistically**, and distinguished from
  liquefaction. It is the discriminating technical content and the reason the question exists.
- **The wobble is the one warning sign a watchkeeper can actually detect**, and IMO records it
  expressly, so it earns a place in both the answer and a retrieval card.
- **The care limb is answered in voyage order** - before loading, during loading, on passage -
  because that is how a management-level engineer would use it, and because an unordered list of
  precautions is much harder to mark.

## 6. Deliberate omissions

- **No TML value for bauxite**, and no stowage factor or angle of repose. TML is a property of the
  **consignment**, determined by test for each shipment; any general figure would be wrong by
  construction and would suggest a misunderstanding of what TML is.
- **No claim about what the adopted 05-19 schedule text says**, as distinct from the test
  procedure annexed to Rev.1. See section 2.
- **No reliance on amendment 08-25.**
