## 1. What the question demands

> Explain the concept of CII rating and critically discuss the role of measuring metrics of AER and EEOI. What are the measures required to be taken by the vessel to improve its CII ratings. (16)

**Intent.** Three tasks in one unlimbed 16-mark question. First, explain the CII rating concept - which means the mechanism, not a definition. Second, critically discuss AER and EEOI, which is the highest-value task and requires an argument, not two definitions. Third, state the measures a vessel takes to improve its rating. The examiner is testing whether the candidate understands that CII is a comparison against a moving target and that the choice of metric is contestable.

**Mark allocation.** 16 marks printed as a single block. Three tasks. The critical discussion of AER and EEOI is the discriminating task and should carry the largest share of writing; the improvement measures are the easiest marks and must not be allowed to consume the answer, since a long undifferentiated list scores no better than a structured short one.

**Examiner expectation.** That the candidate can write the required-CII formula, name the reduction factor for the year, state where rating C sits, know what a D or E rating actually triggers, and explain WHY the regulation uses a capacity proxy rather than actual cargo. Naming the guidelines G1 to G5 and what each supplies is the mark of someone who has opened the instruments.

The answer is decomposed into these required components:

- Where CII sits in law - regulation 28 of MARPOL Annex VI, and to which ships it applies
- The mechanism: attained CII, required CII, the reduction factor Z and the reference line
- The A to E rating and the consequences that attach to D and E
- What AER actually measures, and why the regulation uses it
- What EEOI measures, its status in the guidelines, and the critical comparison with AER
- Operational measures to improve the rating
- Technical and fuel measures, and the legitimate calculation levers under G5

## 3. The finding that mattered

The intake flagged `IMO INSTRUMENT IN FLUX` at MEDIUM. The flag is **real**, and the value
of the check was that it resolved to a precise position rather than to a general warning.

Three facts fix this question to its sitting, and all three were taken from primary text held
in the corpus:

1. **No ship had yet been assigned a CII rating.** Regulation 28.1 requires the attained CII to
   be calculated *after the end of calendar year 2023*, and regulation 28.2 gives three months
   to report it. At January 2024, 2023 is the first data year and the first reports are not due
   until 31 March 2024.
2. **The reduction factor for the current year is 7 %**, and the factors are set only to 2026
   -- `MEPC.338(76)` table 1 gives 5, 7, 9 and 11 per cent for 2023 to 2026 and expressly leaves
   2027 to 2030 open.
3. **The regulation 28.11 review had not reported.** It was due to complete by 1 January 2026.

Any answer inherited from a 2025 or 2026 sitting would have got all three wrong, which is why
no donor was sought even though the topic is well covered in later years.

The second finding is the one that answers "critically discuss". `MEPC.352(78)` section 5 lists
**EEOI among the metrics encouraged for trial purposes** -- it is not the regulatory metric.
Reading that alongside sections 2.4 and 2.5, which define AER as the *supply-based* CII using
DWT as a **proxy** for cargo, gives the whole argument: **IMO chose an enforceable metric over a
valid one**, and regulation 28.11 places the reference values and Z factors inside its own
review, which is the Organization conceding the choice is provisional.

Four post-sitting instruments held in the corpus -- `MEPC.384(81)`, `MEPC.385(81)`,
`MEPC.392(82)` and `MEPC.395(82)` -- were identified and **deliberately excluded**.

**Technical ambiguities identified before drafting, and resolved in the answer:**

- Whether AER measures transport work or merely distance - it measures distance, with capacity as a proxy
- Whether EEOI is mandatory - it is not; G1 lists it for trial purposes
- Whether a single D rating has consequences - it does not; three consecutive years, or an E, is the trigger
- Whether CII carries a penal sanction - it does not; the pressure is commercial and administrative
- Whether time at anchor helps or harms AER - it harms it, and this is the clearest test of understanding
- Whether calculation-side measures such as G5 adjustments are legitimate - they are, within G5's own stated boundary
- Whether the Z factors beyond 2026 were known at this sitting - they were not

**Target answer shape.** Mechanism first in three steps - where it sits in law, how the number is built, what the letter means and does - because the critical discussion is unintelligible without it. Then the two metrics in two steps, AER described and defended, EEOI described and the trade-off argued explicitly. Then the improvement measures in two steps, operational before technical, closing on the two levers candidates never mention: data quality and the G5 adjustments, and the SEEMP as the place all of it is administered.
