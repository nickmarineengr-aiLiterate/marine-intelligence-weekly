## 1. What the question demands

> Explain the latest developments in Marine “Tribology”. Discuss the inferences you may draw from a main engine lub oil analysis report with the proposed corrective action in each case. (16)

**Intent.** Two tasks in one unlimbed 16-mark question. First, explain recent developments in marine tribology - an open task that rewards structure and punishes waffle. Second, state the inferences drawable from a main engine lubricating oil analysis report WITH the corrective action in each case - a closed task where the marks are countable. The examiner is testing whether a candidate can connect the science to the report on the desk.

**Mark allocation.** 16 marks in a single block, two tasks, roughly eight each. The second task's printed words "in each case" are decisive: every inference must be paired with an action, which makes a table the correct instrument and prose the wrong one. The first task must be organised or it becomes an essay on friction worth very little.

**Examiner expectation.** That the candidate connects the 2020 sulphur cap to cylinder oil alkalinity, knows that the base number problem runs in both directions, can name real surface and delivery technology rather than gesture at progress, and can read a used-oil report as a trend with a specific corrective action for each finding. Reading aluminium with silicon as catalytic fines is the discriminating detail.

The answer is decomposed into these required components:

- What tribology is, and the specific marine contacts it governs
- The fuel-driven developments since the 2020 sulphur cap, and what they did to cylinder oil selection
- Cylinder lubrication control - timed electronic dosing and feed-rate optimisation
- Surface engineering and bearing materials
- Environmentally acceptable lubricants and water-lubricated stern tubes
- Condition monitoring as applied tribology, including scrape-down analysis
- Reading the physical and chemical tests on an analysis report, with the corrective action for each
- Reading the wear metals and contaminants, with the corrective action for each

This is the paper's clearest `PASTPAPER_PRODUCTION_PROTOCOL` **section 2.1** question: an

The one hard regulatory claim -- the **0.50 % m/m global sulphur limit in force since 1 January

verified against the corpus copy of the revised Annex VI. Everything else is machinery
engineering, and the provenance class is **declared rather than disguised**.

The finding that shapes the answer is that **the largest recent development in marine tribology
is a fuel regulation, not a lubricant**. The sulphur cap changed what is burned, which changed
what the cylinder oil must neutralise, which reopened base number selection as a live decision
on every ship -- and it did so *in both directions at once*. Too little alkalinity gives cold
corrosion; too much is not consumed and deposits, giving bore polish and scuffing. Since 2020
both failure modes exist in the same fleet, because a ship on compliant fuel and a
scrubber-fitted ship on high-sulphur fuel need opposite oils. Most candidates know only the
cold-corrosion half.

  technology.** Those figures are vendor-sourced.

**Technical ambiguities identified before drafting, and resolved in the answer:**

- Whether excess cylinder oil alkalinity is harmless - it is not; it deposits and polishes the bore
- Whether a rising base number in the system oil is good - it usually indicates cylinder oil over-carry
- Whether aluminium indicates wear or catalytic fines - it depends entirely on whether silicon rises with it
- Whether copper indicates bearings or cooler tubes - the water result decides
- Whether spectrographic analysis detects all wear - it under-reports large particles
- Whether nanoparticle additives are established marine practice - they are not, at this sitting
- Whether an EAL is simply a drop-in replacement for mineral oil - it is not, on seals and water tolerance
