## 1. What the question demands

> a) What are the essential elements of preventive maintenance on-board ships. (6) b) Analyse the link between statutory and classification survey of ship machinery and equipment with respect to routine maintenance and how it is effectively merged in ship’s safety management system under the ISM code. (10)

**Intent.** Limb (a) is a straightforward enumeration of what makes a shipboard preventive maintenance system work. Limb (b), worth ten marks and using the verb ANALYSE, sets three connected tasks: distinguish statutory from classification survey, connect both to routine maintenance, and explain how the ISM Code absorbs the whole into the safety management system. The examiner is testing whether the candidate sees these as one system rather than three topics.

**Mark allocation.** 16 printed as 6 + 10. Limb (a) is six marks for seven elements - short, structured, done quickly. Limb (b) carries almost twice the weight and must not be starved. Within limb (b) the two survey definitions are the cheap half; the linking mechanism and ISM element 10 are where the analysis marks sit.

**Examiner expectation.** That the candidate can state ISM element 10 rather than gesture at it, can distinguish the two survey regimes by AUTHORITY rather than by activity, and can name the class-approved planned maintenance scheme as the concrete mechanism joining routine maintenance to survey. An answer that defines both surveys and then writes generally about the ISM Code has not analysed anything.

The answer is decomposed into these required components:

- (a) What preventive maintenance is, and the strategies it sits between
- (a) The essential elements, as a system rather than a list
- (b) What a statutory survey is and who requires it
- (b) What a classification survey is, and how the two differ in purpose and legal character
- (b) The mechanism that links routine maintenance to survey - the class-approved planned maintenance scheme
- (b) How ISM Code element 10 makes maintenance a managed and auditable process
- (b) How the whole chain closes - maintenance, survey, audit, certificate

## 3. The finding that mattered

Two things were established here, one expected and one not.

The expected one: **ISM Code element 10 was read verbatim** rather than paraphrased, and the
four paragraphs turn out to map exactly onto the question. `10.1` pulls both class rules and
statutory requirements inside the SMS by requiring conformity with "the relevant rules and
regulations"; `10.2` supplies the audit trail; `10.3` is the statutory root of the criticality
ranking that limb (a) asks about; and **`10.4` is the Code's own word for what the question
calls merging** -- the inspections and measures "should be integrated in the ship's operational
maintenance routine". Quoting 10.4 answers the printed phrase "effectively merged" directly.

The unexpected one: the companion instrument had moved, and it moved **before** this sitting in
the useful direction. **`A.1186(33)`, the HSSC Survey Guidelines 2023, was adopted 6 December
2023** -- one month before the examination -- and revoked `A.1156(32)`. Better still, its annex
header carries its own currency statement: the Guidelines *"take into account relevant IMO
mandatory instruments and amendments thereto entering into force before or on 31 December
2023"*. That is very nearly an exact fit to this sitting and it is quoted in the answer.

The seven survey definitions were then taken verbatim from `A.1186(33)` sections 2.1 to 2.7, and
one of them earns its place in the analysis: the **annual survey** is defined as a general
inspection to ensure the items *"have been maintained"*. The convention text itself points the
surveyor at the maintenance record, which is the link the question is asking about.

**One boundary is recorded.** The class-approved planned maintenance scheme is described by what
it does and declared as class practice. No rule number is attributed, because class rules are
society-specific and citing one society's numbering as though it were general would be exactly
the false precision the production protocol forbids.

**Technical ambiguities identified before drafting, and resolved in the answer:**

- Whether preventive maintenance means only time-based work - it does not; condition-based work is preventive
- Whether class survey and statutory survey differ in activity or in authority - in authority
- Whether delegation to an RO transfers responsibility - it does not
- Whether a class-approved PMS removes the surveyor - it changes the method of verification, not the requirement
- Whether ISM element 10 requires a separate inspection regime alongside the PMS - 10.4 requires the opposite
- Which class rule number governs approved maintenance schemes - society-specific, deliberately not cited
