## 1. What the question demands

> Explain the principle of "Uberrimae Fidei" in Marine Insurance. What are the different disclosures which must be made by the insured to the insurer in Marine Insurance? List the circumstances which need not be disclosed to the insurer. What happens to the Marine Insurance contract if the disclosures are not made to the insurer at the time of the conclusion of the contract? (16)

**Intent.** Four tasks inside one unlimbed 16-mark question: explain the principle of utmost good faith; state what the assured must disclose; list what need not be disclosed; and state the legal effect of failure to disclose at the conclusion of the contract. The examiner is testing whether the candidate has read a statute, not whether he can discuss good faith in the abstract.

**Mark allocation.** 16 marks printed as a single block with no limb marks. Four tasks, so approximately four marks each. The list limb is a closed statutory enumeration and is the fastest four marks on the paper; the principle limb is the one that invites over-writing.

**Examiner expectation.** Statutory treatment under the Indian Act with section numbers. The tell-tales are the words "prudent insurer", the phrase "in the absence of enquiry" in front of the four exceptions, and the word "voidable" rather than "void". A general essay on good faith with no sections scores poorly however well written.

The answer is decomposed into these required components:

- Which statute governs marine insurance at an Indian examination
- The principle of utmost good faith and what makes it different from an ordinary contract
- What the assured must disclose, and the test that decides whether a fact is disclosable
- The closed statutory list of circumstances which need NOT be disclosed
- The legal consequence of non-disclosure at the conclusion of the contract, and its limits
