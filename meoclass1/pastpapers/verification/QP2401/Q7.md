## 1. What the question demands

> (a)Briefly discuss the concept of vetting inspections and their significance in maritime oil trade. How does vetting inspections contribute to continuous improvement and safety culture within the oil tanker sector? (8) (b) Discuss emerging trends or advancements in vetting procedures, including the integration of technology and data analytics. (8)

**Intent.** Five tasks across two limbs. Limb (a): what vetting is, why it matters commercially in the oil trade, and how it drives continuous improvement and safety culture. Limb (b): emerging trends and advancements, specifically technology and data analytics. The examiner is testing whether the candidate understands that vetting is commercial rather than regulatory, and whether the candidate knows where the inspection regime had actually got to at this date.

**Mark allocation.** 16 printed as 8 + 8. Limb (a) holds three tasks and limb (b) two, so five tasks for sixteen marks and nothing gets a long paragraph. In limb (a) the safety-culture task is the discriminating one because it invites judgement; in limb (b) the marks are in naming the specific SIRE 2.0 architecture rather than writing generally about digitalisation.

**Examiner expectation.** That the candidate knows vetting is not statutory, knows SIRE publishes reports rather than approving ships, can name TMSA as the shore-side half of the system, and can describe SIRE 2.0's actual architecture - the compiled questionnaire, the four question types, the three observation tools. Above all, that the candidate places SIRE 2.0 correctly in time as emerging rather than operative.

The answer is decomposed into these required components:

- (a) What vetting is, and how it differs from statutory and class inspection
- (a) How the SIRE programme actually works, and who makes the decision
- (a) The instruments - VIQ, HVPQ and TMSA
- (a) Why it is commercially decisive in the oil trade
- (a) How it drives continuous improvement and safety culture - and the honest criticism of it
- (b) SIRE 2.0 - what stage it had reached at this sitting
- (b) What SIRE 2.0 actually changes in the inspection itself
- (b) Data analytics, remote inspection and the limits of both

## 3. The finding that mattered

**This is the question on the paper where a later donor would have been most damaging, and
the currency position was established before a word was written.**

At **January 2024**:

- **VIQ7 was still the operative SIRE inspection tool.** SIRE 2.0 had not replaced it.
- SIRE 2.0 was in a **four-phase roll-out**, and phase 3 -- the unlimited beta of the full
  end-to-end process, open to all programme users as a transition period -- was anticipated to
  commence **towards the end of Q4 2023**. So the transition phase had opened by the sitting.
- **SIRE 2.0 launched, and VIQ7 was withdrawn, on 2 September 2024 -- eight months after this
  examination.**

That last date is used in this record **only to fix the boundary**. It is not presented to the
candidate as the position at the sitting, and no part of the answer describes SIRE 2.0 as
operative. SIRE 2.0 is answered as the **emerging** regime, which is precisely the word limb (b)
uses -- so the correct answer and the accurate answer coincide.

Any material written for a 2025 or 2026 sitting states the opposite. Had a donor existed, using
it would have inverted the answer. **No donor was sought for this question for that reason**, and
that decision is itself the finding.

The second finding is structural rather than temporal: **the two limbs of this question answer
each other.** Limb (a)'s honest criticism -- that a predictable inspection regime breeds
preparation rather than safety -- is exactly what SIRE 2.0's **rotational** and **campaign**
questions and its **human factors** observation tool were designed to attack. Ending limb (a) on
that criticism and opening limb (b) by answering it turns two lists into one argument.

One conceptual point was checked and is worth recording because it is so often got wrong:
**SIRE is an information programme, not an approval scheme.** OCIMF publishes reports; the
vetting decision belongs to the individual recipient company against its own criteria.
