## 1. What the question demands

> a) Discuss the key provisions and objectives of the Convention on Facilitation of International Maritime Traffic (FAL Convention). (6) b) Briefly discuss the impact of the FAL Convention on enhancing efficiency and Security in international maritime traffic. (4) c) Discuss the significance of the FAL Convention in promoting global cooperation and standardization in maritime trade. (6)

**Intent.** Three limbs on one instrument. Limb (a) asks for the Convention itself - objectives and key provisions - for six marks. Limb (b) asks for its effect on two things that are usually presented as opposed, efficiency and security, for four. Limb (c) asks why it matters to global cooperation and standardization, for six. The three limbs map onto the Convention's own three stated objectives, which is the structural insight the question is built on.

**Mark allocation.** 16 printed as 6 + 4 + 6. Limb (a) hides four tasks and must be split or the six marks collapse into one paragraph. Limb (b) is four marks and splits two and two, efficiency and security. Limb (c) is six and must go beyond restating limb (a) - the mechanism of standardization is what earns it.

**Examiner expectation.** That the candidate knows this is a real treaty with a real structure and not simply a good idea about paperwork. The tell-tales are the Standards versus Recommended Practices mechanism with its notification duty, the seven Annex sections, the FAL Forms, the maximum-information rule, and above all the correct currency of the single window at this sitting. A discussion-only answer with no dates and no structure scores poorly.

The answer is decomposed into these required components:

- What the Convention is, when it was adopted and when it entered into force
- (a) Its stated objective, in the Convention's own terms
- (a) Its structure - Articles and Annex - and the Standards / Recommended Practices mechanism with the notification duty
- (a) The subject sections of the Annex and the FAL Forms as the concrete provisions
- (b) How it produces efficiency, and how the same machinery serves security rather than fighting it
- (c) Why a facilitation treaty produces global cooperation and standardization - and the single window as the current instance
- The amendment position at the January 2024 sitting: what is in force and what is only adopted

## 3. The finding that mattered

**The intake was wrong about this question and the error was in the safe direction only by
luck.** The intake recorded `STABLE / LOW` with no temporal flag. In fact this is the most
date-sensitive question on the paper.

Resolution **`FAL.14(46)`**, adopted 13 May 2022, **entered into force on 1 January 2024** --
days before this examination. The mandatory maritime single window was therefore already law at
the sitting, and a candidate sitting this paper was sitting in the first month of the new
regime. An answer written even six months earlier would have said "will become mandatory" and
been wrong.

The mirror-image trap was checked at the same time. **`FAL.15(47)`** was adopted **17 March
2023** but did **not** enter into force until **1 January 2025**. At this sitting it is
**adopted but not in force**, and the answer says so explicitly rather than eliding it. Having
both of those correct in one answer is the strongest available demonstration of the
adopted-versus-in-force distinction that this syllabus tests repeatedly.

The honest limitation is recorded rather than hidden: the consolidated Convention text is
**not held in the local True Source corpus**, so the Annex structure, the Standards and
Recommended Practices mechanism and the FAL Form titles come from the IMO's own published
account of the instrument and not from the instrument. No Standard number is quoted that this
source does not support.
