## 2. What "issued to the ship" was taken to mean, and why

The phrase is not a term of art. It was construed as the **owner's bill** — issued by the master,
or by an agent signing *"for the Master"*, so that the **shipowner is the contracting carrier** —
as against the **charterer's bill**, under which the charterer is the carrier and the shipowner is
not a party to the contract of carriage.

Three things support that construction, and the Model Answer **states the reading explicitly**
rather than assuming it:

1. **Limb (c) names shipowner and carrier as separate parties.** On any other reading they are the
   same person and the distinction is empty.
2. **Article I(a)** of the Rules provides that *"Carrier" includes the owner **or the charterer**
   who enters into a contract of carriage with a shipper* — the Rules themselves contemplate the
   split.
3. **Article I(b)** brings a bill issued *under or pursuant to a charterparty* within the Rules
   *from the moment at which it regulates the relations between a carrier and a holder* — the
   drafters legislated for exactly this document.

The Study Guide also tells the candidate what to do if unsure in the hall: **state the reading in
one line and answer it.** A clearly stated reasonable construction cannot be penalised; silently
answering a different question can.

## 6. Limitations accepted

1. **The repealed Indian Acts are not retrievable.** India Code publishes **current law** and
   removed both the 1856 and 1925 Acts on repeal; a targeted search for *"Indian Bills of Lading"*
   returns nothing but two Pondicherry extension Acts. The 1856 Act's **identity and citation are
   established from primary text** (BoLA 2025 s.6(1)) and the **subject matter of its three
   sections from the 2025 re-enactment headings**. What the answer states is the **long-settled
   effect** of those sections, which follow the English Bills of Lading Act 1855. **Reliance is on
   section numbers and effect, not on quoted wording.** `C_ACCEPTED_LIMITATION`.
2. **The Indian Schedule to the 1925 Act was not read directly**, for the same reason. The
   Hague-Visby Rules **were** read in an official scheduled text, and it is the Rules — a single
   international instrument — that carry every substantive claim in limb (c). The 1925 Act appears
   only as the vehicle. `C_ACCEPTED_LIMITATION`.
3. **Unit of account.** The IMF Special Drawing Right is named; the conversion machinery in the
   Indian enacting provision was not read and **no rupee figure appears anywhere**.
4. **Enactment-date discrepancy** on the Bills of Lading Act 2025 — noted at §4, immaterial, not
   resolved.
