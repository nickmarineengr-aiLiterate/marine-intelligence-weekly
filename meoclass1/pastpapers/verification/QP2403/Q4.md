## 2. Provenance posture — stated, not disguised

**`ENGINEERING_JUDGEMENT` is the sole substantive provenance class on this question, by design.**

**No instrument prescribes a propeller type.** There is nothing to cite, and the answer does not
pretend otherwise. The content is settled naval architecture, explained **mechanistically** so that
merits and demerits are *derived* rather than recited:

- propulsive efficiency as the product of **hull efficiency × open-water efficiency × relative
  rotative efficiency**;
- the four open-water loss mechanisms — **axial (slipstream), rotational (swirl), frictional,
  tip vortex**;
- **cavitation** as the ceiling on blade loading, through erosion, vibration and noise;
- **thrust loading** as the parameter that decides whether an accelerating duct helps or hurts.

Each device is then mapped to the loss it attacks: **duct → axial and tip; Kappel → tip vortex;
contra-rotation → rotational**. That mapping is the spine of the answer and is what makes it
derivable rather than memorised.

## 3. The deliberate omission

> **No percentage efficiency gain is quoted anywhere in this answer, for any device.**

Published gains for ducts, tip-modified propellers and contra-rotating arrangements **vary widely,
depend entirely on hull form, wake field, loading and operating profile, and originate largely from
manufacturers**. None was verifiable from a primary source.

This follows the standing instruction in §16 of the brief — *"Do not invent numerical efficiency
gains unless strongly sourced and genuinely useful. A qualitative, technically accurate explanation
is preferable to marketing percentages."*

**Recorded here explicitly so that a later editor does not "improve" the answer by adding them.**
