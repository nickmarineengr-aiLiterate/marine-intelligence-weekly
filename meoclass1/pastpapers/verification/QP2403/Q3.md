## 3. "Open the source" — what reading Rule XVII actually changed

The plan predicted contribution would need research. It did, and three propositions that appear in
general circulation are wrong or incomplete against the text:

| Commonly stated | The Rules actually say |
|---|---|
| "Sacrificed cargo is compensated and contributes nothing." | **Rule XVII(b)** — the amount allowed as general average for property sacrificed is **added back** to the contributory value. Sacrificed property **contributes as if it had been saved**. |
| "All property saved contributes." | **Rule XVII(e)** — **mails, passengers' luggage, accompanied personal effects and accompanied private motor vehicles do not contribute at all**. And **XVII(a)(ii)** now lets the adjuster **exclude cargo entirely** where including it would cost more than it contributes. |
| "Contributory value is the value at the port of destination." | **Rule G.1** — values at the time and place **where the common maritime adventure ends**; **XVII(a)(i)** — cargo at its value **at the time of discharge**, from the **commercial invoice rendered to the receiver**. |

**Ninth consecutive paper on which reading the instrument corrected something.** The first of the
three is the error a candidate is most likely to carry into the exam hall, and it inverts the
arithmetic of the adjustment.

## 5. Temporal review — **CONFIRMED STABLE, checked not assumed**

The intake recorded `STABLE / LOW`, and on this question that is correct — but the reason is
recorded rather than assumed, per the standing rule.

- **YAR 2016 is the current edition at both sittings.** No edition was adopted between March 2024
  and October 2025.
- **Which edition applies is contractual, not chronological.** The answer says so expressly. This
  removes the sitting date from the analysis almost entirely, which is why this question is the
  safest reuse on the paper after Q6.
- **The Marine Insurance Act 1963 was in force throughout.** It is untouched by the Merchant
  Shipping Act 2025 boundary of 15 March 2026, which post-dates both sittings, and untouched by
  the Bills of Lading / Carriage of Goods by Sea replacement Acts of 2025 that affect **Q2**.
- **One forward-looking observation, recorded and deliberately NOT written into the Model Answer
  as a change:** Rule XXI prints an **ICE LIBOR**-based interest rate, and LIBOR has since been
  withdrawn as a published benchmark. The Rule is quoted **as printed**. No claim is made about how
  the rate is applied in practice at either sitting. This is a `C_ACCEPTED_LIMITATION`, not a
  temporal correction.

**Nothing later than the sitting appears in the Model Answer.**

## 6. Limitations accepted

1. **Statutory source authority.** The Marine Insurance Act 1963 was read from a **complete
   third-party reproduction** of the Act; `indiacode.nic.in` refuses automated retrieval, as
   previously recorded for this corpus. The s.66 wording used was **corroborated** against the
   section as summarised on India Code and Indian Kanoon and is the long-settled statutory formula.
   Reliance is on **section numbers and substance**; no argument turns on fine wording.
   `C_ACCEPTED_LIMITATION` — re-confirm against an official copy before publication.
2. **The York-Antwerp Rules are a private code** with no official gazette. The text read is the
   International Group's published copy of the CMI text, cross-checked against the club circular
   describing the 2016 changes.
3. **Rule XXI and LIBOR** — see §5.
4. **The carrier's lien and the bond/guarantee practice** are contractual and common-law
   mechanisms, not York-Antwerp Rules. They are described at the level of practice and no authority
   is cited for them. `C_ACCEPTED_LIMITATION`.
