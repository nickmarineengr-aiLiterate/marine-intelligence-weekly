## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: `evaluate`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route.

1. **Why the hull form governs the power a ship needs**
   - Resistance divides into frictional, wave-making and viscous pressure components
   - Frictional resistance scales with wetted surface and dominates at low speed
   - Wave-making rises very steeply with speed and is what the forebody attacks
   - Delivered power = resistance x speed / propulsive coefficient - the hull sets both terms
   - So hull form acts twice: on the force to be overcome, and on how efficiently the propeller converts power into thrust
2. **How hull form optimisation is now done, and what changed**
   - Parametric hull modelling - the form is described by variables a computer can move
   - Computational fluid dynamics evaluates hundreds of variants; the tank verifies the chosen few
   - The decisive change is optimising over an operating profile - a range of speeds, draughts and trims - not a single design point
   - Slow steaming and part-load trading made the old single-point optimum wrong: hulls designed for a service speed were being run far below it
   - The result is a form that is slightly worse at the contract speed and materially better across the speeds actually used
3. **(i) Forebody optimisation**
   - Scope - bulbous bow geometry and volume, entrance angle, stem profile, forward shoulder, and the distribution of fullness forward
   - It targets the wave-making component, and separately the added resistance in waves
   - The bulb works by generating its own wave system to partially cancel the bow wave - which is why it is draught- and speed-sensitive
   - Impact - the largest single gain available where the operating profile has moved away from the design point
   - Retrofit bulb replacement is one of the few genuine hull-form measures available to an existing ship
   - But a bulb optimised for one speed and draught can be worse than none at another - the gain is conditional, not automatic
4. **(ii) Aftbody Optimisation**
   - Scope - the run aft, buttock slopes, stern shape, skeg and bossing form, and the propeller aperture
   - Its job is not primarily resistance - it is the quality of the water delivered to the propeller
   - A uniform wake field governs cavitation, hull pressure pulses, vibration and blade erosion
   - It also controls flow separation, which is the viscous pressure component
   - Impact - improves the propulsive coefficient, so the same thrust is obtained from less delivered power
   - Distinguish hull shape from bolt-on energy saving devices - ducts, fins and pre-swirl stators - which treat the same problem by other means
5. **(iii) Twin Skeg Design**
   - Scope - two longitudinal skegs, two shafts and two propellers in place of a single centreline screw
   - Suits high block coefficient and restricted draught - the case where a single propeller cannot be given enough diameter
   - Each propeller sits in cleaner, more axial inflow, so it works closer to open-water efficiency
   - Allows larger effective diameter at lower rpm for the power absorbed, which is the efficient direction
   - Impact - better propulsive efficiency, lower vibration and cavitation, and redundancy in propulsion
   - Costs - greater wetted surface and hence frictional resistance, two shaft lines, more machinery to buy, install and maintain, and higher build cost
6. **What limits the gains, and where they are measured**
   - The three measures are not additive - they interact, and the total is always less than the sum
   - Every gain is ship-specific and conditional on the operating profile actually flown
   - Hull form is one of the few genuine levers on attained EEDI for a new ship and on attained EEXI for an existing one
   - EEXI and the operational carbon intensity indicator have applied since 1 January 2023 and were therefore live at this sitting
   - In service the benefit shows in the operational indicator, which measures what the ship actually did rather than what she was designed to do
   - Fouling can erase the whole gain: a hull optimised in the tank and left unmaintained is not an optimised hull
