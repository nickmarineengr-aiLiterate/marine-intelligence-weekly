## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: `explain`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route.

1. **What GISIS is, and the problem it solves**
   - The Global Integrated Shipping Information System - a web-based platform maintained by the IMO Secretariat
   - It exists because IMO has no inspectorate and no ships; it knows only what Member States report
   - Before it, reporting was by paper circular - slow to compile, scattered, and effectively unsearchable
   - It consolidates into one system the information States are required or invited to report under the instruments
   - Classify it correctly at the outset: it is an administrative platform, not an instrument, and it creates no obligation of its own
2. **The aim of the system**
   - A single point of collection and dissemination for information related to IMO instruments
   - To support Member States in implementing and enforcing those instruments, by giving Administrations and port State control access to what others hold
   - To give the Organization the evidence base on which instruments are reviewed, amended and developed
   - To promote transparency and information sharing between Administrations, and with industry where the data is public
   - To reduce duplicated reporting - each item collected once and made available to all who need it
3. **The functions - how it works and what it holds**
   - Organised into modules, each corresponding to a reporting obligation or a defined information set
   - Those in use include - and this list is not exhaustive - marine casualties and incidents; piracy and armed robbery; reception facilities; contact points; recognized organizations; ship and company particulars; port State control; and modules covering pollution prevention equipment, survey and certification, security and facilitation
   - Data is entered by designated national focal points; the Secretariat administers rather than generates the content
   - Access is tiered - some modules public, others restricted to Member States or registered users, according to sensitivity
   - Records are searchable and exportable, and that is the feature which converts a pile of reports into usable evidence
   - In doing so it facilitates data sharing among Member States, without bilateral correspondence
4. **The fuel oil module and MARPOL Annex VI**
   - Annex VI regulation 14 caps the sulphur content of fuel oil used on board - 0.50 % m/m globally since 1 January 2020, 0.10 % m/m in emission control areas
   - But the Annex recognises that a ship cannot burn fuel that is not on sale
   - Regulation 18, fuel oil availability and quality, requires Parties to promote availability of compliant fuel
   - Where a ship cannot obtain compliant fuel oil despite best efforts, it reports that fact to its Administration and to the competent authority of the port of destination - the fuel oil non-availability report
   - Regulation 18 also provides for notifying a supplier's failure to meet requirements, for example on the bunker delivery note or the representative sample
   - The GISIS module collects these reports centrally
   - Distinguish regulation 27 - collection and reporting of ship fuel oil consumption data - a separate stream with a different purpose
5. **Usefulness for reporting**
   - A single standard channel and format, replacing ad hoc correspondence with several authorities in several forms
   - The ship's own report becomes a preserved record, which is what supports her position if the fuel used is later questioned at an inspection
   - The report identifies the port and the supplier, so it is actionable rather than merely noted
   - Reports of genuine non-availability stay distinct from reports of supply that failed to meet requirements - two different problems needing two different responses
   - The Administration and the port State receive the same record, removing an obvious source of dispute about what was reported and when
6. **Usefulness for analysing**
   - Aggregation changes what the data means - one report is an anecdote, several hundred searchable by port and date are evidence
   - Where compliant fuel is genuinely unavailable - a map of the real supply position rather than the assumed one
   - Which ports recur, and which suppliers repeatedly fail on quality or documentation
   - Risk-based targeting for port State control - concentrate on ships that bunkered where problems cluster, rather than inspecting at random
   - Evidence for the Organization in reviewing how regulation 18 is working and whether availability is adequate
   - Separating genuine non-availability from evasion - the point that matters most to an honest operator, because the pattern corroborates a genuine claim as readily as it exposes a false one
   - Commercial use - bunker planning, supplier selection and voyage planning, where the data is publicly accessible
7. **The limits of the data**
   - It is a reporting system. Its completeness depends entirely on States and ships actually reporting, and under-reporting is likely
   - Therefore the absence of reports from a port is not evidence that compliant fuel was available there - it may simply mean nobody reported
   - Module membership and structure are administrative - modules are added, merged and reorganised with no instrument amended, so any list dates quickly
   - A GISIS record is evidence that a report was made, not a finding of fact - whether the ship's efforts were genuinely reasonable remains for the authorities
   - It supplements and does not replace the ship's own evidence - the bunker delivery note and its retained representative sample, the oil record book, and the correspondence showing the attempts made
