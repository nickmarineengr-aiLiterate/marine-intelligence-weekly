## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: `explain`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route.

1. **Where the survey obligation comes from, and the two regimes running in parallel**
   - Statutory surveys come from the conventions - SOLAS, Load Lines, MARPOL - and are the flag State's responsibility
   - Class surveys come from the society's own rules and are contractual, between society and owner
   - In practice one society usually does both, acting as a recognized organization under SOLAS XI-1/1 and the RO Code
   - The Administration delegates the work, never the responsibility
   - The harmonized system put both onto one five-year cycle with a common anniversary date
   - The survey types on that cycle are annual, intermediate, periodical, renewal, plus docking and tailshaft surveys
   - At this sitting the applicable Assembly guidelines are A.1156(32) of 2021 - the survey TYPES, however, are created by SOLAS chapter I, not by the guidelines
2. **A) The purpose of annual surveys**
   - To confirm that the ship is still in the condition in which she was certified - the certificate is five years, the confidence must be renewed yearly
   - It gives effect to SOLAS chapter I regulation 11: conditions are to be MAINTAINED after survey, and no change made without approval
   - It is a general inspection, not a re-examination - the surveyor confirms maintenance rather than re-proving design
   - To detect deterioration, neglect, unapproved modification and missing or expired equipment
   - To provide the endorsement on the certificate without which it ceases to be valid, whatever its printed expiry
   - To keep the flag State and, through it, port State control informed that the ship remains compliant
   - Held within three months either side of each anniversary date, which is what makes it plannable
3. **B) Items examined - hull, closing appliances and load line**
   - Shell plating and weather decks so far as accessible; visible corrosion, wastage, cracking, indents and previous repairs
   - Hatch covers and coamings - condition, cleats, compression bars, gaskets, drainage
   - Watertight and weathertight closing appliances - doors, side scuttles, ventilators, air pipes and their closures
   - Freeing ports, guard rails, gangways, walkways and means of safe access
   - Load line marks and draught marks - legible, correctly positioned, not obscured
   - Bulwarks, hatchway beams, ventilator coamings and their securing
   - Anchoring and mooring arrangements so far as they are covered by the certificates
4. **B) Items examined - machinery, electrical, fire and life-saving**
   - General condition of main and auxiliary machinery, seatings, and evidence of oil leakage onto hot surfaces
   - Steering gear, including the auxiliary and emergency arrangements, communications and the posted instructions
   - Emergency source of electrical power and its changeover, tested on load; emergency lighting
   - Bilge pumping and drainage, bilge alarms, quick-closing valves and remote stops
   - Fire main, pumps and hydrants, hoses and nozzles; fixed and portable extinguishing systems and their surveys
   - Fire detection and alarm, dampers, ventilation stops, fire control plan and the means of escape
   - Life-saving appliances - boats, davits and launching gear, liferafts and their servicing, lifebuoys, lifejackets, immersion suits, EEBDs, muster list and embarkation arrangements
   - Radio and navigational equipment as covered by the relevant certificate; lights, shapes, sound signals, charts and publications
5. **B) Items examined - pollution prevention, and the documentary set**
   - Oil filtering equipment and the 15 ppm alarm and automatic stopping device; no bypass fitted; sludge and bilge arrangements
   - Oil Record Book reconciling with the plant, and the IOPP certificate supplement matching what is actually installed
   - Sewage plant and the discharge arrangement; the garbage management plan and record book; incinerator
   - Annex VI items - bunker delivery notes and retained samples, engine documentation, and the ship's energy efficiency documentation
   - Certificates - validity, endorsements, and that the ship matches what they describe
   - Continuous Synopsis Record, safe manning document, ISM DOC and SMC, ISPS certificate, MLC documentation
   - The class survey status itself - outstanding conditions of class and their due dates, and the record of previous port State inspections
   - The list is representative and not exhaustive - the scope follows the certificates the ship carries and her type
6. **C) Condition of class - what it is and how one arises**
   - A requirement imposed by the classification society that a specific measure be carried out by a specific date in order that class be retained
   - Some societies call it a recommendation; the concept is the same and the vocabulary varies
   - It is a classification term, not a convention term - say so, it is the precision the examiner is looking for
   - It arises when a survey finds a defect that does not make the ship unfit to proceed but must still be corrected
   - It is therefore permission to continue on terms, and it sits between full compliance and suspension
   - It carries an owner, a scope and a due date, and is closed only by a surveyor's attendance and confirmation
   - It is recorded in the ship's class status, which is the fact that generates everything in step 8
7. **C) The technical consequences**
   - It identifies a real defect and fixes a date by which it must be rectified - the defect does not become optional
   - It may impose operational restrictions in the meantime - a tank or space not to be used, a loading restriction, a limitation on a system
   - It drives repair and docking planning, spares procurement and the timing of the next class attendance
   - It feeds into the planned maintenance system and into the ship's own defect and risk registers
   - If it falls overdue, class is liable to be suspended or withdrawn
   - And because SOLAS II-1/3-1 requires the ship to be maintained in compliance with recognized class rules, loss of class becomes a SOLAS non-compliance
   - Statutory certificates issued by that society as a recognized organization are then liable to fall with the class - which is how a technical item becomes a detention
8. **C) The commercial consequences, and why they are the larger ones**
   - The class status is visible to third parties, and that visibility is the whole commercial mechanism
   - Charterers - most forms warrant that the vessel is and will be maintained in class; an overdue item can put the owner in breach
   - Vetting and terminal acceptance - open items are read as evidence of how the ship is run, and can cost a fixture outright
   - Insurance - hull cover is conventionally conditioned on class being maintained, so suspension can prejudice cover; P&I likewise
   - Port State control - an outstanding condition of class is a targeting factor and can supply clear grounds
   - Sale and finance - it affects price, due diligence and loan covenants that require class to be maintained
   - The asymmetry is the point. The technical consequence is bounded by the defect; the commercial consequence propagates to everyone who reads the record
   - Hence the management response: close conditions of class early, never at the due date, and treat a recurring one as a system failure rather than a repair
