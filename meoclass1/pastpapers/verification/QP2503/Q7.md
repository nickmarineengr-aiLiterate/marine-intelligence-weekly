## 2. WHAT THE QUESTION ASKS

A single 16-mark design question with a false premise printed into it. The examinable content is the set of DESIGN features by which a new build meets a required EEDI, and it is testing whether the candidate can organise them by mechanism rather than recite them. The premise - that Phase 2 is the present framework - is out of date at this sitting, and handling that correctly is a second, smaller demand the strong candidate will meet.

**Mark allocation.** 16, undivided, and the stem's own emphasis is on 'the various design features'. Sections 4 to 7 therefore carry the bulk. Sections 1, 2 and 3 are framing and are kept tight - but section 2 cannot be cut, because the phase position is the paper's sharpest temporal point, and section 3 cannot be cut, because without the equation the design features are a list.

**Examiner expectation.** The correct post-2021 regulation numbering - 22 attained, 24 required, Table 1 - and the required EEDI formula. Then design features grouped so that their effect is explained: hull form and operating-profile optimisation, reduced installed power and design speed, larger slower propeller and matched engine, coatings, auxiliary demand; the fuel, which is the largest single lever, with methane slip acknowledged; waste heat recovery, shaft generator, propeller-zone energy-saving devices, air lubrication and wind assistance; and capacity measures. A strong answer also reaches the minimum propulsion power floor in regulation 24.5 and the two-stage verification.

**Required answer components:**

- Where the EEDI duty comes from - the regulations, and the correct post-2021 numbering
- What the phases actually are, and what 'Phase 2' means at a March 2025 sitting
- The equation, because every design feature is an attack on one side of it
- Design features that reduce the required power - hull and propulsion
- Design features that reduce the carbon factor - the fuel, which is the largest single lever
- Design features that recover or displace energy - waste heat, shaft machinery, wind and air
- Features that raise the denominator, and the floor that stops power being cut indefinitely
- How the result is verified and certified

**Ambiguities adjudicated:**

- 'The present EEDI framework under Phase 2' is false at this sitting on both timetables. Preserved and adjudicated: the design content is delivered in full and the position is stated precisely.
- '(of 20% -30% reduction)' spans two phases. The recurring Table 1 values are 0, 10, 20 and 30, with 20 characteristically Phase 2 for the larger bands and 30 Phase 3. Treated as a stringency band rather than a single factor, and no cell is quoted.
- 'Design features adopted in the new build vessels to comply' excludes operational measures. Slow steaming in service, in-service trim optimisation and hull cleaning belong to the SEEMP and the CII and do not affect the attained EEDI. The distinction is stated rather than assumed.
- The stem prints 'EEDl' with a lower-case L. A printing artefact; preserved in text_verbatim, not reproduced in the answer.
- Reduced design speed is both a design feature and, in appearance, an operational one. It is a design feature here because the reference speed is a design parameter fixed at the trial, and that is worth saying.

## 9. ANSWER ROUTE AS BUILT

Archetype `explain`. The route is the single canonical sequence; the model answer's principal
headings **are** these steps, and `validate_spec.py` enforces that correspondence.

1. **Where the duty comes from, and the numbering that matters**
   - chapter 4 of MARPOL Annex VI as revised by MEPC.328(76), in force 1 November 2022 - use the post-2021 numbering
   - regulation 19 - 400 GT and above; 19.3 disapplies 22 to 25 to non-conventional propulsion
   - regulation 22 - attained EEDI, ship-specific, the EEDI technical file, verification, method referred out by 22.2
   - regulation 24 - required EEDI, and where the phased scheme lives
   - regulation 5.4 initial survey, regulation 6 the International Energy Efficiency Certificate
   - regulation 24.1 - attained EEDI shall be less than or equal to (1 - X/100) x the reference line value
2. **What 'Phase 2' means at this sitting - and the answer is not what the stem assumes**
   - Table 1 prints TWO phase timetables, split by ship type
   - gas carriers 15,000 DWT and above, containerships, general cargo ships, LNG carriers, cruise passenger ships: Phase 2 to 31 March 2022, Phase 3 from 1 April 2022
   - bulk carriers, smaller gas bands, tankers, reefers, combination carriers, all ro-ro: Phase 2 to 31 December 2024, Phase 3 from 1 January 2025
   - at this sitting Phase 2 has closed on BOTH timetables, the second only ten weeks before the paper
   - Phase 2 nonetheless remains operative law, because required EEDI is fixed by the phase the ship was contracted or built into
   - the phase is fixed by the construction datum - regulation 2.2.18, with 5.4 for converted ships and 24.2 for type and size
   - the bracket 20 to 30 per cent spans two phases; recurring Table 1 values are 0, 10, 20 and 30
3. **The equation - because every design feature attacks one side of it**
   - attained EEDI is CO2 emitted over transport work: power x specific fuel consumption x carbon factor, over capacity x reference speed
   - correction factors for ice class, power limits, cubic capacity, weather, structural enhancement and innovative technologies
   - the full method is referred out by regulation 22.2 - give the structure, not the coefficients
   - four mechanisms follow: reduce power, reduce carbon factor, recover or displace energy, raise the denominator
4. **Design features that reduce required power - hull and propulsion**
   - hull form optimised by CFD and model test for the REAL operating profile, not one contract condition
   - bulbous bow designed for the operating envelope rather than a single draught and speed
   - reduced installed power and lower design speed - the most powerful single design decision, because resistance rises far faster than speed
   - larger, slower-turning propeller with matched aperture; propulsive efficiency rises with diameter
   - high-efficiency propeller design using the actual wake field as the design input
   - hull and propeller interaction - shaft line, skeg arrangement, rudder profile chosen with the propeller
   - low-friction and foul-release coatings specified from new
   - derated long-stroke low-revolution main engine with electronic control for low consumption across the load range
   - reduced auxiliary demand - variable-frequency drives, correctly sized auxiliaries, efficient HVAC and lighting
5. **Design features that reduce the carbon factor - the fuel**
   - the carbon conversion factor is a straight multiplier in the numerator, so fuel is the largest single lever
   - LNG dual fuel - the largest EEDI reduction available from an otherwise conventional design
   - LPG, particularly where the cargo is the fuel; methanol dual fuel, liquid at ambient conditions
   - ammonia-ready and methanol-ready designs with space, foundations and safety arrangements built in
   - biofuel capability in fuel system materials and specification
   - QUALIFY IT: methane slip is a greenhouse gas the carbon factor does not fully capture, and the index is tank-to-wake at a design condition
6. **Design features that recover or displace energy**
   - waste heat recovery - exhaust gas economiser with steam turbine, power turbine, combined turbo-generator
   - shaft generator taking the electrical load from the main engine; shaft motor for power take-in
   - energy saving devices in the propeller zone - pre-swirl ducts and stators, wake-equalising ducts, boss cap fins, rudder bulbs, twisted rudders
   - designed WITH the hull and propeller on a new build, which is why they outperform retrofits
   - air lubrication, with the compressor power correctly debited
   - wind assistance - rotor sails, rigid wings, suction wings, kites - integrated from the design stage
   - solar for hotel load and battery hybridisation for peak shaving
   - several enter through the innovative energy efficiency technology terms of the formula
7. **Raising the denominator - and the floor that stops power being cut**
   - increased deadweight or cubic capacity for the same principal dimensions - high-tensile steel, optimised scantlings, reduced lightship
   - beamier, shallower forms at lower speed, raising capacity and reducing power together
   - design draught and trim optimisation so the reference condition matches the trading condition
   - regulation 24.5 - the minimum propulsion power requirement, because the cheapest way to pass the index is to install less power
   - the floor exists to preserve steerage and manoeuvrability in adverse conditions; its numerical criteria are in Organization guidelines
   - regulation 24.6 carries the review trigger for the phase scheme itself
8. **How the result is verified and certified**
   - the shipbuilder calculates the attained EEDI and compiles the EEDI technical file
   - preliminary verification at the design stage against the technical file and approved drawings
   - final verification at the sea trial, where reference speed is established from the measured speed-power relationship
   - the Administration or authorized organization VERIFIES - it does not calculate and does not approve
   - initial survey under regulation 5.4 and the International Energy Efficiency Certificate under regulation 6
   - innovative technology claims must be substantiated in the technical file or they earn nothing
