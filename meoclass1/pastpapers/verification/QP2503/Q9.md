## 2. WHAT THE QUESTION ASKS

A 16-mark scenario question with two express demands - WHAT steps and WHO initiates them - against a two-part object printed in the stem: the safety of the SHIPS and the MARINE ENVIRONMENT. The hidden demand is the statute boundary: the stem prints 'the Merchant Shipping Act' with no year, and at this sitting it can only be the 1958 Act. The scenario detail that changes the law is the loss of lives, which makes a marine safety investigation mandatory.

**Mark allocation.** 16, undivided, with the stem's own two-part object suggesting the weight. Sections 4 and 5 answer the printed objects directly and carry the most. Sections 2 and 3 carry the immediate and reporting steps, section 6 the investigation limb where marks are most often lost, and section 7 the liability layer briefly. Seven sections give every actor and every object a home.

**Examiner expectation.** The 1958 Act identified as the governing statute. The Master's immediate duties in the right order - life, ship, environment, evidence - including the duty to stand by and assist after collision. The reporting chain with its several addressees. For the ships: damage survey and class, detention under the unseaworthy and unsafe ships power, salvage, a place of refuge under A.1184(33), and the Part XA directions power. For the environment: SOPEP, the national contingency plan with the Indian Coast Guard responsible at sea, the response techniques, and above all the Central Government and Director-General's power to direct, to act and to recover cost. Then the two investigation systems held apart, with the mandatory Code investigation engaged BY THE DEATH. Finally the liability and insurance layer that funds it.

**Required answer components:**

- What has actually happened, in legal terms - four distinct events in one casualty, each with its own machinery
- The immediate duties on the Master and on the ship, and who owes them
- The reporting chain - to whom, by whom, and under what obligation
- Steps for the safety of the ships - salvage, refuge, and the State's power to intervene
- Steps for the marine environment - the national response machinery and the Central Government's directions power
- Investigation and inquiry - the two systems, national and international, and which is engaged here
- Liability, compensation and the certificates that fund the response

**Ambiguities adjudicated:**

- 'The Merchant Shipping Act' is printed without a year. At this sitting it can only be the 1958 Act; saying so explicitly costs one line and protects the answer.
- 'The ships' is plural. A second vessel is involved, so collision duties, her flag State's interest in the investigation and the apportionment of liability all enter.
- The other vessel's flag is not stated. Both possibilities are addressed, because a foreign flag makes her flag State a substantially interested State.
- 'Loss of lives' could be read as scenery. It is the fact that engages the mandatory marine safety investigation, and the answer says which limb of the test it satisfies.
- 'Steps ... for the safety of the ships AND the marine environment' is two objects. Each is given its own section in the stem's own order.
- Whether the wreck and salvage machinery is engaged depends on whether the casualty leaves a wreck. Stated conditionally rather than assumed, unlike the grounding-and-abandonment donor where it was central.

## 9. ANSWER ROUTE AS BUILT

Archetype `procedure`. The route is the single canonical sequence; the model answer's principal
headings **are** these steps, and `validate_spec.py` enforces that correspondence.

1. **What has actually happened - four events in one casualty**
   - a collision - engaging both masters' duties and the law of collision
   - loss of life - which makes this a VERY SERIOUS MARINE CASUALTY and the safety investigation mandatory
   - damage to the vessels - seaworthiness, survey, class, detention, possibly salvage
   - an oil spill - the pollution response, the directions power and the liability conventions
   - the statute is the MERCHANT SHIPPING ACT, 1958, and it is enabling as much as prohibitory
2. **Immediate steps on board - the Master, and the order matters**
   - muster, account for all persons and search for the missing - the overriding first duty
   - render assistance to the other vessel and to persons in danger so far as he can without serious danger to his own ship; stand by; exchange particulars
   - sound tanks, assess damage, contain flooding, and assess stability and residual strength before moving
   - stop the source - isolate the damaged tank, transfer internally, deploy SOPEP
   - broadcast and report - distress or urgency, and a danger message where navigation is endangered
   - PRESERVE EVIDENCE - VDR, charts, logs, engine and alarm records, position and time
3. **The reporting chain - who tells whom**
   - Master to owner and DPA - the ISM emergency response is activated
   - Master and owner to the Directorate General of Shipping through the nearest Mercantile Marine Department - the statutory casualty report
   - Master to the Indian Coast Guard and MRCC - search and rescue, and pollution response at sea
   - Master to the nearest coastal State and port authority; and to the other vessel's master
   - owner to P&I club, hull underwriters and classification society
   - Administration to the other vessel's flag State if foreign - a substantially interested State
   - alongside these: a MARPOL Protocol I report and a SOLAS chapter V danger message
4. **Steps for the safety of the ships**
   - damage assessment and survey by class and the Administration; repairs approved before proceeding; conditions of class
   - DETENTION under the unseaworthy and unsafe ships power, sections 334 to 336, exercised by the Director-General or his surveyor
   - salvage where she cannot proceed - Lloyd's Open Form, no cure no pay - within the Act's wreck and salvage Part, sections 390 to 404, and the Wrecks and Salvage Rules 1974
   - a PLACE OF REFUGE - the coastal State decides, on guidance resolution A.1184(33) adopted 6 December 2023, revoking A.949(23)
   - DIRECTIONS to move, act or refrain, under Part XA - the power that makes it compellable
   - navigational safety - marking, warnings, and an exclusion zone where necessary
5. **Steps for the marine environment**
   - the ship acts first - SOPEP implemented, source stopped, containment with the ship's own means
   - the national response under the National Oil Spill Disaster Contingency Plan, with the INDIAN COAST GUARD responsible at sea, on a tiered structure
   - response operations - surveillance, booms, skimmers, authorised dispersant, shoreline protection and clean-up, waste reception
   - THE LEGAL ENGINE: Part XA sections 356J, 356K and 356L - the Central Government and the Director-General may DIRECT the owner, master or salvor, may ACT themselves, and may RECOVER THE COST
   - coordination with State pollution control, fisheries, environment departments and port authorities
   - funding through P&I and the compulsory insurance behind the CLC or Bunkers certificate, with direct action against the insurer
6. **Investigation and inquiry - two systems, and both are engaged**
   - NATIONAL: the Act's investigations and inquiries Part - report, preliminary inquiry by an appointed person, then formal investigation before a court with assessors
   - the formal investigation may find fault and may CANCEL OR SUSPEND a certificate - its orientation is partly disciplinary
   - INTERNATIONAL: the Casualty Investigation Code, MSC.255(84), mandatory via SOLAS XI-1/6, whose purpose is to prevent recurrence and expressly NOT to apportion blame
   - MANDATORY HERE - a very serious marine casualty includes one involving A DEATH; the spill alone would engage it only if environmental damage were severe
   - India as flag State is the marine safety investigating State; a foreign second vessel's flag State is a substantially interested State; the report goes to the IMO
   - hold them apart: one can take a certificate away, the other must not be used to apportion blame
7. **Liability, compensation and the certificates behind them**
   - CLC 1992 where she is a CLC ship carrying persistent oil; the 2001 Bunkers Convention otherwise - and CLC applicability is never stated absolutely
   - collision liability apportioned by fault, pursued in the admiralty jurisdiction of the High Courts under the Admiralty Act, 2017
   - loss of life claims and the owner's obligations to crew and dependants
   - limitation of liability for maritime claims
   - wreck removal if a wreck results
   - THE ANSWER TO 'WHO': Master initiates, owner responds, the Director-General directs and inquires, the Coast Guard executes, the coastal State decides on refuge
