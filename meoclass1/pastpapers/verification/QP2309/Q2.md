## 2. DECOMPOSITION GATE

**Question intent.** Three questions printed as one sentence, moving from identity to instrument to supervision. The examiner is testing whether the candidate understands that statutory certification is the flag State's duty performed by somebody else, and whether he knows the instrument that governs the arrangement and the machinery by which the arrangement is policed. The third question is the discriminator: most candidates can define an RO and name the Code, and few can describe oversight as a programme with mechanisms and indicators.

**Mark allocation.** Sixteen marks on the stem with no printed split. The three questions are not equal in weight: the definition is a short answer, the salient features are the bulk of the instrument knowledge, and monitoring carries the applied marks. The answer allots sections 1 and 2 to the definition and the distinction that makes it usable, sections 3 to 6 to the Code, and sections 7 and 8 to monitoring - deliberately giving monitoring more space than its one clause of stem suggests, because it is where the marks are actually earned.

**Examiner expectation.** That the delegation is of function and not of responsibility. That the Code is named with its adopting resolutions and its mandatory date. That the three-part structure is given with the mandatory boundary correct. That monitoring is answered with real mechanisms rather than the word 'audit'. And that the answer closes on the flag State's own accountability, which is what turns a description into an understanding of the scheme.

**Freshness risk.** MEDIUM - The RO Code is stable and needs no reversal at all. The single moving part is the Assembly obligations list, where the donor's A.1187(33) is three months future at this sitting and must be reversed to A.1157(32). That is a small edit with a large consequence, because it is exactly the kind of number a reviewer would 'correct' back to the later resolution.

**Jurisdiction risk.** MEDIUM. Three levels must not be blurred: the Code is an IMO instrument binding through the conventions; the delegation is an act of national law by a flag Administration; and the classification work the same society does is contractual and not statutory at all. An answer that treats an IACS resolution or a class rule as part of the statutory chain has the levels wrong. Where India is concerned the Administration is the Directorate General of Shipping acting under the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958.

**Technical ambiguities.** 'Salient features' is undefined and could mean the Code's structure, its requirements, or its legal effect; the answer covers all three rather than choosing, because each is separately markable. 'Monitor' is likewise undefined and is taken to include the before-and-after acts of assessment and sanction as well as the during-delegation supervision, since an answer confined to the middle would omit the written agreement and the power to withdraw.

**Target answer shape.** Eight numbered sections. Prose for the definition and the distinction, because they are arguments rather than lists. Bulleted lists for the Code's parts, the organization's requirements, the flag State's procedural duties and the oversight mechanisms, so that each is separately countable. A before/during/after frame inside the monitoring section. A single-sentence closing proposition on the undelegable responsibility.

**Primary source plan.** Build on the RO Code itself - Part 1 sections 4 and 5, Part 2 sections 2 to 8 with appendices 1 to 3, and Part 3 sections 4 to 7 - as established in the MIW verification record for QP2408 Q1, which worked through the text section by section. Take the adoption and mandating chain from the QP2401 record. Corroborate against the corpus copy at 03-imo-instruments/RO-Code but do not rest any section number on it alone, its edition being unverified (RQ-25). Add the III Code and the Member State Audit Scheme for the closing accountability chain. Deliberately NOT planned: any quotation from A.1157(32), which is not held.

## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: **explain**. The principal headings of the model answer ARE these steps.

1. **What a Recognized Organization is**
   - An organization to which a flag Administration has delegated statutory survey and certification work
   - Usually but not necessarily a classification society
   - The delegation is of function, not of responsibility
   - Class and RO are different capacities - same society, different hats, flag by flag
2. **Recognition and authorization are two separate acts**
   - Recognition is assessment against the mandatory requirements
   - Authorization is the later delegation of specified functions
   - An organization may be recognized and still not authorized for a ship type or a convention
   - A flag State shall not authorize functions beyond the RO's capabilities
3. **The RO Code, and how a code becomes binding**
   - Adopted by MSC.349(92) of 21 June 2013 and MEPC.237(65)
   - A harmonised, transparent and independent international standard replacing scattered Assembly criteria
   - Mandatory from 1 January 2015 by MSC.350(92), MSC.356(92) and MEPC.238(65)
   - Amending SOLAS, MARPOL Annexes I and II and the 1988 Load Line Protocol; SOLAS XI-1/1 is the hook
   - Recognise the technique - a standard made binding through the conventions that refer to it
4. **Salient feature - three parts, and which of them bind**
   - Part 1 general; Part 2 mandatory requirements; Part 3 oversight guidelines
   - Parts 1 and 2 mandatory, Part 3 recommendatory - the most examinable structural fact
   - Part 2 says shall, Part 3 says should
   - The resolution recommends Part 3 unless national requirements give at least equivalent safety
   - The scope paragraph establishes four things and they map onto this question
5. **Salient feature - what the organization itself must satisfy**
   - Demonstration is the organization's, confirmation is the flag State's
   - General requirements including legal personality, competence, ethics and independence
   - Management and organization; resources; processes including subcontracting, complaints and appeals
   - Performance measurement including vertical contract audit and control of statutory deficiencies
   - A quality system no less effective than ISO 9000, certified by an accredited body independent of the ROs and their associations
   - Independence is the head the Code is really defending
6. **Salient feature - what the flag State must do when it authorizes**
   - Rest the delegation on its conventional basis and only for its own ships
   - SPECIFY the functions - function by function, convention by convention, including withdrawal and cancellation
   - Make a formal written agreement covering legal basis, reporting, interpretations and information exchange
   - Notify the Secretary-General of the list of ROs and the conditions of the authority delegated
7. **How Administrations monitor - the oversight programme**
   - Before: assess against Part 2, confirm capability, reduce to a written agreement with scope and limits
   - During, mechanism one - ACCESS to the QMS, internal instructions, fleet documentation, casualty statistics
   - Mechanism two - a system to ensure adequacy: reporting, additional flag State inspections, inter-RO consultation
   - Mechanism three - auditing, with permission to rely on accredited independent audits; ethical conduct, fair presentation, due professional care
   - Mechanism four - a managed programme with objectives, resources, records and periodic review
8. **Performance indicators, and the duty that cannot be delegated**
   - Port State performance; internal audit results; third-party QMS audit results; previous monitoring; condition of the ships certified
   - A specified authorization can be narrowed as readily as withdrawn
   - Failure to report is a compliance failure, not poor service
   - The Administration is itself audited under the Member State Audit Scheme against the III Code and A.1157(32)
   - The flag State may delegate the work; it cannot delegate the responsibility
