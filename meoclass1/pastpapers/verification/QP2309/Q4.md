## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: **explain**. The principal headings of the model answer ARE these steps.

1. **What the Organization is, and the organs through which it works**
   - The UN specialised agency for safety and security of shipping and prevention of pollution from ships
   - Two-tier governing structure - Assembly and Council
   - Five main committees supported by seven sub-committees
   - A permanent Secretariat headed by the Secretary-General
   - Governing organs decide policy, budget and membership; the committees produce what reaches a ship
2. **The Assembly - the governing body**
   - Highest governing body; all Member States sit in it
   - Meets in regular session once every two years
   - Approves the work programme, votes the budget, determines financial arrangements
   - Elects the Council; receives the reports of Council and committees
   - Adopts Assembly resolutions - recommendations, guidelines and codes
   - Worked example at this sitting: A.1155(32), Procedures for Port State Control, 32nd Assembly, December 2021
3. **The Council - the executive organ**
   - 40 Members elected for two years in three categories - ten shipping services, ten seaborne trade, twenty geographical
   - Performs all Assembly functions between sessions EXCEPT making recommendations to Governments on safety and pollution prevention
   - Supervises the work of the Organization and co-ordinates its organs
   - Considers the draft work programme and budget and submits them with comments
   - Appoints the Secretary-General subject to Assembly approval
   - A.1152(32) would enlarge it to 52 but was not in force at this sitting - write 40
4. **The five main committees and what each owns**
   - MSC - the highest technical body: navigation, construction and equipment, manning for safety, casualty investigation, salvage and rescue
   - MEPC - pollution prevention and control: MARPOL, ballast water, anti-fouling, greenhouse gases
   - Legal Committee - any legal matter, liability and compensation in particular
   - Technical Cooperation Committee - technical assistance, particularly for developing States
   - Facilitation Committee - elimination of unnecessary formalities; the FAL Convention
   - All five open to all Member States; MSC and MEPC adopt amendments to the mandatory instruments
5. **The seven sub-committees and the support they provide**
   - HTW, III, NCSR, PPR, SDC, SSE and CCC
   - Preparatory and technical - a committee cannot draft detailed regulation in a week
   - They develop draft text, guidelines and unified interpretations and resolve the technical argument
   - They report back to MSC, MEPC, or both where a subject straddles them
   - A sub-committee adopts nothing: it prepares, the committee approves, the committee or Assembly adopts
6. **What a convention is**
   - A formal legally binding treaty between States
   - Adopted at a diplomatic conference or by the Assembly, then opened for signature, ratification, acceptance, approval or accession
   - Enters into force only on the conditions written into it - typically a number of States and a share of world tonnage
   - Binds only States party to it, and reaches the ship through flag State law
   - Example: SOLAS 1974
   - Adoption, signature, ratification and entry into force are four separate events
7. **Protocol, Code and Resolution distinguished, with examples**
   - Protocol - a separate treaty amending or supplementing a parent convention, itself requiring ratification; the 1988 SOLAS and Load Lines Protocols, the 1978 and 1997 MARPOL Protocols
   - Code - a detailed technical instrument, recommendatory of itself, mandatory only where a convention makes it so; the ISM Code through SOLAS chapter IX, the RO Code through SOLAS XI-1/1
   - Resolution - a formal decision of an organ; may be recommendatory or may carry a mandatory amendment; A.1155(32) against MEPC.328(76)
8. **The pattern that ties the four together**
   - Binding force is a property of the route an instrument has taken, not of the name it carries
   - A convention and a protocol bind through ratification
   - A code binds only where a convention reaches out and makes it mandatory
   - A resolution may be a recommendation or the vehicle of a mandatory amendment
   - Tacit acceptance - an amendment enters into force on a stated date unless a specified proportion of Contracting Governments objects
   - That is why the committees, not the Assembly, are where an engineer's regulation comes from
