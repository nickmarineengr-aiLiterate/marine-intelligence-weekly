## 2. DECOMPOSITION GATE

**Question intent.** A three-limb question that moves from a conceptual distinction to four applied scenarios and then to a rule set. The examiner is testing whether the candidate understands that safety management is verified by a different process from the one that verifies the ship, and then - in the four scenarios - whether he understands the limit on delegated authority. The scenarios are chosen with care: two of them are matters of technical judgement, which the RO decides, and two are matters of the certificate's terms, which it does not. A candidate who sees that pattern answers all four correctly in very little space.

**Mark allocation.** Printed 4 + (2+2+2+2) + 4, summing to 16, and the distribution differs from the way this question is usually set. Limb (a) at four marks needs a worked comparison, not a sentence, and gets two sections. Each scenario in limb (b) carries its own two marks and must therefore be a discrete, self-contained answer - which is why the shared principle is stated once, in its own section, before them. Limb (c) at four marks is given cleanly as two lists of grounds without the extended commentary a six-mark version could support.

**Examiner expectation.** In limb (a), a distinction expressed in terms of object, method and finding - and the vocabulary used correctly, deficiency against non-conformity. In limb (b), a specific action for each scenario, with the recognition that the RO refers rather than decides where the certificate's terms are in question, and the recognition that no general power of extension exists. In limb (c), both certificates treated separately, with the dependency of the SMC on the DOC stated expressly.

**Freshness risk.** HIGH - The mandatory instrument is completely stable - the Code has been unamended since 1 January 2015 - but the recommendatory guidelines changed three months after this sitting, and the donor is built on the later edition. The risk is unusually severe because the two editions are substantively similar, so an imported citation reads as correct rather than as obviously anachronistic. The mitigation is structural rather than editorial: the answer is built on the Code, which did not move, and the guidelines are named without being relied upon.

**Jurisdiction risk.** MEDIUM, and it is the subject of limb (b) rather than a hazard to it. Three levels must stay distinct: the Code, mandatory through SOLAS chapter IX; the flag Administration, which certifies and withdraws; and the recognized organization, which acts within a scope specified in its authorization. The specific error to avoid is attributing withdrawal to the RO. For an Indian ship the Administration acts under the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958 at this sitting.

**Technical ambiguities.** The stem spells the same term two ways in adjacent sub-limbs - 'major non conformity' at (b)(i) and 'nonconformities' at (b)(ii) - which is preserved as printed and not remarked on. '(b)(iii) When an extension of the Safety Management Certificate is requested for' does not say by whom or on what grounds; it is answered generally, and the honest core of the answer is that no general extension power exists. 'Revision of an entry' is ambiguous between a clerical correction and a change in what was certified, and the answer resolves it by treating both, since the distinction is what carries the marks. 'Invalidated' in limb (c) is read as covering both active withdrawal and a certificate ceasing to be valid by operation of the Code.

**Target answer shape.** Eight numbered sections. A three-column comparison table for limb (a), because a table makes a four-mark distinction countable at a glance, followed by a short prose section on why it matters. A single principle section before the scenarios. Then four short scenario sections, each with its own bulleted action list sized to two marks. Limb (c) as two separate bulleted lists, DOC then SMC, closing on the direction of authority.

**Primary source plan.** Anchor on the ISM Code itself rather than on the implementation guidelines, and do so deliberately. Section 13 supplies the validities, the verification windows and the grounds of withdrawal; the Code's own definitions supply non-conformity and major non-conformity; sections 9 and 12 supply reporting and the Company's ownership of corrective action. The corpus holds the full adopting and amending chain from A.741(18) to MSC.353(92) and records MSC.353(92) as the latest amendment, so the held text is the text operative at this sitting. Deliberately NOT planned: any paragraph number from A.1188(33), and any cross-walk between the two guideline editions.

## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: **compare**. The principal headings of the model answer ARE these steps.

1. **(a) Audit and survey distinguished**
   - A survey examines the ship against prescriptive technical requirements
   - An audit is a systematic and independent examination of the safety management system and its implementation
   - The ship versus the system and the people who run it
   - Different object, method, finding and certificate - deficiency against non-conformity
2. **(a) Why the distinction matters on a real ship**
   - A ship can pass every survey and fail an audit, and the reverse
   - A deficiency is corrected; a non-conformity requires its cause to be found and corrected
   - An audit samples objective evidence, a survey examines
   - Different people, different standards, often the same visit
3. **The position of the recognized organization**
   - The Administration remains responsible for verification and for issuing the DOC and SMC
   - The RO acts on its behalf within a scope specified in the authorization
   - The RO makes the technical judgements verification requires
   - It has no authority to alter the terms on which the flag State certified the ship
   - VERIFICATION the RO decides; TERMS the Administration decides - this settles all four scenarios
4. **(b)(i) Where there is evidence of a major non conformity**
   - Definition: serious threat to personnel or ship or serious risk to the environment, requiring immediate corrective action; includes lack of effective and systematic implementation
   - The certificate must not be issued and an existing certificate must not be endorsed
   - It must be downgraded to a non-conformity by immediate corrective action first
   - Administration and port State informed under the established procedures
   - If not corrected, report to the Administration, which decides on withdrawal
5. **(b)(ii) Where non-conformities are found**
   - Definition: objective evidence indicating non-fulfilment of a specified requirement, without the serious threat
   - The certificate may still be issued or endorsed
   - The COMPANY determines and initiates the corrective action, within an agreed time
   - Corrective actions recorded and their completion verified
   - Failure to correct may itself become a major non-conformity
6. **(b)(iii) Where an extension of the Safety Management Certificate is requested**
   - Neither the Code nor the guidelines contain a general power of extension
   - The RO therefore has nothing of its own to grant
   - It refers the request to the Administration, which alone may decide
   - It advises on the technical conditions and verifies the ship's actual position
   - It does not endorse or annotate the certificate on its own authority
7. **(b)(iv) Where revision of an entry on a certificate is requested**
   - The entries record what the flag State certified - Company, address, ship, ship types
   - The RO may not amend that record on its own authority
   - Administrative change: verify it is genuine, refer, and the certificate is re-issued on the Administration's authority
   - Substantive change - change of Company or added ship type: verification appropriate to the change, and an interim certificate may be the proper instrument
   - In both cases record and report; do not amend the certificate in hand
8. **(c) When the DOC and the SMC may be invalidated**
   - DOC: annual verification not requested, within three months before or after each anniversary date
   - DOC: evidence of major non-conformity; expiry without renewal; five-year validity; the Company ceasing to be the responsible Company
   - SMC: withdrawal of the DOC withdraws ALL associated SMCs and interim certificates - the trigger most often missed
   - SMC: intermediate verification not requested, at least one between the second and third anniversaries
   - SMC: evidence of major non-conformity; expiry without renewal; change of Company or of flag
   - Failure to correct non-conformities may affect validity, and may itself be a major non-conformity
   - The RO establishes the facts and reports; the Administration withdraws
