## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: **explain**. The principal headings of the model answer ARE these steps.

1. **What is being carried, and why grain is regulated apart**
   - Grain is a free-flowing granular cargo that will not stand steeper than its angle of repose
   - Governed by the International Grain Code, MSC.23(59), mandatory through SOLAS chapter VI
   - Grain is expressly OUTSIDE the IMSBC Code - do not answer with groups and TML
   - A shifting cargo is not a liquefying cargo: grain slides once and stays
2. **Why grain shifts, and why the shift does not come back**
   - It settles, conventionally of the order of two per cent by volume, leaving a void under the deckhead
   - The void is what gives the surface somewhere to go
   - Rolling slides the high-side grain down to a new angle of repose
   - IRREVERSIBLE - nothing carries it back up, so the heel is permanent and can compound
   - G moves transversely, generating the heeling moment, and slightly vertically as well
3. **From heeling moment to heeling arm**
   - Approved grain loading information gives an assumed volumetric heeling moment per compartment
   - Filled and trimmed compartments carry a far smaller assumed moment than partly filled ones
   - Heeling arm at zero = total assumed volumetric heeling moment / (stowage factor x displacement)
   - Heeling arm at forty degrees = 0.8 times the value at zero
   - Between them the heeling arm is assumed to vary as a STRAIGHT LINE
4. **Drawing the diagram**
   - Angle of heel horizontally, lever in metres vertically
   - Plot the GZ curve for the loaded condition with GM already free-surface corrected
   - Mark forty degrees and the angle of flooding
   - Plot the heeling arm as a straight line from its zero value to 0.8 of it at forty degrees
   - Mark the first intersection - the angle of heel due to grain shift
   - Shade between the curves from that angle to the least of maximum-difference, forty, and flooding
   - Draw the tangent at the origin to 57.3 degrees to read GM
5. **What the shift does to the curve of statical stability**
   - Effective righting lever reduced at every angle - the ship works to the RESIDUAL curve
   - Equilibrium moves from upright to the crossing angle and stays there
   - Maximum righting lever reduced and displaced outwards
   - Range of stability reduced
   - Dynamical stability - the area - reduced; this is what actually capsizes ships
   - The loss is not recovered, because the list is permanent
   - Initial GM also reduced by the small vertical rise of G
6. **The three minimum intact stability criteria**
   - Angle of heel due to grain shift not more than 12 degrees, or deck edge immersion for ships built on or after 1 January 1994, whichever is the lesser
   - Residual area between the heeling arm and righting arm curves not less than 0.075 metre-radians
   - Measured to the angle of maximum difference, or 40 degrees, or the flooding angle - whichever is LEAST
   - Initial GM, corrected for free surface of liquids in tanks, not less than 0.30 m
   - Cumulative - all three - and satisfied throughout the voyage, not just on departure
7. **Reading the three criteria off the one diagram**
   - Criterion 1 is a point on the horizontal axis - the first crossing
   - Criterion 2 is the shaded area between the two curves
   - Criterion 3 is the slope at the origin, read at 57.3 degrees
   - They are not independent - a larger heeling arm fails 1 and 2 together, and the cure for both is the same
8. **Reducing the heeling moment, and the document that proves it**
   - Fill and trim - the cheapest stability the ship will get
   - Minimise slack compartments; keep any that are unavoidable low
   - Secure the surface - overstowing, strapping or lashing, saucer and bundling
   - Longitudinal divisions and shifting boards halve the transverse travel
   - Plan consumption so the criteria hold all voyage as double bottom weight is burned off
   - Document of Authorization plus approved grain loading information; the master demonstrates compliance BEFORE loading
