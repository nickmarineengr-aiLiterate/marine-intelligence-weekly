## 2. DECOMPOSITION GATE

**Question intent.** Two limbs testing quite different things. Limb (a) tests whether the candidate understands that UNCLOS is a framework rather than a rulebook - that it creates duties and allocates enforcement without setting a single discharge limit. Limb (b) tests precision: five zones, each with a limit, a datum and a character of entitlement, and the real discriminator is whether the candidate can distinguish sovereignty from sovereign rights from control. Five separately weighted definitions is an invitation to be exact rather than discursive.

**Mark allocation.** Printed 6 for limb (a) and 2 for each of five zones, totalling 10 for limb (b) and 16 overall. That is the reverse emphasis from the way this question is often set, where the environmental limb carries the larger share. The answer follows the marks: four compact themes for limb (a), and five self-contained zone answers each built on the same three elements so that each is complete within its two marks and none is padded.

**Examiner expectation.** In limb (a): article 192 stated, the source-by-source structure, the mechanism by which rules are made through the competent international organization with national law no less effective, and the three-way enforcement division. In limb (b): for each zone a limit, the statement that it is measured from baselines, and the correct characterisation of the entitlement - with innocent passage named for the territorial sea, the four exhaustive subjects for the contiguous zone, and the seabed-only nature of the shelf.

**Freshness risk.** LOW - the lowest of any donor-carrying question on the paper. The Convention was concluded in 1982, entered into force in 1994 and is unamended, so the donor required no reversal at all. The only live edge is an instrument that opened for signature inside the sitting month, which is excluded entirely rather than dated.

**Jurisdiction risk.** MEDIUM, but as the subject rather than as a hazard: the whole of limb (b) is an exercise in getting jurisdiction right. The specific errors to avoid are attributing sovereignty to the EEZ, treating the contiguous zone as a general enforcement zone, and blurring the shelf into the EEZ. A second and separate risk is level rather than zone - describing UNCLOS as though it contained operational rules, when it obliges States to make them elsewhere.

**Technical ambiguities.** 'Important highlights' is undefined and could mean the obligations, the enforcement scheme or the institutional machinery; at six marks the answer takes it to mean the four load-bearing features and does not attempt a survey of Part XII. 'Briefly explain' in limb (b) is taken at its word - each zone gets a compact answer rather than an essay - but 'briefly' is not licence to omit the limit or the datum, which are the markable facts. The printed punctuation is inconsistent between the five sub-limbs and is preserved.

**Target answer shape.** Nine numbered sections. Limb (a) as four sections mixing short prose with bulleted lists so each theme is countable. Limb (b) as five short sections, each opening with a prose definition and using a bulleted or numbered list only where the content is genuinely a list - the contiguous zone's two controls, the EEZ's two categories of entitlement, the shelf's three distinguishing features and the six high seas freedoms. ## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: **explain**. The principal headings of the model answer ARE these steps.

1. **(a) The general obligation, and what makes Part XII unusual**
   - Article 192 - States have the obligation to protect and preserve the marine environment
   - A free-standing duty, not confined to any zone - the zones allocate rights, this imposes a duty across all of them
   - The sovereign right to exploit resources is expressly subject to the duty to protect and preserve
   - Article 194 - all measures necessary, from any source, by the best practicable means and in accordance with capabilities
   - Activities under a State's jurisdiction or control must not cause damage by pollution to other States
2. **(a) The sources of pollution, and the rule-making pattern**
   - Land-based sources, seabed activities, dumping, vessels and the atmosphere, plus technologies and alien or new species
   - For vessels, States act through the competent international organization or general diplomatic conference
   - National law must be no less effective than the international rules and standards
   - The rules are to be re-examined from time to time
   - No transferring damage from one area to another or transforming one type of pollution into another
   - UNCLOS contains no discharge limit, and every discharge limit exists because of it
3. **(a) Enforcement - the three-way division**
   - Flag State - ensure compliance by vessels flying its flag, wherever the violation occurs
   - Port State - where the vessel is voluntarily in port, may investigate and institute proceedings for discharges OUTSIDE its own waters
   - Coastal State - proceedings for violations within its territorial sea or EEZ, graded by seriousness and evidence
   - After a maritime casualty, measures beyond the territorial sea proportionate to the actual or threatened damage
4. **(a) Responsibility, liability and the limits**
   - States responsible for fulfilment of their obligations and liable in accordance with international law
   - Recourse must be available for prompt and adequate compensation - the basis of the civil liability conventions
   - Enforcement powers are qualified by the Part's own safeguards
   - Monitoring, environmental assessment, scientific cooperation and assistance to developing States
5. **(b)(i) Territorial sea**
   - A belt adjacent to the coast under the full sovereignty of the coastal State
   - Sovereignty extends to the air space above and to the bed and subsoil
   - Up to 12 nautical miles measured from the baselines
   - Exercised subject to the Convention - in particular the right of innocent passage
6. **(b)(ii) Contiguous zone**
   - No sovereignty - the coastal State may exercise the control necessary
   - To prevent infringement of customs, fiscal, immigration or sanitary laws in its territory or territorial sea
   - And to punish infringement of those laws committed there
   - Not beyond 24 nautical miles from the baselines
   - The four subjects are exhaustive, and the control is referable to the land or territorial sea
7. **(b)(iii) Exclusive economic zone**
   - Beyond and adjacent to the territorial sea, under a specific legal regime, not beyond 200 nautical miles from the baselines
   - Sovereign rights over natural resources living and non-living, of the waters, seabed and subsoil
   - And over other economic activities such as energy from water, currents and winds
   - Jurisdiction over artificial islands and installations, marine scientific research, and protection of the marine environment
   - Freedoms of navigation and overflight preserved for other States
   - Sovereign rights are resource rights, not sovereignty
8. **(b)(iv) Continental shelf**
   - Seabed and subsoil beyond the territorial sea, through the natural prolongation to the outer edge of the continental margin
   - Or 200 nautical miles from the baselines where the margin does not extend that far
   - The margin comprises shelf, slope and rise, and excludes the deep ocean floor
   - Sovereign rights to explore and exploit its natural resources
   - Exclusive - nobody else may without express consent
   - Not dependent on occupation or on any express proclamation
   - Seabed and subsoil only, not the water column
9. **(b)(v) High seas**
   - Defined by exclusion - all parts of the sea outside the EEZ, territorial sea, internal waters and archipelagic waters
   - Open to all States, coastal or land-locked; no valid claim of sovereignty
   - Six freedoms: navigation, overflight, cables and pipelines, artificial islands, fishing, scientific research
   - Exercised with due regard for the interests of other States and for rights relating to the Area
   - A ship there is under the exclusive jurisdiction of her flag State
