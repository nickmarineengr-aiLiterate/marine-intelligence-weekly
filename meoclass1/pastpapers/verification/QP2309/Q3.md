## 2. DECOMPOSITION GATE

**Question intent.** A status question disguised as a knowledge question. The examiner names three classes of measure and asks what is ongoing, so he is testing three things at once: whether the candidate can distinguish the classes, whether he knows the content of each, and - the discriminator - whether he knows which of them are enforceable requirements today and which are a work programme. A candidate who reports all three in the same voice has failed the third test even if every fact is right.

**Mark allocation.** Sixteen marks on the stem, no printed split. Three named classes suggests roughly equal thirds, but that would misallocate: the technical and operational measures are settled and can be stated compactly, while the market-based limb is the newest, the least well known and the one the donor question did not contain. The answer gives sections 3 to 5 to the measures in force, section 6 the single largest share to the market-based limb, and sections 2 and 8 to the status framing that the word 'ongoing' requires.

**Examiner expectation.** The current Strategy named with its resolution and adoption date. The Annex VI measures named with what each actually does, not merely listed as acronyms. The basket given as a technical element plus an economic element rather than as a vague reference to carbon pricing. An explicit statement of what has not yet happened. And the regional European scheme correctly placed as regional and, at this sitting, not yet applying.

**Freshness risk.** HIGH - the highest on this paper. The subject was moving fast at this sitting and almost all published material on it is written for a later one. Three distinct forward traps exist: MEPC 81 and everything after it; the European emissions trading system's application date; and the temptation to write on the revoked 2018 Initial Strategy, which is a backward trap of only two months. The Strategy itself is two months old, which means the answer must be current to within weeks in one direction and clean of a six-month forward window in the other.

**Jurisdiction risk.** MEDIUM. The question says 'at IMO', so regional measures are context and not content. The European scheme must be named once, identified as regional, and dated correctly - at this sitting it is legislated but not yet applying to shipping. The second jurisdictional point is internal to IMO: the Strategy is a policy instrument of the Committee and binds nobody; only amendments to MARPOL Annex VI bind ships. Presenting the Strategy as law is a jurisdiction error, not merely a loose phrase.

**Technical ambiguities.** The stem prints 'and' twice - 'the technical and operational and market-based measures' - which is preserved and not remarked on. 'Ongoing developments' could mean only work in progress, which would exclude the measures already in force; that reading is rejected, because the examiner names the technical and operational classes expressly and they are in force. It is instead taken to mean the current state of the whole programme, which is why the answer states status for every class. 'Invoked on board ships' is taken to include measures that reach the ship through the owner's economics rather than through a survey.

**Target answer shape.** Eight numbered sections. Prose for the Strategy and for the class distinction; bulleted lists for the levels of ambition, the basket, the instrument types, the wider work programme and the closing status list. The status statement is placed last and given its own section so that it cannot be lost in the body. A single closing proposition contrasting enforceable measures with a dated mandate.

**Primary source plan.** Anchor the in-force measures on MARPOL Annex VI chapter 4 regulations 22 to 28, as established in the MIW verification record for QP2402 Q5. Anchor the Strategy's architecture and the basket on MEPC.377(80), as read at source in the QP2402, QP2409 and QP2408 records. Take the three economic instrument types from the QP2408 record, where they are established as analytical categories. Cite MEPC.376(80) for the well-to-wake basis and MEPC.346(78) for the SEEMP guidance. Deliberately NOT planned: any quotation from MEPC.376(80) or MEPC.377(80), whose corpus copies are recorded as blocking for citation; any price or rate; anything from MEPC 81 onwards.

## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: **explain**. The principal headings of the model answer ARE these steps.

1. **The 2023 Strategy - the frame every measure now serves**
   - MEPC.377(80), adopted 7 July 2023, two months before this sitting; revoked the 2018 Initial Strategy
   - A policy instrument, not law - it becomes law through MARPOL Annex VI amendments
   - Vision: phase out as soon as possible, just and equitable transition
   - Four levels of ambition, including at least 40% CO2 per transport work by 2030 and net zero by or around 2050
   - Two indicative checkpoints: at least 20% striving 30% by 2030; at least 70% striving 80% by 2040
   - GHG not CO2 alone; measured well-to-wake
2. **The three classes of measure, and what separates them**
   - Technical acts on the ship as built - verified once by calculation
   - Operational acts on how she is run - verified continuously from reported data
   - Market-based acts on price - mandates nothing, changes the arithmetic
   - The division that decides the answer: in force versus under development
3. **The technical measures in force - EEDI and EEXI**
   - EEDI, regulations 22 and 24 - attained no greater than required, tightening in phases
   - EEXI, regulations 23 and 25 - in force 1 January 2023, extends the design calculation to existing ships
   - Assessed once, at the first annual, intermediate or renewal survey on or after 1 January 2023
   - Commonly met by engine or shaft power limitation, or energy saving devices
   - Both are one-off design compliance - neither restricts subsequent operation
4. **The operational measures in force - SEEMP Part III and the CII**
   - SEEMP, regulation 26, in three Parts; Part III on board on or before 1 January 2023
   - 2022 SEEMP Guidelines, MEPC.346(78), are the operative guidance
   - CII, regulation 28 - ships of 5,000 gross tonnage and above
   - Attained against required CII; rating A to E
   - D for three consecutive years or E for one year triggers a corrective action plan in SEEMP Part III
   - 2023 is the first data year - no rating issued yet
5. **The data foundation the whole scheme rests on**
   - Regulation 27 - fuel oil consumption data collection since calendar year 2019
   - Ships of 5,000 gross tonnage and above; Administration verifies and issues a Statement of Compliance
   - Data transmitted to the IMO Ship Fuel Oil Consumption Database
   - Not a reduction measure, but every carbon-intensity calculation and every future policy decision depends on it
6. **The market-based measures - what is under development**
   - A basket of candidate mid-term measures comprising BOTH elements together
   - Technical element - a goal-based marine fuel standard on phased reduction of GHG intensity
   - Economic element - a maritime GHG emissions pricing mechanism
   - The standard compels the transition; the price funds it and closes the cost gap
   - Types under discussion: levy fixes price, ETS fixes emissions, feebate transfers least but needs a ratcheting benchmark
   - To be finalized and agreed by 2025, well-to-wake, with a comprehensive impact assessment on States
   - Non-discrimination and no more favourable treatment against common but differentiated responsibilities - the negotiation
7. **The rest of the ongoing work programme**
   - Review of the short-term measures to be completed by 1 January 2026
   - MEPC.376(80) life cycle guidelines, adopted alongside the Strategy
   - MSC developing a safety regulatory framework for new technologies and alternative fuels
   - Methane and nitrous oxide, first movers, onshore power, national action plans, research and development
   - Long-term measures deliberately undefined, for the 2028 review
8. **Where the work stands at this sitting**
   - The Strategy is two months old and the Initial Strategy is revoked
   - No mid-term measure adopted - no IMO carbon price and no fuel GHG-intensity standard anywhere
   - The impact assessment is not complete and agreement is conditional on it
   - No CII rating has been issued to any ship
   - The only carbon pricing reaching shipping is regional, and it applies from 1 January 2024 - after this sitting
   - Closing proposition: the technical and operational measures are enforceable today; the market-based measures are a mandate with a date on it
