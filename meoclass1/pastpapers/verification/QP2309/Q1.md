## 2. DECOMPOSITION GATE

**Question intent.** A three-limb question that moves deliberately from principle to institution to the candidate's own ship. The examiner is testing whether a prospective Chief Engineer can distinguish an aspiration from a binding obligation, can attribute the right activity to the right actor, and can then convert both into things he would actually do in his own department. The applied limb is the discriminator: any candidate can argue that diversity is good, and few can say what they would check before she joins.

**Mark allocation.** Sixteen marks printed against the stem, with no mark shown against any limb. The stem asks for a brief introduction plus three limbs, so four blocks of work exist. The answer allots section 1 to the introduction and keeps it short as instructed; section 2 to limb (a) as seven countable propositions; sections 3 and 4 to limb (b), split by actor because the stem names two; and sections 5 to 8 to limb (c), which carries the applied marks and is given the largest share. No internal split is invented and none is asserted.

**Examiner expectation.** The introduction to name the goal and stop. Limb (a) to lead with the rights argument rather than the business case, and to give several separable reasons rather than one. Limb (b) to keep the Organization and the Government of India distinct and to describe what each actually does. Limb (c) to consist of actions a Chief Engineer can take, anchored on obligations that already bind the ship, and to include the recognition that over-protection is itself a form of exclusion.

**Freshness risk.** LOW - The whole regulatory spine predates both this sitting and the donor's. The only moving parts are the 2022 MLC amendments, in force 23 December 2024 and therefore future here, and the Merchant Shipping Act, 2025, commenced 15 March 2026 and far future. The 2023 GHG Strategy example became available on 7 July 2023 and is therefore usable at this sitting but would not have been at the January or April 2023 sittings.

**Jurisdiction risk.** MEDIUM, and it is the reason limb (b) is split. The Organization acts by programme and recommendation and certifies nothing; India acts by statute under the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958 and its subordinate rules. An answer that attributes regulatory force to the Organization, or international obligation to a national circular, has the jurisdiction the wrong way round. The Convention reaches the Indian ship through national implementation, not directly.

**Technical ambiguities.** The stem is not a grammatical sentence, quoting the goal's title without quotation marks; this is preserved and not remarked on in the answer. 'Initiatives' is not defined and could be read as covering welfare programmes, training schemes or legal measures - the answer covers all three rather than choosing. 'Desired impact' is not defined either, and is taken to mean retention and equal professional development rather than any numerical target, because no obligation imposes a proportion.

**Target answer shape.** Eight numbered sections following the printed limb order, with the binding obligations placed as a hinge between limb (b) and limb (c). Limb (a) as a bulleted list of separable propositions. Limb (b) as two parallel bulleted lists, one per actor, each closing with an explicit statement that no instrument number is asserted. Limb (c) as three bulleted lists in the order the problem is actually met - ship, work, people - closing on the proposition that the shore initiatives can only reach the gangway.

**Primary source plan.** Anchor on the Maritime Labour Convention, 2006 - Article III(d) and Article IV for the rights, Standard A3.1 for accommodation, Regulation 5.1.5 with Standard A5.1.5 for complaints and victimization, and Guideline B4.3.1 for harassment as an OSH matter. These were established in the MIW verification record for QP2407 Q1 and are re-checked here for the 2018 amendment set. The 2030 Agenda supplies the framework. The Merchant Shipping Act, 1958 supplies the Indian statutory basis. Deliberately NOT planned: any Assembly resolution number, any DG Shipping instrument number, any workforce statistic - none is held.

## 5. ANSWER ROUTE

Archetype: **explain**. The principal headings of the model answer ARE these steps.

1. **What the goal says, and what equality means on a ship**
   - The 2030 Agenda's seventeen goals; this stem quotes the fifth
   - Access to job, training, promotion and a safe environment must not depend on sex
   - A ship is also a home, so accommodation, sanitation, medical provision and behaviour are in scope
   - Opportunity is not outcome; balance is an aspiration but non-discrimination is law
2. **(a) Why gender balance is required**
   - It is a right, not a favour - the sufficient reason, given first
   - The industry needs the people; a shrinking officer pool
   - A wider pool raises quality of selection
   - Diversity of perspective changes how risk is managed - never a claim about the qualities of a sex
   - Retention, commercial expectation, and better standards for everyone
   - Quote no statistic
3. **(b) What the International Maritime Organization does**
   - Programme work, not regulation - no convention, no certificate
   - Women in maritime programme under technical cooperation
   - Regional women in maritime associations
   - Fellowships and reserved places at the Organization's academic institutions
   - A World Maritime Day theme
   - Gender parity written into ordinary technical programmes - the 2023 GHG Strategy appendix example, adopted 7 July 2023
   - Name no resolution number
4. **(b) What the Government of India does**
   - Directorate General of Shipping under the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958
   - Admission to approved training on the same terms
   - No restriction on certification or service by sex
   - Institute approval conditions are the practical lever
   - Training-berth availability is the real obstacle
   - Convention obligations carried into national law
   - Name no circular number
5. **What already binds the ship, before any initiative**
   - Article IV - safe and secure workplace, decent living conditions
   - Standard A3.1 - individual sleeping room, separate sanitary facilities
   - Regulation 5.1.5 and Standard A5.1.5 - complaint procedure, impartial adviser, no victimization
   - Guideline B4.3.1 - harassment as an occupational safety and health matter
6. **(c) As Chief Engineer - the physical ship**
   - Cabin allocated and lockable, private secure sanitary facilities - confirmed before she joins
   - Protective equipment stocked in the full range of sizes
   - Lifting aids and correct tooling as routine
   - Medical provision matched to the crew carried
   - Safety equipment sizing proved in drills
7. **(c) As Chief Engineer - the allocation of work**
   - Allocate by rank and competence and nothing else
   - Protecting her from heavy or dirty work is exclusion, not consideration
   - Same watch and project responsibility, same standard
   - Appraise honestly; withheld feedback ends careers quietly
   - Mentor deliberately; include her in departmental decisions
   - Read the hours-of-rest records for workload distortion
8. **(c) As Chief Engineer - behaviour, complaints and accountability**
   - Set the standard at the first departmental meeting
   - Correct the first incident immediately - a remark uncorrected is a permission
   - Make the complaint route real and known
   - Protect the complainant; victimization is prohibited
   - Treat harassment as occupational safety and health
   - Record under the SMS so the Company sees the pattern
   - Brief the reliefs; a standard that lapses is not a standard
