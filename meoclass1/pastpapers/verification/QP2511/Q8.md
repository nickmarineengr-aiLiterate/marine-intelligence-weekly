## The subtlety: the conclusion survived, the reasoning did not

This is the part worth recording, because it is the hardest defect class to see and a naive
date-strip would have missed it entirely.

**India's recycling law is identical at both sittings.** The **Recycling of Ships Act, 2019** and the
**Recycling of Ships Rules, 2019** are separate legislation with their own machinery; neither general
Act is their source. So the donor's *conclusion* — that the 2019 Act governs — is **correct at this
sitting too**.

What is wrong is the **reason**:

| | Donor's reasoning (March 2026) | **This sitting** |
|---|---|---|
| Why does the 2019 Act govern? | Because s.324(1) of the 2025 Act repeals only the 1958 Act and the Coasting Vessels Act 1838 | Because the 2019 Act is the operative recycling statute, and the general Act alongside it is the **1958** Act — the 2025 Act having **not commenced** |

Inherited unchanged, the answer would have reached a right conclusion through a **premature** premise:
it would have asserted the effect of a repeal provision that had not yet taken effect. Nothing
downstream catches that — the sentence is internally coherent, confidently written, and about the
right statute. It is `TEMPORAL_AND_DONOR_VERIFICATION_PROTOCOL.md` §8 in its exact form.

**Also removed as future material:** the donor's reverify note that *"the Merchant Shipping Rules 2026
were still in draft"*. Those Rules did not exist in any form at this sitting.

## Reasoning recorded

- **The Convention is answered as in force.** Five months is enough; the obligations were operative.
- **The facility obligations are given equal weight to the ship obligations.** This is one of the very
  few IMO instruments that reaches ashore and regulates a yard, and candidates routinely omit half the
  Convention.
- **"Benefit or be impacted" is read as both sides plus a judgement**, not as a choice. The archetype
  is `evaluate` and evaluation is marked on whether a reasoned conclusion actually appears.
- **The Convention does not ban beaching**, and compliance is not EU acceptance. Kept as the stated
  major trap — it is the most overstated proposition in this topic.
- **No percentage share of world recycling is asserted.** Published figures differ widely; Alang-Sosiya
  is named instead. An unverifiable quantity is omitted, not quoted.

## LAPTOP CORRECTION -- Hong Kong Convention source state and Article 3.3 scope

Found during Desktop Batch 1 integration review, and proved against the corpus
copy of the Convention at `official-sources/HONGKONG_CONVENTION.pdf` read
directly: 47 pages, complete, ARTICLE 17 present verbatim.

**1. False provenance, corrected.** This record previously declared that *"MIW
holds no licensed copy of the Hong Kong Convention text"* and that the Article
and appendix content rested on IMO briefing material and classification-society
guidance alone. **MIW does hold the complete text.** The source state and the
verification confidence are corrected, and a PRIMARY source entry is added. The
Party count and world-tonnage figures still rest on the IMO briefing, which
remains the correct source for them because the Convention text does not state
them.

**2. Wrong scope, corrected.** The answer stated application as *"500 gross
tonnage and above on international voyages"*. Article 3.3 reads:

> This Convention shall not apply to ships of less than 500 GT or to ships
> operating throughout their life only in waters subject to the sovereignty or
> jurisdiction of the State whose flag the ship is entitled to fly.

The exclusion is **purely domestic operation for the whole life of the ship**,
not "international voyages". The difference is real: a ship trading domestically
today, which has traded internationally at some point in its life, is still
caught. Corrected in the model answer, the answer route, the recall card and the
critical-numbers list, not only in the sources.

**Nothing temporal was altered.** The Convention entered into force 26 June 2025
and is in force at this sitting; that is unchanged, as is the contrast this
answer draws with the October 2024 sitting at which it was adopted but not yet
in force. This is a source-state and scope correction only.
