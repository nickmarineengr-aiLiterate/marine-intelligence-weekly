## Three things deliberately NOT asserted

This is the substance of the verification work on this question, and each omission is a decision.

**1. No casualty statistic.** IMO's own material describes *a spate of losses* and **does not quantify
them** — no ships-lost figure, no lives-lost figure. Published figures in secondary sources differ
substantially. The answer **characterises** the record — sudden loss, often without a distress
message, frequently with the entire crew, in weather similar ships had survived — and **counts
nothing**. An unverifiable quantity is omitted, not quoted (`PASTPAPER_PRODUCTION_PROTOCOL.md` §2.1).
The model answer says so to the candidate in an italic aside, so the omission reads as discipline
rather than ignorance.

**2. No date for the *Derbyshire*.** The corpus establishes only that IMO's FSA studies **followed the
re-opened investigation** and that **MSC 76 sat in December 2002**. The answer says **no more than
that**. A summary of the IMO page produced during research asserted a *1998* sinking date, which is
wrong; it was not used. **This is exactly why a paraphrase of a source is not the source.**

**3. No regulation number that was not separately verified.** MIW holds no licensed copy of SOLAS.
Numbers are asserted for **XII/6, XII/11, XII/12 and XII/13** only — the first two verified in MIW
records, the last two on IMO's own material read this session. **Every other chapter XII provision is
cited by name and effect**: the damage stability requirement, the structural strength requirement, the
survey and maintenance requirement, the cargo density declaration, and the restriction on sailing
with a hold empty. `unresolved` records this explicitly, and `reverify_before_publication` flags it
for correction if a licensed SOLAS is ever acquired.

## Reasoning recorded

- **The two halves are matched to each other, not listed separately.** The failure chain is set out
  first — **frame → shell → hold → bulkhead → bow** — and chapter XII is then shown cutting it link by
  link. The stem's word *"contribute"* is a **causal claim**, and an answer that lists causes and then
  separately lists regulations has written two answers rather than one.
- **The contribution is argued as defence in depth** — *prevent, contain, detect, respond, verify* —
  so that the point is that **the chain must now break in several places at once**.
- **The limits are stated.** The strongest structural and stability provisions are largely
  **prospective and threshold-limited**; the chapter cannot compel good maintenance, only inspect for
  its absence; and it does not reach the **commercial pressure** that produced the condition. What it
  removed was the possibility of a ship being lost **because nobody was required to look**.
- **Double-side skin is stated as an ALTERNATIVE**, which is what IMO's own material says. Reversing
  the mandatory/alternative distinction is a marked error, and it is flagged for re-verification.
- **The failure mechanism is engineering explanation, and is labelled as such.** No regulation
  prescribes why a side shell frame fatigues. The **high-tensile steel** argument — thinner plate for
  the same strength, so the same corrosion consumes a larger *proportion* of the margin, with fatigue
  not improving in proportion to yield — is classification-society-level material of the period,
  recorded at that provenance class.
