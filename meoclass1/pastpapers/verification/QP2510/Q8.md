## 3. What was REUSED after re-verification

The complete diagnostic and reporting sequence, in order, because the order **is** the answer:

1. **Bridge / engine-room communication** — establish what the bridge did, what the telegraph shows,
   and what the engine actually did. Inform the bridge of the loss of remote control at once.
2. **Transfer of control** — take control at the engine-room console, then **local** if necessary.
3. **Permissives and interlocks** — the safest and commonest cause. Confirm the control-position
   selector, the safety-system status and any active shutdown or slowdown before touching anything.
4. **Starting air** — pressure, the automatic valve, the distributor, the interlocks on the turning
   gear and the air-start line.
5. **Fuel** — supply pressure, viscosity and temperature, the fuel-rack linkage and its actuator.
6. **The control system itself** — control air pressure, governor and actuator, the remote-control
   electronics, the telegraph loop and its feedback.
7. **Mechanical readiness** — turning gear disengaged, shafting, and the astern function.
8. **Controlled restoration and test**, with the bridge informed and in a safe situation.
9. **Record and root cause**, and the report.

**The report structure is reused unchanged** — summary, sequence of events, damage, cause,
temporary measures instituted, permanent measures recommended — together with the two disciplines
the donor rightly emphasises: **separate fact from opinion and label the opinion as opinion**, and
**do not use the first report to allocate blame** before the movement recorder has been read. The
attachment list (engine movement and telegraph logger, alarm history, control fault log, VDR, bell
book, logs, maintenance records) is carried.

## 4. The safety discipline was preserved exactly

Recorded here because it is the proposition most at risk from careless "tidying":

> **No protective trip is reset and no interlock bypassed before the cause is established.**
> Resetting destroys the evidence and may re-create the hazard.

**No blanket reset or bypass instruction was introduced.** The donor's latent-defect hypothesis —
a fault in the remote control or control air system manifesting only under the rapid, repeated
manoeuvring of a dock approach, on a system whose astern function had not recently been proven under
load — is carried unchanged, as a hypothesis to be tested rather than a conclusion.
