## 2. **THE CHANGE — five elements to six, and Govern comes first**

**Rev.3 annex 3.5, verbatim heading text:**

| | Element | Rev.3 definition (opening words) |
|---|---|---|
| .1 | **GOVERN** | *"Establish and monitor risk management strategy, expectations and policies. Define personnel roles and responsibilities for cyber risk management. Ensure business continuity, such as backup management and disaster recovery, and crisis management."* |
| .2 | Identify | *"Determine the current cyber risk to ships and ship/port interfaces."* |
| .3 | Protect | *"Implement risk control processes and measures to protect CBSs…"* |
| .4 | Detect | *"Develop, implement and practise activities necessary to detect a…"* |
| .5 | Respond | *"Develop, implement and practise activities and plans to provide…"* |
| .6 | Recover | *"Identify and implement measures to restore onboard CBS including…"* |

**Rev.2 annex 3.5 listed exactly five: `.1 Identify · .2 Protect · .3 Detect · .4 Respond ·
.5 Recover`.** Verified by the same extraction against the Rev.2 PDF.

**Govern's two named sub-controls** (Rev.3 3.5.1.1–.2):

1. **Designate a person or entity accountable** for the planning, resourcing and execution of
   cybersecurity activities.
2. **Ensure that person or entity has the necessary authority and support**, and sufficient
   knowledge and expertise in cyber risk management.

**Preserved from Rev.2 and still true:** the elements are **not sequential** — all should be
concurrent and continuous and incorporated into a risk-management framework. **New in Rev.3:** the
controls listed under each element are the **minimum** controls; more may be needed on the risk
evaluation.

## 3. Limb (A) — the system list grew from eight to nine, and moved

Limb (A) asks the candidate to *"name all the on-board systems which are prone of cyber risks due to
vulnerabilities created by accessing, interconnecting or networking of such systems"* — wording taken
straight from the circular, so the answer must track the current edition exactly.

| Rev.2 annex **2.1.1** — eight | Rev.3 annex **2.2.1** — nine |
|---|---|
| .1 Bridge systems | .1 Bridge systems **(such as navigation systems, ship safety systems, communications systems, etc.)** |
| .2 Cargo handling and management systems | .2 **Cargo, bunkering, lubrication, ballast and other pumping** handling and management systems |
| .3 Propulsion and machinery management and power control systems | .3 Propulsion, **fuel** and machinery management and power control systems |
| .4 Access control systems | .4 **Security,** access control **and surveillance** systems |
| .5 Passenger servicing and management systems | .5 Passenger **and crew** servicing and management systems |
| .6 Passenger facing public networks | .6 Passenger, **crew and subcontracted service personnel** facing public networks |
| .7 Administrative and crew welfare systems | .7 Administrative and crew welfare systems |
| .8 **Communication systems** | *(folded into .1)* |
| — | .8 **Ship-port interfaces** |
| — | .9 **Ship-to-shore integrated systems (e.g. remote control systems / Maritime Autonomous Surface Ships)** |

**This is a restructure, not an append.** Communication systems ceased to be a separate head and
became an example under bridge systems; six of the seven surviving heads were widened; two genuinely
new heads appeared. The donor's memory device — *"walk the ship from forward to aft and outward"* —
still works, but the list it walks is different.

## 4. Limb (D) — IT and OT are now formally DEFINED

**Rev.2 annex 2.1.2** described the distinction loosely: IT *"may be thought of as focusing on the
use of data as information"*, OT *"on the use of data to control or monitor physical processes"*.

**Rev.3 promotes both to defined terms at annex 2.1**, with worked examples the candidate can quote:

> **Information Technology (IT)** — CBSs focused on the use of data as information, including
> software, hardware and communication technologies *(e.g. commercial information, or data about the
> crew, such as salaries, certificates)*.
>
> **Operational technology (OT)** — CBSs focused on the use of data to control or monitor physical
> processes *(e.g. the main engine's oil temperature levels, which are forwarded to the control
> room)*.

**The OT example is a marine-engineering example.** For an Engineering Management paper that is the
single most useful sentence in the circular, and limb (D) expressly asks for *"suitable examples"*.

**Rev.3 annex 2.2.2 adds a duty the donor could not have stated:** OT systems **should be segmented
from IT systems, protected from Internet-facing systems, and have appropriate protection tools.**

**Also new — Computer Based System (CBS)** is defined at 2.1 and becomes the organising concept of
the whole circular: *"a programmable electronic device, or interoperable set of programmable
electronic devices… CBSs on board include IT and OT systems."*

## 5. Limb (C) — importance of cyber security: largely stable, one addition

The discursive limb. The donor's reasoning stands. Rev.3 supplies two supports worth adding:

- **2.2.5** — where vulnerabilities are exposed, there are implications for **confidentiality,
  integrity and availability** of data *and* for the safety and security of the ship, particularly
  where **bridge navigation, main propulsion or cargo on/off-loading systems** are compromised.
- **2.2.6** — effective management must consider **third-party vendors, embedded systems and the
  software and hardware supply chains**, and CBS **maintenance devices**.

**MSC.428(98) is unchanged** and remains the reason a recommendatory circular becomes a real
obligation: cyber risks must be addressed in the safety management system. That route step reuses
unaltered.
