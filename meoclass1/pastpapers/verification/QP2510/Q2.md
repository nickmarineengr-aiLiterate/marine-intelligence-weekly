## 4. **THE CENTRAL FINDING — the carriage regime changed in four material respects**

The preamble is explicit that the rules are enacted *"as so amended and as set out **with
modifications** in the Schedule"*. Those modifications are not cosmetic.

### 4.1 "Goods" now INCLUDES live animals and deck cargo — **the donor's proposition is inverted**

**Schedule Article I(d), verbatim:**

> *"'goods' includes any property **including live animals** as well as containers, pallets or
> similar articles of transport or packaging supplied by the consignor, **irrespective of whether
> such property is to be or is carried on or under deck**."*

Hague-Visby Article I(c) does the opposite — it excludes live animals and deck cargo stated as such
and so carried. This is a **Hamburg-Rules-style definition** grafted onto a Hague-Visby Schedule,
and it widens the compulsory regime to two categories previously outside it.

> **The donor record carries as a P1 primary claim:** *"'Goods' excludes live animals and deck cargo
> stated as such and so carried — Art I(c)."* **True for March 2024. FALSE for October 2025.**
> This single line is the reason this question could not be adapted without reading the Act.

### 4.2 Article IV bis is **not reproduced**

The Schedule runs **Articles I, II, III, IV, V, VI, VII, VIII, IX**. A regex over the full extracted
text returns **zero** occurrences of `bis`.

The Visby Article IV bis — defences and limits apply whether the claim is framed in contract or
tort; servants and agents may invoke them; independent contractors may not — **is absent from the
enacted Schedule**. The donor cites *"Article IV bis rules 1–3"* as a P1 claim. It has no referent
in the operative Indian text at this sitting.

### 4.3 A three-month judicial extension of the one-year time bar

**Article III(6)(c) proviso, verbatim:**

> *"Provided that a suit may be brought after the expiry of the period of one year referred to in
> this sub-paragraph within a further period of not more than **three months** as allowed by the
> court."*

Hague-Visby allows extension **only by agreement of the parties after the cause of action arose**.
The Indian Schedule adds a discretionary judicial extension on top.

### 4.4 Article I and Article IV(5) are **re-lettered throughout**

Every Article I citation in the donor answer points at the wrong sub-clause for this sitting.

| Subject | Hague-Visby | **2025 Schedule** |
|---|---|---|
| carriage of goods — loading to discharge | I(e) | **I(a)** |
| carrier — owner **or charterer** | I(a) | **I(b)** |
| contract of carriage — bill or similar document of title; charterparty bills once they regulate carrier and holder | I(b) | **I(c)** |
| goods | I(c) | **I(d)** |
| ship | I(d) | **I(e)** |
| limit lost for intent or recklessness with knowledge | IV r.5(e) | **IV(5)(c)** |

**Indian enacting provisions worth citing** (these are the vehicle, and limb (c) asks for laws):

- **s.3** — the applicable rules have effect for carriage **from any port in India to any other
  port, whether in or outside India**. **Outward voyages only** — unchanged in effect from 1925.
- **s.4** — no implied absolute undertaking of seaworthiness.
- **s.5** — every bill of lading issued in India must contain an **express statement** that it takes
  effect subject to the applicable rules (the statutory clause paramount).
- **s.7** — third-party-ascertained bulk weight is not prima facie evidence against the carrier.
- **s.9** — Central Government may amend the Schedule by notification.

**Bills of Lading Act 2025 — re-enactment confirmed section by section:**

| 2025 | Subject | 1856 equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| **s.2** | rights of suit transferred to and vested in consignee/endorsee, subject to the same liabilities, as if the contract had been made with them | s.1 |
| **s.3** | stoppage in transit, freight claims against the original shipper, and consignee/endorsee liability preserved | s.2 |
| **s.4(1)** | bill in the hands of a consignee/endorsee for value is **conclusive evidence of shipment** against the master or signer, with a fraud proviso | s.3 |
| **s.4(2)** | not so where the holder had **actual notice** the goods were not laden | — |

**The donor's mitigating finding is confirmed from primary text: BoLA 2025 re-enacts, it does not
reform.** For limb (c)'s transfer-of-rights content the change really is a citation update.
