## Sitting-date finding

**No statutory regression was required, and establishing that is the finding.** Every instrument
this answer relies on was already in force in August 2025:

| Instrument | Status at this sitting |
|---|---|
| **UNCLOS** | in force; stable at both sittings |
| **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita 2023**, s.1(5) | in force since **1 July 2024** - over a year before this paper. Citing the **Indian Penal Code** was *already* an error at this sitting. |
| **Merchant Shipping Act 1958**, ss.358, 359, 452 | in force, and remained so until **15 March 2026** - seven months after this paper |
| Merchant Shipping Act 2025 | assented **18 August 2025**, in the month of this sitting, but **not commenced**. Did not apply. See Q5. |

The temporal flag on this question in the intake spec is `INDIAN STATUTE BOUNDARY / HIGH`. The flag
was correct to raise, and the review resolves it: the boundary lies **after** this sitting, so the
1958 Act governs, exactly as it did for the February 2026 donor. The risk was never that the answer
needed different law - it was that a donor or any post-March-2026 source would answer on the 2025 Act.

## Reasoning recorded

- **The jurisdictional point leads limb (b), not the action list.** The stem asks "which laws will
  apply" *before* "what actions will be taken", and answering in that order is worth more than any
  amount of procedural detail.
- **Both flags are answered.** The paper does not state the flag; choosing one would be assuming
  away the test. This is recorded in the gate as a technical ambiguity, not as an unresolved.
- **Search and rescue is kept off the jurisdiction axis.** SAR is owed regardless of flag under
  Art 98 and the SAR Convention. Merging it into the jurisdiction analysis is the error that makes
  candidates conclude India investigates a foreign ship.
