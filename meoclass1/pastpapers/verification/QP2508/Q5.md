**Question.** The meaning of "Unseaworthy vessels" under the Merchant Shipping Act (MSA), 1958, as
amended; the difference between "Unseaworthy ships" and "Unsafe ships"; and the obligations of
owners to crew with respect to seaworthiness. (16)

## Sitting-date finding

**The Merchant Shipping Act 1958 was the operative Indian statute throughout August 2025 and
remained so until 15 March 2026.** The answer is given on the 1958 Act, and that is not a
concession - it is the only correct basis.

The seam is unusually close at this sitting and is worth stating precisely:

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **August 2025** | **<- this examination.** The Merchant Shipping Act 1958 in force. |
| **18 August 2025** | The **Merchant Shipping Act, 2025** (**Act No. 24 of 2025**) receives the assent of the President. Gazette of India, Extraordinary, Part II Section 1, No. 29. |
| 10 March 2026 | **S.O. 1244(E)**, Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways, appoints 15 March 2026 for commencement. |
| 15 March 2026 | The 2025 Act commences. **Section 324(1)** repeals the 1958 Act (except Part XIV but not including section 411A therein) and the Coasting Vessels Act, 1838. |

**Assent is not commencement.** The 2025 Act received assent in the very month of this examination
and still did not become law for another seven months. That is the sitting-specific teaching point
this paper makes better than any other in the set, and it has been written into the answer and the
study guide rather than left implicit.

The source copy does not record the calendar day of the sitting, so it is unknown whether the paper
was sat before or after 18 August. **It does not matter**: on every day of that month the 1958 Act
governed.

## Sources actually read

| **MSA 1958 s.334**, via MIW record `verification/QP2602/Q5.md` (read in the Act; cross-checked against a second independent reproduction) | P1 | The description of unseaworthiness - materials, construction, master's qualifications, number/description/qualifications of crew including officers, weight/description/stowage of cargo and ballast, condition of hull, equipment, boilers and machinery - "not such as to render her **in every respect fit for the proposed voyage or service**". Offence of sending or attempting to send an Indian ship to sea from an Indian port "in such an unseaworthy state that the **life of any person is likely to be thereby endangered**"; defences of "**used all reasonable means**" and "under the circumstances, **reasonable and justifiable**"; s.334(2) master who **knowingly** takes her to sea, second defence only; s.334(3) evidence; **s.334(4)** prosecution only by or with **Central Government** consent. |
| **MSA 1958 s.335**, same record | P1 | Implied into every contract of service, express or implied, and every contract of apprenticeship, "**notwithstanding any agreement to the contrary**", binding owner, master and "every agent charged with the loading ... or the preparing thereof for sea, or the sending thereof to sea", to "use **all reasonable means to ensure the seaworthiness** of such ship for the voyage **at the time when such voyage commences**, and to **keep her in a seaworthy state during the voyage**". s.335(2) Central Government may arrange a survey of hull, equipment or machinery. |
| **MSA 1958 s.336**, same record | P1 | Unsafe ship - "by reason of the defective condition of her hull, equipment or machinery, or by reason of overloading or improper loading, unfit to proceed to sea **without serious danger to human life**, having regard to the nature of the service for which she is intended".
