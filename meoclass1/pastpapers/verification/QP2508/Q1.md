**Question.** (a) Three main principles of LLMC 1976 and its linkage with two other IMO liability and
compensation conventions. (5) (b) The liabilities covered under LLMC 1976. (5) (c) The principles of
the Salvage Convention 1989; the rationale for extending limitation to salvors; and the current
limit applicable to them. (6)

## Sitting-date finding: the stability argument

**No regression was required, and the reason is recorded rather than assumed.**

The donor verified, as at February 2026, that the LLMC limits were last raised with effect from
**8 June 2015** (2012 amendments to the 1996 Protocol) and that the tacit acceptance machinery had
not been used since; and that the Salvage Convention 1989 is unamended.

> **A finding that an instrument was unchanged as at a LATER date necessarily covers every earlier
> date back to the last change.** August 2025 lies between 8 June 2015 and February 2026, so the
> donor's own verification establishes the position at this sitting *a fortiori*.

This is the one direction in which a later verification is safely reusable, and it is worth stating
explicitly because the general rule in this series runs the other way: currency corrections made for
a later sitting must be **reversed**, not inherited. Here nothing was corrected for the later
sitting, so there is nothing to reverse.

The single date-sensitive element is **India's implementing vehicle**: LLMC is given effect through
**Part XA of the Merchant Shipping Act 1958**, which was in force at this sitting and until
15 March 2026. The donor's sentence was correct but bare; here it is made explicit and cross-linked
to Q5, where the statutory boundary is worked through.

## Reuse handling - the three steps

**1. Question delta.** Identical wording, including the printed comma splice in limb (c),
"as established by the IMO, Furthermore, examine...".

**2. Sitting-date regression.** None required on the substance - see above. The Indian
implementation sentence was rewritten to state the Act in force on the day.

**3. Assembled-answer sweep.** Full read. Checked and clear of: any suggestion that the limits had
changed, any reference to the Merchant Shipping Act 2025, and any "current" claim that is not tied
to an identified instrument. The answer deliberately retains the warning that a State bound only by
the unamended 1976 Convention applies **lower** figures, so the figure is never stated free-floating.

## Reasoning recorded

- **Two headings per printed limb.** The paper prints 5 + 5 + 6 and the examiner marks limb by limb,
  so the route is built as three pairs rather than six free-standing steps.
- **The figure is treated as the object of limb (c).** The stem says "specify the current liability
  limit", so the answer runs the full chain - Art 6(4) deemed 1,500 tons, therefore the band not
  exceeding 2,000 GT, therefore 3.02m / 1.51m SDR under the amended Protocol - and warns that the
  instrument binding the State must be identified first.
- **Salvage appears on both sides and is kept apart.** It is an **excepted claim** under Art 3 and
  salvors are **persons entitled to limit** under Art 1. Stated together without separation the
  answer reads as self-contradictory; the gate records this as a technical ambiguity.

## Flags

- **B_CURRENCY_CHECK** - the SDR figures. The 1996 Protocol carries tacit acceptance for raising
  amounts; verified unchanged as at February 2026, which covers this sitting, but re-check before
  publication and at each new sitting. Source is the IMO convention page, not the Protocol text.
- **C_ACCEPTED_LIMITATION** - India's Part XA implementation, from practitioner commentary and not
  read in the statute; and Part XA sat in an Act repealed from 15 March 2026.
