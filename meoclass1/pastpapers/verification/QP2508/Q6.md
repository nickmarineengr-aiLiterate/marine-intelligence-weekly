### Rule VII, verbatim

> "Damage caused to any machinery and boilers of a ship which is **ashore and in a position of
> peril**, in **endeavouring to refloat**, shall be allowed in general average when shown to have
> arisen from an **actual intention to float the ship for the common safety at the risk of such
> damage**; but where a ship is **afloat** no loss or damage caused by working the propelling
> machinery and boilers shall **in any circumstances** be allowed as general average."

This is a rule written for precisely the facts in the question, which is why limb (b) is answerable
with confidence rather than by analogy.

## The four conditions, and which one is actually in issue

1. **Ashore** - met on the facts.
2. **In a position of peril** - **must be established, not assumed.** A vessel lying safely aground
   on soft mud in fine weather awaiting the tide is not in peril. **This is the condition on which
   such a claim actually turns**, and an answer that asserts it rather than testing it has missed
   the analytical mark.
3. **An actual intention to float her for the common safety** - working ahead and astern to shake
   her loose is exactly that. The examiner's own wording supplies it.
4. **At the risk of such damage** - overloading bearings by working an engine hard against a
   grounded hull is a known consequence, and knowingly accepting it is what converts damage into a
   **sacrifice**.

**The converse is half the marks.** Where the ship is **afloat**, Rule VII prohibits allowance "in
any circumstances". Identical bearing damage would then be **particular average** - the owner's own
loss. Same damage, opposite answer, decided by the ship's condition and the master's purpose.
