## The wrong-edition hazard, carried forward deliberately

The donor records a **VERIFIED CORRECTION** that is more valuable than most of its content, and it
is reproduced here in full force:

> An IMO **STCW.6/Circ.1** copy of section A-VIII/1 was consulted first and gives a five-paragraph
> **pre-Manila** text: "**70 hours** of rest ... each seven day period", and **no 14-hour interval
> provision at all**. That is the **1995** Code, not the current one.

It is freely available, on an official domain, and superseded. A candidate revising from it writes
**70** where **77** is required and omits the 14-hour rule entirely. The study guide gives this its
own section, because the general lesson - *ask which edition, not whether it is official* - is the
same failure mode this series has now hit three times (the pre-Manila STCW text, a "not yet in
force" secondary source on the MS Act 2025, and pre-October-2025 GHG material).

The Manila figures are therefore taken from a **competent-authority restatement**, not from that
circular: **10** hours in any 24; **77** hours in any 7 days; no more than **two** daily periods, one
of at least **6** hours; interval between consecutive rests not exceeding **14** hours; authorised
exceptions not below **70** hours weekly, not more than **two consecutive weeks**, daily rest then in
no more than **three** periods.

## Reasoning recorded

- **Limb A is not a fatigue question.** Fatigue belongs in A only as *fitness for duty*; the rest of
  A is competence, teamwork and company responsibility. If fatigue fills limb A, limb B has nothing
  new to say and eight marks evaporate. The route enforces the split.
- **Limb B must name the circular.** "The IMO guidance" is a specific document; naming it and its
  predecessor is most of the limb.
- **Step 6 is the management-level judgement**: the mandatory formula is a floor, and an
  always-exactly-compliant rest record is a warning rather than a reassurance. That is the
  difference between a Class I answer and a recitation of numbers.
