**Answer route:**

1. **The three cost families, and what distinguishes them** - Separated by what makes the money go out, not by size; Capital - owning; operating - keeping her fit to trade; voyage - performing the voyage; The trigger test is what shows where leverage exists
2. **What falls into each family** - Capital - purchase price, debt service, depreciation, required return; Operating - crew, stores, spares, maintenance, drydocking, insurance, administration; Bunkers, port dues, canal dues, pilotage and towage are VOYAGE costs
3. **Where the Chief Engineer has leverage - and where he has none** - Capital - effectively no short-run influence; Voyage - the largest lever, because fuel dominates and fuel follows condition; Operating - shared with the office
4. **Optimising voyage cost** - Hull and propeller condition is usually the largest recoverable loss; Machinery condition, correct operation for the load, fuel management, auxiliary load; Efficiency is now a compliance outcome - and there is no carbon price in the account at this date
5. **Optimising operating cost** - Choose the right maintenance strategy per item rather than one for all; Off-hire avoidance dominates the arithmetic; The drydock specification is the largest single event he controls
6. **Inventory control - classifying the stock** - Not every item deserves the same control; ABC by value, VED by criticality, FSN by movement, SDE by procurement difficulty; Combine them - the ABC-VED matrix, with criticality anchored in element 10.3
7. **Inventory control - ordering, and the limits of just-in-time at sea** - Economic order quantity, reorder level and safety stock; Lead time is the hard variable because the ship moves; Just-in-time is wrong for critical spares
