**Answer route:**

1. **What a classification society is, and its two distinct roles** _(a))_ - Publishes rules, assigns and maintains class - private and contractual; Statutory work is delegated, acting as a recognized organization under SOLAS XI-1/1 and the RO Code; The Administration transfers the work, not the responsibility
2. **How class rules are made** _(a))_ - Service experience and defect returns from the surveyed fleet; casualty feedback; Research and direct calculation - finite element analysis, fatigue assessment; Technical committees, unified requirements, and the common structural rules
3. **What the rules actually cover** _(a))_ - Scantlings, hull girder strength, fatigue, corrosion additions, close-up survey; Propulsion, shafting, boilers, piping, steering gear, type approval; Generation and distribution, discrimination, essential and emergency services, high voltage, hazardous areas
4. **Implementation through the ship's life** _(a))_ - Plan and type approval at design; material, welding, testing and trials at construction; In service - annual, intermediate, renewal, docking, tailshaft and boiler surveys; Continuous survey and planned maintenance schemes; condition of class, suspension and withdrawal
5. **Why compliance is now mentioned in SOLAS chapter II-1** _(a))_ - SOLAS never contained scantling rules - class was indispensable but legally optional; Regulation II-1/3-1 requires ships to be designed, constructed AND MAINTAINED to recognized class rules, or equivalent national standards; Loss of class became a SOLAS non-compliance, inspectable by port State control; II-1/3-10 goes further and audits the rules themselves
6. **The harmonized system, and where these surveys sit** _(b))_ - Introduced by the 1988 SOLAS and Load Lines Protocols, extended to the MARPOL Annexes; Common five-year cycle, common anniversary dates, three-month window, combinable surveys; The IMO survey guidelines are recommendatory; a revision was adopted within the month of this sitting and no side is asserted
7. **Annual versus periodical - the difference** _(b))_ - Annual applies to the construction, equipment, radio and MARPOL certificates at every anniversary; Periodical applies ONLY to safety equipment and safety radio; Equipment at the second or third anniversary; radio at EVERY anniversary, replacing the annual
8. **Annual versus periodical - the relevance** _(b))_ - A five-year certificate needs mid-cycle checkpoints to stay credible; Equipment is consumed in a way structure is not; distress communication cannot degrade unnoticed; The graded ladder matches survey depth to deterioration rate, and harmonisation makes it practical in one attendance
