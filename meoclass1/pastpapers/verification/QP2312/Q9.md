**Answer route:**

1. **What FSA is, and its objectives** - A structured and systematic methodology using risk analysis and cost-benefit assessment; A tool for making RULES, not for running a ship - the framing distinction; Objectives: rational process, evaluate or compare regulations, demonstrate benefit against cost, aid consensus
2. **The characteristics that define it** - Proactive, not reactive - it argues before the casualty; Structured, transparent, documented and auditable; generic rather than ship-specific; Proportionate, iterative, and explicitly cost-aware
3. **The process - and the step that is not one of the five** - Decision makers define the problem FIRST - not one of the five steps; Hazard is a potential to threaten; risk is frequency combined with severity; Five steps: hazard identification, risk analysis, risk control options, cost benefit assessment, recommendations
4. **Step 1 - Hazard Identification** - Creative element - structured group review by an experienced, balanced team; Analytical element - casualty data, defect records, previous studies; Coarse analysis and ranking on a risk matrix; output is a prioritised list of hazards and scenarios
5. **Step 2 - Risk Analysis** - Fault trees backwards for causes; event trees forwards for consequences; The risk contribution tree shows which contributors dominate; Expert judgement identified as such; uncertainty and sensitivity must be reported
6. **Step 3 - Risk Control Options** - A measure acts on one element; an option is a combination evaluated together; Act on frequency or on consequence - they are not interchangeable; Engineering, operational or managerial, aimed at the dominant contributors
7. **Step 4 - Cost Benefit Assessment** - Base case from step 2; whole-life-cycle costs, not purchase price; Interested entities - those who pay are frequently not those who benefit; Gross and net cost of averting a fatality; gross considered first; no mandatory monetary criterion
8. **Step 5 - Recommendations, and what FSA does not settle** - Auditable, traceable and understandable by non-specialists; Options keeping risk as low as reasonably practicable, presented in actionable terms; It does NOT say how much risk is acceptable - it requires the criterion to be stated
