**Technical ambiguities identified before drafting:**

- Whether 'type of ships' means the physical type of vessel or the legal test of application. It means the legal test, and the distinction matters: CLC does not apply to 'tankers' as a class but to any sea-going vessel ACTUALLY CARRYING persistent oil in bulk as cargo - so a tanker in ballast is outside it, and a combination carrier is inside it only while carrying such cargo and on the following voyage unless it is proved that no residues remain. The answer states the legal test and then names the practical vessel classes it produces.
- Whether the two conventions can both apply to one incident. THEY CANNOT, and the mechanism that prevents it is the point. The Bunker Convention expressly excludes damage that is pollution damage as defined in CLC, WHETHER OR NOT COMPENSATION IS PAYABLE UNDER CLC. So a laden tanker's bunker spill is a CLC matter and falls outside the Bunker Convention altogether - even where, on the facts, no CLC compensation is recoverable. The two instruments are complementary by design and the seam between them is closed deliberately.
- Whether the printed '0il' with a zero is anything other than a typographic corruption. It is not; it is read as 'Oil' and the anomaly is preserved. So is 'Convention2001' with the missing space.
- Whether the pollution damage definitions genuinely differ. THEY DO NOT, in substance, and identifying that is a positive finding rather than an omission: both cover loss or damage caused outside the ship by contamination resulting from the escape or discharge, both restrict compensation for impairment of the environment to the costs of reasonable measures of reinstatement actually undertaken or to be undertaken, and both cover the costs of preventive measures and further loss caused by preventive measures. WHAT DIFFERS IS THE OIL THAT TRIGGERS THE DEFINITION AND THE SHIP THAT CARRIES IT, NOT THE DEFINITION.
- Whether the printed head 'limits of liability' can be answered without figures. IT CAN, AND IT MUST BE HERE. What the comparison requires is the STRUCTURE of limitation - whose limit it is, what fund it forms, who else competes for that fund, and what stands behind it - and that is a more discriminating answer than a pair of numbers would be. The answer says positively that no figure is given and why.
- Whether the geographical coverage differs between the two. IT DOES NOT, and again that is the answer. Both reach damage in the territory including the territorial sea of a State Party and in its exclusive economic zone or equivalent area, and both reach preventive measures WHEREVER TAKEN. The examinable consequence is that it is the place where the DAMAGE occurs, not the place of the incident and not the flag of the ship, that decides.

**Regulations and instruments engaged.**

- International Convention on Civil Liability for Oil Pollution Damage, 1992 (CLC'92) - IN FORCE 30 MAY 1996, twenty-seven years before this sitting. Applies to any sea-going vessel or seaborne craft of any type ACTUALLY CARRYING PERSISTENT HYDROCARBON MINERAL OIL IN BULK AS CARGO, and to a combination carrier only while carrying such cargo and on any voyage following unless it is proved that no residues remain.
- CLC'92 - the definition of pollution damage: loss or damage caused outside the ship by contamination resulting from the escape or discharge of oil, with compensation for impairment of the environment other than loss of profit LIMITED TO THE COSTS OF REASONABLE MEASURES OF REINSTATEMENT ACTUALLY UNDERTAKEN OR TO BE UNDERTAKEN; and the costs of preventive measures and further loss or damage caused by preventive measures.
- CLC'92 - STRICT LIABILITY of the registered owner, subject to three defences: act of war, hostilities, civil war or insurrection, or an exceptional, inevitable and irresistible natural phenomenon; damage wholly caused by the act or omission of a third party done with intent to cause damage; and damage wholly caused by the negligence of a government or other authority responsible for maintaining lights or other navigational aids. Contributory fault of the claimant reduces or exonerates liability.
- CLC'92 - CHANNELLING. Liability rests on the registered owner ALONE, and no claim for pollution damage may be made against the servants or agents of the owner or members of the crew, the pilot, the charterer, the manager or operator, the salvor, or persons taking preventive measures, unless the damage resulted from their personal act or omission committed with intent or recklessly and with knowledge that such damage would probably result.
- CLC'92 - LIMITATION. The Convention contains ITS OWN limitation regime, banded by the ship's tonnage with an overall ceiling, the fund being constituted with the court or other competent authority of a State Party and distributed among pollution claimants. The right to limit is lost where the damage resulted from the owner's personal act or omission committed with intent, or recklessly and with knowledge that such damage would probably result. NO FIGURE IS ASSERTED - see unresolved.
- CLC'92 - COMPULSORY INSURANCE. Required of the registered owner of a ship carrying MORE THAN 2,000 TONS OF PERSISTENT OIL IN BULK AS CARGO, evidenced by a certificate carried on board, with a right of DIRECT ACTION against the insurer, who may invoke the defences available to the owner and may limit even where the owner has lost that right.
- 1992 Fund Convention and the 2003 Supplementary Fund Protocol - the SECOND AND THIRD TIERS behind CLC, financed by contributions from receivers of contributing oil cargo. Named as the structure that has no counterpart under the Bunker Convention. NO FUND LIMIT OR CEILING IS ASSERTED.
- International Convention on Civil Liability for Bunker Oil Pollution Damage, 2001 - ADOPTED 23 MARCH 2001, IN FORCE 21 NOVEMBER 2008, fourteen years before this sitting. Applies to ANY SEAGOING VESSEL AND SEABORNE CRAFT OF ANY TYPE WHATSOEVER.
- Bunker Convention 2001 - 'BUNKER OIL' means any hydrocarbon mineral oil, INCLUDING LUBRICATING OIL, used or intended to be used for the operation or propulsion of the ship, and any residues of such oil. PERSISTENCE IS NOT REQUIRED.
- Bunker Convention 2001 - 'SHIPOWNER' includes the registered owner, the bareboat charterer, the manager and the operator, who are JOINTLY AND SEVERALLY LIABLE. Liability is strict, subject to the same three defences as CLC.
- Bunker Convention 2001 - EXCLUSION OF CLC DAMAGE. The Convention does not apply to pollution damage as defined in CLC, WHETHER OR NOT COMPENSATION IS PAYABLE IN RESPECT OF IT UNDER THAT CONVENTION. Warships and State ships on non-commercial service are also excluded.
- Bunker Convention 2001 - LIMITATION. The Convention SETS NO LIMIT OF ITS OWN. It preserves the shipowner's right to limit under any applicable national or international regime, in practice the Convention on Limitation of Liability for Maritime Claims as amended, so a bunker pollution claim competes within the GENERAL limitation fund for the incident. THERE IS NO SECOND-TIER FUND. NO LLMC FIGURE AND NO LLMC RESOLUTION NUMBER IS ASSERTED - see unresolved.
- Bunker Convention 2001 - COMPULSORY INSURANCE. Required of the REGISTERED OWNER of a ship of MORE THAN 1,000 GROSS TONNAGE registered in a State Party, up to the applicable limit, evidenced by a certificate in the annexed model form carried on board, with DIRECT ACTION against the insurer; and States Parties enforce the certificate requirement against ships of ANY FLAG entering their ports or offshore facilities.
- GEOGRAPHICAL APPLICATION - IDENTICAL IN BOTH. Pollution damage caused in the territory, including the territorial sea, of a State Party; pollution damage caused in the exclusive economic zone of a State Party or, where none has been established, in an equivalent area beyond and adjacent to the territorial sea determined in accordance with international law and extending not more than 200 NAUTICAL MILES from the baselines; and PREVENTIVE MEASURES, WHEREVER TAKEN, to prevent or minimise such damage.

## 7. ANSWER CONSTRUCTION

**Answer route** - the principal headings of the model answer ARE this route:

1. **Background - the gap the Bunker Convention was written to close** *(limb framing)*
   - CLC reaches only ships carrying persistent oil in bulk as cargo
   - But a large container ship or bulk carrier burns and carries thousands of tonnes of fuel, and fell outside it
   - Before 2001: no strict liability, no compulsory insurance, no certificate for bunkers
   - Adopted 23 March 2001, in force 21 November 2008; it deliberately borrows CLC's architecture
   - The map: CLC for tanker cargo oil · Bunkers for every ship's fuel · HNS for other cargoes
2. **Head 1 - the type of ships** *(limb head 1)*
   - CLC: any sea-going vessel ACTUALLY CARRYING persistent oil in bulk as CARGO - so a tanker in ballast is outside it
   - Combination carriers: inside only while carrying such cargo, and on the following voyage unless proved no residues remain
   - Bunkers: any seagoing vessel and seaborne craft of ANY TYPE WHATSOEVER - no cargo test at all
   - Warships and State non-commercial ships excluded from both
   - No overlap: Bunkers excludes CLC pollution damage, whether or not compensation is payable under CLC
3. **Head 2 - the oil covered, and the nature of the pollution damage** *(limb head 2)*
   - CLC oil = PERSISTENT hydrocarbon MINERAL oil - both requirements, kept distinct. Crude, fuel oil, heavy diesel, lubricating oil. Petrol, kerosene and light diesel are OUT
   - Bunker oil = ANY hydrocarbon mineral oil, INCLUDING LUBRICATING OIL, used or intended for the operation or propulsion of the ship, and any residues. Persistence is NOT required
   - The DAMAGE definition is essentially IDENTICAL in both - and saying so is the answer
   - Loss or damage outside the ship by contamination; environmental impairment limited to reasonable reinstatement actually undertaken or to be undertaken; plus preventive measures and loss caused by them
4. **Head 3 - limits of liability** *(limb head 3)*
   - Both: strict liability, the same three defences, and the right to limit lost on personal intent or recklessness with knowledge that such damage would probably result
   - CLC has its OWN limit - tonnage-banded, with an overall ceiling; the fund is constituted with the court and reserved to pollution claimants alone
   - And two further tiers behind it - the 1992 Fund and the Supplementary Fund, financed by cargo receivers, so ship and cargo share the burden
   - Bunkers sets NO limit of its own - it refers out to the applicable national or international limitation regime, in practice the LLMC
   - Consequence: the bunker claim competes in ONE general limitation fund with every other claim from the incident, and there is NO second tier at all
   - No figure is given here, and the answer says why
5. **Head 4 - geographical coverage** *(limb head 4)*
   - Territory, including the territorial sea, of a State Party
   - The exclusive economic zone, or an equivalent area up to 200 nautical miles where none is declared
   - Preventive measures WHEREVER TAKEN - including on the high seas
   - It is where the DAMAGE occurs that decides - not where the incident happened, and not the flag of the ship
6. **The machinery under head 3 - channelling, insurance and direct action** *(limb head 3)*
   - CLC channels INWARD - liability on the registered owner ALONE, with claims barred against crew, pilot, charterer, manager, operator and salvor
   - Bunkers spreads OUTWARD - 'shipowner' includes registered owner, bareboat charterer, manager and operator, JOINTLY AND SEVERALLY
   - CLC insurance threshold is the CARGO - carrying more than 2,000 tons of persistent oil in bulk
   - Bunkers insurance threshold is the SHIP - over 1,000 gross tonnage
   - Both: a certificate carried on board, a P&I blue card behind it, and DIRECT ACTION against the insurer
   - Port States enforce the certificate on ships of ANY flag
7. **Conclusion - the one asymmetry that explains the rest** *(limb closing)*
   - They agree on far more than they differ - by design, the later Convention being drafted on the earlier
   - Four differences: the ships · the oil · the channelling - and the structural one
   - CLC has two cargo-financed tiers behind it; the Bunker Convention has nothing
   - Because a ship’s own fuel has no receiver to finance a tier. Every practical difference traces back to that
