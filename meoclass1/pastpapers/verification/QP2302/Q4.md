**Technical ambiguities identified before drafting:**

- Whether 'maritime salvage' means salvage under the general maritime law as opposed to salvage under contract, or whether it means salvage of maritime property as opposed to something else. It means the former - pure or common-law salvage, arising from the act itself without prior agreement - and the stem's later phrase 'salvage of maritime property' confirms that maritime property is a separate concept it uses elsewhere. The answer states which reading it takes.
- Whether contractual salvage is therefore NOT salvage in the legal sense. It is. The distinction is about how the entitlement arises, not about whether the service is salvage. A LOF service is contractual AND is salvage, which is exactly why the Convention's criteria are used to fix the award under it. Treating the two as mutually exclusive categories is the standard error.
- Whether towage is contractual salvage. It is not - ordinary towage under a towage contract is a service for an agreed price and carries no salvage element unless the tow encounters danger beyond the scope of the contract, at which point a salvage claim can arise. The distinction is worth stating because it is the clearest illustration of the boundary.
- Whether the printed '.JS per this form' is anything other than a typographic corruption of 'as per this form'. It is not; it is read as 'as' and the anomaly is preserved.
- Whether special compensation and SCOPIC are within scope. They are, briefly, because the stem asks who is responsible for paying and how the award is calculated, and both answers are modified where environmental damage is threatened - special compensation is paid by the SHIPOWNER alone, not by the property interests, which is a genuine exception to the answer given to demand 3.

## 7. ANSWER CONSTRUCTION

**Route archetype:** `legal`  ·  **Steps:** 7  ·  **Model answer blocks:** 40  ·  **Study note blocks:** 45  ·  **Retrieval cards:** 14

**Answer route** - the principal headings of the model answer ARE this route:

1. **The three preconditions - what makes any claim a salvage claim** *(limb framing)*
   - Recognised subject in danger · voluntary service · success - no cure, no pay
2. **Demand 1 - the difference between maritime salvage and contractual salvage** *(limb d1)*
   - The test is whether the service was rendered under a pre-existing agreement, not whether money is paid
   - Pure salvage: entitlement arises from the act itself, by operation of maritime law
   - Contractual salvage: the parties agree terms first - but it is still salvage, and still usually no cure no pay
3. **Demand 2 - simple examples** *(limb d2)*
   - Pure - a passing ship rescues a drifting vessel with no agreement made
   - Contractual - the master signs LOF with a professional salvage tug before work begins
   - Contrast: ordinary towage is neither
4. **Demand 3 - who is responsible for payment of the award** *(limb d3)*
   - The owners of the property saved - ship, cargo, bunkers, freight at risk - each in PROPORTION to the saved value of its own interest
   - Not the shipowner alone; the shipowner pays only the ship's share
   - Secured by salvage security and a maritime lien; H&M and cargo underwriters and the P&I club indemnify behind
5. **Demand 4 - the form popularly used** *(limb d4)*
   - Lloyd's Open Form (LOF) - LOF 2020 is the current edition at this sitting
   - It is an agreement to arbitrate, not a price
6. **Demand 5 - how the amount of the award is calculated under the form** *(limb d5)*
   - Arbitration in London by a Lloyd's arbitrator, applying the Article 13 criteria
   - Ten criteria, unranked and weighed together; the salved value is the ceiling; the award is then apportioned among the salvors
7. **The environmental exception - which qualifies the answers above** *(limb qualification)*
   - Where environmental damage is threatened and the ordinary award is inadequate: special compensation, payable by the SHIPOWNER alone
   - SCOPIC is the contractual replacement, invoked by the salvor, backed by P&I security

**Major trap.** Answering demand 3 with 'the shipowner'. The award is payable by the owners of ALL the property saved, each in proportion to the saved value of its own interest - so on a laden ship the cargo interests pay their share, which may exceed the ship's. Saying 'the insurers' is also wrong: they indemnify behind the liability, they are not the party responsible. The second trap is distinguishing pure from contractual salvage by whether money was paid rather than by whether there was a prior agreement.
