**Technical ambiguities identified before drafting:**

- Whether 'Compliant Procedures' could mean something other than complaint procedures. IT COULD NOT. No instrument, procedure or defined term in MLC 2006 is called a compliant procedure. The Convention provides an ON-BOARD COMPLAINT PROCEDURE at Regulation 5.1.5 and an ONSHORE SEAFARER COMPLAINT-HANDLING PROCEDURE at Regulation 5.2.2, and the printed pairing of 'On-board' with 'On-shore' maps onto that pair exactly. The reading is confirmed independently by QP2307-Q4, five months later, which prints the same question with the word spelled correctly.
- Whether limb a) is one limb or two. It is printed as one - 'Flag State & Port State responsibilities' - and it must be answered as one, but the two mechanisms are genuinely different and the answer must show that it knows they are. It is written as one limb in two clearly separated parts.
- Whether 'detainable deficiencies' is a term of art in the Convention. IT IS NOT, and this matters. The Convention does not publish a list of detainable deficiencies; it provides TWO GROUNDS on which a ship may be detained. The published lists that circulate belong to regional port State control regimes and to industry guidance, not to the Convention. The answer therefore states the two grounds as the law and gives illustrations of what falls under each as illustrations, saying so.
- Whether limb c) overlaps limb a) so far as to be redundant. It does not, but it is close enough that the answer must be organised to avoid saying the same thing twice: limb a) states the port State's INSPECTION powers and the triggers for going further, and limb c) states what a more detailed inspection may find that justifies DETENTION.
- Whether limb d) should be answered on the Convention or on Indian law. On BOTH, and in that order. The Convention supplies the route every seafarer has, and the Indian layer supplies the domestic machinery. Answering only the Indian layer misses that the Convention's own escalation is itself a grievance mechanism available to an Indian seafarer, and answering only the Convention ignores the word 'Indian' in the printed limb.
- Whether India's relevant capacity in limb d) is as a flag State. FOR MOST INDIAN SEAFARERS IT IS NOT. The great majority serve on foreign-flag ships, so India's obligations towards them arise principally under the LABOUR-SUPPLYING responsibilities of Regulation 5.3 and under its regulation of recruitment and placement services, not under its flag State responsibilities. This is the structural insight that makes the limb answerable without inventing Indian sources, and it is Convention architecture rather than an assertion about India.

## 7. ANSWER CONSTRUCTION

**Route archetype:** `legal`  ·  **Steps:** 6  ·  **Model answer blocks:** 51  ·  **Study note blocks:** 48  ·  **Retrieval cards:** 17

**Answer route** - the principal headings of the model answer ARE this route:

1. **Which edition applies, and how the Convention is built** *(limb framing)*
   - ILO instrument, in force 20 August 2013 - the 'fourth pillar' alongside SOLAS, MARPOL and STCW
   - Articles · Regulations · Code Part A Standards (mandatory) · Code Part B Guidelines (guidance)
   - Five Titles; Title 5 is compliance and enforcement and splits into flag State, port State and labour-supplying responsibilities
   - At this sitting: as amended through the 2018 set. The 2022 amendments are ADOPTED BUT NOT IN FORCE - 23 December 2024, twenty-two months away
2. **Limb a) - Flag State and Port State responsibilities** *(limb a))*
   - FLAG: an effective system of inspection and certification; may authorise recognized organizations but remains fully responsible
   - Maritime Labour Certificate + DMLC at 500 GT on international voyages; 5 years, intermediate inspection between the 2nd and 3rd anniversaries; interim 6 months
   - DMLC Part I by the competent authority, Part II by the SHIPOWNER - the classic trap
   - Inspection interval shall in no case exceed three years; inspectors may prohibit sailing
   - PORT: any foreign ship may be inspected; the certificate and DMLC must be accepted as prima facie evidence, so the ordinary inspection is a document review
   - Inspection limited to the Articles, Regulations and Part A only of the Code
   - Article V(7) no more favourable treatment - a non-ratifying flag gains no advantage
3. **Limb b) - the on-board and onshore COMPLAINT procedures** *(limb b))*
   - ON-BOARD - Regulation 5.1.5. Fair, effective and expeditious; resolution at the lowest level, but direct access to the master and to external authorities preserved
   - A copy of the procedure to every seafarer, with contacts and the name of an impartial adviser on board
   - Victimization is PROHIBITED AND PENALIZED - the provision that makes the rest usable
   - ONSHORE - Regulation 5.2.2. Complaint to an authorized officer in port; seek resolution on board first
   - Unresolved → notify the flag State for advice and a corrective plan; still unresolved → report to the Director-General of the ILO
   - Confidentiality of the source is safeguarded throughout
4. **Limb c) - detainable deficiencies** *(limb c))*
   - The Convention publishes no list. It gives TWO GROUNDS
   - (1) Conditions clearly hazardous to the safety, health or security of seafarers
   - (2) A serious or repeated breach of the requirements, including seafarers' rights
   - Reached only through a more detailed inspection, opened on one of four triggers: documents missing or invalid; clear grounds; flag changed to avoid compliance; or a complaint
   - Then: rectification, or an accepted plan of action; the flag State is notified forthwith; compensation for undue detention
5. **Limb d) - grievance redressal mechanisms for Indian seafarers** *(limb d))*
   - Layer 1 - the Convention's own route, which every Indian seafarer has: on board → master → flag State → authorized officer in port → Director-General of the ILO
   - Layer 2 - India's capacity. Flag State for Indian ships; but for most Indian seafarers, who serve on foreign flags, the operative head is LABOUR-SUPPLYING responsibility, Regulation 5.3, plus Regulation 1.4 licensing of recruitment and placement services and the no-fees rule
   - Layer 3 - the domestic frame. Merchant Shipping Act, 1958; the shipping master for engagement, discharge and wage disputes; the Directorate General of Shipping as the maritime administration
   - Practical actors, not law: seafarers' unions and the ITF inspectorate
   - NOT ASSERTED: no circular number, no named office or cell, no named forum or tribunal, no section number
6. **Closing - the thread that joins limbs b) and d)** *(limb closing)*
   - Flag State certifies and port State verifies - and both can be satisfied while a seafarer sits unpaid on a certified ship
   - What closes that gap is the complaint - which is why a complaint is also one of the four triggers
   - Limb d) is the same thread followed to its Indian end: regulate the agent who is within reach, because the foreign owner is not

**Decomposition - the components the answer must contain:**

- Limb a): the flag State's responsibilities under Title 5 - the inspection and certification system, recognized organizations, the certificate and the DMLC, inspection and enforcement - AND the port State's, which is a different mechanism with different limits, compressed into one limb
- Limb b): the ON-BOARD complaint procedure, and separately the ONSHORE complaint-handling procedure. The printed word is 'Compliant' and it is a misprint for 'Complaint' - the question cannot be answered at all until that is resolved
- Limb c): detainable deficiencies - the two grounds on which a ship may be detained under this Convention, and what in practice falls under each
- Limb d): grievance redressal for INDIAN seafarers - which requires the Convention's own escalation route AND the Indian statutory architecture, and which is the limb with no donor and no held Indian source

- The edition question, which decides what may be asserted at all: which set of amendments is in force at this sitting

**Major trap.** Two traps, and the first is fatal to the limb. Reading limb b) as printed. The paper prints 'Compliant Procedures'; the word is COMPLAINT, and MLC 2006 contains no such thing as a compliant procedure. Answering the printed word is answering nothing; the limb is Regulations 5.1.5 and 5.2.2. The second is temporal: writing the answer from the 2022 amendments.
