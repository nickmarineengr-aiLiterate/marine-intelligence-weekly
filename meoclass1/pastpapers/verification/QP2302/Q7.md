**Technical ambiguities identified before drafting:**

- Whether the printed 'Cortra rotating Propellers' could be a device other than contra-rotating propellers. It could not. No such device exists, both exact donor instances print 'Contra rotating Propellers' in the same list position with the same three companions, and the corruption is a straightforward transposition. The device is answered as CONTRA-ROTATING and the answer says once, plainly, that the paper prints it otherwise.
- Whether a duct always improves efficiency. IT DOES NOT, and this is the most important single discrimination on the question. The duct's benefit rises with THRUST LOADING; on a lightly loaded, fast, fine-form ship the duct's own frictional and form drag exceeds the thrust it generates and the installation is a net loss. An answer that presents the duct as an unqualified improvement has not understood it.
- Whether the Kappel propeller is an add-on device. It is not. The tip curvature is INTEGRAL BLADE GEOMETRY, formed in the casting, not a bolted-on end plate or fin. This matters because the whole of its merit case - no added appendage, no added drag, no added maintenance item, straight replacement retrofit - depends on it being integral.
- Whether contra-rotation is limited by hydrodynamics or by mechanics. BY MECHANICS. The hydrodynamic case is the strongest of the three, because swirl is the largest single recoverable loss and the after propeller recovers it directly. What has restricted the arrangement in large merchant service is coaxial shafting, the gearing and above all the concentric shaft SEALING arrangement. Saying this shows the candidate knows why a theoretically superior device is rare.
- Whether azimuth propulsion belongs in a question about high-efficiency PROPELLERS at all, given that it is a propulsion and steering arrangement rather than a blade or a duct. The stem prints it as one of the four, so the examiner has decided the point. The answer treats it on its own terms - the efficiency gain is real but comes from the clean inflow and the deletion of shafting, struts and rudder, not from the blade - and says so, because a candidate who claims the propeller itself is more efficient has misplaced the mechanism.
- Whether any quantified gain can be defended. NOT FROM ANY VERIFIABLE SOURCE. No percentage appears anywhere in this answer and the reason is stated positively, so that a candidate does not supply one.

   - Operative strategy is the Initial IMO GHG Strategy of 2018
2. **Where the losses occur - the map the rest of the answer hangs on** *(limb framing)*
   - Propulsive efficiency = hull efficiency × open-water efficiency × relative rotative efficiency
   - AXIAL loss - kinetic energy left in the accelerated slipstream
   - ROTATIONAL loss - the race leaves the disc spinning; that swirl is wasted
   - FRICTIONAL loss - viscous drag on the blade surfaces
   - TIP VORTEX loss - pressure equalises round the tip and sheds a vortex
   - Cavitation sets the ceiling on how hard a blade may be loaded
   - Each device attacks a different loss - that is the organising idea
3. **(a) Ducted propellers - an attack on the axial loss** *(limb a))*
   - An annular aerofoil round the disc; an accelerating duct raises velocity through it and cuts the thrust each blade must make
   - The duct is itself a lifting surface, so it generates thrust; it also closes the tip gap
   - Benefit rises with THRUST LOADING - tugs, trawlers, slow full-form ships
   - MERITS - large thrust augmentation and bollard pull; propeller protection; course stability, or steering with a nozzle
   - DEMERITS - a net penalty at light loading; fouling, corrosion, debris and ice; internal cavitation erosion; weight, survey and maintenance
4. **(b) Kappel propellers - an attack on the tip vortex loss** *(limb b))*
   - Blade tips curved smoothly towards the suction side as an integrated continuous winglet - not a bolted-on device
   - Load is carried further outboard while the tip vortex is weakened and displaced; the analogy is the aircraft winglet
   - MERITS - tip loss cut at the same diameter; no appendage, so no added drag or maintenance item; smoother wake into the rudder, so less vibration; straight replacement retrofit
   - DEMERITS - modest gain, highly wake-dependent; hull-specific design, CFD and model testing; complex geometry harder to cast, repair and re-pitch; benefit falls away off the design condition
5. **(c) Contra-rotating propellers - an attack on the rotational loss** *(limb c))*
   - Two coaxial propellers turning in opposite directions; the after one recovers the swirl left by the forward one
   - Swirl is the largest single recoverable loss in a conventional installation
   - MERITS - highest theoretical gain; thrust split, so lower loading per propeller, less cavitation and vibration; torque reaction largely cancelled; smaller diameter for a given thrust where draught limits it
   - DEMERITS - limited by mechanics, not hydrodynamics: coaxial shafting and epicyclic gearing, and above all the concentric shaft seal; harder alignment, maintenance and drydocking; little large-merchant service experience
6. **(d) Azimuth propulsion - an attack on hull-side and appendage losses** *(limb d))*
   - A propeller on a unit that rotates through 360°; mechanical Z-drive, or a podded electric unit with the motor in the pod
   - Efficiency comes from the clean, uniform inflow and the deletion of shafting, struts, brackets and rudder - not from the blade
   - A pulling unit works in undisturbed water, which is the main gain
   - MERITS - clean inflow and no appendage drag; full thrust in any direction, so no tunnel thrusters and no rudder; diesel-electric plant flexibility; aft-body freed for cargo
   - DEMERITS - electrical conversion losses offset part of the gain; high capital cost and complexity; the pod cannot be opened at sea and needs drydocking; slipring, bearing and seal reliability; appendage and structural drag of the pod at high speed
7. **Comparative judgement, and the instruction printed in the stem** *(limb closing)*
   - The choice follows thrust loading and operating profile, not fashion
   - Heavily loaded and slow → the duct earns its drag. Lightly loaded and fast → the duct makes it worse
   - Moderately loaded single screw, clean wake → Kappel. Large swirl and complexity acceptable → contra-rotating. Manoeuvring-dominated or DP → azimuth
   - ANSWER THREE, NOT FOUR, and give merits AND demerits for each
   - Give no percentage figures - they are vendor claims and hull-specific
