**Technical ambiguities identified before drafting:**

- THE PRINTED 'PHASE 2 ( f 20% - 30% reduction)' IS SUBSTANTIVELY WRONG AND NOT MERELY MISPRINTED. The stray 'f' is a typographic error for 'of' and is preserved. But the underlying framing conflates two schemes: the PHASED Phase 0 / 1 / 2 / 3 structure belongs to the REQUIRED EEDI table at regulation 24 Table 1, which applies to NEW ships by delivery date. EEXI takes its required reduction factor from REGULATION 25 TABLE 3, which is banded by ship type and size and is NOT phased. The answer states the framing as printed, then distinguishes the two, because a candidate who simply adopts the printed framing will describe the wrong table.
- Whether 'design features' excludes operational measures. It does. Limb a) asks about design, and operational measures belong in limb b) where improvement of the CII is asked. Keeping them apart is part of what is being tested, and putting slow steaming under limb a) is the standard error.
- Whether power limitation is a 'design feature'. It is treated as one, because it is a permanent, verified, certified modification to the ship recorded in her technical file, and because it is what the fleet overwhelmingly did. But the answer says plainly what it is - a limitation on the ship rather than an improvement to her - because that honesty is itself examinable.
- Whether AER and EEOI are alternatives for regulatory purposes. They are not. AER is the metric used for the regulatory rating of most ship types; EEOI is a voluntary and more meaningful commercial measure. Presenting them as interchangeable misses the whole point of the critical comparison.
- Whether the CII applies to all ships. It does not - the regulation 28 obligation attaches at 5,000 gross tonnage and above, matching the data collection threshold, and certain ship types are outside it. The threshold is stated; a full type-by-type applicability list is not.

## 7. ANSWER CONSTRUCTION

**Route archetype:** `explain`  ·  **Steps:** 8  ·  **Model answer blocks:** 39  ·  **Study note blocks:** 38  ·  **Retrieval cards:** 13

**Answer route** - the principal headings of the model answer ARE this route:

1. **Where EEXI sits in law - and what the printed Phase 2 framing refers to** *(limb a))*
   - Chapter 4 of Annex VI as revised by MEPC.328(76), in force 1 November 2022, chapter 4 applying from 1 January 2023
   - Reg 23 attained EEXI, reg 25 required EEXI: attained ≤ required
   - The phased Phase 0/1/2/3 scheme belongs to reg 24 Table 1, required EEDI, for NEW ships. EEXI takes its factor from reg 25 Table 3, banded by type and size, not phased
2. **Hull and propulsive design measures** *(limb a))*
   - Bulbous bow retrofit, hull coating, propeller retrofit and polishing, energy saving devices - ducts, fins, PSS
   - Air lubrication; wind assistance and the innovative-technology route
3. **Machinery and energy-recovery measures** *(limb a))*
   - Waste heat recovery, derating and engine tuning, shaft generator, efficient auxiliaries, alternative and low-carbon fuels through the CF term
4. **Power limitation - what most existing ships actually did** *(limb a))*
   - EPL and ShaPoLi - reduce P in the numerator; overridable in an emergency, recorded, verified
   - The honest point: it limits the ship rather than improving her, and it does nothing for the CII unless the speed is actually reduced
5. **Limb b) - the concept of the CII rating** *(limb b))*
   - Reg 27 data in, reg 28 rating out; 5,000 GT and above
   - Attained annual operational CII against a required value from a reference line and a tightening reduction factor; five bands A to E; D three years running, or E once, triggers a corrective action plan in the SEEMP
6. **AER and EEOI critically compared - the denominator is the whole argument** *(limb b))*
   - Both are CO₂ per transport work; the argument is entirely about the denominator
   - AER = deadweight × distance - capacity offered, always available, rewards sailing full-speed empty
   - EEOI = cargo actually carried × distance - real transport work, but needs cargo data and is not comparable between trades
7. **Measures to improve the rating - operational** *(limb b))*
   - Speed reduction - the largest single lever, cubic in power; voyage and weather routeing; just-in-time arrival; trim and draught optimisation; hull and propeller cleaning; reduced auxiliary and boiler load; cargo and ballast utilisation
8. **Measures to improve the rating - technical, and the SEEMP as the instrument** *(limb b))*
