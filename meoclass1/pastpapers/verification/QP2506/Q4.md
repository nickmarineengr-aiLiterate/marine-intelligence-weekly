**Question.** a) What SCOPIC is and how it solves the difficulties of salvors. b) After a collision
between two ships on the high seas, which State has the right and responsibility, with
justification and the salient points of the applicable convention, for (i) disciplinary action
against a crew member or the master, (ii) cancelling COCs, (iii) payment of compensation. (16)

## The provision that answers two-thirds of limb (b)

Most candidates never reach it, and it answers limbs (i) and (ii) almost word for word.

> **UNCLOS Article 97(1)** - in the event of a collision or any other incident of navigation
> concerning a ship **on the high seas**, involving the penal or disciplinary responsibility of the
> master or of any other person in the service of the ship, **no penal or disciplinary proceedings
> may be instituted against such person except before the judicial or administrative authorities
> either of the flag State or of the State of which such person is a national**.
>
> **Article 97(2)** - in disciplinary matters, **only the State which issued a master's certificate
> or a certificate of competence or licence** is competent, after due legal process, to pronounce
> the **withdrawal of such certificates, even if the holder is not a national of that State**.
>
> **Article 97(3)** - **no arrest or detention of the ship, even as a measure of investigation**,
> may be ordered by any authorities other than those of the flag State.

The examiner has built a question whose answer **narrows** from (i) to (ii) - two candidate States,
then exactly one - and then **changes legal category** at (iii). Giving the same State three times
is the commonest failure and the route is built to prevent it.

The closing words of 97(2) settle the case actually being tested: an officer holding the
certificate of a State he is not a national of. The answer also states what is *not* restricted - a
flag State may withdraw **its own endorsement** recognising a foreign certificate, and a port State
may act against the **ship** under port State control. Neither cancels the certificate.

## Limb (b)(iii) is not a jurisdiction question, and saying so is the mark

Compensation is **civil liability between the parties**; UNCLOS Article 97 does not touch it. The
applicable convention is the **Collision Convention 1910**: accident, *force majeure* or doubt -
each bears his own loss; fault of one vessel - she bears it; both at fault - **liability in
proportion to the degree of fault**, divided equally **only** where the degrees cannot be
established; **no presumption of fault**; **joint and several** liability for death and personal
injury but **proportionate** for property damage. Forum is then a separate question under the
**Brussels Convention 1952 on Civil Jurisdiction in Matters of Collision**, and in practice the
claimant chooses by arrest. The total is then capped by **LLMC** - which is **Q5 of this paper**.

## Reasoning recorded

- **The stem asks how SCOPIC solves "the difficulties of salvors"**, so the Article 14 problems must
  be stated *before* the cure or the cure has nothing to bite on. Four problems - the environmental
  threshold, "expenses" carrying no profit element, assessment long after the event, and no security
  - pair off against four cures.
- **The two balancing provisions are what separate a good answer from a recitation.** SCOPIC is
  payable only so far as it **exceeds** the Article 13 award, and where the award is greater it is
  **discounted by 25% of the difference**. The salvor gets the better of the two, not both, and pays
  for the safety net if it turns out he did not need it.
- **The two 25 per cents pull in opposite directions** - a bonus *on the tariff*, a discount *on the
  Article 13 award*. Recorded as the question's headline trap.
- **SCOPIC is contractual, not law.** It is supplementary to a Lloyd's Form and binds because the
  parties agree to it. Describing it as part of the Salvage Convention is a category error.
- **"On the high seas" is doing all the work in limb (b).** Inside a territorial sea the coastal
  State's criminal jurisdiction is engaged and the answer changes completely.
