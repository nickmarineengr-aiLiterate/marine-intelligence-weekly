## Reasoning recorded

- **The paper prints 8 + 8, so limb (b) is worth as much as the entire Convention.** Candidates
  routinely write three-quarters of a page on LLMC and four lines of dictionary definitions. Three
  route steps each side is the shape that scores.
- **The two limbs are one piece of law and the answer says so.** Privity is the *old* test for
  breaking limitation, Article 4 is the *new* one, unlimited liability is what happens when the
  limit breaks, and plaintiff and defendant are the two sides of the fight over whether it breaks.
- **"Two heads of claims" is precise language.** Article 6 has exactly two - loss of life or
  personal injury, and **any other claims**. The second head is residual and wider than "property
  damage". Listing categories from Article 2 answers a different question.
- **Article 7 passengers sit alongside the two heads, not among them.** Stating that is a mark;
  promoting it into the pair is an error. Recorded in the gate as a technical ambiguity.
- **Salvage appears on both sides of the line** - an Article 3 excepted claim, and Article 1 makes
  the salvor a person entitled to limit. Stated together without separation the answer reads as
  self-contradictory.
- **Unlimited liability is explained by its three routes** - the right **lost** under Article 4, the
  right that **never applied** under Article 3, and **no Convention governing** - rather than by its
  name.
