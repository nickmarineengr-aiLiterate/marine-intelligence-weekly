# QP2506 Q1 - verification record

**Paper:** QP2506, June 2025. **Built:** 2026-08-10. **State:** Pilot Review Ready.
**Reuse:** Tier **C**. **No donor and no adjacent built material anywhere in the corpus.**

**Question.** Rudder-efficiency improvement devices currently in use, with reference to
(a) Rudder Thrust Fins (4) (b) Asymmetric Rudders (4) (c) Rudder Bulbs (4) (d) Grim Vane
Wheels (4).

## Sitting-date finding

**Checked, no temporal issue.** Hydrodynamics is not dated law. All four devices predate this
sitting by decades - the vane wheel was patented by Grim in **1966** - and none of the physics
changed between June 2025 and today.

The only dated element is the regulatory framing in step 6. EEXI and the operational carbon
intensity rating under MARPOL Annex VI chapter 4 were both in force well before this sitting, so
referring to them is correct here. **The IMO Net-Zero Framework is deliberately not mentioned**:
at this sitting it had been circulated and not adopted - see Q2 - and it has no bearing on a
retrofit decision taken in June 2025.

## The corpus position, and why this question matters out of proportion to its marks

This is the **first naval-architecture question in the solved corpus**. A search of all 81 built
questions returned nothing on propulsive efficiency, energy-saving devices or rudder
hydrodynamics; the solved set is heavily weighted to law and management. It is therefore the only
question on this paper researched entirely from scratch, and the only one whose family - January
2023 Q1, August 2023 Q3, April 2024 Q4, September 2024 Q9 and this sitting - contains no built
answer at all.

## Sources actually read

| Source | Class | What it established |
|---|---|---|
| IMO GreenVoyage technology material on **propulsion-improving devices** | P2 | The taxonomy: devices that recover the axial and rotational losses inherent in propeller operation, divided into pre-swirl, post-swirl and rudder-region devices. |
| Published literature on the **Grim vane wheel** | P2 | Patented by Otto Grim in **1966** as the "Leitrad". Freely rotating, mounted aft of and coaxial with the propeller, **larger in diameter**, with **no mechanical connection**. **Inner portion acts as a turbine** extracting energy from the slipstream; **outer portion, outside the race, acts as a propeller** returning it as thrust. |
| Published CFD studies on **rudder bulbs and rudder thrust fins** | P2 | Propulsive gain is sensitive to bulb diameter and to fin span, chord and angle of attack; model-scale results do not transfer to full scale without a **scale-effect correction**. |
| The quoted gain ranges, off-design and interaction cautions, and the acquisition reasoning | ENGINEERING JUDGEMENT | Stated as ranges with dependencies, never as figures for any hull. |

## The honest weakness, recorded rather than concealed

`validate_spec.py` reports **`no claims recorded as P1_PRIMARY_VERIFIED`** on this question. That
warning is **correct and is not a defect to be engineered away**. No primary instrument governs
rudder hydrodynamics, and the alternative - dressing a manufacturer's datasheet up as primary
authority - would be worse than the warning.

Consequently:

- **No manufacturer datasheet, model-test report or sea-trial record was read.** Every percentage
  in the answer is an industry-quoted range, explicitly conditioned on hull form, draught, speed
  and wake field. The answer says in terms that no figure should be repeated as a guaranteed
  saving.
- MIW has **not** established how many vane-wheel installations survive in service. The answer
  says the device is "of more importance as a principle than as a device in general service" and
  deliberately does **not** assert that none survives.

## Reasoning recorded

- **The printed 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 locks the shape.** The commonest failure is a good general essay on
  energy-saving devices - ducts, pre-swirl stators, boss cap fins, air lubrication - none of which
  is asked for. The route enforces four equal blocks.
- **One idea unlocks all four**: rotational loss. Two devices *recover* it (fin, twisted rudder,
  and the vane wheel mechanically); one *stops further waste* (the bulb). Classifying a device
  correctly lets a candidate reconstruct its explanation.
- **The bulb is not a recovery device** and the answer refuses the loose usage. The defensible
  statement is suppression of the hub vortex and pressure recovery behind the boss.
- **The Grim vane wheel is not, strictly, a rudder device** - it is shaft-mounted, not
  rudder-mounted. The examiner has grouped it under "rudder efficiency". The answer treats it in
  the family because the demand is clear, but does not pretend it is fitted to the rudder. Recorded
  in the gate as a technical ambiguity.
- **Step 6 is where the management mark is.** Anyone can recite a mechanism; the Class I candidate
  interrogates the claim - in what condition was it measured, at what scale, against what operating
  profile, interacting with what else, and approved by class on what basis.

## Flags

- **C_ACCEPTED_LIMITATION** - the quoted gain ranges (2-5% thrust fins, 5-10% vane wheel, 8-12%
  rotational loss). Industry-quoted, not verified for any hull. Either substantiate against a named
  source before publication or soften further to comparative statements.
- **C_ACCEPTED_LIMITATION** - that the vane wheel is of principally historical importance. Not
  surveyed; deliberately framed as a comparative statement rather than an assertion.
- No `B_CURRENCY_CHECK`. Nothing in this question is date-driven.
