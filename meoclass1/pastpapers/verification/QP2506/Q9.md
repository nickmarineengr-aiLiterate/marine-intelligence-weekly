**Question.** The meaning of "Unseaworthy vessels" under the Merchant Shipping Act (MSA), 1958, as
amended; the difference between "Unseaworthy ships" and "Unsafe ships"; and the obligations of
owners to crew with respect to seaworthiness.

## Sitting-date finding - and the donor's best material is future contamination here

**The Merchant Shipping Act 1958 was the operative Indian statute throughout June 2025 and remained
so until 15 March 2026.** The answer is given on the 1958 Act, and that is not a concession - it is
the only correct basis.

The donor's sharpest teaching point cannot come with it. **QP2508 Q5 turns on the fact that the
Merchant Shipping Act, 2025 received the assent of the President on 18 August 2025 - the very month
of its own examination - and still did not commence until 15 March 2026**, which makes August 2025
the ideal sitting at which to teach *assent is not commencement*.

**All of that is after this paper.** At June 2025 the Act had not been assented, had not been
passed, and did not exist as an Act at all.

June 2025 has its own point on the same ladder, one rung earlier, and it is verifiable:

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **10 December 2024** | The **Merchant Shipping Bill, 2024** is introduced in the **Lok Sabha**. |
| **June 2025** | **<- this examination.** The Bill is before Parliament, **passed by neither House**. The Merchant Shipping Act 1958 is in force. |
| 6 August 2025 | Passed by the **Lok Sabha**. |
| 11 August 2025 | Passed by the **Rajya Sabha**. |
| 18 August 2025 | Assent of the President. **Act No. 24 of 2025**, Gazette of India, Extraordinary, Part II Section 1, No. 29. |
| 10 March 2026 | **S.O. 1244(E)**, Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways, appoints 15 March 2026. |
| 15 March 2026 | The 2025 Act commences. **Section 324(1)** repeals the 1958 Act (except Part XIV but not including section 411A therein) and the Coasting Vessels Act, 1838. |

So the teaching point here is **"a Bill is not an Act"**, and it is arguably better than the donor's:
a candidate in June 2025 could well have read about the new Bill and assumed the 1958 Act was gone.
The ladder has **four rungs** - *introduced, passed, assented, commenced* - and in June 2025 the
Bill was still on the first. The study guide ties this to **Q2 of this paper**, where adoption,
ratification and entry into force are the same staged problem in treaty law.
