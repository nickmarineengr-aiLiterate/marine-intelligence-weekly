**Question.** With reference to Marine Insurance, short notes on (a) Port of Refuge (b) Particular

## Sitting-date finding - the paper's only backward-running temporal issue, and it was not flagged at intake

> **The IMO Guidelines on places of refuge for ships in need of assistance are resolution
> A.1184(33), adopted 6 December 2023. Its operative paragraph 4 reads, in terms: "REVOKES
> resolution A.949(23)."**

The revocation is **eighteen months before this sitting**. A.1184(23)'s predecessor - the 2003
resolution **A.949(23)** - is therefore not merely dated for June 2025, it is **wrong for it**.

This is the protocol's *most dangerous single error* in its usual disguise. A.949(23) is the number
carried by practically every circulating note set and by a great deal of published material; it is
freely available on an official domain; and an answer built on it would be internally coherent,
confidently written and wrong. **The intake temporal review recorded this question as STABLE / LOW
with no classes.** It was not flagged, and the sweep - not the flag list - is what caught it.

The resolution was **read in the IMO text this session**, including the preamble recital that
coastal States are **not, under international law, under any obligation to grant places of refuge**.

Everything else on this question is settled: the York-Antwerp Rules 2016 were the current CMI text
at this sitting (established for Q6), and the marine insurance definitions are long-standing.

## The framing decision that governs the whole answer

The stem opens **"With reference to Marine Insurance"**. Limb (a) is therefore answered principally
on the **general average footing** - York-Antwerp Rules X and XI, and the deviation position - with
the IMO Guidelines as the **regulatory backdrop**, not the other way round. Answering limb (a) as a
seamanship or SAR question is the commonest way to lose it.

## Reasoning recorded

- **Two route steps per limb, the second always answering "who pays".** "Short notes" is an
  instruction about length, not depth; the examiner still wants the test and the consequence.
- **Limbs (b) and (c) are pairs and the marks are in the contrast.** Two unconnected paragraphs
  answer half of each limb.
- **A single port of refuge call generates both general and particular average**, and separating
  them is the adjuster's task. This is the sharpest practical point in limb (a).
- **General average entitlement survives fault** - the party at fault may still claim contribution,
  the others being left to their separate remedies. Stated because the opposite is a confident and
  common error.
- **"Constructive Loss" is printed loosely.** The answer uses the term of art, constructive **total**
  loss, and notes the printed wording rather than silently altering the stem.
- **Notice of abandonment is the practical point of limb (c)** and its omission is the commonest
  gap in an otherwise correct answer.
