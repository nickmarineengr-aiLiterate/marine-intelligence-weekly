## 2. WHAT THE QUESTION ASKS

Two unrelated halves at equal weight. Limb (a) is institutional description with one narrow sub-question
buried in it - what role the sub-committees play in supporting the main committees - which is the part most
candidates omit. Limb (b) is definitional and comparative: define a convention, then distinguish Protocol,
Code and Resolution from it and from each other, with at least one example of each.

**Mark allocation.** 16, set as 8 + 8, printed against each limb. Limb (b) is quicker to write than limb (a)
and carries the same marks, so under-writing it is the standard way to lose half of eight marks.

**Examiner expectation.** Organ-by-organ FUNCTION rather than a list of acronyms; the sub-committee role
stated positively and negatively; and, in limb (b), the understanding that binding force is a property of the
treaty route rather than of the instrument's title.

**Required answer components:** what IMO is and which organs do the work; the Assembly's composition,
frequency and reserved functions; the Council's composition, categories, term and executive function; the
five main committees and what each owns; the seven sub-committees and their preparatory role including the
absence of adoption power; the definition of a convention; and Protocol, Code and Resolution each
distinguished and exemplified.

**Ambiguities adjudicated:**

- "Main committees" is undefined in the stem. The Organization recognises five and the answer says so, rather
  than listing only MSC and MEPC.
- Whether the Secretariat belongs to "the structure" - it does, and is included, although the stem names only
  the Assembly, Council and committees.
- "Resolution" is used loosely in industry speech for anything IMO publishes. The answer separates the ACT of
  adopting from the instrument adopted.

**Target answer shape.** Seven numbered sections under two limb headings. Sections 1-5 are limb (a), ending on
the sub-committees because that is what the stem specifically asks. Sections 6-7 are limb (b): a definition,
then one three-column table with a worked example in each row, closing on adoption versus entry into force.

## 9. ANSWER ROUTE AS BUILT

Archetype `explain`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route - `validate_spec.py` enforces
the correspondence, so the candidate cannot be taught one order and shown another.

**1. What IMO is, and the organs that do the work** - limb **(a)**

- UN specialised agency for safety, security and pollution prevention
- Assembly and Council govern; committees produce the technical output
- A Secretariat serves all of them

**2. The Assembly - the governing body** - limb **(a)**

- All Member States; regular session once every two years
- Approves work programme and budget; elects the Council
- Adopts Assembly resolutions under Article 15(j) - worked example A.1049(27), adopted on MSC 89's
  recommendation

**3. The Council - the executive organ** - limb **(a)**

- 40 members in three categories, elected for two years
- Performs Assembly functions between sessions, with one reserved exception
- Supervises the work of the Organization; appoints the Secretary-General subject to Assembly approval

**4. The five main committees and what each owns** - limb **(a)**

- MSC and MEPC are the technical engines; Legal, TC and FAL complete the set
- All five open to all Member States

**5. The seven sub-committees and the role they perform** - limb **(a)**

- HTW, III, NCSR, PPR, SDC, SSE, CCC
- Preparatory and technical; report to MSC, MEPC or both
- No adoption power: they prepare, the committee approves, the committee or Assembly adopts

**6. What a convention is** - limb **(b)**

- A binding treaty between States
- Enters into force only on its own stated conditions
- Binds only States party to it - example SOLAS 1974

**7. Protocol, Code and Resolution - how each differs, with examples** - limb **(b)**

- Protocol - a separate treaty that amends or supplements a parent convention; the 1978 and 1997 Protocols
- Code - detail, mandatory only when a convention makes it so; RO Code parts 1/2 mandatory and part 3
  recommendatory under SOLAS XI-1/1; 2011 ESP Code under XI-1/2
- Resolution - the decision of a body; A.1049(27), MEPC.377(80), MEPC.385(81)
- Close on adoption versus entry into force, using MEPC.385(81) at this sitting
