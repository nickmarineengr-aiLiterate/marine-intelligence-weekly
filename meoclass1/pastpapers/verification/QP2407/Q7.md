## 2. WHAT THE QUESTION ASKS

**Limb A carries two commands and both must be answered:** *explain* the significance of propeller curves,
and *enumerate* the safety margins. A script that explains the curve well and names one margin loses half of
limb A.

**Limb B is a drawing question.** It says expressly "using typical engine power versus engine speed diagram
and propeller demand curves", and it asks for the **relation** between two specific quantities: the plant's
maximum rated output and the power for which the propeller is designed.

**Mark allocation.** 8 + 8. Within limb A the enumeration is marked as heavily as the explanation.

**Examiner expectation.** That the candidate understands the propeller curve as a **family** rather than a
line; can name and distinguish the three design margins and say what each is for; can place the propeller
design point, the service point and the specified MCR correctly relative to one another and to the light
propeller curve; and can explain that the relation is a **deliberate mismatch** rather than a compromise.

**Ambiguities adjudicated:**

- "Maximum rated output of the propulsion plant" is read as the **specified maximum continuous rating**, not
  the overload limit. The answer says so by placing SMCR explicitly on the diagram.
- "The power for which the propeller is designed" is read as the **propeller design point on the light
  propeller curve** - design speed in the trial condition. It is the only reading that makes limb B a question
  about margins.
- The stem does not distinguish fixed pitch from controllable pitch. The answer is written for fixed pitch,
  which is the default reading, and states the CPP case **separately** rather than silently assuming one.

**Target answer shape.** Seven numbered sections under two limb headings, with the margins in a four-column
table and the four diagram points in a two-column table.

### 5.1 THE DECLARED DEPARTURE FROM THE ANCHOR

The anchor at section 6 directed that **no margin figure be quoted on Q7**, on the footing that such figures
are unverifiable quantities that `PASTPAPER_PRODUCTION_PROTOCOL.md` section 2.1 requires to be omitted.

**That direction is departed from, deliberately and openly.** Three reasons:

1. **The figures are not invented quantities.** They are attributable engine-builder project guidance, and
   they are **already published in the MIW built set at house standard**, in `QP2511-Q2`, with that
   attribution. Suppressing them here while publishing them there would leave the product inconsistent with
   itself.
2. **The stem expressly says "enumerate the safety margins".** A margin named without any indication of its
   order is not an enumeration of a safety margin; it is a list of words. Omitting the ranges would produce a
   **wrong** answer, not a cautious one.
3. **The protocol's rule is against false precision, not against attributed ranges.** The figures are given as
   *typical ranges from the engine builder's published guidance, not requirements*, with the express statement
   that a particular ship's values come from her own project documents.

The departure is recorded in four places so it cannot be lost: the answer's own uncertainty section, the
question's `unresolved` list, this record, and the paper anchor. **Founder review should confirm the departure

## 9. ANSWER ROUTE AS BUILT

Archetype `explain`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route.

**1. The propeller law, and what a propeller demand curve is** - limb **A** - cube and square; a family of
curves, not one; the light propeller curve as reference; every deterioration moves it left.

**2. The significance of the propeller curve** - limb **A** - the matching tool; it defines the engine's
working line; it shows whether the operating point is safe; it is the diagnostic instrument for hull and
propeller condition; it is the basis of the margins; it underlies performance monitoring; the CPP case.

**3. The engine layout and load diagram** - limb **A** - layout diagram bounds the choice of rating, load
diagram bounds operation; speed limit, torque/speed limit, mep limit, overload limit, minimum speed; the
torque limit binds at reduced speed and is a thermal protection; heavy running moves up and to the left.

**4. The safety margins, enumerated** - limb **A** - light running, sea and engine margins in a table with
what each buys and its typical order; the torque and overload limits as boundaries rather than margins; the
margins are not interchangeable; the 20%-to-1% sensitivity.

**5. The four points on the diagram** - limb **B** - propeller design point on the light curve; service
propulsion point = PD + sea margin; SMCR = SP + engine margin; layout displaced right by the light running
margin.

**6. The relation itself** - limb **B** - rated output exceeds propeller design power and sits at higher
revolutions; vertical and horizontal displacement explained; the deliberate mismatch; the life cycle from
light running to heavy running; the over-generous case; the CPP case; the overload and power-limitation
ceilings.

**7. When the relation breaks down - and what the Chief Engineer does** - limb **B** - recognise from the
diagram; confirm instrumentation; exclude environment; separate hull from propeller; protect the engine;
remedy; report, and where authority stops.
