## 2. WHAT THE QUESTION ASKS

Two limbs, each with an internal three- or four-part structure. Limb (a) is really three asks: the ship
identification number requirements, the CSR requirements, and - the reasoning task - why the two together
enhance maritime safety. Limb (b) is four asks: what the ESP is, which vessels are subject to it, its key
components, and its objectives.

**Mark allocation.** 16, set as 8 + 8, printed against each limb. Within limb (a) the significance question is
the part most often truncated and it carries real weight. Within limb (b) four asks share eight marks, which
forces the components into a table rather than into prose.

**Examiner expectation.** Regulation-accurate detail - the two marking locations and their two minimum
heights, the CSR's issuing authority and its content, the ships caught by regulation XI-1/2 and the
instrument that makes the ESP mandatory - and then the understanding that chapter XI-1 exists to stop a ship
shedding her identity and her record on a change of owner.

**Required answer components:** the purpose of chapter XI-1; regulation XI-1/3 application, marking locations,
heights and method; regulation XI-1/3-1 as the companion identity requirement; regulation XI-1/5 issuer,
purpose and minimum content; the CSR keeping rules - amendment within three months, language, prohibition on
erasure, left on board on transfer, previous records appended, available for inspection; the safety
significance reasoned rather than asserted; SOLAS XI-1/2 and A.1049(27); the ships caught by the SOLAS
definitions; the components of an enhanced survey; the four load-bearing ESP definitions; and the objectives.

**Ambiguities adjudicated:**

- The stem writes "Enhanced Survey Program" where the instrument is the *International Code on the Enhanced
  Programme of Inspections during Surveys of Bulk Carriers and Oil Tankers*. The answer gives the Code's own
  title once and uses the examiner's term thereafter. **The stem is not corrected.**
- "Key components" is not a defined term. It is read as the elements the Code itself sections - planning,
  documentation, conditions and access, overall survey, close-up survey, thickness measurement, tank testing,
  dry-dock survey, reporting and evaluation.
- "What type of vessels" is answered from the SOLAS definitions that regulation XI-1/2 cites - IX/1.6 and
  II-1/2.22 - rather than by a general description of bulk carriers and tankers.

**Target answer shape.** Eight numbered sections under two limb headings. Limb (a): purpose, the number with a
two-row marking table, the CSR content as a numbered list because the regulation is a list, the keeping rules,
then the significance reasoned as a list. Limb (b): what the ESP is and who is caught, a nine-row component
table with the four load-bearing definitions beneath it, and the objectives closing on the same
continuity-of-record theme as limb (a).

## 9. ANSWER ROUTE AS BUILT

Archetype `explain`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route.

**1. Where these requirements sit, and what they were written to solve** - limb **(a)**

- Chapter XI-1, special measures to enhance maritime safety; the problem is a ship that can change identity
- Operative text at this sitting is the Consolidated Edition 2024
- The rest of the chapter named so the two regulations are placed in context

**2. The ship identification number - regulation XI-1/3** - limb **(a)**

- Passenger ships 100 GT and upwards; cargo ships 300 GT and upwards
- Allocated to the hull; does not change on sale, rename or re-flag; inserted on I/12 and I/13 certificates
- Two markings: externally visible 200 mm, internally accessible 100 mm
- Contrasting colour, clear of other markings, not easily expunged; Administration approves for non-metal ships
- Regulation 3-1 completes the identity set

**3. The Continuous Synopsis Record - regulation XI-1/5** - limb **(a)**

- Issued by the Administration to every ship to which chapter I applies
- An on-board record of the history of the ship; from 1 July 2004 for older ships
- The fifteen minimum content items

**4. Keeping the CSR - the rules that give it its value** - limb **(a)**

- Changes with their history; revised CSR or amendments within three months; interim amendment by Company or master
- English, French or Spanish; format developed by the Organization
- Previous entries never modified, deleted, erased or defaced
- Left on board on sale or transfer; previous records forwarded and appended; always available for inspection

**5. Why the two together enhance maritime safety** - limb **(a)**

- Defeat identity laundering; give the PSCO a targeting picture; make flag- and class-hopping visible
- Tie the ship to a traceable Company and registered owner; support casualty investigation; make certificate
  fraud harder

**6. What the ESP is, and which ships are subject to it** - limb **(b)**

- A prescribed depth of hull inspection, carried out during the I/10 surveys - not an extra survey
- XI-1/2 catches bulk carriers (IX/1.6) and oil tankers (II-1/2.22); mandatory through A.1049(27)
- Annex A bulk carriers, Parts A and B for single- and double-side skin; Annex B oil tankers, double-hull part
- Annex A Part A applies to self-propelled bulk carriers of 500 GT and above
- XI-1/2-1 harmonization for ships outside the Code

**7. The key components of an enhanced survey** - limb **(b)**

- Survey programme and planning meeting; documentation on board; conditions and access
- Overall survey; close-up survey within reach of hand
- Thickness measurement by a certified company; tank pressure testing; dry-dock survey
- Reporting and evaluation, and the Condition Evaluation Report
- The four load-bearing definitions: transverse section, suspect areas, substantial corrosion at 75% of
  allowable margins, coating GOOD / FAIR / POOR - and coating condition driving survey frequency

**8. The objectives of the ESP** - limb **(b)**

- Find wastage and cracking before casualty; standardise the extent; make condition measurable
- Escalate automatically on finding defects; force planning and owner participation
- Build a structural history that survives the change of owner - the same theme as limb (a)
