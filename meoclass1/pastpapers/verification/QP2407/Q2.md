## 2. WHAT THE QUESTION ASKS

Four short notes on one subject, from four directions: the overall comparison, the hydraulic supply and its
local distribution, the control valve that replaced the cam, and the injection actuator itself. The examiner
is testing whether the candidate understands the **architecture** of the engine rather than whether he can
recite a parts list.

**Mark allocation.** 16, printed as 4 + 4 + 4 + 4. Equal marks means equal time, and the specific risk here is
**limb (a)**, which invites an over-long list and can consume the time the other three limbs need. **Limb (b)
contains two distinct questions** - the purpose of the HPS, and what the HCU is and contains - and must be
split.

**Examiner expectation.** For limb (a), well-chosen differences that show the architecture. For limb (b), the
**purpose** of the HPS stated as a purpose, and a genuine component list for the HCU. For limb (c), the
function of the valves **and the ELFI-versus-FIVA distinction**. For limb (d), a **comparison** - the stem
says "differences" - centred on how pressure is generated and on the independence of injection pressure from
engine speed.

**Ambiguities adjudicated:**

- The stem writes **"ELFI/FIVA valve"** as though they were one item. They are **alternatives, not a pair**,
  and the answer distinguishes them explicitly rather than treating the solidus as a synonym.
- **"The ME engine" covers a family of variants** which differ, most importantly in whether the exhaust valve
  actuation is electronic or remains mechanically driven. The answer says **variants differ** rather than
  presenting one arrangement as universal.
- **"HPS system"** is tautologous as printed. The term is used as the examiner uses it and is not corrected.

**Target answer shape.** Six numbered sections, no limb headings: one establishing the single architectural
change; one for limb (a) as a thirteen-row comparison table; two for limb (b); one for limb (c) with a
two-row ELFI/FIVA table; and one for limb (d) built as an explicit before-and-after comparison table.

distribution and actuation block; block and galleries, booster, ELFI or FIVA, exhaust valve actuator and its

control unit; the two-row table distinguishing them; position feedback; what electronic control buys; the
exhaust valve's gained freedom; the NOx Technical File constraint; the contamination vulnerability.

**6. The fuel pressure booster compared with the conventional fuel pump** - limb **(d)** - the jerk pump and
its two consequences; the booster as an intensifier working on area ratio; an eight-row comparison table;
what the change buys operationally and what it costs.
