## 9. ANSWER ROUTE AS BUILT

Archetype `legal`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route.

**1. Where these obligations live, and the one rule that makes Title 5 different**

- Articles, Regulations, Code Part A mandatory and Part B guidance; Title 5 divides into 5.1, 5.2 and 5.3
- Article VI(3) and (4) substantial equivalence **does not apply** to Part A of the Code in Title 5
- Equality before the law; Title 5 does not determine jurisdiction or venue

**2. Flag State - the general responsibility and the system it must build** - limb **(a)**

- Responsible for implementation on ships flying its flag; effective inspection and certification system
- Clear objectives and standards; a copy of the Convention on board; article 22 reporting

**3. Recognized organizations - what may be delegated, and what may not** - limb **(a)**

- May authorize; **remains fully responsible**
- Competency and independence; four capability tests
- Authorization must at minimum empower rectification and inspection at a port State's request
- Current list to the ILO, made publicly available

**4. The Maritime Labour Certificate and the DMLC** - limb **(a)**

- 500 GT and over internationally or between foreign ports; others on request; prima facie evidence
- Five years; intermediate between the second and third anniversary dates; renewal windows; five-month
  extension; six-month interim with four verifications and no second interim
- DMLC Part I competent authority, Part II shipowner, Part II certified
- Carriage, posting, English translation; five cessation grounds; withdrawal on seriousness or frequency
- Appendix A5-I, sixteen matters

**5. Inspection and enforcement** - limb **(a)**

- Regular inspections; interval **shall in no case exceed three years**
- Qualified independent inspectors; board, examine, require rectification, prohibit sailing; right of appeal
- Discretion to advise; confidentiality of the source; no conflict of interest
- Report to authority, master and notice board; annual report within six months; major-incident report within
  one month; avoid unreasonable detention; compensation; adequate penalties

**6. On-board complaint procedures and marine casualties** - limb **(a)**

- Fair, effective, expeditious; victimization prohibited and penalized
- Lowest level, but direct access to the master and to external authorities preserved
- Copy of the procedures, contacts, and a named impartial adviser on board
- Regulation 5.1.6 casualty inquiry, report normally made public

**7. Port State - the starting position** - limb **(b)**

- Any foreign ship calling may be inspected under Article V(4)
- **Must accept** the certificate and DMLC as prima facie evidence; inspection limited to reviewing them
- Limited to the Articles, the Regulations and **Part A only** of the Code

**8. The more detailed inspection, and detention** - limb **(b)**

- Four triggers; mandatory where a clear hazard or serious breach is in issue; Appendix A5-III scope
- Deficiencies to the master with deadlines; referral to organizations, flag State and next port; report may
  go to the Director-General
- **Two detention grounds**; rectification or an accepted plan of action; flag State notified forthwith;
  compensation for undue detention

**9. Onshore complaint handling, and the third leg of Title 5** - limb **(b)**

- Complaint to an authorized officer; initial investigation; resolution sought on board
- Unresolved - flag State notified for advice and a corrective plan of action
- Still unresolved - report to the Director-General; statistics submitted; confidentiality safeguarded
- Regulation 5.3 labour-supplying responsibilities complete the scheme
