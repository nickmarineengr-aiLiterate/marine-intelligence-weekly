## 2. WHAT THE QUESTION ASKS

**Six asks in two limbs.** Limb (a): what the clause is, how it protects the shipowner in general average, and
its implications for **both** shipowners **and** cargo owners. Limb (b): what the clause is, its implications
for **both** insurer **and** shipowner, and how it **influences shipowner behaviour** in risk management and
vessel operation.

**Mark allocation.** 8 + 8. Within each limb the definition is perhaps a third of the marks; the implications
and the behavioural question are the rest - which is the reverse of how most candidates allocate their time.

**Examiner expectation.** That the candidate knows **which contract each clause belongs to**; can explain the
New Jason Clause as the repair of a gap between the carriage exemption and the no-contribution rule, with the
seaworthiness condition; can state what the collision clause covers, what it excludes and where the excluded
heads go; and can connect the whole to how a shipowner actually behaves.

**Ambiguities adjudicated:**

- "Maritime emergency" is not a term of art. It is read as the casualty situation in which a general average
  act is made - stranding, fire, machinery failure requiring salvage, or deviation to a port of refuge.
- "The 3/4ths Collision Clause in maritime insurance" is read as the collision liability provision of a hull
  and machinery policy **in its standard market form**. The answer states expressly that the fraction is a
  feature of the policy form and that some modern forms give four-fourths.
- "How does this clause influence the behaviour of shipowners" is read as a real question about incentives,
  not as an invitation to repeat the definition.

**Target answer shape.** Eight numbered sections: two framing, three for limb (a), three for limb (b), with a
two-column implications table in each limb.

### 9.2 Why Rule D matters here

The desktop build described the historical gap correctly but never cited the rule that creates it.
Rule D provides that rights to contribution **shall not be affected** by the fault of a party,
*"but this shall not prejudice any remedies or defences which may be open against or to that party
in respect of such fault."* The adjustment proceeds; the fault is relocated into remedies and
defences under the applicable law. Whether the shipowner **keeps** what the adjustment awards him is
therefore an open question — and that residue is precisely what the New Jason Clause closes.

### 9.3 A corpus object was found to be wrong

`TRAP-RULE-D-FAULT` states that the "YAR 2016 Paramount Clause reinforces: GA barred where fault of
party claiming". The Rule Paramount concerns whether sacrifice or expenditure was **reasonably made
or incurred** — a reasonableness gate, not a fault gate — and no definition object in the package
states it at all.

**This answer was therefore written from the verbatim `YAR-D` text, not from the trap gloss.** Had
the gloss been followed, the answer would have told candidates that fault bars contribution outright,
which is the opposite of what Rule D says. Raised as **TSCR-6**; not corrected in place.
