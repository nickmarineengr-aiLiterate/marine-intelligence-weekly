## 2. WHAT THE QUESTION ASKS

Four asks in one unlimbed 16-mark question: the key **objectives**; the key **measures**; the **short-,
mid- and long-term goals**; and the **potential impact on the maritime industry**. The fourth is an
evaluation rather than a recall and carries real marks.

**Mark allocation.** 16, printed as a single block. The absence of limbs is the difficulty. The allocation
adopted gives roughly a quarter to ambitions and checkpoints, a quarter to the measures by timeline, a
quarter to industry impact, and the remainder to identification, principles, timetable and review.

**Examiner expectation.** The correct resolution, named and dated; the four levels of ambition and the two
indicative checkpoints stated with their figures and correctly distinguished; the measures classified by the
Strategy's own timeline definitions; and an industry impact that goes beyond "fuel will cost more".

**Required answer components:** the instrument and what it revoked; the character of the instrument as policy
rather than regulation; vision and objectives; the four levels of ambition; the two indicative checkpoints and
the intensity-versus-absolute distinction; the guiding principles and the tension within them; the short-term
measures and their 1 January 2026 review deadline; the basket with its technical and economic elements and its
standing at this sitting; the long-term definition and the 2028 review; the follow-up timetable; the
impact-assessment criteria; and a reasoned industry impact.

**Ambiguities adjudicated:**

- "The IMO's revised strategy" is not further identified. At this sitting it can only mean the 2023 Strategy,
  `MEPC.377(80)`, and the answer says so in its first line.
- "Goals" in the stem maps onto **two** different things in the Strategy - levels of ambition and indicative
  checkpoints. The answer keeps them apart rather than merging them, because merging them is the standard
  error.
- "Long-term goals" has **no content** in the Strategy beyond the definition of long-term measures and the
  2028 review. That is stated honestly rather than filled in with invented content.

## 9. ANSWER ROUTE AS BUILT

Archetype `explain`. The principal headings of the model answer ARE this route.

**1. Which instrument is 'the revised strategy', and what it replaced** - `MEPC.377(80)`, 7 July 2023, MEPC
80; revokes `MEPC.304(72)` as from that date; a policy instrument, not a regulation; the Strategy's own
emission-share figures.

**2. The vision and the objectives** - phase out as soon as possible with a just and equitable transition; the
three objectives at 1.10 with their Paris Agreement and SDG 13 links.

**3. The levels of ambition** - new-ship efficiency requirements to be strengthened; intensity down at least
40% by 2030; zero or near-zero energy at least 5% striving for 10% by 2030; net zero by or around 2050; the
net-zero-GHG and "by or around" qualifications; well-to-wake.

**4. The indicative checkpoints** - at least 20% striving for 30% by 2030; at least 70% striving for 80% by
2040; absolute against intensity, and why that makes the 2023 Strategy a step change.

**5. The guiding principles** - no more favourable treatment; CBDR-RC; full effect regardless of flag;
impacts on States; evidence-based with the precautionary approach; and the tension between the first two.

**6. Short-term measures - and where they stand at this sitting** - the 2018-2023 definition; EEDI and SEEMP;
the DCS; EEXI, CII and the enhanced SEEMP; the 1 January 2026 review deadline, running and unreported.

**7. The basket of candidate mid-term measures** - to be finalized and agreed by 2025; the technical element,
a goal-based marine fuel standard; the economic element, a GHG emissions pricing mechanism; the other
candidate measures; CII synergies; and the exact standing at this sitting - neither approved nor adopted, and
nothing in force.

**8. Long-term measures, the timetable and the review** - beyond 2030 via the 2028 review; the follow-up
timetable; the eight impact-assessment criteria; the five-yearly review.

**9. The potential impact on the maritime industry** - fuels judged well-to-wake; newbuilding and design;
existing tonnage under EEXI and CII; operating cost; chartering and contracts; safety and the human element;
training and manning; developing States; regional measures; and the plain closing summary for a Chief
Engineer.
