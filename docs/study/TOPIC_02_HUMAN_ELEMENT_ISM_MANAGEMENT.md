## MENTAL SKELETON

Twelve branches. This is the largest topic in the corpus and the skeleton is
correspondingly wide — but branches 1–3 carry the examiner chain and branches
4–9 carry the oral. Own those nine and 195 examiner-evidenced questions become
constructible.

1. **The ISM architecture** — objective and functional requirements, what an
   SMS must contain, the DPA's role and direct access, the Master's overriding
   authority, the company's responsibility and authority. *(Node 02; the single
   most examiner-broad question in the topic, `QB4_C#q16`, sits here.)*
2. **Certification and the verification cycle** — DOC and SMC, interim DOC/SMC,
   initial / annual / intermediate / renewal verification, **additional
   verification**, what makes a DOC withdrawable, and the distinction between an
   **ISM audit and an ISM survey**. *(Node 02; the D01 hinge.)*
3. **Non-conformity to correction** — observation → NC → **major NC** → root
   cause → corrective and preventive action → timescales → close-out → master's
   review → management review → continuous improvement. *(Node 02; this is the
   chain examiners walk.)*
4. **The human element itself** — human element vs human factors vs human
   error; active failures and latent conditions; the socio-technical system;
   just culture; how HE factors are examined in a casualty investigation.
   *(Node 13; written ×2 directly, plus the whole fatigue cluster.)*
5. **Fatigue, fitness for duty and rest** — STCW Chapter VIII, MSC.1/Circ.1598
   fatigue guidance, hours of work and rest under MLC and STCW, rest splitting,
   watch composition, drug and alcohol. *(Node 13; **the ×8 written family — the
   most repeated family in the whole D03 corpus**.)*
6. **MLC 2006** — the five Titles and the Regulation / Standard A / Guideline B
   structure; Maritime Labour Certificate and DMLC Part I vs Part II; financial
   security and abandonment; the onboard and onshore complaint procedure; MLC
   detainable deficiencies at PSC; RPSL and the Indian grievance route.
   *(Node 08; a 10-question written cluster.)*
7. **STCW competence and the CE as trainer** — Manila 2010 amendments, CoC vs
   CoP, competence vs performance, training needs analysis, TEAP / META / VICT,
   appraisal of a junior engineer, mentoring, counselling, discipline.
   *(Node 14.)*
8. **Leadership, motivation and team management** — Maslow, principles of
   motivation, leadership styles and theories, assertiveness and empathy,
   delegation and prioritisation, conflict management, Johari window,
   organisational behaviour, multinational and cross-cultural crews.
   *(Node 13.)*
9. **Communication and information flow** — Engine Room Resource Management,
   authority gradient, briefing and toolbox talk, barriers to communication,
   how a shore-office instruction is turned into shipboard action, emergency
   communication under ISM element 8. *(Node 13; a 6-question written cluster
   and the larger half of QP2608-Q8.)*
10. **Risk, safety culture and formal assessment** — hazard vs risk, the risk
    assessment matrix, ALARP, HAZID / HAZOP / JSA, permit to work, Management
    of Change, Formal Safety Assessment, root cause analysis and fault tree
    analysis, TMSA and vetting-driven safety culture. *(Nodes 12 and 13.)*
11. **Management response to an engine-room emergency** — this is the
    *management* half of node 12, and it is why questions that look like
    machinery questions are filed here: sudden loss of propulsion, main engine
    failing to respond to bridge control, safe isolation and restoration of the
    main switchboard, bunker shortage. Answer them as **command, reporting,
    delegation and restoration**, not as fault-finding. *(Node 12.)*
12. **Ship management economics and information** — capital / voyage /
    operating cost and who bears each; inventory control, ABC and VED analysis,
    economic order quantity, re-order point and safety stock; MIS and Decision
    Support Systems; records, standing orders and the night order book; and the
    digitalisation cluster (big data, IoT, cyber risk, VR/AR, 3D printing).
    *(Nodes 15, 16, 17 and supporting 19.)*
